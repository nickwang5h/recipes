*Yasai itame* is a Japanese home-style vegetable stir-fry, crisp-tender and lightly sweet, with soy sauce and oyster sauce only thinly coating the ingredients; thin pork is often added, and it is eaten with rice. Cabbage brings sweetness, while the carrot, snow peas and bean sprouts each have their own texture. The pork is cooked first and the vegetables go in one after another, so everything in the dish finishes at the same time.

What you control in this dish is the water in the wok. Drain the vegetables well after washing, cut the carrot thin and slice the thick cabbage ribs thin, so everything cooks through in a short time (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). On a home stove, piling in wet vegetables all at once drops the wok temperature, and the dish turns into boiled vegetables; spreading them out, tossing, and seasoning at the end reduces pooled liquid (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the pork into bite-sized pieces, toss with the marinade soy sauce and sake, and refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the slices have taken up the seasoning. Use this time to cut the vegetables and drain off the wash water.
2. Preheat a wide, flat-bottomed pan over high heat for 1 minute, add the vegetable oil, turn to medium heat and stir-fry the ginger and garlic for 0.5 minutes, until fragrant and the garlic is still pale.
3. Add the pork and spread it out. Stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the slices loosen and show no raw colour. Cut one open: when the centre reaches 71°C (160°F) it is done. Move it to a clean plate.
4. In the same pan over high heat, stir-fry the onion for 1 minute, until the layers separate and the edges begin to turn translucent. Add the carrot and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the thin strips can bend.
5. Add the cabbage and snow peas and stir-fry for 2 minutes, until the cabbage leaves are slightly soft and the thick ribs still have some bite. Keep turning the vegetables up from the bottom of the pan so every part touches the hot pan.
6. Add the bean sprouts and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the stems are heated through and slightly softened. Return the pork, add the stir-fry soy sauce and oyster sauce, and stir-fry for 1 minute more, until the meat and vegetables are evenly seasoned and there is no puddle of water at the bottom of the pan.
7. Taste, then add the salt and black pepper, drizzle in the toasted sesame oil, and toss for 0.5 minutes, until the aroma spreads, then take it off the heat. Serve the meat and vegetables out together so they do not keep sitting in the hot pan and go soft.

## Key points

- **A lot of water on the plate**: the vegetables were not drained, or the pan was too small to evaporate the water as it came out. Use a wide pan, move on to the next step as soon as the vegetables soften, and leave the salt to the end. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) and [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Tough pork and hard carrot**: the pork cooked first but stayed in the pan, or the carrot was cut too thick. Take the pork out first, cut the carrot into even thin strips, and combine them at the end. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Vegetables wet and soft in a lunch box**: residual heat and reheating keep cooking the vegetables. Stop the first stir-fry when they are just cooked and the pan has no pooled liquid; before packing, spread them out to cool for about 10 minutes, until they no longer give off obvious steam, then refrigerate promptly. The next day, reheat to 74°C (165°F) in the centre. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
