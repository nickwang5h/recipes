*Cotoletta alla milanese* has a golden, crisp crust outside and thick, juicy veal inside. Bone-in veal rib chops are coated in beaten egg and then dry breadcrumbs, and fried in butter. The Milanese tradition keeps the rib bone and the thickness of the chop; the egg makes the breadcrumbs stick, and the hot fat dries the outer layer into a crisp crust (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).

The hard part is getting the outside and the inside done together. The crust touches hot butter directly and colours quickly, but heat takes time to reach the centre (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep the butter golden with no black bits, and fry the breadcrumbs only to golden. Using butter all the way through leaves a milky flavour.

## Steps

1. Blot the chops dry and snip a few shallow cuts through the tight fascia around the edge. Tap the raised areas lightly with the flat side of a meat mallet to even out the thickness, still keeping about 2 cm of thickness; do not pound them into large thin sheets.
2. Beat the eggs in a shallow dish and put the breadcrumbs in another, dry dish. Dip each chop in the egg, lift it and let the excess drain off, then press breadcrumbs onto both sides. Fill in around the edges, leaving the bone handle uncoated.
3. Melt the butter in a wide frying pan in which the chops can lie in a single layer, and heat over medium heat until it foams. If the pan is small, cook in two batches and divide the butter into two portions; before each batch there should be enough fat to wet the bottom of the pan.
4. Put in the chops and keep the pan bubbling briskly with the butter golden. Fry for about 7–8 minutes first, until the underside has formed a firm golden crust, then flip with a wide spatula and tongs. If dark brown bits appear in the butter, lower the heat at once.
5. Fry the other side for about 7–8 minutes, then insert a thermometer from the side of the chop, away from the bone, and check that the thickest part has reached 71°C (160°F). If the crust is already golden but the centre has not reached that temperature, turn to low heat and keep frying.
6. Set the chops on a rack to drain briefly, sprinkle with salt and serve at once with lemon wedges. Keep the chops apart from each other and do not cover them; squeeze the lemon juice over your own piece only just before eating.

## Key points

- **Crust black but the meat by the bone not done**: the heat was too high, or the chops vary in thickness. Lower the heat once coloured and check the temperature of the thickest part; colour does not mean the centre is cooked (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Coating slides off in a sheet**: the surface of the meat was wet, too much egg was left on, or the breadcrumbs were not pressed on firmly. Blot dry, drain the egg before pressing in the crumbs, and support the whole chop when flipping (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Bitter taste and black specks all over the pan**: the milk solids in the butter and fallen breadcrumbs have been overheated. Keep it golden and clear burnt bits out between batches; replace butter that has turned burnt and bitter (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Crisp out of the pan, soft once plated**: the hot chops were stacked or covered, and the steam wet the crust again. Spread them out to drain, and add the lemon juice only just before eating (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
