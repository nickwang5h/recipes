Guilin's eighteen stuffed delicacies are a set of dishes in which a filling is packed into another ingredient, and peppers, bitter melon, eggplant and tofu can all be the shell. The pork-stuffed green pepper below stands in for them, and making one plate practises filling, pan-frying to set and braising until cooked.

The ground pork is first stirred with soy sauce until sticky; salt helps the meat proteins bind to each other (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Pan-frying sets the exposed meat face, and the covered braise afterwards carries heat to the centre of the filling. Only once the [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) is complete does the filling hold its full shape.

## Steps

1. Wash and dry the green peppers, cut off the stem end, and scoop out the seeds and white ribs with a small spoon or chopstick, keeping the pepper wall intact. Choose a batch of similar size; wide peppers can be halved lengthwise to make the filling thinner.
2. Finely chop the green onion and ginger and mix them into the ground pork, along with the 15 ml of light soy sauce and 2 ml of dark soy sauce for the filling. Stir in one direction until the filling is sticky and gathers into a mass, and refrigerate for 10 minutes.
3. Blot the inside of the peppers dry and dust thinly with 5 g of starch, tipping out any extra loose starch. Fill with the meat in portions using a chopstick, pressing lightly each time so the filling sticks to the wall with few gaps, and smooth the opening level. The pepper skin should not be stretched open.
4. Stir the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and 100 ml of water together for the braising liquid. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-low and put the peppers meat face down first to pan-fry and set, then turn them onto the skin side. Fry large peppers in batches so the pan is not crowded.
5. Turn the peppers gently and fry until each side has light brown spots, the skin wrinkles and the meat face no longer feels loose. Pour in the mixed braising liquid, bring to a boil, cover, and braise over medium-low for 5–8 minutes so steam surrounds the peppers.
6. Check the largest pepper by pushing a thermometer in through the opening to the centre of the filling, which must reach 71°C (160°F). If it has not, keep braising covered, and add hot water as the bottom of the pan is about to dry.
7. Once cooked through, uncover and reduce the sauce until it thinly coats the peppers. If you want to thicken, mix the optional starch with 15 ml of cold water and drizzle it into the boiling liquid in portions, pushing gently until it turns glossy; stop when it is thick enough, and you do not need to use it all.
8. Lift the peppers by the body onto a plate and spoon the remaining sauce over. The pepper skin should be soft and intact, and the filling should stay in as one piece; when cut open, the centre is hot all through with no runny raw filling.

## Key points

- **The filling falls out when turned**: the inside was wet, the filling was not stirred until sticky, or the meat face had not set. See [salt](/principles/salt/); stir until sticky and pack it tight, then wait until the meat face has set in the pan before turning.
- **Pepper skin scorched but the filling is raw**: there was pan-frying only and no braising to carry heat to the centre. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/); add the liquid, cover the pan and extend the time according to the temperature of the largest filling.
- **Hard filling that gives off lots of water when cut**: it was cooked on long after it was done, and the protein network tightened. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/); finish when it reaches doneness, and if there is a lot of sauce, take out the peppers first and then reduce it.
