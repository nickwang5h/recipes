In Chinese sausage and potato rice, each grain of rice is coated with sausage fat, salty-fragrant with a little sweetness, and the potato is soft but keeps its shape. The sausage is rendered first, the aroma of ginger and green onion goes into the fat, and then it coats the surface of the rice (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Cantonese sausage is already sweet, so soy sauce adds saltiness and colour and no sugar is added.

The potatoes are cut small so that they and the rice finish cooking at nearly the same time. The rice is soaked first so the grains take up water, then cooked with a measured amount of water, and the [starch in the rice gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) into soft rice. The lid holds in steam, and after the heat is off the leftover heat finishes the cooking, so the rice at the bottom doesn't need to stay on the heat the whole time (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Rinse the rice, put it in 600 ml of cold water and soak for 20 minutes, until the grains swell slightly and look white on the surface. Pour into a sieve and drain for 2 minutes, until the water no longer runs off in a stream. While the rice soaks, cut the potato and sausage and mince the ginger and green onion; once the potato is cut, rinse off the starchy bits and pat dry.
2. Use a heavy-bottomed pot about 20 cm across with a lid that fits snugly, add the oil and potatoes, and pan-fry over medium heat for 5–6 minutes, until several sides are pale gold and the corners are still intact; the centres can still be raw.
3. Push the potatoes to the side of the pot and put the sausage, minced ginger and green onion whites in the middle. Stir-fry over medium-low heat for 2 minutes, until the fat in the sausage turns translucent, the bottom of the pot smells of rendered fat, and the minced onion hasn't blackened.
4. Add the drained rice, light soy sauce and dark soy sauce and stir over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until the grains are evenly coloured. Pour in the 260 ml of water for cooking the rice, gently scrape the bottom loose, and level the rice, keeping most of the potato and sausage on top.
5. Cook over medium-high heat for about 3 minutes, until small bubbles appear both at the edge and in the middle. Cover tightly, turn to the lowest heat and cook for 15–18 minutes, until the water is absorbed and only a faint steam sound is left at the edge of the pot. Turn off the heat, move the pot off the burner if it is still hot, and leave it covered for 10 minutes.
6. Open the lid and check: the rice centres are soft, there is no loose water at the bottom, a chopstick goes through the potato easily, and the centre of the thickest sausage slice reaches 71°C (160°F). If the rice still has a hard core, add 30 ml of hot water around the edge, cover and cook on low heat for 3 minutes, then turn off the heat and leave covered for 5 minutes, until the hard core is gone.
7. Sprinkle with the green onion greens and fluff gently from the bottom with a rice paddle, so the sausage and potato are scattered through the rice, taking care not to mash the soft potato. Taste, and add salt if it is bland.

## Key points

- **The rice on top is undercooked and the bottom scorches first**: the heat was too high or the lid leaked, and the water ran out first (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a well-fitting lid, turn to low as soon as it boils, and add water only a little at a time around the edge.
- **The potato turns to mush and the rice clumps together**: the pieces were cut too small, or the rice was pressed and stirred repeatedly after cooking (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep pieces about 1.5 cm, leave them on top of the rice after frying, and fold gently when serving.
- **It sets into a hard lump in a lunch box**: the starch in cold rice retrogrades (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Fluff before packing, make sure there is no water at the bottom of the pot, divide into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly; the next day, stir once while reheating and check that the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
