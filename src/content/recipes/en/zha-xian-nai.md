Fried milk has a golden, crisp shell and, when you bite it open, a warm, smooth milky centre. The method is to cook milk first into a custard that can be cut, then coat it in flour, beaten egg and bread crumbs and fry it briefly until golden. The custard holds its shape through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) plus the gel structure that forms as it cools; chilling milk alone will not set it.

With 250 ml of milk to 30 g of cornstarch, the milk paste has to be cooked through for the custard to hold up. The oil only sets the outer layer and dries it; the milk centre was already cooked long before. Fry in batches and hold the [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/), so the strips spend less time in the oil. This batch makes about twelve strips and needs at least two hours of chilling, so plan ahead.

## Steps

1. Stir 80 ml of cold milk with the 30 g of cornstarch until there are no visible lumps. Put the other 170 ml of milk and the 25 g of sugar in a pot and heat on low, stirring, until the sugar dissolves.
2. Stir the starch liquid that has sunk to the bottom, then pour it slowly into the pot while scraping the bottom and corners of the pot constantly. Cook on low heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the milk paste is clearly thicker and starts to bubble, and the track scraped through it holds for about 3 seconds before closing; then turn off the heat.
3. Grease the inside of a small square heatproof container with about 2 ml of oil, pour in the milk paste and level it. Choose a container that lets this batch spread to about 2 cm thick; about 12 cm square is a place to start, and do not spread it into a thin sheet.
4. When the paste no longer gives off much steam, cover it and refrigerate for at least 2 hours; it does not need to come to room temperature. When you take it out, the whole block should come out of the mould and not flow when lightly pressed. Cut into twelve strips, about 6 cm × 2 cm.
5. Set out the flour, the beaten egg and the bread crumbs separately. Coat the milk strips in turn with a thin layer of flour, egg and bread crumbs, covering every side and the ends. Press the crumbs on lightly and shake off any loose bits.
6. Add about 700 ml of frying oil to a small deep pot and heat to 165–175°C (325–350°F). Fry the strips in two or three batches, about 60–90 seconds per batch, turning once or twice gently, and lift out when evenly golden. Wait for the oil temperature to recover before the next batch.
7. Spread on paper towels to drain for about 2 minutes, without stacking. Break one open to let the centre lose some heat, and eat while warm, when the contrast of crisp shell and soft centre is strongest.

## Key points

- **The custard can't be cut**: the starch has not fully [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), or it was not chilled enough. While cooking, watch both the bubbling and whether the scraped track holds; after chilling, check that it comes out of the mould before you coat it.
- **Shell splits and the filling leaks**: the ends were left uncoated, the strips are too thin, or they were fried too long. Keep the thickness and coat every side; fry briefly at the [right oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/), and do not keep poking them in the pot.
- **Shell greasy and not crisp**: too many went in at once and the oil temperature dropped sharply. Put in fewer per batch, drain as soon as they come out and spread them out to let off steam, so the crisp shell is not softened by moisture running back.
