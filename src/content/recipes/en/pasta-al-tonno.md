This tuna and tomato pasta is savoury and rich, with a glossy, thick red sauce clinging to every strand. The anchovy melts into the sauce, so you taste no fillets, only savouriness, and the parsley adds a light freshness.

The passata is cooked down first in the pan, then cooked together with the tuna. Olive oil and the water in the passata do not mix on their own, so the oil is dispersed as droplets in the water, which keeps the sauce thick and glossy without separating (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). After the pasta is boiled, the gelatinized starch on its surface carries into the sauce. The starch thickens the liquid and keeps the oil droplets from merging, and the force of tossing breaks the oil into even smaller droplets. So the pasta goes into the sauce pan with a little cooking water and is tossed as it goes, which makes the sauce cling (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

## Steps

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Drain the oil from the tuna and flake it into small pieces with a fork. Chop the anchovies and mince the parsley.
2. Put the olive oil and garlic in a skillet and heat over low heat until the garlic turns golden. Add the anchovies and stir while heating until they melt into the oil.
3. Add the passata and cook for 10 minutes, until the colour deepens and the sauce thickens enough that a spatula dragged across the bottom of the pan leaves a visible track. Once the water boils, add salt and cook the spaghetti until it still has some bite: bite one strand and the centre should be slightly firm.
4. Add the tuna to the tomato sauce, season with salt and black pepper, and cook for about 5 minutes more, until the tuna has taken on the flavour and the sauce has thickened. Stir in the parsley.
5. Drain the pasta and put it into the sauce pan. Toss until every strand is coated and no oil collects at the bottom of the plate, then serve.

## Key points

- **The sauce is too dry**: the liquid in the pan reduced too fast. Watch the heat, and add a little pasta cooking water when tossing to help the sauce coat the pasta (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Oil floats on the sauce and the pasta will not hold the sauce**: the oil and water have not emulsified and the sauce is too thin, so the oil droplets gather on the surface ([emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Add a little pasta cooking water when tossing and keep tossing until the sauce thickens, turns glossy and clings to the pasta.
- **The garlic is bitter**: the oil was too hot and the garlic scorched. Heat over low heat and add the anchovies as soon as the garlic turns golden.
- **The sauce is too salty**: the tuna and the anchovies are both salty. Season with a little salt first, taste, then add more.
