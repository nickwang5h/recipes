Red-braised carp first sets the fish skin in hot oil, then braises the fish through in a soy sauce and vinegar liquid. Frying gives the outer layer its colour and aroma, and the centre of the fish is cooked by the simmering that follows (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The fat rendered from the pork belly adds fragrance to the sauce, and the meat slices stay in the pan as a side.

Once the fish is braised through, cut down on further heating. If the [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) keeps shrinking, the meat dries out, so when reducing the sauce you can lift the fish out and boil down only the liquid. A lot of green onion and ginger is used; take it out after braising, leaving the salty-sweet savoury flavour and the sourness of the vinegar.

## Steps

1. Clean out any leftover innards and blood along the bone in the belly, rinse, pat dry inside and out, and cut several diagonal slashes in the thick flesh on each side. Cut the white part of the leek into sections about 4 cm long and split them lengthwise, slice the ginger about 3 mm thick, snip the dried chilies into sections and slice the pork belly thin.
2. Add oil to a deep pot big enough for the whole fish and heat to about 180°C (350°F), measured with an oil thermometer. Slide the fish in gently along the side of the pot, leave it alone at first, and fry for about 1 minute until the skin is slightly firm and the whole fish can be lifted out intact, then lift out and drain.
3. Move the frying oil to a heatproof container and leave a layer of oil in the pot. Add the pork belly and cook over medium heat until the edges are slightly yellow and fat has rendered out, then add the leek, ginger, garlic cloves and dried chilies and stir-fry for about 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Return the fish, add the Shaoxing wine and aged vinegar along the side of the pot, then the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and water. The water should just cover the fish; keep the salt until the end. After it comes to a boil, turn to low heat, cover and braise for 15 minutes, keeping the surface of the liquid at a gentle bubble.
5. When the fish next to the bone at the thickest part is completely opaque and can be gently flaked with a fork, it is done; if any part is still translucent, keep braising gently. Once cooked, carefully take out the fish, pick out the leek, ginger, garlic and chilies, and leave the meat slices.
6. Reduce the sauce over high heat to about one quarter of its original amount, until it is deep and glossy and coats the back of a spoon. Taste and add salt if needed, add the minced garlic and cook it briefly until fragrant, then pour the sauce and meat slices over the fish.

## Key points

- **Skin sticks and tears, oil spatters**: the surface has too much water, or the fish was moved before the skin set (see [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Dry the fish, support the whole fish with a spatula, and lower the heat first if the oil smokes.
- **Flesh falls apart and is dry**: the liquid boiled hard, or the cooked fish stayed in the pot during a long reduction (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Braise gently and take the fish out first once it is cooked.
- **Too salty in the end**: the soy sauce and oyster sauce get saltier the more the sauce reduces (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add salt last, and stop reducing early if the liquid is already salty.
