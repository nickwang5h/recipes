Yaki udon has springy noodles, a sauce that only lightly coats them, and the moisture of the meat and vegetables tossed into the noodles. *Yaki udon* is a Japanese noodle dish of thick udon stir-fried with meat and vegetables. It uses thin pork belly, cabbage and shiitake, seasoned with soy sauce, sake, mirin and a little dashi granules.

Frozen precooked udon is loosened in boiling water first so it can be tossed whole; pulling the block apart by force breaks the noodles. The vegetables are covered and briefly steamed so the steam carries heat into the stalks, then uncovered to cook off the excess water. The two stages each have a purpose (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use the same wide, deep skillet throughout: after loosening the noodles, pour off the water, wipe the pan dry and carry on frying. The noodles, pork and vegetables are tossed together at the end, so the pan should be large, with room to turn from the bottom.

## Steps

1. Cut the pork and vegetables as described in the ingredients, and drain the washed vegetables. Stir the dashi granules, soy sauce, sake and mirin together in a small bowl until the dashi granules are evenly dispersed.
2. Put 1500 ml of water in a wide, deep skillet about 30–32 cm across, cover and bring to a boil over high heat for 5–7 minutes, until the water boils steadily. Add the frozen precooked udon and cook for 0.5–0.75 minutes, until the blocks can be gently teased apart, then lift into a colander right away and drain.
3. Pour the water out of the skillet and wipe the inside dry. Return it to the stove over medium heat for 1 minute, until there are no water droplets inside, and add the vegetable oil.
4. Add the pork belly, spread it out and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the slices turn white and curl slightly at the edges and the centre reaches 71°C (160°F). Sprinkle with black pepper, add the onion and stir-fry for 2 more minutes, until the layers are soft and the edges translucent.
5. Add the cabbage, carrot, shiitake and green onion and toss for 0.5 minutes, until everything is coated in oil. Cover, turn the heat to medium-low and steam for 3 minutes, until the cabbage is slightly soft and the shiitake have shrunk, but the stalks still hold their shape.
6. Uncover, turn the heat to medium-high, add the drained udon and toss gently with tongs for 1 minute, until the noodles separate from the meat and vegetables and are hot through.
7. Pour in the seasoning mixture and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the noodles are evenly coloured and no sauce pools in the bottom of the pan. Taste, and add salt if it is bland. Serve, topped with bonito flakes and beni shoga as you like.

## Key points

- **Udon breaks when tossed**: the frozen noodles were still hard, or were pulled apart roughly while loosening. Cook them in boiling water only until they separate naturally, drain, and lift and turn with tongs. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Noodles are wet and the flavour is diluted**: the water from loosening wasn't drained well, or the lid stayed on after the vegetables had steamed. Uncover as soon as they are steamed, and let the moisture cook off at the end until no liquid pools before serving. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Carrot is hard but cabbage is too soft**: the two vegetables differ too much in thickness. Cut the carrot into even thin sticks and shave down the thick cabbage stalks, so they cook through together in the short steam. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
