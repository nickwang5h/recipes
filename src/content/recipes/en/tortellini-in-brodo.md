*Tortellini in brodo* is the meat-filled egg pasta in broth of Emilia. The classic Bolognese filling uses pork loin, cured ham, mortadella and Parmigiano, shaped into small rings and cooked directly in a clear broth of beef and chicken. The savouriness of the filling goes into the broth, and the broth moistens the pasta. The cheese is already in the filling, so taste at the table before deciding whether to add more.

The pasta must be thin yet strong, held up by the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) network that forms once it is kneaded evenly. The egg in the filling sets in the hot broth and binds the finely ground meat and cheese together (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cooking the pork before grinding the filling lets the pasta cook through in a shorter time.

## Steps

1. Pan-fry the pork loin in the butter until browned on both sides and cooked through inside, then take it out and let it cool.
2. Grind the pork, ham and mortadella very fine and mix in the cheese, the egg for the filling and the nutmeg into an evenly bound filling. Cover and refrigerate for at least 12 hours.
3. Knead the flour and the eggs for the dough into a smooth, stiff dough, cover and rest for about 30 minutes, until the dough springs back less when pressed. Roll it thinner in stages, in pieces, until thin enough that the shadow of your hand shows faintly through; keep the dough you are not working on covered at all times.
4. Cut the sheet into squares of about 3 cm. Put a little filling in the middle of each square, leaving the four edges free. Fold into a triangle, squeeze out the air and press the edges tight, then wrap the two bottom corners around a fingertip, overlap and pinch them firmly to form a small ring. Cut only a small piece of dough at a time, to keep the edges from drying.
5. Bring both broths to a boil, taste and add salt, add the tortellini in batches and push them gently apart. Once the broth returns to a gentle boil, cook for about 3–5 minutes. Take one out to check that the sealed edge has no hard white core, then use a thin-probe thermometer in the centre of the egg-bound filling, and once it reaches 74°C (165°F) ladle into warm bowls with the broth.

## Key points

- **Dough springs back when rolled and the edges tear**: the dough is still tight, or the surface has dried out. Cover and rest it longer, thin it gradually setting by setting on the machine, and do not dust with lots of flour again and again. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **They open and leak filling in the pot**: filling got on the edges, air was left inside, or the dough dried out too much to stick. Keep the edges clean when shaping, brush the seal with a damp fingertip if needed and pinch tight; keep the broth at a gentle boil and avoid a rolling boil knocking them about. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **The wrapper is soft but the filling is not cooked through**: they are shaped too large or overfilled, so the centre lags behind the wrapper. Test-cook one first and decide the time by centre temperature; measure the centre of a filling that contains egg with a thermometer. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
