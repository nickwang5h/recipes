*Tom kha kai* is a coconut milk chicken soup built around galangal. The base is mellow, lime juice at the end brings out the sourness, and fish sauce holds up the salty savouriness. Galangal smells cool and woody; in Canada, look for galangal, fresh or frozen.

This soup manages the aromatics and the chicken separately. Slicing the galangal, smashing the lemongrass and tearing the leaves gets their fragrance into the soup faster (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Coconut milk is naturally a fat-containing [emulsion](/principles/emulsification/), and gentle heat keeps its texture even. The chicken only needs to be cooked through; keep boiling and the [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tighten more and more and squeeze out juice.

## Steps

1. Cut the chicken thighs into thin slices of even thickness. Wash the mushrooms and cut into pieces, then drain off the surface water; wash the board and knife used for raw chicken before handling the herbs.
2. Peel off the tough outer layers of the lemongrass, keep the pale tender core at the bottom, cut it into four lengths and smash with the back of a knife until split. Slice the galangal thinly and tear the lime leaves, and set them together.
3. Pour the chicken stock and the well-stirred coconut milk into a pot and heat until just about to boil, then turn to low at once. Add the galangal, lemongrass and lime leaves and simmer gently for about 1 minute, until you can smell the fragrance, then go to the next step.
4. Add the chicken, mushrooms and fish sauce, and pull apart any slices stuck together. Keep small bubbles at the edge of the pot and cook until the mushrooms are slightly soft and the chicken is cooked through. Insert a thermometer from the side into a thicker slice, and take the pot off the heat at **74°C (165°F)**.
5. Off the heat, stir in the lime juice and the smashed chilies. Taste a spoonful with a mushroom in it: the sourness should be clear and the savouriness should hold it up. If not, add fish sauce or lime juice a little at a time.
6. Lift out the tough aromatics, scatter on the cilantro leaves and ladle into bowls while hot. If you serve the galangal slices, lemongrass lengths and lime leaves in the soup, remind everyone to pick them out; they are there for fragrance and are too tough to chew.

## Key points

- **Oil floats on the surface and the soup tastes rough**: the coconut milk was boiled too long or too hard, and the fat droplets clumped together. Cook gently at small bubbles; if the soup starts to separate, take it off the heat and stir lightly, and do not cook it down further. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **Galangal is faint and the lime is not fragrant**: the aromatics were not cut open and smashed, or cooked too long. Simmer briefly first for the base fragrance, and add the lime juice and cilantro last. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Chicken is dry and tough**: the slices are uneven, so the thin ones overcook along with the thick ones. Cut even thin slices against the grain and take the pot off the heat once cooked; if the soup still needs adjusting, lift the chicken out first. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
