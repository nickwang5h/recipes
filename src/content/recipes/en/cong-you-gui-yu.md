Steamed mandarin fish with green onion oil has tender white flesh under a layer of green onion whose aroma is brought out by hot oil. The fish and the oil are done in two steps. The fish is steamed through first; steam condenses on the surface of the fish and gives up heat, which then moves slowly into the thick flesh and the parts next to the bone (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). How long to steam depends on whether the thickest part is cooked, not only on the weight of the fish.

The green onion goes on only once the fish is steamed. Hot oil poured over it heats the onion briefly and releases a fresh onion aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Drain the washed green onion so the oil can go straight onto it. The liquid on the steaming plate is juice from the fish plus condensed water; keep a little to make the sauce, as too much dilutes the soy sauce.

## Steps

1. Clean out the gills, the black membrane in the belly and the blood along the bone, then rinse and dry. Cut a slash about every 3 cm in the thick flesh on both sides, only into the thick flesh and without cutting the fish through. Slice the ginger, cut the bird's eye chili into thin rings, and cut the green onion into lengths and drain them.
2. Rub the salt and Shaoxing wine evenly over the fish, into the slashes and the belly, and massage for about 1 minute. Tuck half the ginger slices into the slashes and the belly and cure for 10 minutes; meanwhile bring the water in the steamer to a boil, with plenty of water.
3. Pour off the liquid drawn out, discard the used ginger, quickly rinse the outside and belly of the fish and dry it. Put the fish on a heatproof plate that fits the whole fish and lay the rest of the ginger slices over it. Leave room around the plate for steam to pass, and do not fill the steamer tightly.
4. When the water is boiling, put in the plate of fish, cover, and adjust the heat so steam keeps coming. Steam for about 10 minutes, then check the thickest part: the fish is done when the flesh is fully opaque, a fork gently separates it into flakes and the flesh by the bone is no longer translucent; take it out right away. If not, keep steaming.
5. Take the plate out with a heatproof gripper and remove the ginger. Spoon off the excess steaming liquid, leaving just enough to moisten the bottom of the plate, pour on the steamed fish soy sauce, then lay the green onion and bird's eye chili over the fish. Leave the sauce on the bottom of the plate, just enough for the fish to be dipped in as you eat.
6. Heat the vegetable oil in a small pot until ripples flow on the surface of the oil but it is not yet smoking. Test it with a small piece of drained green onion, which should immediately form fine, dense bubbles on contact. Take the pot off the heat and pour the oil slowly over the green onion on the fish to release the aroma, and serve at once.

## Key points

- **Surface cooked but flesh by the bone still translucent**: heat has not reached the thick part (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Check the thick flesh, not the tail; a bigger fish also needs a longer steaming time.
- **Fish is dry**: it was kept steaming after it was cooked, so the proteins contracted and squeezed out water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take it out as soon as it is done; it does not need to steam the full 20 minutes.
- **Green onion scorched black or almost no aroma**: the oil was poured too hot or too cool (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Test with a piece of green onion first and look for normal bubbling; if the oil smokes, take it off the heat to cool first.
