*Bún chả* is Hanoi's grilled pork with rice noodles. Slices of pork and small meat patties come to the table together, sitting in a fish sauce dip with pickled vegetables. The thin rice noodles, lettuce and herbs are served apart, and you pick up a small bundle for each mouthful. The surface of the grilled meat has to lose water first for the toasty flavour of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) to develop, and the [acidity](/principles/acid/) of the dip makes the fatty pork taste clean.

The patties need some fat to be moist once grilled. Spread the pork slices out and press the patties thin, so that browning on the surface and cooking through in the centre finish at about the same time (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). When seasoning the dip, taste it with the noodles, because that is the only way to judge the saltiness.

## Steps

1. Pickle the vegetables. Toss the papaya and carrot slices with the salt for drawing out water, and after about 20 minutes rinse and squeeze them dry, then soak them in the rice vinegar and the sugar for the pickles, cover and refrigerate; overnight gives more flavour.
2. Make the caramel colour. Cook 50 g sugar with 15 ml water over low heat to a deep brown, take it off the heat, and when the violent bubbling has eased, slowly add 30 ml hot water. Let it dissolve and cool, topping up with water to 60 ml if needed; take 1 tbsp for marinating the meat.
3. Marinate the pork. Pound the shallot, the marinade sugar and the pepper into a wet paste, and mix with the caramel colour, the marinade fish sauce and the oil. Divide it by the amount of meat between the pork slices and the ground pork, mix each through, then cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours.
4. Make the dipping sauce. Stir the lime juice, the dipping sauce sugar and the warm water until the sugar dissolves, then add the dipping sauce fish sauce and taste until the sour, sweet and savoury notes are all clear. Add the chili separately, and put the drained pickles in just before serving.
5. Cook the noodles. Cook them following the package until there is no hard core, rinse in cold water and drain, divide into small bundles that are easy to pick up, and arrange on a plate with the lettuce, mint and cilantro.
6. Grill both meats. Press the ground pork into small patties about 1 cm thick and spread out the pork slices, then grill them over medium-high heat on an oiled grill or grill basket, turning until both sides are browned and fragrant with a few charred edges. Insert a thermometer from the side; the centres of both the patties and the slices should reach 71°C (160°F).
7. Serve in bowls. Put the freshly grilled meat and the pickles into shallow bowls and pour in the dipping sauce. Each time, pick up a little rice noodle and some herbs and dip them; if the sauce is too strong, add a little warm water to the bowl.

## Key points

- **Black outside, raw inside**: the sugar burned before the centre of the meat caught up (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Move the meat to a lower-heat zone and keep grilling, judge doneness by the centre temperature, and press the next batch of patties thinner.
- **The meat surface is wet and soft with no grilled flavour**: the meat is piled up and the water cannot escape (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Shake off the extra marinade, spread it out in batches, and turn only once the surface has coloured.
- **The dip is too sour or too salty**: the strength of lime and fish sauce varies by product (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Add warm water to dilute first, then a little sugar; taste it with noodles and meat together.
