In these pancakes, ground meat is stirred into a batter that is spread into thin rounds and cooked. The meat is scattered through the pancake, the pancake itself picks up a fried fragrance, and the bite is soft and chewy. The flour takes up water and forms some [gluten](/principles/gluten/). In the pan, the starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and if you add egg, it helps the pancake set too.

The batter should flow slowly, so it spreads easily with a spatula. Flour with only half its weight in water makes a dough, which cannot be spread thin. So mix it smooth first, then add water according to how it flows. Count the water that the egg and vegetables bring too. The meat is cooked first, so when you fry the pancakes you only need to manage [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) through the pancake and whether the centre is cooked.

## Steps

1. Mix half the salt, the sugar, soy sauce, cumin, the oil for seasoning and all the starch into the ground meat. Cover and refrigerate to marinate for about 30 minutes. Press apart any clumps of meat with a spoon so the seasoning is spread evenly.
2. Mix the flour, the remaining seasonings and the oil for seasoning together. You can add the egg here. First add 300 ml of water and stir into a batter with no dry flour, then add the rest slowly, until a scoop of it runs off in a continuous, slow stream and can be spread out.
3. Mince the garlic, finely slice the green part of the leek, and finely mince the carrot. Cut the additions small, so they are less likely to break through the pancake when it is spread thin.
4. In a pan, use a little of the reserved oil to stir-fry the garlic until fragrant, then stir-fry the meat, pressing it apart as you go, until it has fully changed colour and is cooked through in the centre. If you are using carrot, add it and stir-fry until soft.
5. Spread the cooked meat out to cool for about 2 minutes. Once it has cooled slightly, stir it gently into the batter. The batter should still flow, and the meat should not gather into large lumps.
6. Wipe a flat pan with a little of the reserved oil and take it off the heat. Pour in the batter in portions and spread it into a thin pancake with a spatula. Return it to low heat. While the surface has not yet set, sprinkle on the sliced leek. Cook until the bottom has set and the edge can be lifted, then flip.
7. Cook the other side until it sets, then cook each side for about 20 seconds more. Cut one open and check the centre: there should be no wet, sticky raw batter. If it is still raw, keep cooking over low heat until done, then take it out and cut into pieces.

## Key points

- **Batter won't spread, pancake is thick**: not enough water, or [gluten](/principles/gluten/) has tightened the batter after stirring. Add water slowly until it flows, and spread the batter thin in batches. Do not pour the whole bowl into one thick pancake.
- **Golden on top, still sticky in the centre**: the pancake is too thick and the heat has not yet [passed](/principles/heat-transfer/) to the centre. Lower the heat, keep cooking, and cut one open to check. A browned surface does not mean the inside is done.
- **Breaks when flipped**: the bottom has not set, the starch has not fully [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), or the meat is too clumped. Press the meat apart first, wait until the edge can be lifted, then support the whole pancake as you flip.
