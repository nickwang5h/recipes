This dish is about thin potato strips that are cooked through but still crisp. Cutting breaks the potato's cells and leaves free starch on the surface. Rinsing it off reduces the gummy paste that forms when heated; see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The brief blanch only preheats the strips. They still have to be cooked in the wok.

Even thickness is easier to cook right than chasing the thinnest cut; see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/). The vinegar goes in once the potatoes are cooked. That keeps the sour fragrance, and adding it early would keep the potatoes from softening; see [acid](/principles/acid/). The garlic goes in two parts: the first half is fried in the oil until fragrant, and the second half is stir-fried briefly with the seasoning for a sharper garlic flavour.

## Steps

1. Peel the potato. Cut it into thin slices of even thickness first, then into fine strips. If you use a shredder, pick the fine-strip blade and do not grate it into crumbs.
2. Gently loosen the potato strips in clean water. Change the water and rinse until the cloudiness is clearly reduced. Bring a separate pot of water to a boil, add the strips, loosen them and blanch for about 10 seconds. Lift them out right away and drain.
3. Remove the stems and seeds from the green and red bell peppers and cut them into strips. Cut the dried chilies into pieces. Mince the garlic and divide it into two equal portions. Chop the green onion and keep the white and green parts apart. Measure all the seasonings.
4. Heat the wok, add the vegetable oil and turn the heat to low. Add the white part of the green onion, the first half of the garlic and the dried chilies, and stir gently. When you smell garlic and chili and the garlic has not darkened, go to the next step.
5. Add the bell pepper strips and stir a few times. Right away add the drained potato strips and turn the heat to high. Spread them out and keep lifting from the bottom so every part gets heat.
6. Stir-fry until the potato strips go from raw white to slightly translucent. Check one of the thicker strips: the centre should have no raw, chalky feel, and it should bend but still be crisp when bitten. If there is still a hard core, keep cooking; do not go by colour alone.
7. Add the light soy sauce, aged vinegar, the remaining garlic and the salt. Toss quickly so the strips are coated in seasoning. Scatter in the green part of the green onion, toss once more and plate. Do not cook for long after adding the vinegar.

## Key points

- **The potato strips stick into a clump**: there is too much starch on the surface, and it gelatinizes into a paste when heated (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Loosen and rinse the strips after cutting. Drain well after blanching so cloudy water does not go into the wok.
- **Thin strips are soft while thicker ones still have a hard core**: uneven thickness means the centres heat at different speeds (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut evenly, and after the blanch still check that the thickest strip is cooked through.
- **It tastes sour but has no vinegar fragrance**: the vinegar was cooked too long and its volatile aromas left (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Add vinegar once the potatoes are cooked and crisp, toss and plate. The late garlic also needs only a short stir-fry.
