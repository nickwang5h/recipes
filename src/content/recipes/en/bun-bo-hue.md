*Bún bò Huế* comes from Huế in central Vietnam: thick round rice noodles with beef shank and skin-on pork. The [collagen](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) in the beef bones and pork trotter slowly turns to gelatin and gives the broth body. Smashed lemongrass releases a clean fragrance into the broth, while the chili and minced lemongrass are fried separately in oil to bring out their aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

Huế shrimp paste, mắm ruốc, is the base flavour of this bowl. [Fermentation](/principles/fermentation/) gives it a strong smell and savour. Dissolving it in water and clarifying it before it goes into the broth removes the coarse sediment. Keep half of the chili-lemongrass oil for the table and add it as you eat, which gives a brighter aroma.

## Steps

1. Blanch the bones and trotter. Add the water for blanching and heat over high heat for 10–15 minutes, until the pot boils, then keep it boiling for 5–10 minutes, until a lot of scum floats up. Pour off the water and wash the bones, trotter and pot; add the beef shank, the water for the broth, half an onion, the salt and the sugar for the broth.
2. Cook the meat until tender. Heat over high heat for 10–15 minutes, until the pot boils, skim, then turn to a bare simmer for 60 minutes, until a chopstick goes into the beef shank and the pork skin is soft with some spring; the centre of the trotter must reach at least 71°C (160°F). Whichever softens first, lift it out first. Cool for 15 minutes, until it can be held steadily, then slice the beef shank thin against the grain, put the meat in a shallow container, cover and refrigerate.
3. Keep simmering the bones. Leave the bones in the pot and simmer for another 90–120 minutes, for about 3 hours of broth time in total, until the broth is fragrant and rich and slightly sticks to the lips. Add the lemongrass for the last 60 minutes, and once you smell a clear lemongrass aroma, strain out the bones and spices.
4. Clarify the shrimp paste. Stir the shrimp paste into 120 ml cold water and let it stand for 10 minutes, until the coarse sediment settles. Pour off the upper liquid, add 240 ml water and heat for 3–5 minutes until it boils; take it off the heat at once and let it stand for 15 minutes, until the sediment settles again. Add the upper liquid to the broth and leave the sediment behind; if the broth is under 2400 ml, top it up with hot water to that amount.
5. Fry the chili-lemongrass oil. Add the minced lemongrass, shallot and garlic to the oil and stir-fry over medium-low heat for 2–3 minutes, until light golden and richly fragrant. Take it off the heat and stir in the chili flakes, then add the fish sauce, sugar and 2 tbsp of the beef broth from the pot, and cook over low heat for 5 minutes, until the oil is red and glossy and the sugar has dissolved. Stir half into the broth and keep half for the table.
6. Cook the noodles. Cook them in a separate pot of boiling water for 10–15 minutes, until there is no white hard core in the centre but they still have spring; if a white core remains, cook 1 minute longer each time. Rinse in cold water to remove surface starch and drain. Soak the onion for the bowls in cold water for 30 minutes, until the sharpness eases.
7. Assemble the bowls. Return the beef and trotter to the broth and warm over low heat for 3–5 minutes, until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Divide the noodles among four bowls, top with beef, trotter, onion, green onion and cilantro, and pour about 600 ml of hot broth into each bowl; serve with shredded lettuce, mint, lime and the rest of the chili-lemongrass oil.

## Key points

- **The beef shank is hard to chew**: the connective tissue has not softened yet (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Keep simmering gently, check every 15 minutes, and cook it soft before slicing.
- **The chili oil is bitter and dark**: the fine mince burned in the hot oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Take the pan off the heat as soon as the garlic is light golden, add the chili, then cool it down with the beef broth.
- **The shrimp paste overpowers the lemongrass and the broth is too salty**: fermented shrimp paste is very concentrated (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Clarify it in the amounts given, add most of it first and then taste; if the broth is short, top up with hot water first rather than reducing it further.
