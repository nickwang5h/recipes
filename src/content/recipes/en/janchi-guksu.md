Janchi guksu (*janchi guksu*, "banquet noodles") is thin wheat noodles in a light broth with a few toppings; the name comes from the custom of eating long noodles at weddings and other gatherings to wish for a long life. At home, an anchovy and kombu broth with a few vegetables and shredded egg is enough. The broth should be clear and savoury, and each topping keeps its own colour and texture.

Anchovy and kombu bring different savoury compounds, and together they strengthen the [umami](/principles/umami/). The thin noodles are cooked in a separate pot and rinsed of their surface starch, so the clear broth is not clouded by noodle water. Rinsing cools the noodles, so they are warmed with boiling broth before going in the bowl, which washes the surface and still keeps the bowl hot.

## Steps

1. Split open the bellies of the large anchovies and remove the black innards; wipe the kombu with a slightly damp cloth to remove grit. Put both in a pot with 1500 ml of water and heat over medium heat for about 5–8 minutes, until bubbles form at the edge and in the centre.
2. Without a lid, keep it at a gentle simmer for 10 minutes, until the broth smells clearly of kelp and dried fish and is pale gold. Strain out the solids and measure out 1200 ml of broth; if it falls short, top it up with the reserve water. Add the soup soy sauce, taste and add salt, and keep it warm over low heat for about 10 minutes, until the surface just steams lightly.
3. Toss the zucchini with a little salt and let it sit for 5–10 minutes, until the strips soften and water collects at the bottom of the bowl; squeeze out the water. Cut the carrot into strips of the same thickness, slice the shiitake thin, and set each aside.
4. Add 1 tsp of oil to a skillet and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows quickly. Stir-fry the zucchini for 1–2 minutes, until soft but still bright green, and remove. In the same pan, stir-fry the carrot and shiitake for 1–2 minutes, until the carrot bends and the shiitake softens, and season with salt and pepper.
5. Beat the egg. Add the remaining 1 tsp of oil to the pan and fry the egg into a thin sheet over low heat for about 1–2 minutes, then flip and cook for about 30 seconds, until both sides are set with no runny egg and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Take it off the heat and cool for 2 minutes, until you can hold it steadily, then roll it up and cut it into thin strips.
6. In a separate pot, bring water to a boil over high heat for about 5–10 minutes, until it is at a rolling boil, then add the noodles, separate them and cook for about 3 minutes, checking against the package time, until no hard core remains when bitten. Lift them out and rinse under cold water for about 1 minute, until they are no longer warm and no longer slick to the touch, then drain for 1 minute, until they no longer drip continuously. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
7. Divide the noodles between two bowls. Bring the clear broth back to a boil for about 1 minute, until bubbles break continuously at the centre. Ladle a spoonful over each portion of noodles, holding the noodles back with the ladle and pouring the broth back into the pot; once the noodles are warmed, add 600 ml of hot broth to each bowl, top with the vegetables and egg strips and eat right away.

## Key points

- **Broth bitter and fishy**: the innards of the large anchovies were not removed, or it was simmered too long. Remove the innards, do not cover the pot, and strain once the savoury flavour has come out. See [Umami](/principles/umami/).
- **Broth cloudy and noodles limp**: the noodles were cooked directly in the clear broth, so surface starch got into the soup and the noodles kept absorbing water. Cook and rinse them in a separate pot, and combine only in the bowl. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Egg strips dry, hard and crumbly**: the heat was too high and the proteins contracted too far. Fry it thin over low heat, take it off the heat once set, and cut it after it cools. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
