Cucumber is crisp, egg is soft and ham sausage is salty and savoury, and this quick stir-fry puts the three together. The eggs are cooked first and taken out, so the [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) sets only to a soft texture. The cucumber is then stir-fried on its own and the egg goes back in last, which keeps both textures.

Cucumber holds a lot of water. Slice it to an even thickness and drain it after washing, so the heat in the pan goes into a short stir-fry (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Both the ham sausage and the light soy sauce carry salt, so wait to taste until everything is combined. Choose a ready-to-eat cooked ham sausage, which only needs heating through at the end. A raw sausage whose package says to cook it must be cooked according to the directions before slicing.

## Steps

1. Cut the cucumber. Wash it, pat off the surface water, halve it lengthwise and cut into half-moon slices of similar thickness. If the core is very loose and the seeds large, scrape out the soft part first so it releases less water in the short stir-fry. Young cucumbers can be cut as they are.
2. Prepare the other ingredients. Peel the cooked ham sausage, halve it lengthwise and cut into thin half-moon slices, and chop the red hot pepper. Keep the light soy sauce and salt at hand, and hold back half the salt so you do not find out only after it hits the hot pan that it is too salty.
3. Beat the eggs. Stir the two eggs until the white and yolk are blended. Heat 5 ml of oil in the pan, turn the heat to low and pour in the egg. When the edges set, cut it into pieces with chopsticks or a spatula. Take it out while there is no liquid egg left and the surface of the pieces is still shiny.
4. Stir-fry the cucumber. Without washing the pan, add the remaining 5 ml of oil, turn the heat to high and quickly stir-fry the cucumber for about 1 minute, until the surface is glossy and the edges are slightly translucent, with the slices still keeping their shape. As you stir, give every slice a turn at the bottom of the pan and do not heap them in a thick layer.
5. Fold in the eggs. Return the egg to the pan, drizzle on 3 ml of light soy sauce and half the salt, and add the cooked ham slices and chopped red pepper at once. Toss from the bottom so the seasoning coats the cucumber and egg in a thin layer, and cook until the ham is hot through.
6. Taste and serve. Take a small bite with cucumber and ham to judge the saltiness, then decide how much of the remaining salt to add. Toss and turn off the heat, and plate right away. Even if a lot of liquid comes out in the pan, do not overcook the cucumber and egg trying to dry it.

## Key points

- **Hard egg while the cucumber has just cooked**: the egg was not taken out in time, or was stir-fried too long after returning. Following [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/), take the egg out as soon as it sets, and at the end only heat through and toss.
- **Limp cucumber and water on the plate**: uneven slices, wet cucumber going in, or an overfull pan means the heat goes into boiling water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain, slice evenly, stir-fry in batches, and check the edges and firmness after about 1 minute.
- **The whole plate is too salty after the ham goes in**: the ham and the light soy sauce both contain salt (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The order of adding does not change the total salt, so count it all together and add table salt last.
