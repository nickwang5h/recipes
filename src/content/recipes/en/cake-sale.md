The French savoury cake (*cake salé*) is soft and moist when cut, with an eggy flavour, and diced ham and cheese are set evenly through it, so each bite has a salty cheese savouriness. It suits eating hot or sliced after cooling.

Baking powder makes the batter rise, and the eggs set with heat, fixing the filling in the cake; see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). The set protein network holds water inside, which is what keeps the cake moist. If it is overbaked, the network tightens, squeezes the water out, and the cake dries.

The eggs and milk in the batter both carry water, and flour grows gluten as soon as it meets water. Mix until combined and stop; with long mixing, gluten develops and the texture turns tough; see [gluten](/principles/gluten/). So once the flour is in, the less you handle it the better.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F). Grease the loaf pan with a layer of butter.
2. Dice the ham and grate the cheese. Mix the flour and baking powder in a large bowl.
3. Crack in the eggs, gradually add the milk and stir to combine, then add the oil and mix, stopping as soon as no dry flour is visible.
4. Add the ham and cheese, sprinkle in salt and black pepper, and fold gently until the ham and cheese are evenly spread, then pour the batter into the pan.
5. Bake for about 40 minutes, until the top is golden. Insert the tip of a knife into the centre; if it comes out with no wet batter, it is done.
6. Let it cool a little, then unmould. For a lunch box, slice it and pack it, and refrigerate promptly once it stops steaming.

## Key points

- **Cake is hard**: the batter was mixed too long after the flour went in, and gluten developed ([gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Mix only until even.
- **Centre is not cooked**: pans and ovens differ, and so does the speed at which heat reaches the centre ([heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). If the knife tip still comes out wet, bake a little longer.
- **Too salty**: the ham and cheese are already salty. Add only a little salt at first.
- **For lunch boxes**: store it refrigerated, and the next day leave it at room temperature for about 15 minutes before eating. Keep it cold in an insulated bag when you carry it.
