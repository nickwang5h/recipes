With zucchini and eggs, each ingredient has to come off the heat at the right moment. Beaten egg sets into a soft network of protein when heated, and more stir-frying makes it tighten and lose water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). So cook the eggs first and take them out, then cook the zucchini until soft, and finally return the eggs to catch the vegetable juices.

Cut the zucchini into slices of similar thickness so they cook evenly (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). The tomato is optional: with it you get a little sweet-sourness and more juice; without it you mostly taste the zucchini and eggs. Salt draws water out of vegetables, and a little juice helps the slices cook through over low heat, but salting heavily too early softens the slices (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).

## Steps

1. Wash the zucchini, trim both ends and cut into thin diamond-shaped slices with sides of about 4 cm, keeping the thickness as even as possible. If the seeds in the middle are already hard and the flesh around them is loose, remove that part and keep the firm flesh.
2. If you use tomato, wash it and cut it into small pieces; skip this step if you do not. Crack the eggs into a clean bowl and stir until the whites and yolks are mixed.
3. Heat the pan, add half the oil, turn to medium heat and pour in the eggs. Push gently with a spatula so the egg sets into large curds. When the curds have set but the surface is still soft and moist, take them out; do not cook until the edges are dry and hard.
4. Add the remaining oil to the pan. If you use tomato, add it first and stir-fry until the edges soften and a little juice comes out; if not, add the zucchini directly.
5. Add the zucchini and toss, and sprinkle in half the salt first. Turn to low heat and simmer for about 4–5 minutes, turning now and then so the top and bottom layers swap places. There should be a thin layer of vegetable juice in the bottom of the pan; if it is dry, add a little water to keep it from sticking and burning.
6. Check one of the thicker slices: the colour should be slightly translucent and the slice should bend, with no hard raw centre when you bite it, but the edges should still be intact. If it is still hard, keep simmering; once it is soft and translucent, add the eggs.
7. Return the eggs and stir-fry over medium heat for about 15 seconds, so the curds pick up the vegetable juice and are heated through, with no runny raw egg left in the pan. Taste a slice, add salt as needed, toss well and plate right away.

## Key points

- **Eggs are crumbly and dry**: they were overcooked the first time and tightened further when returned to the pan (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook them into soft large curds, take them out to wait, and finish with a short stir-fry until cooked through.
- **Slices are soft outside and hard inside**: the side length was controlled but the slices were cut too thick or uneven (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut them evenly, and check the thickest slice while simmering; the time is only a guide.
- **Too much liquid or too salty**: the tomato and the slices release water, and the salt was added too early or in too large an amount (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Use only half the salt at first; if there is too much juice after the slices are cooked, reduce it slightly before returning the eggs, and taste and adjust the salt at the end.
