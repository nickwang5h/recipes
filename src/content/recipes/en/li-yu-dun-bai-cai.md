Stewed carp with napa cabbage puts pan-fried fish and tender cabbage in one pot, with a broth that carries the salty heat of doubanjiang and the fragrance of star anise and cassia. The fish is fried until the skin sets, then stewed in water. The cabbage shrinks with the heat, the soft leaves take up the fish juices, and the stems keep a little bite.

The fragrance of the fish skin comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The spices release their aroma in oil first and then spread through the broth; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). Doubanjiang and whole spices burn easily, so frying the fish first and the paste afterwards is easier to control than laying the seasonings under the fish.

## Steps

1. Pat the cleaned carp dry and cut several diagonal slashes on each side, through the flesh but not through the bone. Wash the cabbage leaf by leaf, split the thick stem parts lengthwise and drain.
2. Heat the pan, add 10 ml oil, turn to medium heat and add the fish. Fry one side first. After about 3 minutes check the skin: when the edges have colour and the whole fish can be shaken loose, flip it. Fry the other side until set, then take the fish out.
3. In the oil left in the pan, add the ginger, garlic, cassia, star anise and optional dried chilies, turn to low heat and stir-fry until fragrant. Add the doubanjiang and stir-fry until the oil turns red but the bits of paste have not blackened, then add the water at once.
4. Pour 500 ml water in along the side of the pan, add the light and dark soy sauces and stir the paste in. Put the fish back. The water should come about level with the fish, so don't choose too large a pot. If the back of the fish shows above the liquid, spoon the broth over it; there is no need to keep turning the fish.
5. Put the cabbage around the fish, bring to a boil, turn to medium-low, cover and stew for 15–20 minutes. Keep the surface at a gentle roll. When the cabbage has collapsed, press it gently into the broth, and look at the bottom of the pot once partway through to check there is enough liquid.
6. Pull back a little flesh at the thickest part: if it is opaque and separates into flakes, the fish is done. When chopsticks can break a cabbage stem, take the lid off and let the broth reduce slightly. Taste the cabbage and broth first, then add a little salt, and lift the fish out with support under the body.

## Key points

- **The fish skin breaks up on the bottom of the pan**: it was flipped too early or too often, before the surface had set (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Fry until it can be shaken loose before flipping, and baste instead of turning during the stew.
- **The broth is bitter and the aroma harsh**: the doubanjiang or dry spices burned on the bottom of the pan (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add water right after they turn fragrant; if there are already burned black bits, change the pot and make the sauce base again.
- **The cabbage is salty enough to taste bitter**: the doubanjiang, soy sauce and any extra salt add up, and reducing concentrates the salt (see [Salt](/principles/salt/)). Add no salt at first and taste near the end; if the broth is already salty enough, don't add any.
