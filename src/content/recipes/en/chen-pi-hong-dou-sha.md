Red bean soup with aged tangerine peel is a Cantonese sweet soup. The bean paste is fine and hangs in the syrup, with a sandy mouthfeel, and it still flows. The tangerine peel cooks together with the beans, so its citrus aroma seeps into the soup (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The beans first take up water and cook through, and then the soft beans are mashed. The starch inside the beans [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat, and once mashed, it thickens the soup together with the fine bits of bean. The finished paste clings to a spoon on its own, and all of the thickness comes from the red beans themselves.

## Steps

1. Pick out any bad beans, rinse the red beans, and add 1000 ml of soaking water. Cover and soak in the refrigerator for 8 hours, until the beans have clearly swollen and the skins look smooth. Discard the soaking water, rinse once more and drain.
2. Soak the tangerine peel in 100 ml of water for 20 minutes, until the peel bends and the inner side is soft. Gently scrape off the thick white inner layer with a small knife and cut the peel into thin strips. Discard the soaking water. You can do this while the beans are almost done soaking.
3. Put the beans, tangerine peel and 1200 ml of cooking water in a heavy-bottomed pot. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat for about 10 minutes, until the whole surface boils. Skim off the foam, turn the heat to low and set the lid slightly ajar, so the surface keeps bubbling gently.
4. Simmer over low heat for 90 minutes, until the bean skins split and a bean, cooled, mashes easily between your fingers. Stir every 20 minutes, all the way to the bottom. If the beans are not yet soft and the water has clearly dropped, add 120 ml of the reserve hot water. If a hard core remains, keep cooking in 15-minute steps until the beans mash.
5. Press the soft beans against the side of the pot with the back of a ladle, then stir them back into the soup. Mash until most of the beans break down into fine paste, leaving only a few soft whole beans. For a finer texture, pulse briefly with an immersion blender, stopping as you go, and stop once the soup is evenly thick.
6. Add the rock sugar and salt and stir over low heat for 5 minutes, until the rock sugar has completely disappeared and the paste thinly coats the back of a spoon while still pouring in a continuous stream. If it is too thick, stir in the rest of the reserve hot water a little at a time. Divide among 6 small bowls while hot.

## Key points

- **Beans still have a hard core**: they were not soaked long enough or not cooked long enough (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep a gentle boil with plenty of water, and cook until a bean mashes under light pressure before adding sugar and mashing.
- **Water is water, beans are beans**: the soft beans were not mashed (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep mashing the soft beans and stir until the paste is suspended in the soup. Boiling hard to reduce the water only scorches the bottom first.
- **The tangerine peel tastes bitter and heavy**: the inner layer is too thick, or too much peel went in (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Gently scrape off the thick white pith after softening, and use 1 piece per pot. If it is already bitter, the only fix is to dilute it with another batch of bean paste made without tangerine peel.
