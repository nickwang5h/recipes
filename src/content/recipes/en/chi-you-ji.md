Soy sauce chicken has a glossy brown skin and tender, salty-savoury meat. It is a poached dish from Cantonese roast-shop cooking: a whole chicken is cooked slowly in a soy sauce liquid seasoned with rock sugar, ginger, green onion and star anise. The liquid is made strong to colour the skin, and only a small spoonful is drizzled over the plate.

The key is to let each part of the chicken take turns in the hot liquid: use a narrow, deep pot, turn the chicken partway, and spoon liquid over the exposed skin. That gives even colour and lets heat enter the meat from all directions (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The ginger, green onion and star anise are stir-fried in oil first, so the liquid gets the [fragrance of spices](/principles/aromatics/). As soon as the chicken is cooked, take it out of the hot liquid so the meat does not keep shrinking and losing water.

## Steps

1. Take the giblet bag out of the cavity and blot the chicken dry. Heat the 1000 ml of water for scalding the skin for about 5 minutes, until the whole surface boils. Put the chicken in a deep dish and ladle the boiling water over all of the skin with a long-handled ladle for about 30 seconds, until the skin tightens slightly. Pour off the water in the dish and drain the chicken cavity-side down for 2 minutes, until it no longer drips steadily.
2. Add the oil to a pot and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily. Add the ginger slices, shallots, star anise and green onion pieces in that order, and stir-fry for 1 minute, until clearly fragrant and lightly yellow at the edges.
3. Add 3 tbsp of the rice wine and the light soy sauce and cook for 30 seconds. Add the 475 ml of water and cook for 30 seconds. Add the rock sugar and cook for 30 seconds. Add the dark soy sauce and cook for 20 seconds, then pour in the remaining 3 tbsp of rice wine. Simmer over low heat for 2–3 minutes, until the sugar has completely dissolved and small bubbles keep rising at the edge.
4. Put the chicken in a narrow, deep pot that just holds it, and pour in the liquid so it comes more than halfway up the chicken; if there is not enough, switch to a narrower pot. Ladle some liquid into the cavity, cover and cook over low heat for about 15 minutes, until the lower half of the skin is an even brown and the liquid only trembles gently without a hard boil. During this time, spoon liquid over the exposed skin every 5 minutes.
5. Support the chicken and turn it over, cover and cook over low heat for 15 minutes to colour the other side, then turn it again and cook for 10 minutes. Insert a probe into the thickest part of the thigh joint and the breast, away from the bone. The chicken is cooked when the centre reaches 75°C (165°F). If not, cook 5 minutes more at a time and test again after turning.
6. Lay one bag of ice on a platter. Drain the liquid out of the cavity and set the chicken on the ice bag, then cover the back with the other bag of ice. Cool for 10–15 minutes, until the skin is cool and the chicken no longer steams visibly. Leave the ice bags on, so melted water does not wash away the flavour on the skin.
7. Take 150 ml of the liquid from the pot and bring it to a boil in another pot for about 1 minute, until the whole pot bubbles. Stir the cornstarch with 2 tbsp of cold water, pour it in while stirring, and cook over low heat for 30–60 seconds, until the sauce is clear and lightly coats a spoon. Take it off the heat and stir in the toasted sesame oil.
8. Separate the wings and legs at the joints, cut the breast off with the skin on, then chop into pieces about 2–3 cm wide. If you have no cleaver, debone the meat and slice it. Spoon the thin sauce over the pieces, keep the skin intact and serve right away.

## Key points

- **Patchy skin colour**: the liquid did not take turns reaching the exposed parts (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Do not use too wide a pot, turn and baste on a schedule, and do not add extra dark soy sauce to make up the colour.
- **The skin is done but the meat near the bone is not**: the skin took colour first while the centre has not heated through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Take the temperature at the thickest part of the thigh joint and the breast. The colour of the liquid does not tell you whether it is cooked.
- **Tight meat and split skin**: the liquid boiled hard the whole time and the outer layer was heated too long, so [protein shrinkage](/principles/protein-coagulation/) squeezed out more water. Keep the heat low, and once the chicken is cooked, move it out of the liquid to cool.
