This pork and onion stir-fry is sweet, with a little tangy-sweet sauce flavour. The onion is soft but not mushy, and the pork slices are tender. The sweetness of the onion goes with the soy sauce and ketchup. The pork and onion are cooked separately, then combined in the sauce, so each is just right and the pork does not have to cook as long as the onion needs to soften.

Pork shoulder is trimmed of tough connective tissue and sliced thinly across the grain, so there are fewer long fibres to chew through (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). A quick stir-fry gives the surface a chance to undergo the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Once the pork is cooked, stop the heat promptly, or the [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) more tightly and squeeze out moisture.

## Steps

1. Peel and slice the onion, keeping the thickness as even as you can. Trim the tough connective tissue from the pork, slice it thinly across the grain, and blot the surface dry with paper towels. Smash the garlic. Handle the raw meat and the vegetables separately, and have the seasonings ready before you light the stove.
2. Mix the light soy sauce, sugar, ketchup, Shaoxing wine and black pepper, stirring until the sugar has mostly dissolved. The vegetable oil is for the wok; keep the toasted sesame oil aside for the finish and do not put it in this sauce.
3. Heat the wok and add 15 ml of vegetable oil. When the oil flows easily but has not started to smoke, spread in the pork slices. If the pan is small, cook in batches and leave gaps between the slices so they do not pile up in a pool of meat juices.
4. Stir-fry the pork until both sides change colour and the edges turn light brown, add the garlic and stir-fry until fragrant, then take it out right away and leave it on a plate to wait for its return to the pan.
5. Put the onion in the same wok and stir-fry for about 3–4 minutes, until the edges turn translucent and the slices soften but keep their shape. When meat juices appear at the bottom of the wok, keep stirring so the extra steam escapes; otherwise the sauce will turn the onion watery as soon as it goes in.
6. Pour in the sweet-salty sauce and toss until the onion is evenly coated. Return the pork with the juices from the plate and stir-fry until the centre of the thickest slice is cooked through. The sauce should only coat the meat and onion in a thin layer, with no pool of thin liquid at the bottom of the pan.
7. Stir in the toasted sesame oil, taste, and take the pan off the heat. After plating, spoon the sauce from the bottom of the pan over the top and eat while hot.

## Key points

- **Dry, hard pork slices**: the pork was stir-fried over and over after it cooked, so it kept shrinking and losing water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take it out first; on its return the pan only finishes the cooking and coats it with sauce.
- **Pork too chewy to bite**: the connective tissue was not trimmed, or the slices were cut with the grain (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Choose thin slices suited to quick stir-frying, and do not leave tough sinew for a short stir-fry to deal with.
- **Pork not browned and a pool of water in the pan**: too much went in at once or the surface was too wet, so the conditions for browning were not met (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Dry the slices, cook smaller batches, and add the sauce only after the onion has softened.
