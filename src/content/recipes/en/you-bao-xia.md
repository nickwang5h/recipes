Oil-fried shrimp is a dish from the Jiangsu and Zhejiang region, made with river shrimp in the shell: the shell is fragrant, the flesh is still plump, and the sauce coats only a thin layer on the outside. The shrimp are fried briefly in oil, lifted out and drained, fried again, and only then tossed with the green onion and ginger sauce in the wok.

The point is to dry out the shells in a very short time without toughening the flesh. Hot oil evaporates the water on the surface of the shell quickly, and the second fry dries it once more (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) in the flesh coagulate as soon as they are heated, and frying too long makes them shrink and harden. Mix the sauce ahead, so the fried shrimp do not wait in the hot wok for the next ingredient.

## Steps

1. Snip off the shrimp's feelers, legs and the sharp spike on the head. Use a toothpick to pull out the vein from between the segments of the shell on the back, keeping the shell whole. Wash, drain for 5 minutes until they stop dripping, then blot the head and belly dry with paper towels.
2. Mix the wine, chicken stock, sugar and vinegar, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Cut the green onion whites into sections and slice the ginger; keep the toasted sesame oil on the side. Have a spider strainer and a draining tray ready, and divide the shrimp into two batches.
3. Pour the frying oil into a round-bottomed wok and heat over medium-high heat for about 3 minutes, until the oil surface moves fast and shows fine ripples but is not smoking. Fry the shrimp in batches, about 5–10 seconds per batch first; lift them out and drain at once when the shells have just turned pink and the outer flesh is white. Wait about 30 seconds between batches, so the oil surface returns to its former movement.
4. Heat the oil for about 30 seconds more, until the surface shows fine ripples again. Fry again in two batches, about 5–10 seconds per batch, until the shell colour deepens and the flesh is opaque. Peel one and look at the thickest part; if it is still translucent, fry 5 seconds longer each time, and lift them out when cooked through; do not fry longer just for a darker colour.
5. Turn off the heat, scoop out the frying oil with a dry ladle, and leave 1 tbsp in the wok. Turn on low heat and stir-fry the green onion whites and ginger for 2 minutes, until the onion sections soften and are clearly fragrant without the ginger burning (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
6. Pour in the mixed sauce, turn to medium-high heat and cook for about 30 seconds, until the sugar has fully dissolved and the sauce boils at the bottom of the wok. Return the shrimp, drizzle in the toasted sesame oil, and toss for about 5–10 seconds, so the shells are evenly coated in sauce. Taste the sauce, add salt and plate at once.

## Key points

- **Soft shells and a lot of oil**: too many went in at once, so the oil temperature dropped and took a long time to come back. Fry in batches as in [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/), and spread out the lifted shrimp to drain instead of piling them in a bowl to go soft.
- **Hard, dry flesh**: the second fry or the toss in the wok dragged on too long, so the [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) kept tightening. Fry large and small shrimp separately, and peel one to see whether the flesh is cooked before deciding whether to lift them.
- **Tastes like boiled shrimp after plating**: the shrimp stayed in the sauce too long after returning to the wok, and the crisp shells absorbed water and went soft. Finish the sauce first, then toss the shrimp in briefly; when plating, lift the shrimp out and leave the leftover sauce in the wok.
