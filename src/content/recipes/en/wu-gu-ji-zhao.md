Boneless chicken feet are a sour and spicy cold dish, with soft skin and chewy tendons that are springy when eaten cold. The skin and tendons of the feet soften when cooked and spring back after cooling, because of changes in [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). To debone them whole, the feet must be cooked through and cooled completely, with the skin and flesh still firm; if they break at a pinch, they have been cooked too long.

Aged vinegar and lemon juice give the sourness, while garlic, chili and Sichuan pepper oil add fragrance. Judge the sourness in portions as you mix the dressing (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Deboning takes about two hours and the mixed dish needs at least 6 hours in the refrigerator, so plan ahead; while deboning, keep most of the feet in the refrigerator and take out only a few at a time.

## Steps

1. Snip off the nails and the hard tips of the toes, pick off any remaining horny skin and remove any visible dirt. Put the chicken feet in a large pot with the ginger, Shaoxing wine, green onion and cold water, with the water covering the feet.
2. Bring to a boil over high heat and skim off the foam. Adjust the heat to keep a boil and cook for about 10 minutes. Insert a thermometer into the thickest skin and flesh of the largest foot, away from the bone; the centre must reach 74°C (165°F). Lift them out when the skin and flesh can bend but are not yet falling apart, and discard the cooking water.
3. Put the feet into ice water made from drinking water and food-safe ice right away to cool them down. Once cold, drain them and spread them in a shallow container in the refrigerator. Do not let cooling and storing take more than 2 hours at room temperature, and keep the refrigerator at 4°C (40°F) or below.
4. Take out about 10 feet at a time and keep the rest refrigerated. Cut from the tip of each toe along the back to the back of the foot, then from the back of the foot toward the ankle. Bend the joints gently to loosen the skin and flesh, push the flesh off each toe bone with your fingers, and finally pull out the main bone. After each one, feel it over for bone fragments.
5. Return the deboned feet to the refrigerator. On a clean cutting board, chop the garlic, bird's eye chilies, onion and cilantro, and juice the lemons, straining out the seeds. Wash the knife, board and bowl used for raw chicken feet before they touch the cooked feet.
6. Mix the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, aged vinegar, sugar and Sichuan pepper oil, then add the garlic, chilies, onion and cilantro, and pour in the juice of one lemon first. Toss a small amount of the chicken feet with the dressing and taste. If it is not sour enough, add the juice of the second lemon, and only then decide whether to add salt.
7. Combine the cold chicken feet and all the dressing in a clean container, toss, cover and refrigerate for at least 6 hours. Turn once partway through with clean chopsticks so the top and bottom both get dressing. Toss again before eating, take out what you need and keep the rest refrigerated.

## Key points

- **The skin tears at a push and the bones are hard to pull out**: they were cooked too soft, or deboned while still hot. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Cool them as soon as they reach doneness and, once cold, cut open along the back of the bone.
- **Sourness and bitterness cover the garlic**: too much lemon juice was added at once, or the peel soaked for a long time. See [acid](/principles/acid/). Use only strained juice and adjust one lemon at a time.
- **It tastes saltier after refrigeration**: the saltiness of the soy sauce, oyster sauce and salt adds up and becomes more obvious as it sits in the refrigerator. See [salt](/principles/salt/). Add a little salt at the end.
