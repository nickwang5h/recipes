Drunken chicken is a cold chicken dish common in the Jiangnan region. Cooked chicken is soaked in a brine of Shaoxing wine and chicken stock, and eaten cold it has a clear wine aroma and a soft jelly between the skin and the meat. At home, boneless skin-on chicken legs are rolled into cylinders and, once fully cold, sliced for neat layers of skin and meat.

The chicken is steamed through first, then soaked in the wine. Steaming and cooling decide the texture once the [protein has set](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and the [salt in the brine](/principles/salt/) moves slowly into the cooked meat by diffusion. The Huadiao wine goes in after the brine has cooled, so its fresh wine aroma does not escape in a hot liquid (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The flavour only seeps into the middle of the meat during the cold soak in the refrigerator.

## Steps

1. Bone the chicken legs and lay them skin-side down. Make a few shallow cuts in the thicker parts so the thickness is more even. Sprinkle 0.25 tsp of salt on the inside of each piece, roll up tightly along the long edge, wrap each in food-safe aluminum foil into a cylinder, twist the ends tight and put them in a steaming dish.
2. Put 2000 ml of water in the steamer and heat over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water boils and steam rises steadily. Put in the dish, cover and steam over high heat for 20 minutes, until the chicken rolls hold their shape. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 5 minutes. Open one end and put a thermometer into the centre: it must reach 74°C (165°F). If it has not, steam 3–5 minutes more and measure again.
3. Put the foil-wrapped rolls in the ice water and cool for 10 minutes, until the rolls no longer feel warm. If the centre of a thick roll is still warm, keep cooling in ice water for 5 more minutes and check again. Drain off the water on the outside, remove the foil, and switch to clean tongs.
4. Pour the 350 ml of chicken stock into a shallow metal bowl and set it in a basin of ice water for 15–20 minutes, until the bowl and the stock are clearly cold and no longer steaming. Stir in the salt and sugar for the brine until the crystals dissolve, then add the 240 ml of Huadiao wine and the goji berries.
5. Put the rolls in a glass container that fits them snugly and pour in the brine, so the rolls are completely submerged. Cover and refrigerate for 24 hours in a refrigerator no warmer than 4°C (40°F). Once fully cold the rolls are firmer, the cut faces hold their shape, and both skin and meat carry the wine aroma.
6. When ready to eat, take out a roll and cut it into rounds about 1 cm thick. Arrange them in a shallow bowl and spoon over 2 tbsp of the brine. Serve cold, and keep the remaining chicken soaking in the brine in the refrigerator; eat it within 3 days of cooking.

## Key points

- **Rolls loose, skin falls off when sliced**: they were not rolled tightly, or not fully cooled. The cooked [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) has already set, so roll the chicken tightly while it is raw, then cool and chill it thoroughly, and slice with a sharp knife.
- **Harsh, bitter wine that covers the chicken flavour**: too much wine, or a coarse, strong cooking wine. A good wine keeps its aroma (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Use 240 ml of wine to 350 ml of stock, and do not add stronger liquor.
- **Salty outside, bland inside**: the wine or stock already contains salt and more was added, or the soaking time was too short (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Use unsalted stock and Huadiao wine without added salt, soak for the full chilling time, and keep it in the refrigerator the whole time.
