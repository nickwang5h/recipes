*Gà xào sả ớt* stir-fries the fresh scent of lemongrass and the heat of chili into chicken. This is a home version with curry powder, a little coconut milk and red bell pepper. The coconut milk is reduced until it is almost invisible, so the result is spice-coated pieces of chicken with only a little thick sauce on the plate. It goes well with white rice: first you smell the lemongrass, then taste the savoury fish sauce.

The lemongrass is minced fine and stir-fried briefly, so its aroma is released into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The chicken is browned before the coconut milk goes in. First you get the roasted aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/), then the coconut milk cooks the meat through, so the meat doesn't have to be fried for long in a dry pan. Don't use too much coconut milk, or the chicken will be cooked while the liquid is far from reduced.

## Steps

1. Cut the thigh meat into pieces of about 2.5 cm and toss with the salt, sugar, curry powder and fish sauce. Refrigerate to marinate for at least 15 minutes, until each piece has taken on the colour of the marinade.
2. Peel off the tough outer layer of the lemongrass, cut off the woody upper part and the root end, slice the tender lower core thin across, and chop it very fine. Pinch a bit between your fingers; there should be no long tough fibres. Measure out 3 tbsp, and mince the shallot and chili too.
3. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok. Add the shallot, chili and lemongrass and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until you smell a strong fragrance and the shallot has softened a little, then add the chicken. The lemongrass should not turn deep brown.
4. Add the chicken and bell pepper and toss so every piece picks up the spices. Spread the chicken in a single layer and leave it for about 1 minute, until the underside browns, then flip to fry the other side. If the pan is too small, do this step in two batches.
5. Pour in the well-stirred coconut milk, turn to medium-low, and keep the sauce at a clear simmer, turning the chicken from time to time. After about 6–8 minutes, most of the white coconut milk has cooked away and a thick spiced sauce coats the meat thinly. A little clear oil may appear at the edge of the pan.
6. Insert a thermometer sideways into a large piece of chicken, and take the pan off the heat only when it reads 74°C (165°F). If the chicken is cooked but there is still a lot of sauce, take out the meat first, reduce the sauce until it thinly coats the spatula, and then toss the meat back in. Scatter the cilantro and serve hot.

## Key points

- **A mouthful of tough fibres**: the outer layers or upper part of the lemongrass were used, or it was only cut into rings and not chopped fine. Use only the tender core, slice across it, then chop. Stir-frying won't soften tough fibres. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **The chicken is grey and the roasted aroma is weak**: the pan is crowded with meat and the water on the surface hasn't cleared. Use a large pan or fry in batches, and add the coconut milk only after the pieces have browned. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The sauce is thick but the meat is dry**: too much coconut milk went in, so the chicken cooked along with the sauce for a long time. Take the meat out once it is cooked and reduce the sauce on its own. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
