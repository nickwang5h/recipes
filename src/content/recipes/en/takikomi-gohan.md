Takikomi gohan (Japanese mixed rice) cooks chicken, shiitake, burdock root and carrot together with raw rice in one pot. The grains absorb soy-flavoured dashi, and the flavour of the chicken and vegetables stays in the rice. The aburaage adds fragrance and the konnyaku keeps a little bounce; fluff the rice before serving so the fillings are evenly distributed.

The key to this pot is that [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) needs enough water. Soak the rice in plain water first and add the seasoning for cooking; count all the liquid from the dashi, the shiitake water and the seasonings. The fillings sit on top of the rice so the grains underneath stay in continuous contact with the liquid, and the steam rises to heat the fillings (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Rinse the rice gently under running water 3–4 times, until the rinse water goes from milky white to slightly cloudy. Add 1000 ml of water and soak for 30 minutes, until the grains are opaque white throughout; pour into a sieve and drain for 15 minutes, until nothing continuously drips from the bottom of the sieve.
2. Meanwhile, soak the dried shiitake in 120 ml of warm water for 15 minutes, until the caps are soft and bend even in the centre. Cut off the hard stems and slice thin. Pass the soaking water through a fine sieve into a measuring cup and top up with unsalted [dashi](/recipes/dashi/) to a total of 360 ml.
3. Heat another 1000 ml of water over high heat for about 5 minutes, until the surface is constantly rolling. Blanch the konnyaku for 1 minute, until the slices are hot through; lift out, then blanch the aburaage for 1 minute, until the surface oil spreads out. Drain both, cut into short thin strips and discard the blanching water.
4. Scrape the burdock lightly and shave into thin slices; rinse off the crumbs under running water and drain. Cut the carrot into thin strips and the chicken into thin pieces of similar thickness. Prepare all the fillings before adding any liquid to the rice.
5. Put the drained rice in the rice cooker and add the mirin, soy sauce and the measured 360 ml of mixed dashi. Stir the liquid in gently and level the rice. Spread the chicken evenly over the top first, then the root vegetables, konnyaku, shiitake and aburaage, keeping the fillings on top of the rice.
6. Start the "Mixed Rice" program right away; it usually takes 45–60 minutes, until the program ends and no loose liquid remains on the rice. If you used the plain white rice program, let the rice sit covered for 5 minutes after it ends, until the surface moisture has lessened and the grains are plump. The centre of the chicken must reach 74°C (165°F), and the centre of the grains must have no hard core.
7. Run a rice paddle down the side to the bottom and fold the rice over in sections, cutting and folding to spread the fillings through the rice. Gently separate any clumps and keep the grains whole. Serve immediately and sprinkle with chopped green onion.

## Key points

- **Rice is like porridge and the grains clump**: the shiitake soaking water was added on top of the dashi, or the fillings weren't drained. The soaking water is only part of the 360 ml of dashi, and the seasonings are added separately. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Top layer of rice is undercooked**: the fillings were mixed in with the raw rice and some grains were lifted above the liquid, or the rice wasn't soaked first. Put only levelled rice at the bottom, keep the fillings on top, and finish cooking with the lid closed. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Colour is very dark but the flavour is thin**: a salted stock was used instead of dashi, and extra soy sauce was added to make up the flavour. Build the [umami](/principles/umami/) first with kombu and bonito dashi and the shiitake water, and keep the amount of soy sauce steady.
