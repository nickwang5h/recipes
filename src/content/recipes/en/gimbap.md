Gimbap is rice seasoned with toasted sesame oil and salt, rolled in nori around cooked meat, vegetables and pickled radish. It is a common lunch box staple in Korea. The beef, egg, spinach, carrot and pickled radish are each seasoned on their own, so one bite is savoury, sweet, crisp and nutty with sesame.

When short-grain rice cooks, its [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) makes the grains stick together, so a thin layer is enough to hold the fillings in place. The rice and fillings should be warm or cooled, with no extra water on the surface. The spinach is blanched and squeezed dry first (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)), which cuts down on water seeping out after rolling. Keep the nori dry so the cut ends are clean.

## Steps

1. Mix the beef with the light soy sauce, sugar, 1 clove of minced garlic, black pepper and 2 tsp of toasted sesame oil. Marinate in the fridge for 10 minutes, until the surface is evenly coated with the seasoning. Toss the carrot separately with 0.5 g of salt and leave for 5–10 minutes, until it gives off water and softens slightly. Squeeze out the water.
2. Heat 2000 ml of water over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until at a rolling boil. Blanch the spinach for 30–60 seconds, until the leaves collapse and the stems bend. Lift it into the prepared cold water to cool for 1 minute, until it is no longer hot to the touch. Squeeze dry, cut into short lengths and toss with 2 cloves of minced garlic, 1.5 g of salt and 2 tsp of toasted sesame oil.
3. Add 1 tsp of vegetable oil to a skillet and heat over medium for 1 minute, until the oil spreads easily. Stir-fry the carrot for about 1 minute, until soft but still with some crunch. Remove and spread out.
4. Beat the eggs with 0.5 g of salt. Add the remaining 1 tsp of vegetable oil to the skillet and heat over low for 1 minute. Pour in the egg to make a thin sheet and cook for 2 minutes, until the underside is set. Flip it, take the pan off the heat and cook in the hot pan for about 5 minutes, until the egg is fully cooked and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Remove and cut into long strips.
5. Put the marinated beef in the skillet and stir-fry over medium-high for 2–3 minutes, until the strips are cooked through and the juices they release have mostly cooked off. Spread it out with the other fillings for about 5 minutes, until they stop steaming. Arrange the drained pickled radish with them.
6. Toss the warm rice with 1.5 g of salt and 2 tsp of toasted sesame oil, fluffing it gently. Spread it out for about 5 minutes, until the heat has gone but the rice is still warm and soft. Divide into five portions of about 140 g each.
7. Lay a sheet of nori shiny side down on a bamboo mat and spread a portion of rice over it, leaving about 4 cm bare at the far end. Lay one fifth of each of the five fillings across the rice. Lift the near edge to wrap over the fillings, tighten evenly along the roll, and keep rolling to the bare end, pulling the mat out as you go.
8. Rest the roll seam side down for 1 minute, until the nori sticks. Repeat to roll all five. Brush with the remaining toasted sesame oil and sprinkle with the sesame seeds if using. Cut with a sharp knife into pieces about 1.5 cm thick, wiping the rice paste off the knife every few cuts. Plate and eat.

## Key points

- **Loose roll that falls apart when cut**: the rice is spread too thick or the fillings are piled too high. Spread the rice thin, wrap the fillings tightly on the first roll, then press evenly along the whole roll. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Soggy nori, water at the bottom**: the spinach was not squeezed dry, or hot fillings trapped steam inside the roll. Drain the pickled radish, cook the fillings dry, and let the heat escape before rolling. See [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/).
- **Flattened cut ends, rice dragged into long tails**: the knife is dull or coated in rice paste. Cut through in one stroke with a sharp knife, wipe it clean often, and do not saw back and forth. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
