*Lasagne alla bolognese* is built from thin spinach egg pasta, Bolognese ragù, béchamel and Parmigiano. Every layer of sauce is thin, so the sheets of pasta can still be seen once it is cut. The ragù is simmered ahead and the pasta sheets are boiled ahead; the oven only has to heat the whole dish through and form a thin golden crust on top.

Squeeze the spinach dry so the water in the dough stays under control. Knead it evenly and roll it thin one setting at a time so the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) forms one continuous sheet. The béchamel starts with butter coating the flour, then hot milk is added and it thickens through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), holding the ragù between the layers.

## Steps

1. Put the oil for the ragù, the butter and the onion in a pot and cook over medium heat until translucent. Add the celery and carrots and stir until coated in fat, then add the ground beef, breaking it up with a spoon, and stir-fry until no raw red remains.
2. Pour in the milk for the ragù and cook over low heat until no loose milk can be seen, then add the nutmeg. Pour in the white wine and cook until the liquid is gone, then add the tomatoes, salt and pepper.
3. Turn the ragù to the lowest heat and simmer uncovered for at least 3 hours, with only an occasional bubble in the pot. When the bottom starts to dry, add a small cup of water. At the end reduce it until there are no pools of liquid, a little fat has separated out and the meat is tender.
4. Cook the spinach until soft, drain, cool and squeeze until it no longer drips, then chop it to a fine mince. Knead it with the eggs and flour into a smooth dough, roll it one setting at a time to the thinnest setting, and cut into long sheets that suit the baking dish.
5. Put the pasta sheets in boiling salted water in batches and lift them out a few seconds after the water returns to a boil. Dip them in cold water, rinse gently and lay them flat on a clean cloth to dry. Spread the sheets out; don't stack them or they will stick together.
6. For the béchamel, melt the butter in a small pot, add the flour and stir over low heat for about 2 minutes, keeping it pale. Stir in the hot milk in portions, mixing smooth each time before adding the next. Add salt and keep stirring until it is like thick cream and flows slowly.
7. Preheat the oven to 205°C (400°F). Butter a baking dish of about 23 × 30 cm and spread a small spoonful of béchamel on the bottom. Mix the rest of the béchamel into the warm ragù.
8. Lay down a layer of pasta, spread on a thin layer of the mixed sauce and sprinkle with Parmigiano. Repeat until there are at least six layers. Overlap the edges of the sheets only slightly. Leave enough sauce to cover the top layer, sprinkle on the remaining cheese and dot with the butter for the top.
9. While the pasta and sauce are still warm, bake on the upper rack for about 10–15 minutes, until the top is golden with a thin crust and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). If it hasn't, keep baking, and cover with foil if the top has already coloured. Take it out and rest for 10 minutes, then cut into portions and serve.

## Key points

- **The sheets are sticky and soft, and the layers disappear**: the pasta was rolled thick, or boiled too long (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Blanch the sheets only briefly, because they keep cooking in the oven.
- **The béchamel is lumpy**: the flour was not coated in butter first, or too much milk went in at once (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add the milk in small amounts at the start; if lumps have already formed, whisk them out.
- **The top is burnt and the centre still cold**: a whole dish that has been refrigerated conducts heat more slowly than one assembled warm (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Bake it longer after refrigeration, and cover with foil once the top has coloured; don't copy the short baking time.
