Fuqi feipian is a cold chili oil dish from Chengdu, usually made by pairing beef with tripe, tongue and other offal. This version uses beef brisket and tripe. The brisket is tender and the tripe keeps its chew. After braising, the meat is sliced thin and tossed with chili oil, braising liquid and spiced soy sauce.

Braising gradually softens the collagen in the connective tissue (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)), but you stop when the meat can be sliced and hasn't yet fallen apart. Once cooled, slice thin against the grain (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). It is easier to chew, and the dressing touches more surface. The spiced soy sauce is first cooked down with sugar and whole spices to a body. The chili oil and ground Sichuan pepper stay in the dressing to keep their aroma (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. First make the spiced soy sauce. Soak the star anise, bay leaf, cassia, fennel seeds, cloves, tangerine peel and tsaoko in 250 ml of hot water for 30 minutes, until the spices have taken up water and smell fragrant. Drain and discard the soaking water.
2. Put the soaked spices and the rest of that group's ingredients in a small saucepan and heat over medium for 3–5 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and the edges bubble. Cook at the lowest heat with the lid half on for 30 minutes, until the liquid is slightly thick and smells of soy. Turn off the heat, cover and steep for 1 hour, until the liquid is no longer hot to the touch, then strain out the solids. Use 2 tbsp for this dish and keep the rest in a clean container in the refrigerator.
3. Put the brisket and tripe in 2000 ml of cold water and heat over high heat for about 12–15 minutes to a boil. Then cook over medium heat for 10 minutes and skim off the foam thoroughly, until no large patches of grey scum remain on the surface.
4. Add the seasonings from the braising group, cover and cook over low heat for about 2 hours, keeping the surface at a gentle bubble. The brisket should be pierced through easily with a chopstick but still be in one piece. Cut off a corner of the tripe and bite it: it should bite through and be springy. Judge them separately. Lift out whichever reaches that state first, and cook the one still tough for another 15 minutes.
5. Spread the meats out on separate shallow plates and let them cool for 10 minutes, until the surface no longer steams clearly. Then refrigerate for 1 hour, until the pieces are cool and the surface is firm. Set aside 3 tbsp of the braising liquid in a small bowl, cool it for 10 minutes and refrigerate it for 1 hour, until it is no longer warm.
6. Slice the brisket across the grain into slices about 3 mm thick, and slice the tripe on the diagonal into thin slices. The slices should be thin and whole. Draw the knife through the meat, and avoid pressing it again and again.
7. Mix 2 tbsp of spiced soy sauce, 3 tbsp of braising liquid, the chili oil with sediment, vinegar, ground Sichuan pepper, mashed garlic, grated ginger and chopped green onion. Taste, and add salt as needed. Toss in the sliced meat and turn until every slice is coated in the bright red dressing.
8. Pile into a deep plate and serve, scattered with crushed peanuts, sesame seeds and cilantro. Add the peanuts last to keep them crisp, and leave a layer of dressing at the bottom of the plate so each bite of meat comes with some.

## Key points

- **The tripe is still like rubber**: cooked through and softened are two different things, and the connective tissue hasn't loosened. Keep cooking over low heat and taste a bite every 15 minutes. If the beef is already soft, lift it out first. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **The beef falls apart as soon as you cut it**: it was braised too long, or you cut while the meat was hot and soft. Check it for wholeness once a chopstick pierces it through, and slice thin against the grain after cooling. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **The dressing is salty but not fragrant**: the braising liquid was reduced too far, or the Sichuan pepper powder had lost its aroma. Taste the liquid before you take it, save the salt to add last, and add the ground pepper when you mix the dressing. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
