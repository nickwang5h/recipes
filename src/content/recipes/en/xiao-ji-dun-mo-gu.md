Chicken stewed with mushrooms cooks bone-in chicken and dried mushrooms in one pot: the chicken juices seep into the mushrooms, and the mushroom aroma melts into the broth. A whole chicken is chopped up, with green onion, ginger and one star anise as the base, and the soy sauce stews into a savoury broth.

The chicken pieces are first seared to a light gold, and the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) gives the seared flavour. Then moist heat slowly softens the skin and connective tissue; see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). The [umami](/principles/umami/) of the chicken and the mushrooms comes together in the broth, and a final slight reduction makes the flavour cling to the chicken and mushrooms.

## Steps

1. Rinse the dust off the dried shiitake and soak in 300 ml of warm water for about 20 minutes, until the caps are soft and a pinched-open piece has no hard core. Snip off the hard stems and halve large mushrooms. Let the soaking liquid settle for 5 minutes until the grit sinks, then ladle out 240 ml of the clear liquid from the top.
2. Chop the chicken into bone-in pieces about 4 cm across, pick off any bone chips and blot the surface dry with paper towels. Leave the white parts of the green onions in long sections, chop the green parts, and slice the ginger. Pieces of similar size take similar times to stew soft.
3. Add 1 tbsp of oil to a heavy-bottomed pot and heat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface of the oil ripples slightly. Lay the chicken in the pot in two batches and fry each side for 2–3 minutes, until the skin and meat side are pale golden, then remove.
4. Add the other 1 tbsp of oil to the same pot, and add the green onion whites, ginger slices and star anise. Fry over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, until the edges of the ginger are slightly yellow and it smells fragrant. Return the chicken, pour in 240 ml of the mushroom liquid and 1200 ml of clean water, and add the Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, sugar and salt.
5. Cook over medium-high heat for about 5–8 minutes, until the broth boils, and skim the foam. Add the shiitake, cover, turn to low and stew for about 30 minutes, keeping the surface of the broth bubbling gently. Stir once at 15 minutes so the skin does not stick to the bottom.
6. Take the thickest piece of chicken. When the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), chopsticks go in fairly easily and the meat at the joints begins to loosen, it is done. If the meat is still firm, stew for 5–10 more minutes and test again; with young chicken, start reducing as soon as it is soft.
7. Uncover, turn to medium-high heat and reduce the sauce for about 5–10 minutes, until the broth thinly coats the chicken and the mushroom caps with a layer of flowing sauce still left at the bottom. Keep lifting the ingredients from the bottom of the pot, stir in the chopped green onion at the end and cook for 30 seconds, until it softens, then serve in a deep dish.

## Key points

- **Chicken is pale and has no seared flavour**: the surface was wet, or too much went in at once and the pan temperature dropped (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Dry the chicken and sear it in batches to a pale gold before stewing.
- **Meat is cooked but still firm**: cooked and stewed tender are two different things (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Test the thickest piece first, then keep stewing according to how soft it is; older chicken takes longer, and young chicken should not be stewed until it falls apart.
- **Broth is salty and the mushrooms are gritty**: reducing concentrates the salt, and sediment from the soaking liquid stays in the dish (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). Use only the clear soaking liquid, and stop reducing once the broth clings to the chicken.
