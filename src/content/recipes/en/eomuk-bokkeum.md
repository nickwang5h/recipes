Eomuk bokkeum is a common Korean side dish. Thin fish cake sheets are stir-fried with onion and green onion and coated with a sweet and salty soy sauce glaze, soft when warm and still springy when cold. This is the usual non-spicy version: red and green bell peppers cut thin are stir-fried briefly with the fish cakes as a side dish for rice.

The fish cakes are already cooked, so the point is to heat them until soft and let the sauce cling. A short blanch first removes some of the surface oil and softens the refrigerated sheets again. Once in the pan they need only a short stir-fry; cut the vegetables thin so they reach the right texture at the same time as the fish cakes (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Stir together the soy sauce, mirin, sugar and black pepper. Cut the bell peppers into strips about 5 mm wide and the onion into slices about 3 mm thin, so they soften in a short time.
2. Put 1000 ml of water in a pot and heat it over high heat for about 5–7 minutes, until the surface is rolling. Turn off the heat, put in the fish cakes and soak for 0.5 minutes, until the sheets bend easily, then lift them out right away.
3. Spread the fish cakes in a colander and drain for 2 minutes, until they stop dripping; use this time to finish cutting the green onion and garlic. Cut the fish cakes into pieces about 3 cm wide, separating any pieces that stick together.
4. Add the vegetable oil to a skillet and preheat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows faster. Add the garlic, onion and green onion and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the garlic is fragrant and the edges of the onion turn translucent.
5. Turn to medium heat, add the fish cakes and bell peppers and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the fish cakes are heated through and soft and the pepper strips are slightly bent. Turn with the spatula from the bottom, so you do not break the thin sheets.
6. Pour in the sauce and keep stir-frying for about 1 minute, until every piece of fish cake is stained light brown and there is no pooled sauce at the bottom of the pan. The bell peppers should still have a little crispness and the fish cakes should bend without being dry and hard. Take the pan off the heat when it reaches this state.

## Key points

- **The fish cakes are so salty they cover the savoury flavour**: the fish cakes already contain salt, and the soy sauce also concentrates as it reduces. Use the measured amount of soy sauce, add no extra salt, and do not stir-fry for long to chase a darker colour. See [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/).
- **The garlic is scorched and bitter and the edges of the fish cakes are hard**: the heat was too high or the stir-frying too long. Add the main ingredients as soon as the garlic is fragrant, turn to medium heat after the fish cakes go in, and stop as soon as the sauce clings. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Water pools at the bottom of the lunch box**: the blanched fish cakes were not drained well, or the sauce was not reduced. Before packing, make sure there is no loose liquid in the pan; spread it in a shallow container to let the strong steam escape, refrigerate promptly, and close the lid tightly only when it is fully cold. The next day, reheat to 74°C (165°F) at the centre, turning it midway, so the thin sheets heat evenly. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
