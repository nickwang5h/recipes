In dry-fried beef chow fun, each noodle is distinct and carries a toasty fragrance, the beef is tender and smooth, and the sauce coats the noodles in only a thin layer, with no pool at the bottom of the plate. A home stove has limited heat, so drain the ingredients first and stir-fry in two batches, so the water in the pan evaporates in time and you get the [aroma of browning](/principles/maillard-reaction/).

Fresh rice noodles are already cooked, so in the wok they only need to be heated through and coloured. After refrigeration the noodles stiffen as the starch [retrogrades](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and pulling them apart by force makes them break; loosen them first, and lift them from the bottom as you stir-fry, which keeps the long strands better than poking back and forth with the tip of the spatula. Use the same wok throughout, and keep the beef and the first batch of noodles on a plate in between.

## Steps

1. Slice the beef against the grain and mix in the water, baking soda, cornstarch, oyster sauce, 2 tsp of the Shaoxing wine and 1 tsp of the light soy sauce. Toss until the water is absorbed and the surface is slightly tacky, then marinate in the refrigerator for 30 minutes.
2. While the beef marinates, drain the sprouts thoroughly, cut the green onions, and loosen the fresh noodles strand by strand. If the refrigerated noodles are too stiff to separate, bring the noodle-cooking water to a boil in the wok for 5–8 minutes, add the noodles and blanch for 0.5 minute, until they separate gently, then lift out and drain; pour out the water and wipe the wok dry.
3. Mix the stir-frying light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, sesame oil and sugar. Divide the beef, noodles, sprouts, green onions and ginger, the seasoning sauce, 2 tbsp of Shaoxing wine and 3 tbsp of oil into two equal portions each, and stir-fry two batches one after the other.
4. Heat the wok over high heat for 1.5 minutes, until the surface is dry and hot air is clearly rising. Add half of the oil for this batch, spread half the beef in the wok and sear and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the surface is browned and the slices separated, then move it to a clean plate.
5. Add the rest of this batch's oil and half the ginger to the same wok and stir-fry for 0.25 minute, until you smell the ginger; add half the green onions and noodles, spread out and stir-fry for 0.25 minute, until the noodles are hot and soft with a little searing at the edges, then drizzle half the Shaoxing wine around the rim of the wok.
6. Add half the seasoning sauce and the seared beef, lift and toss for 1 minute, until the colour is even and the beef is cooked through. Add half the sprouts and the white pepper and stir-fry another 30–45 seconds, until the sprouts are hot and slightly softened and there is no pooled liquid in the bottom of the wok, then serve at once.
7. Stir-fry the second batch in the same wok in the same order, which takes about 4 minutes, judged the same way: noodles hot and soft, beef cooked through, sprouts slightly softened. Serve both plates hot.

## Key points

- **Clumped together and broken noodles**: the cold noodles were not loosened, or the spatula kept chopping and pressing. Retrogradation makes noodles stiff, so warm them and loosen them first, then lift from the bottom of the wok as you toss (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Wet noodles and soup at the bottom of the plate**: the vegetables carried water in, or the wok was too full for its temperature to recover. Stir-fry in batches, drain the sprouts, and leave room for water to evaporate (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Soy sauce is dark but the noodles are not fragrant**: the liquid went in too early, and the noodles sat in the sauce. Let the noodles heat against the wok before seasoning, so the surface has a chance to dry and [brown](/principles/maillard-reaction/), and turn them over when they colour.
