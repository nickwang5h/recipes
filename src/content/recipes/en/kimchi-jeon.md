Kimchi jeon fries well-soured napa cabbage kimchi into a thin batter, giving crisp, browned edges and soft kimchi to bite into in the middle. The kimchi and the juice in the jar already carry salt, sourness and heat, and a little sugar rounds off the sourness. The everyday version needs only flour, green onion and water to hold together as a pancake.

The starch in the flour absorbs water and gelatinizes as it heats, binding the kimchi into one pancake (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The underside then keeps losing water, which forms the crisp crust. The oil fills the small gaps between the pan and the pancake and helps with [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Stirring the batter as little as possible limits [gluten](/principles/gluten/) development, so the pancake is not tough to chew.

## Steps

1. First scoop 2 tbsp juice from the jar of kimchi, then weigh out 230 g kimchi and cut it into pieces about 1 cm long. Cut thick stems finer so they do not make the pancake too thick. Chop the green onion and put it with the kimchi.
2. Mix the flour, sugar, cold water and kimchi juice gently, and stop when no dry flour is visible. Fold in the kimchi and green onion. The batter should run off the spoon, with a thin coat of batter joining the pieces of kimchi, and no lump of dry flour at the bottom of the bowl.
3. Use a non-stick frying pan about 24 cm across and preheat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is evenly warm. Add 1 tbsp oil and swirl to coat. Add half the batter, spread the kimchi apart with the back of a spoon and shape it into one thin round pancake, with the centre about as thick as the edge.
4. Fry over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until the edge has set and the underside is golden, the spatula slides under easily and the whole pancake can move gently. Check the middle: if the surface is wet and shiny you can keep cooking, and if the bottom is turning dark brown, lower the heat a little rather than rushing to flip.
5. Hold the pancake with a wide spatula and flip it, then pour 1 tbsp oil around the edge of the pan. Lift the edge of the pancake slightly and tilt the pan so the oil runs underneath. Keep frying for 2–3 minutes, until the second side is golden and the edge is crisp. Press down only on raised spots, so the centre sits against the pan.
6. Flip it back to the first side and fry for about 1 minute, until the underside is crisp again and the centre shows no wet raw batter or floury taste. Slide it onto a plate and lay it flat. The crisp edge should crack audibly when cut. Eat it hot.
7. Fry the second pancake the same way with the remaining 2 tbsp oil: about 3 minutes on the first side, 2–3 minutes after flipping and 1 minute on the final return, going by golden and crisp on every side. Put the second pancake on its own plate too, and do not stack hot pancakes.

## Key points

- **Only the edge is crisp and the middle is soft and wet**: the pancake is spread too thick, or the oil stayed only around the outside. Spread the kimchi out evenly, make the centre thinner, and lift the edge after flipping so the oil gets underneath (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Tough pancake, like thick dough**: too much flour was added, or the batter was beaten too long. Measure the flour, stop mixing when the dry flour is gone, and let the kimchi make up most of the pancake (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Falls apart when flipping**: the starch underneath has not gelatinized and set enough. Wait until the whole pancake moves and the spatula can lift it intact before flipping; in a small pan, making two pancakes is easier to handle (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Goes soft again soon after leaving the pan**: hot pancakes were stacked or covered, so steam stayed in the crisp crust. Lay them flat, fry and eat them right away, and do not cover them on the plate (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
