Youtiao are made of two strips of dough pressed together and fried, with a thin crisp shell and big holes inside. They are often dipped in [soy milk](/recipes/dou-jiang/). The dough must be soft and still stretch: [gluten](/principles/gluten/) traps the gas released by the leavening and the steam produced by heat, and resting makes the dough easier to pull long.

At home, baking powder plus a little baking soda does the leavening. Stack the strips, press a line down the middle, stretch them long and fry. Hot oil makes the dough puff quickly, and the surface loses water and turns into a crisp shell (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Baking soda also helps colour, but use only a little, because too much tastes of lye.

## Steps

1. Mix the flour, baking powder, baking soda and salt. Add the beaten egg, water and the oil for the dough, and mix into a soft dough with no dry flour left. Cover and rest for 15 minutes, so the flour absorbs enough water and the dough is even.
2. Rub a thin coat of oil on your hands. Press the dough lightly with your fist, then fold the four sides toward the centre. Repeat the pressing and folding for about 5–8 minutes, until the surface is smooth and the dough stretches without breaking. The dough is soft by nature; if it sticks to your hands, gather it with a bench scraper instead of adding flour.
3. Divide into two pieces, shape each into a ball and rub with a thin coat of oil. Cover and refrigerate for 12 hours. The next day, take it out and let it come to room temperature for 1 hour, until the centre is no longer cold, the dough is soft and it does not spring straight back when stretched. If it is still cold, give it another 15 minutes and check again.
4. Dust the board thinly with the flour for dusting. Gently press each piece into a rectangle about 10×25 cm, even in thickness. Cut each rectangle crosswise into 10 strips, giving 20 short strips. Once the dough has rested, do not knead it into a ball again, or it will spring back when you stretch it.
5. Stack two strips together and press a line down the long axis with a chopstick, sticking the middle firmly while the edges stay separate. Make 10 youtiao in all. Cover the ones you have pressed so the surface does not dry out, because a dry skin gets in the way of puffing.
6. Heat the frying oil over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until a thermometer reads 190°C (375°F). Hold a strip by both ends, pull it gently to about 20 cm and slide it in close to the surface of the oil. Fry 1–2 at a time, keeping the oil at 190–200°C (375–400°F).
7. Keep turning them gently with long chopsticks and fry for about 1 minute, until both sides are clearly puffed, no longer growing and golden all over. Lift out onto a rack and drain for 1 minute, until they no longer drip steadily. Tear one open: it should have big holes inside, with no wet, raw dough in the seam.

## Key points

- **Short, thick and solid inside**: the dough is too dry, or it was kneaded tight again after resting, so the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) cannot stretch. Keep the egg and water as written, shape gently after a full rest, and stretch the strips long just before they go in the oil.
- **Two strips fry into two separate sticks**: the middle line was not pressed firmly, or there is too much dusting flour where they stack. Brush off the extra flour before stacking, so the middle sticks together and the two sides puff separately.
- **Dark brown outside, raw in the seam**: the [oil](/principles/oil-temperature/) is too hot, or the strips are too thick. Control the heat with a thermometer, cut the strips to an even thickness and keep turning them once they are in the pan.
- **Bitter, strong taste of lye**: too much baking soda. Alkalinity also speeds up the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) and darkens the colour. Use 0.25 tsp, and do not add more soda if the puff is not enough.
