This pot of chicken soup with Chinese yam and pumpkin is golden and a little thick, with floury chestnuts and yam, and red dates adding a touch of sweetness. The chicken makes the base of the broth. The pumpkin is steamed and mashed first and stirred in at the end, and once it spreads through, the soup turns rich. The salt goes in last.

The chicken is first [blanched](/principles/blanching/) to wash off the foam clinging to it, then gently stewed in fresh water. The yam and chestnuts absorb water and soften in the soup, and the pumpkin purée brings in fine bits of flesh and starch that is already cooked (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Because the purée goes in late, the chicken broth is already done and the soup is less likely to stick to the pot.

## Steps

1. Put the chicken pieces in a pot of cold water with enough water to cover the meat, and add half of the ginger slices and 10 ml of Shaoxing wine. Bring to a boil and cook for 3 minutes, skim off the foam, then lift out the chicken, wash off the clinging particles and drain.
2. Wearing gloves, peel the yam and cut it into rolling pieces. Shell the chestnuts and remove the inner skin. Wash and pit the red dates and set aside; pick out every hard pit so none ends up in the thick soup at the end.
3. Wash the small pumpkin and cut it open. Steam until a chopstick goes into the flesh easily, remove the seeds, take the flesh and mash it into a fine purée. You can start stewing the chicken while the pumpkin steams; set the purée aside once mashed.
4. Put the chicken, yam, chestnuts and the remaining ginger slices in the soup pot and add 1200 ml of water, just enough to barely cover the ingredients. Bring to a boil, turn to low heat, cover with the lid and stew for 30 minutes, keeping the edge of the pot at a light bubble.
5. Check the chestnuts and yam; a chopstick should go in easily. The centre of the thickest piece of chicken should reach 74°C (165°F). If the chestnuts are still hard or the chicken is not cooked, keep stewing until done before adding the pumpkin purée.
6. Stir the pumpkin purée into the soup in portions, add the red dates and cook over low heat for 10 more minutes. Push gently along the bottom with a spoon now and then. The soup should be an even golden colour with no clumps of purée, and the date flesh should have softened.
7. Add the salt and stir. Taste, then decide whether to use all 5 g. The soup should run smoothly off the spoon; if it is too thick, add a little hot water, bring it back to a boil and then ladle it out.

## Key points

- **Grey scum on the soup**: the coagulated protein clinging after blanching was not washed off (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Pour out the blanching water and wash the pot before stewing; skim any later foam promptly.
- **Soup sticks to the bottom after the pumpkin goes in**: the flesh and starch thicken the soup and slow the flow at the bottom (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add the pumpkin last, keep the heat low and push gently along the bottom.
- **Chicken is cooked but dry and stringy**: the pieces were too small, the heat too strong, or boneless breast was stewed for a long time (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose bone-in young chicken pieces, and finish once the chicken is cooked and the vegetables are soft.
