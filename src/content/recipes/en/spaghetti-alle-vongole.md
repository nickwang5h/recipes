*Spaghetti alle vongole* in its white version is light and tastes of the sea: the pasta is coated in a thin layer of clam liquor, garlic and olive oil. The liquid the clams release as they open is the base of the sauce. Strain out the sand first, then use it to finish cooking the pasta so the flavour of the sea gets into the noodles.

The starch released by the pasta thickens the liquor slightly, and tossing breaks the olive oil into fine droplets (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The clams must be lifted out first. The pasta needs time to absorb the liquid, and if the clam meat keeps cooking alongside, its protein tightens and loses water, and the meat turns tough.

## Steps

1. Check the live clams and discard any with broken shells or any that do not close when tapped. If the fish counter has not purged the sand, soak them in cold salt water in the refrigerator for about 2 hours; lift them out from the top, avoiding the sand at the bottom, then scrub the shells. For clams already cleaned, rinse them as the fish counter advises.
2. Bring a pot of water to a boil with a little salt. The clam liquor is already salty and the pasta water may also go into the sauce. Add the spaghetti and cook it about two minutes less than the time suggested on the package, until the centre still has a distinct bite.
3. In a wide pan, put 1 tbsp olive oil, the smashed garlic and the optional chili, and warm gently until you can smell the garlic. Pour in the white wine and cook for about 1 minute, then add the clams, cover and cook over medium-high heat for about 3–4 minutes, until they open.
4. Lift the opened clams out promptly and keep checking the rest, discarding any that are still closed after cooking. Keep all the liquid in the pan and strain it into a bowl through a sieve lined with fine cloth; throw away the sand and shell bits left in the sieve.
5. Pour the liquor back into the wide pan, add the not-yet-cooked pasta, and toss over medium heat for about 2 minutes so the pasta absorbs the liquor. When the liquid is nearly gone and the centre of the pasta is still hard, add a little pasta water in small amounts. Finish only when the pasta tastes cooked and has some bite.
6. Add the remaining 1 tbsp oil and toss quickly until only a thin, slightly cloudy, glossy liquid is left in the pan and there are no free oil droplets on the pasta. Toss the clams and parsley back in, and plate as soon as the clam meat is hot. Taste the pasta with sauce before deciding whether to add salt.

## Key points

- **The sauce separates into oil and water**: there is not enough water or not enough tossing. Add a small spoonful of pasta water and keep tossing until the thin sauce clings to the pasta, then plate it right away. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **The clams are like rubber**: they stayed in the pan too long after opening. Lift them out first, and return them to the pan briefly to heat once the pasta is cooked; choose clams of similar size and lift them out in batches. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Sand in the pasta, and the flavour turns salty and bitter**: the liquor was not strained finely, or it was reduced too far. Strain it even after purging, add water as the pasta absorbs the liquor according to how it looks, and season with salt last. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
