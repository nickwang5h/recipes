*Moo tod kratiem* is a Thai home-style garlic pork: the slices are pan-fried golden, the garlic and pepper are strong in every bite, and the fried garlic scattered on top is crisp and fragrant. Serve it with white rice or sticky rice. The garlic and pepper are pounded into the marinade first, and a second batch of garlic is fried after the pork, so the aroma stays in the meat and on the surface too (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The pork is pounded tender first, then mixed with a little starch and the seasonings. Fry over medium-low heat and lift the pork out once both sides are coloured and the meat is cooked through, so long frying does not dry it out. Remove the burnt bits of garlic from the pan before frying the fresh garlic (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).

## Steps

1. Slice the pork and pound it with a meat mallet. Pound half the garlic fine, add the ground coriander seed and white pepper, and pound to a spice paste; coarsely chop the rest of the garlic and set it aside.
2. Mix the pork with the spice paste, cornstarch, fish sauce, light soy sauce, sugar and toasted sesame oil, toss to coat, cover and refrigerate to marinate for at least 30 minutes.
3. Heat the pan, add the oil, and pan-fry the pork over medium-low heat, flipping to cook both sides golden. Cut into the thickest slice; when the centre reaches 71°C (160°F) it is done. Lift the pork out and drain off the extra oil.
4. Scoop out the blackened bits of garlic from the pan, add the chopped garlic, fry until golden and lift it out at once, then drain on paper towels for a few minutes.
5. Scatter the fried garlic over the pork, add cilantro leaves if you like, and serve with rice.

## Key points

- **Bitter, black garlic**: garlic pieces heat quickly and keep darkening after the colour starts to change. Fry only to golden and lift it out at once (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Hard, dry pork**: the slices were fried too long and the moisture was pushed out. Cut an even thickness and lift the pork out as soon as it is cooked through (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Dry after reheating from a lunch box**: pack the pork and the fried garlic separately, spread them shallow to let steam escape, refrigerate promptly, and eat within 2–3 days. Microwave with the lid loosely on, stir halfway, reheat to 74°C (165°F) in the centre, then scatter on the fried garlic (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
