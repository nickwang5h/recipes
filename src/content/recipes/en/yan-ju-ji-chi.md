Salt-baked chicken wings have soft, pliable skin, and when you open the paper packet you smell the chicken juices and sand ginger first. Traditional salt-baked chicken wraps the marinated chicken in paper, buries it in hot coarse salt and bakes it in a covered pot. The wings use the same method, and sand ginger brings the aroma this dish is known for (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The fine salt in the marinade does the seasoning. The coarse salt outside the paper surrounds the packets, holds heat and passes it to the wings slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The paper keeps the salt grains away and holds in some of the chicken juices. The wings must be marinated first and the surface left to dry. If a packet tears, hot salt touches the chicken directly and that spot gets too salty.

## Steps

1. Pat the wings dry and rub them with the Shaoxing wine, sand ginger powder, white pepper and 4 g of fine salt. Lay them on a rack over a drip tray and marinate in the fridge for 12 hours, until the skin is fairly dry and no runny wine clings to it.
2. Divide the wings and 1.5 slices of ginger into two portions and brush the surface thinly with oil. Wrap each tightly in two layers of food-safe parchment paper with the fold facing up. Flatten each packet into a single layer, and set the two packets side by side in the pot, not stacked.
3. Pour the coarse salt into a dry, heavy-bottomed uncoated pot and stir over medium heat for 15 minutes, until the salt grains are dry and loose and slightly pale beige. Use a long-handled spatula to move most of the hot salt into a heatproof metal bowl, leaving a layer about 1 cm thick on the bottom of the pot.
4. Set the packets on the salt layer, shovel the hot salt from the bowl back to fill in around them and cover the top, so the paper doesn't touch the bare pot bottom. Cover the pot and bake over medium-low heat for 35 minutes. Keep the baking dry in the pot; the packets should stay intact with no scorched smoke.
5. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 30–40 minutes so the residual heat of the salt keeps baking. Dig away the salt on top, lift out the packets with tongs, brush off the salt on the outside of the paper and open them. Once the chicken is opaque, insert a thermometer into the thickest part, away from the bone; it is cooked at 74°C (165°F).
6. If the centre hasn't reached that, wrap the wings tightly again, bury them in the hot salt, cover and bake over low heat for 5 more minutes before testing again. Let the cooked wings rest for 5 minutes, until no longer too hot to handle, then plate and spoon the clear chicken juices from the packet back over the wings.

## Key points

- **One piece is very salty, and the paper has a tear**: coarse salt reached the chicken directly through the break (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Use two layers of paper with the folds offset, handle the packets gently once wrapped, and brush off the salt grains outside the paper before opening.
- **Paper scorched on the bottom, wing meat still raw**: the bottom salt layer is too thin or the heat too high, so one spot overheated. Lay the insulating salt layer, control the heat, and judge doneness by the centre temperature (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Skin goes soft after packing for lunch**: take off the baking paper before boxing, so the juices only thinly coat the wings (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Pack in shallow containers and refrigerate promptly, cover tightly only when fully cold, and reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) in the centre.
