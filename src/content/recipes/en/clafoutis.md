*Clafoutis aux cerises* is a home dessert from the cherry season in the Limousin, made by baking sweet cherries in a thin egg and milk batter. The proteins link into a network with heat and the starch in the flour swells as it takes up water, and together they hold up the fruit (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The batter is as thin as crêpe batter, and the baked centre is tender.

The classic version uses dark sweet cherries with the pits in. Here they are pitted, which is easier to eat. Frozen sweet cherries work too, but thaw them and drain off the juice first. The sugar is divided in two: one part goes into the batter and one over the cherries. Choose a shallow dish of about 1.7–1.9 L capacity and about 4 cm deep, and serve it warm.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 175°C (350°F) and lightly butter the bottom and sides of the baking dish. Stem and pit the cherries and drain them; set aside any juice that runs out when pitting, and do not pour it into the batter.
2. Whisk the eggs with the 65 g of sugar for the batter, gradually mix in the flour and a pinch of salt, then add the milk and vanilla in several additions. Mix until there is no dry flour or lumps, then pass through a fine sieve to get a thin batter that pours smoothly.
3. Pour a layer of batter about 5 mm thick into the baking dish and put it in the oven first, baking until the surface has just set into a thin film. Take it out as soon as it can hold up the cherries; this layer does not need to colour.
4. Arrange the cherries over the base layer, sprinkle on the 65 g of sugar for the fruit and pour in the rest of the batter. Shake gently to level it and spread the cherries evenly, not crowding them in the centre, which would be too thick to bake through.
5. Bake on the middle rack for about 50–60 minutes, until the batter is puffed and the edges are golden. When a thin skewer inserted in the centre, in a spot with no fruit, comes out with no liquid batter and a thermometer in the centre reads 74°C (165°F), it is ready to come out.
6. Cut and eat while warm. The batter will sink slightly after it comes out; set edges and a soft centre mean it is right. Cover and refrigerate any leftovers, and do not leave them out overnight at room temperature.

## Key points

- **Wet and thin around the cherries, as if undercooked**: the fruit brought in too much juice and thinned the batter. Drain thawed frozen cherries well, avoid the fruit when checking doneness, and keep baking if the centre still has liquid batter. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Hard at the edges, still liquid in the centre**: the dish is too deep, so the heat has too different a distance to travel to the outer ring and the centre. Use a shallow dish, spread the fruit evenly, and once the top has enough colour, loosely cover with foil and keep baking. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Dry, tough and weeping**: it was baked longer to keep it puffed up and the proteins tightened. Take it out as soon as the centre has just set; a slight sink while cooling is unavoidable. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Small lumps of flour**: the liquid went in all at once and the dry flour was not dispersed evenly. Mix the flour into the egg first, add the milk gradually and sieve before it goes in the dish. Do not count on the oven to dissolve lumps. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
