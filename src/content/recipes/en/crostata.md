*Crostata di marmellata* is an Italian home-style tart that holds jam in a sweet pastry shell. The crust is crumbly, the filling sweet and tart, and the crossed lattice on top is both decoration and a window onto the colour of the jam. The pastry is made from flour, butter, sugar and egg, and the lemon zest keeps the butter flavour fresh. Use a shallow tart pan about 22 cm across.

The key to a crumbly crust is not letting [gluten](/principles/gluten/) develop. Work the butter into the flour first, then add the egg, and stop as soon as the dough comes together. Chilling firms the butter and makes the dough easier to roll. In the oven, the heat has to pass through the jam and the bottom crust to reach the base, so check the colour of the base as well as the top (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Beat the whole egg and the yolk together and keep about 1 tsp aside for brushing. Mix the flour, sugar and lemon zest, add the cold butter and rub it in with your fingertips until it looks like coarse sand with no large pieces of butter. If your hands are warm, cut it in with a scraper instead, and don't melt the butter into grease.
2. Add the rest of the egg and mix lightly until the crumbs hold when pinched, then press them into a dough. Flatten it, wrap it and chill for about 30 minutes, until it doesn't slump when you pick it up and isn't sticky. Don't knead it over and over like bread.
3. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F) and grease the pan thinly with butter. Set aside about one third of the dough for the lattice. Roll the rest on a lightly floured counter into a round about 4 mm thick, lay it in the pan, press it firmly into the base and corners, and trim the edge.
4. Stir the jam loose and spread it evenly over the crust, keeping the thickness as even as possible and leaving a small gap near the edge. Jam so thick it won't move can be warmed slightly before spreading, but never pour boiling-hot filling into the raw crust.
5. Roll the reserved dough to the same thickness and cut it into strips about 1 cm wide. Lay them crosswise over the jam. Press the ends lightly onto the rim to fix them, and brush the top lightly with the reserved egg. Don't press the strips through the jam to the bottom.
6. Bake on the lower-middle rack for about 35–45 minutes, until the rim and lattice are golden, the jam bubbles in places and the bottom crust has colour too. If the top is already dark while the base is still pale, cover loosely with foil and keep baking, judging by the colour of the base.
7. Let it cool completely in the pan, then unmould and slice. A hot crust is still fragile and breaks if you lift it out by force. Once cool, the jam also stays in the slices better.

## Key points

- **Hard crust that is chewy to bite**: the dough was still being kneaded after it came together, so too much gluten formed. Press it together instead of kneading again and again, and chill it briefly if it won't roll out (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Edge shrinks and the lattice warps**: the dough is too warm, or it was stretched too much when rolled. Chill until firm before working, and lay the crust gently in the pan instead of stretching it and pressing it down (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Lattice browned but the base is wet and pale**: the filling is too thin or too thick, so the base gets too little heat. Use thick jam, bake on the lower-middle rack, and check the bottom crust instead of only the colour on top (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
