In Yangzhou fried rice, the egg, shrimp and meat are all cut small and spread evenly, so every mouthful has a different texture. It uses luncheon meat, carrot and optional corn, so it is easy to make at home. Cold rice turns firm and dry on the surface after cooling, so it breaks apart easily in the wok (see the retrogradation in [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

To keep the rice grains separate, drain the fillings first and cook them in batches. If a lot of wet shrimp, wet vegetables and rice go into the wok at once, the heat goes into evaporating water first (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). So cook the fillings through and take them out, then stir-fry the rice together at the end, and even a home stove can make dry rice.

## Steps

1. Cut the carrot and luncheon meat into small dice about 0.5 cm. Cut the white and green parts of the green onion into short sections and keep them apart. Beat the eggs, and loosen the large clumps of cold rice with your hands or a fork.
2. Bring water to a boil and cook the carrot, green peas and optional corn until the carrot can be bitten through and the peas have no hard core. Lift out and drain.
3. Cook the shrimp in the same boiling water until the flesh is opaque and cooked through in the centre, then lift out and drain. Cut larger shrimp into short pieces, so they are close in size to the other fillings.
4. Wipe the wok dry, add 20 ml of oil and heat over medium heat until the oil flows smoothly. Pour in the egg, let the bottom set and gently push it into pieces. Take it out as soon as the surface has just set, and do not fry it until dry.
5. Add 5 ml of oil, add the luncheon meat, cooked vegetables and shrimp, and stir-fry for about 1–2 minutes, until there are no water droplets on the surface of the fillings, then take them out and set aside.
6. Add the remaining 10 ml of oil to the wok and stir-fry the white part of the green onion until fragrant. Turn to medium-low, add the rice, press apart the clumps with the back of the spatula, and stir-fry until the grains have separated and are evenly coated in oil. Do not smash the grains with the edge of the spatula.
7. Add the egg pieces, press them apart and mix in. Turn to high, return the remaining fillings and stir-fry quickly for about 1–2 minutes. If the wok is small, fry in two batches so the rice has room to touch the bottom of the pan.
8. The luncheon meat is salty on its own, so first taste a bite of rice with some luncheon meat in it, and add a little salt only if it needs more. Sprinkle in the green part of the green onion and stir-fry for about 1 minute more. When all the rice is heated through and there is no pooled water at the bottom of the wok, plate it.

## Key points

- **Rice turns into a sticky paste**: the rice was too wet, or the grains were smashed (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Use fairly dry rice, loosen it first and press lightly, and drain the fillings too.
- **Too much water in the wok and the rice has no aroma**: the wok was overfilled and the temperature dropped and did not recover (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Fry in two batches, and do not try to fix it by adding more oil.
- **Fried rice too salty**: the luncheon meat is already salted, so do not add salt by the amount you would for plain rice (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Taste with the meat at the end, then decide whether to add any.
