Zhuque soup is a sweet soup: egg, sugar and toasted sesame oil in water, with a sesame aroma and fine, soft egg ribbons in the bowl. The egg turns into ribbons through [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). The more thoroughly the egg is beaten, the more egg the boiling water touches, and the more evenly it sets.

Boiling water poured into a cool bowl cools as it meets the egg (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), so after the ribbons form the soup goes back into the pot to heat again, so that the whole portion is cooked.

## Steps

1. Crack the egg into a large heatproof bowl that will hold the whole soup. Beat with chopsticks until the white and yolk are mixed and there are no strings of clear white. Add 2 ml of toasted sesame oil and stir a few more times to disperse the oil.
2. Put 500 ml of water in a small pot and heat for about 3–4 minutes, until it boils steadily. Set the large bowl on a stable surface with room to stir, and have the sugar weighed and within reach.
3. Pour a little boiling water into the egg first and stir it gently apart with the other hand, then keep pouring in the rest of the hot water so it reaches all the egg in the bowl. The egg changes from a flowing liquid into fine, soft ribbons.
4. Pour the whole bowl back into the pot, turn to medium-low heat and heat for about 1 minute, pushing slowly along the bottom of the pot with a spoon. Heat until all the egg ribbons are opaque and there is no slippery raw egg. Stir, then measure with a thermometer: it should reach 74°C (165°F).
5. Add 20 g of sugar and stir gently until it has dissolved completely. Taste, and if the sweetness is even, take it off the heat. It does not need to boil hard again. Pour into a clean bowl.
6. Let it cool until you can drink it. Stir gently before drinking so the sweet soup, the egg ribbons and the tiny drops of sesame oil on the surface go in together.

## Key points

- **Egg white in coarse strands, yolk in separate grains**: the egg was not mixed, so each part set on its own (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Break up the strings of clear white before pouring the water, and stir lightly as you pour so the heat is even.
- **Slippery raw egg left after pouring**: the hot water cooled against the cool bowl and the egg, and some spots did not cook (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Return it to the pot, stir and heat to 74°C (165°F). Do not assume the whole soup is cooked because the water was boiling when you poured it.
- **Hard, broken ribbons and a weaker sesame oil aroma**: the soup boiled hard for a long time after the egg set, so the protein tightened further and the aroma escaped (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Take it off the heat once it is cooked and the sugar has dissolved, and stir less vigorously.
