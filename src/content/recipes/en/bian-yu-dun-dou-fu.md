Stewed bream with tofu has lightly browned fish skin and tender flesh, tofu that has soaked up the savoury broth, and a little soup left in the pot. The fish is fried on both sides first and the tofu is then braised in until hot; with firm tofu, both the fish and the tofu stay whole when served. Ginger, leek and garlic form the base, and star anise, cassia and bay leaf are optional.

Frying first sets the fish skin and also brings out the aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The second half only needs a gentle braise: once the fish flesh has coagulated with heat, longer cooking makes it lose water and dry out (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). A little broth left around the tofu makes it taste moister than if it were reduced dry.

## Steps

1. Remove the gills, innards and blood line from the bream completely and cut shallow diagonal slashes on both sides. Marinate in the refrigerator for 5–10 minutes with half the ginger slices and all 5 ml Shaoxing wine. Take it out, discard the marinade and pat the surface dry with paper towels.
2. Cut the firm tofu into pieces of similar size and soak in clean water, draining before it goes in the pot. Cut the leek into sections, smash the garlic and weigh the rock sugar; keep the dried spices separate so they do not go into the pan with the sugar and burn.
3. Heat the pot, add 10 ml oil, turn to medium-low heat and put in the fish. Fry each side for 2–4 minutes, until the skin is golden and a spatula slides under easily; when you turn it, support the belly with a wide spatula.
4. Push the fish gently to one side and put the leek, the remaining ginger slices, the garlic and, if using, the dried chili, bay leaf and star anise in the space, stir-frying briefly until fragrant. If the pot is too small, take the fish out first and put it back after the aromatics are fragrant.
5. Add the light soy sauce, the dark soy sauce if using, the rock sugar and the cassia if using, and pour in 400 ml hot water. Swirl the pot gently so the sugar dissolves, bring to a boil and arrange the tofu around the fish so the lower half sits in the broth.
6. Add a little salt first, turn to low heat and braise for 10–15 minutes. Small bubbles should keep rising at the edge; spoon broth over the fish partway through. When the thickest part of the fish turns opaque and the centre of the tofu is hot, it is done.
7. Uncover and reduce the sauce slightly until the broth coats the tofu in a thin layer, then taste and add salt if needed. Lift the fish onto a plate with a spatula, arrange the tofu around it and spoon over the remaining broth.

## Key points

- **Fish sticks to the pan and the skin falls off**: it was not dried after marinating, or it was forced with a spatula before the skin set (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Wait until the side touching the pan has colour before turning.
- **Dry fish, broken tofu**: the broth boiled too hard and was stirred repeatedly (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Braise gently, spoon the broth over, and do not touch the tofu much with the spatula after it goes in.
- **Too salty after reducing**: the whole reserve of salt went in at once and then the water was reduced away (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). 5 g is a reserve amount, so add it in stages; if the fish is cooked but the broth is still thin, take the fish out first and then reduce.
