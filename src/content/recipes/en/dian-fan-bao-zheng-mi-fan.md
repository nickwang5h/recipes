Rice from a rice cooker has plump grains, a moderate softness, and is neither sticky nor mushy. It joins heating, water absorption and resting into one program: the grains absorb water in the hot water and the starch slowly gelatinizes (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)); once there is little free water left in the pot and the temperature at the bottom changes, the machine stops heating or switches to keep-warm. Resting a while after the program ends lets the moisture and heat spread more evenly through the rice.

For the water, go by your own machine's rice cup and markings. A common rice cup is 180 ml, and the "2" line in the inner pot is the water level for two cups of raw rice plus water. Measuring by finger joint varies with the finger, the width of the pot and the depth of the rice layer, so don't apply a fixed water depth such as 2 cm or 2.6 cm to every machine.

## Steps

1. Measure the dry rice level in the rice cup; here it is two level cups, with the cup taken as the common 180 ml. If the cup that came with your machine is different, check the capacity and minimum cooking amount in the manual first, and convert the dry rice amount into your machine's number of cups.
2. Rinse the rice gently in clean water and pour off the cloudy water. Loosely stir with your fingers; don't rub it until the grains break. It is fine to leave a little water after rinsing, since the next step adds water by the total water line.
3. Spread the rice in the inner pot and set the pot on a level counter. For two cups of rice, add water to the "2" line for white rice or jasmine rice; with a different cup, find the water line from the converted number of cups, reading at eye level with the marking and checking the rice type.
4. Dry the outside and bottom of the inner pot, put it back in the machine and close the lid. Choose the white rice or jasmine rice program in the manual and start it. Don't lift the lid repeatedly to look while it cooks.
5. Wait for the cooking program to end, or for the machine to switch to keep-warm automatically. A regular program takes about 30 minutes, and longer on some models; go by the done signal, and don't unplug it or serve the rice just because the time is up.
6. Keep the lid on and rest for another 10–15 minutes. When you open the lid, the surface of the rice should be fluffy with no obvious free water at the bottom; taste a grain, which should be soft in the centre with no hard white core, and then fluff.
7. Push the rice paddle in from the edge down to the bottom and gently turn the bottom layer of rice up to the top, loosen any clumps and serve. Fluffing lets out extra steam, so the bottom layer is not too wet and the top not too dry; don't press it repeatedly into a dense mass.

## Key points

- **Two cups of rice but it comes out very wet**: the rice was measured with an ordinary measuring cup but water was added by the rice cup's markings, or the water line for the wrong rice type was read (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Settle on one cup and one set of markings first, then adjust to taste.
- **Hard core on top, cooked underneath**: the rice layer is uneven, the amount of rice is beyond what the machine can take, or there is too little water, so heating and water absorption are uneven (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Level the rice, don't go over capacity, and if there is a hard core add a little hot water in stages and continue cooking following the manual.
- **Wet and sticky when the lid first comes off, then clumps while serving**: the rice was pressed and put in bowls as soon as the program ended, so the steam couldn't escape (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Rest for the full time first, then turn it gently up from the bottom and serve it hot.
