The base of this mango pomelo sago is chia seeds instead of sago, paired with grapefruit and mango. Once soaked through, the seeds have a fine, grainy texture, the mango purée is thick, and the coconut milk gives the whole cup a rich, oily base. The fat in the coconut milk is dispersed in water, and it only mixes easily with the purée once stirred even (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

The gel on the surface of chia seeds thickens as it absorbs water, so there must be enough liquid, and you must wait. Milk plus part of the coconut milk is used to soak the seeds, and the rest of the coconut milk is for blending the mango. No sugar is added to this cup: the sweetness of ripe mango and the [acidity](/principles/acid/) of the grapefruit balance each other. Leaving the flesh of half a grapefruit in segments is fresher than blending the white pith in with it.

## Steps

1. Stir the coconut milk and measure out 100 ml. Mix it with all the milk and the chia seeds. Stir well with a small spoon so the seeds spread through the liquid, and scrape the dry seeds off the bottom and sides of the bowl.
2. Leave for 10 minutes and stir again, pressing apart any small clumps. Cover and refrigerate for about 2 hours. When a clear gel has formed around the seeds, the liquid has thickened and the spoon lifts no clumps of dry seeds, they are soaked. If there are dry clumps, break them up and soak until done before using.
3. Peel and pit the mango, dice half of the flesh and cut the other half into small pieces. Remove the peel, white membrane and seeds from the grapefruit and break out the segments, without squeezing out the sour juice.
4. Put the mango for the purée, the remaining 50 ml of coconut milk and two small edible ice cubes in a blender and blend until smooth with no large pieces of fruit. Stop to scrape the sides, then blend briefly again so the thickness is the same from top to bottom.
5. Put the diced mango and the grapefruit segments in a glass, pour in the soaked seed and milk base, then pour in the mango purée. Stir lightly until smooth overall while keeping the fruit pieces, so every spoonful has seeds, milky liquid and fruit.
6. Serve cold and finish it the same day. If you are not drinking it right away, cover and refrigerate, and stir lightly before drinking, lifting the fruit from the bottom, so you do not finish the milky liquid first and end up with a layer of seeds.

## Key points

- **Seeds clump and are still dry inside**: stirring at the start was not enough, and the gel on the outside wrapped the dry seeds. After ten minutes of soaking, break up the clumps and stir again, then keep refrigerating until the water is absorbed evenly.
- **Oil floats on top of the milk base and the bottom is thin**: the coconut milk had separated, and only the watery part was poured out when measuring (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Stir the can well when you open it, and stir the finished cup before drinking.
- **Bitter taste and a dull fruit flavour**: the white pith of the grapefruit got blended into the purée. Take only the segments and put them in the glass, and blend the mango on its own.
- **Curdled bits in the milk**: a lot of sour juice met warm milk directly, and the proteins clump into small bits (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Chill all the ingredients, and mix in the grapefruit segments last.
