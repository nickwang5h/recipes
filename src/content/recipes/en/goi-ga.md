*Gỏi gà* shreds cooked chicken into fine strands and tosses it with cabbage, carrot, onion and herbs. The chicken is tender, the vegetables crisp and the peanuts add richness, and a sour, salty, spicy dressing ties the textures together. Vietnamese coriander (*rau răm*) has a distinct peppery fragrance; when you cannot find it, ordinary cilantro still makes a good home-style salad.

Cook the chicken in gently simmering water, not at a hard boil, because a hard boil tightens the meat and squeezes out water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Toss the vegetables with the fish sauce dressing only just before eating: salt draws water out of finely cut vegetables over time (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The sourness of the rice vinegar should be clear, with the sugar only in support. After tossing, pick up a bite of chicken and vegetable strands together to taste.

## Steps

1. Cook the chicken. Put 680 ml of water, the large onion chunks, the salt and the sugar for cooking the chicken in a pot and heat over medium-high for 5–7 minutes, until the water boils. Add the opened-out chicken thigh, turn to low and cook for 15–20 minutes, keeping small bubbles at the edge of the pot and the surface from rolling. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part: at 74°C (165°F), with the meat opaque all the way through, it is done. If not, cook 2 minutes more and check again.
2. Cool and shred. Lift the chicken onto a shallow plate and let it cool for about 10 minutes, until you can hold it without it burning you. Tear it along the grain into fine strands one bite long, and separate any thick, coarse pieces. The onion used to cook the chicken does not go in the salad.
3. Quick-pickle the onion. Toss the thin onion slices with 10 ml of rice vinegar and 20 ml of water and soak for 10–15 minutes, until the slices are pliable and less sharp. Drain in a sieve for 2 minutes, until it stops dripping. Discard the pickling liquid so it does not dilute the dressing.
4. Make the dressing. Pound the garlic, the optional chili and 0.5 tsp of the dressing sugar to a fine paste. Add the remaining 1 tsp of sugar, the fish sauce and 5 tbsp of rice vinegar and stir for about 1 minute, until no sugar grains are left in the bowl and the liquid is even.
5. Toss together. Wash and drain the cabbage, carrot and herbs, and put them in a large bowl with the shredded chicken and quick-pickled onion. Pour the dressing over just before serving and toss gently for about 1 minute, until the vegetable and chicken strands are all coated and the cabbage is still crisp. Sprinkle with the peanuts and lift onto plates, leaving any dressing that has collected at the bottom of the bowl behind.

## Key points

- **Chicken is dry and tears into coarse chunks**: it was cooked too hard or too long and the water squeezed out (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Open out the meat, cook it at a gentle simmer, lift it out when the centre reaches the target temperature, and tear it along the grain once cooled.
- **Water pools on the plate and the dressing tastes weak**: the washing water and onion pickling liquid were not drained off, and the salty dressing keeps drawing out more water (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Shake the vegetable strands dry, toss only just before serving, and leave the dressing at the bottom of the bowl behind when plating.
- **The first bite tastes only sour**: you tasted the dressing alone, or used seasoned sushi vinegar that already has sugar and salt, which shifts the balance of sour, sweet and salty (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Use unseasoned rice vinegar and taste with chicken and vegetable strands together, not the strong dressing on its own.
