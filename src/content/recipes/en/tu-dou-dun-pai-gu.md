In stewed pork ribs with potatoes, the ribs are soft and falling apart, the potatoes are fluffy but still in pieces, and the liquid is reduced to a glossy cling. The two ingredients cook at different speeds, so they go in separately. The ribs are first coloured with caramel, then stewed slowly in moist heat so the [collagen turns to gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). The potatoes go in for the second half, where the starch inside absorbs water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), softening without falling apart.

The caramel colour comes from [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/), which is a separate change from the final reduction of the stewing liquid to a thick sauce. Reducing concentrates the gelatin, a little potato starch and the seasonings into a glossy coating. Once the potatoes are soft, turn them as little as possible so the sauce stays on the whole pieces of potato and rib.

## Steps

1. Peel the potatoes, cut them into rolling-cut chunks of similar size and put them into clean water right away. Slice the ginger and divide it into two portions. Divide the green onion into a knot and chopped pieces according to the amounts.
2. Mix the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce and 5 g of soybean paste. Set the ginger slices, dried chilies, star anise, Sichuan peppercorns and cassia for stir-frying to one side. Heat the water separately so it is ready to add as soon as the caramel is done.
3. Put the ribs in a pot of cold water with the knotted green onion, the ginger slices for blanching and 15 ml of Shaoxing wine. After it comes to a boil, blanch for 2 more minutes. Lift out, rinse off the foam, drain thoroughly and pat the surface dry.
4. Add the oil and sugar to the wok and stir over medium-low heat until the sugar melts, bubbles and turns reddish brown. Add the dried ribs right away and stir-fry for about 2–3 minutes, until every side is coated in the caramel and lightly coloured.
5. Push an empty space at the bottom of the wok, add the ginger slices and whole spices and stir-fry for about 10 seconds. Drizzle in the remaining wine, add the sauce mixture and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until the ribs are evenly coated.
6. Add 700 ml of hot water, so the water nearly covers the ribs. Bring to a boil, cover and stew at a gentle simmer over low heat for 40 minutes. Pick the thickest piece: the centre must reach 71°C (160°F) and the meat and tendon must start to soften. If it is still tough, keep stewing before adding the potatoes.
7. Drain the potatoes and add them to the pot. Bring back to a boil, cover and keep braising over low heat for 15–20 minutes. When a chopstick pierces the potatoes easily and the rib meat and tendon come apart fairly easily, pick out the large spices.
8. Uncover and add the salt in portions, tasting as you go. Turn to medium-high heat and reduce the sauce for about 3–5 minutes. Nudge the pieces with a spatula or swirl the pan; when the sauce is thick and coats the ribs and potatoes evenly, turn off the heat, sprinkle with the chopped green onion and plate.

## Key points

- **The ribs are cooked but still tough**: [collagen](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) needs more time to soften. Stew the ribs until nearly soft before adding the potatoes, so you are not waiting for the meat to soften while the potatoes break down.
- **The caramel is bitter and the ribs have black spots**: the sugar was heated too far (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Have the meat and hot water ready and move on to the next step as soon as the sugar is reddish brown; throw out burnt caramel and start again.
- **The potatoes fall apart into a paste and the sauce sticks to the bottom**: the pot was stirred hard again and again after the potatoes softened, and a lot of the [gelatinized starch](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) on the surface came off. Add the potatoes late, nudge gently while reducing, and stop once the sauce clings.
