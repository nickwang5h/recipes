When frozen, cooked flower rolls are steamed hot again, the soft texture and the green onion oil fragrance between the layers come back. Steam condenses on cold dough and delivers heat to the surface, and from there heat is [conducted](/principles/heat-transfer/) from the outside in. The surface heats quickly, while the thickest roll centre takes longer, so judge whether the reheating is done by the centre.

When starchy food cools, the [starch retrogrades](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the structure firms; reheating softens it again. While steaming, keep the moisture in, and after taking them out, let the condensation on the surface escape, so the rolls stay soft and the bottoms do not soak and turn sticky. Here we use five frozen cooked flower rolls; sizes vary, and so does how many people they feed.

## Steps

1. Take out the five frozen cooked flower rolls, remove the packaging and arrange them in a single layer on a shallow heatproof plate, leaving gaps between the rolls. Leave space between the plate edge and the pot wall too, so steam can get around the rolls.
2. Pour 400 ml of clean water into the steamer, and set the rack and plate steadily. The water must not touch the bottom of the plate, and there must be enough to keep steaming; for a wide pot, or one that loses steam easily, add more water, then cover with the lid.
3. Bring the water to a boil over high heat and, once it is steaming steadily, turn to medium heat and start timing from then, steaming for about 15 minutes. It is enough for the water to keep boiling; the lid does not need to be bounced by steam.
4. Turn to low heat and lift the lid from the side, so the steam escapes away from your hands and face. Take out one of the thicker rolls with tongs and break it open: the centre should be soft and steaming, with no cold, hard spots. If the centre is still cool, put it back and keep steaming.
5. Once the centre is hot through, turn off the heat and take the plate out with oven mitts or a plate gripper, then pour off any water that has collected. Spread the rolls out and eat once they are cool enough to bite; do not leave them piled on a hot plate with water on it.

## Key points

- **Hot outside, cold in the centre**: the rolls are stacked or too thick, so heat is [conducted](/principles/heat-transfer/) to the centre slowly. Steam in a single layer and check the thickest one first; the reheating time on the package can also be a guide.
- **Wet, sticky bottoms**: the plate is deep and holds water, or the water level is too high and the bottoms sit in water. Use a shallow plate, keep the water below the rack, and pour off the condensation once out of the pot.
- **Still dry and hard after steaming**: not enough steam, or the retrograded [starch](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) has not softened back. Check how much water is left in the pot and whether the lid is on tightly; if the water is nearly gone, add hot water along the edge of the pot and keep steaming.
