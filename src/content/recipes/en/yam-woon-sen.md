*Yam wun sen* is a Thai sour and spicy salad built around mung bean glass noodles, often served with rice. The classic version has shrimp and ground pork, and the amount of noodles is actually small. Lime, fish sauce and chili tie each bite's sourness, saltiness and heat together (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Serve it warm.

Dried glass noodles are soaked until they bend, then cooked until clear, soft and springy. Cook them too long and they lose their spring (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Warm noodles lifted from the water go straight into the dressing, which helps them toss evenly and keeps extra rinse water out of the bowl.

## Steps

1. Soak the wood ear in hot water for about 20 minutes, snip off the hard base and cut into thin strips. Soak the glass noodles separately in room-temperature water for about 20 minutes, until pliable, then drain and snip into lengths of about 15 cm. Shell the shrimp and devein them.
2. Stir the lime juice, fish sauce, sugar, fresh chilies and dried chili powder together until the sugar crystals disappear. Cut the shallot, tomato and celery leaves, and keep the dressing in a large bowl.
3. If using dried shrimp, pat the soaked dried shrimp dry and fry them with the oil in a small skillet until golden and crisp, then remove and cool. Keep the peanuts for the end as well, so they don't soak up the dressing early.
4. Put the ground pork and 80 ml of water in a small pot, bring to a boil over medium heat, and break the pork up as it cooks. Once the pork no longer clumps and the centre reaches 71°C (160°F), keep the meat together with the little cooking liquid in the pot.
5. Boil a separate pot of water. When it boils, add the glass noodles and wood ear. Lift the noodles out when they turn clear and have no hard centre when bitten, usually in under 1 minute. Shake off the excess water and put them straight into the bowl with the dressing.
6. Put the shrimp in the same pot, lower the heat to low and cook until the flesh is opaque and the thickest part reaches 74°C (165°F), then lift out and drain. Toss the shrimp and shallot into the warm noodles, then add the ground pork and its liquid, the tomato and celery leaves, and mix gently.
7. Taste a mouthful of noodles with the other ingredients; it should be sour first, then savoury, with only a light sweetness. Scatter the dried shrimp and peanuts on top just before eating and plate right away. The longer it sits, the more the noodles soak up the dressing at the bottom of the bowl.

## Key points

- **Noodles snap when tossed, or turn to mush**: they were boiled hard for too long after soaking. Lift them out as soon as they are clear and can be bitten through, and toss with tongs using a light turning motion. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Dressing is watery and the bite has no flavour**: the noodles and shrimp weren't drained, and the water they carried diluted the sourness and salt. Shake off the water after lifting them out, and add the pork with only the little cooking liquid from its pot. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
- **Shrimp curled tight, pork dry and hard**: the protein was overheated and lost more water. Take them out or off the heat once they reach doneness, and don't leave them waiting in the hot pot while the other ingredients are prepared. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
