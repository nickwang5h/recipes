Poached shrimp should be pearly white and springy, with the dipping sauce mixed separately and dipped into as you eat. The usual method is to cook them quickly in boiling water; this version cooks them covered with little water: onion and ginger go on the bottom of the pot, the shrimp on top, and they are cooked covered with a little Shaoxing wine and the liquid from the onion. The heat from the bottom of the pot and the steam inside the lid heat them together; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). The onion must cover the bottom, and the lid must be able to hold in the steam.

Eat the shrimp as soon as they are cooked. If they keep cooking covered, [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) makes them contract, and the texture goes from springy and tender to tight and hard. Making the sauce separately keeps the shrimp's own flavour, and makes the salt and acid easy to control.

## Steps

1. Cut the onion into small pieces and the ginger into slices, and spread them evenly to cover the bottom of a small, flat-bottomed pot. Do not cut the onion into fine mince; it needs some thickness to hold the shrimp off the bottom of the pot. If the pot is too wide to cover, use a smaller one.
2. Wash the shrimp, trim off the feelers and legs and remove the vein as you like, drain, and lay them on the onion and ginger. Arrange the shrimp in one layer, without letting their bellies touch the bare bottom of the pot, drizzle with 20 ml of Shaoxing wine and cover.
3. Heat over medium heat for 1 minute first, then turn to low heat for about 5 minutes. There should be steam inside the lid, and you should hear a light, moist sizzle from the bottom. If you smell burning, or the sizzling sounds dry, lower the heat at once, lift the lid and add a little water so the bottom does not scorch.
4. After turning off the heat you can leave it covered for up to 5 minutes more, checking the largest shrimp midway. If the flesh is pearly white and opaque, take them out early; if the area where head and body join or the back is still translucent, return to low heat until cooked.
5. While the shrimp cook, put the green onion, garlic and sesame seeds in a dry heatproof bowl. In another pot, heat 15 ml of oil, take it off the heat before it smokes, and pour it over the green onion and garlic in portions; after the sizzle, stir, let it cool slightly, then add the light soy sauce, vinegar and oyster sauce.
6. Lift the cooked shrimp onto a clean plate, put the dipping sauce beside them, and peel and eat while hot. Do not pour the onion juice from the bottom of the pot into the dipping sauce; once the shrimp are cooked through, do not leave them in the warm pot while you wait for other dishes.

## Key points

- **The onion has burned but the shrimp are not cooked**: the pot is too wide, the bed too thin, or the water cooked off too fast (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a smaller pot, keep it covered, and add a little water if the pot goes dry.
- **The shrimp are hard and curled into tight rings**: they stayed in the hot pot for a long time after cooking, and [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) contracted them. Judge by the colour and springiness of the flesh, and move them out of the pot once cooked.
- **The sauce is burnt and bitter, or the oil spatters**: the green onion and garlic were wet, or the oil was too hot (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Pour hot oil over drained green onion and garlic, and add the watery soy sauce and vinegar only after it has cooled a little.
