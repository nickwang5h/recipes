*Kaeng matsaman* is a Thai curry shaped by Muslim cooking, usually made with beef or chicken. This version uses beef shank, potato, onion and peanuts. Cassia and cardamom give a warm, dry spice aroma, and tamarind lifts the richness of coconut milk and sugar with some sourness. The finished curry should connect sweet, salty and sour, with soft meat and potato and a thick sauce that can still be spooned over rice.

Stew the beef shank until its sinew is soft first, and add the potato later, so each keeps its own shape. A slow stew relies on collagen gradually turning into gelatin, not on hard boiling to break the meat apart (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). The dry spices are toasted briefly and then left to steep in the sauce. The tamarind goes in last, so you can judge the [sourness](/principles/acid/) together with the sweet and salty base.

## Steps

1. Cut the beef shank into even pieces, the potatoes into slightly smaller chunks, and the onion into chunks. Rub the tamarind apart in 30 ml of hot water, strain, and measure 2 tbsp of juice, topping up with plain water if short. Discard the hard seeds and fibres.
2. Put the oil, the thick coconut cream and the massaman paste in a heavy-bottomed pot and stir-fry over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the paste smells strong and tiny oil droplets show at the edges. Add the beef and stir to coat, then add the coconut milk and 400 ml of water, so the liquid just covers the meat.
3. Bring to a gentle simmer, then turn to low heat and stew with the lid half on for about 3 hours. Nudge the bottom of the pot now and then, and add water from the rest as the liquid drops. Move on once a fork slides easily through the thickest piece and the sinew is soft.
4. While the beef stews, boil the potatoes in a separate pot of water with a pinch of salt for about 10–12 minutes, until cooked through at the centre with the edges still intact, then drain. Toast the cassia, the cracked cardamom and the star anise briefly, one at a time, in a dry pan, and take each out as soon as it smells fragrant.
5. Once the beef is soft, add the dry spices, bay leaves, onion, palm sugar, tamarind juice and half the fish sauce, and simmer gently for about 10 minutes. When the onion is translucent and soft at the centre and the sugar has fully dissolved, taste the sauce and add more fish sauce.
6. Add the potatoes and peanuts, push gently to mix, and heat through. Reduce the sauce briefly with the pot uncovered, until it coats the back of a spoon in a thin layer and the meat and potatoes are still in chunks. Remove the whole spices before serving and let the curry rest for about 10 minutes, then serve with hot rice.

## Key points

- **Beef is tough but the potatoes have turned to mush**: stewed together for a long time, the potato softens first while the collagen in the shank has not yet converted. Stew the beef soft first, boil the potatoes separately and combine them later (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).
- **Spices taste bitter or scorched**: they were toasted too long, or the pot ran dry while the paste was frying. Toast whole spices only until fragrant, stir the paste and add liquid in time (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Both sour and salty, with the coconut milk lost**: concentrated tamarind liquid was used, or the salt already in the paste was forgotten. Extract from pulp blocks, add the fish sauce in stages and taste before and after reducing (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)).
