Hunan-style stir-fried pork is glossy and savoury: pork belly fried until it gives up its fat, coated in the strong flavours of fermented black beans, doubanjiang and two kinds of fresh chili. Fry the slices first to render part of the fat and give the lean meat a fragrant surface, then stir-fry the sauces into the pork fat. Colour and aroma only come once there is less steam in the pan; see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).

The savoury depth of fermented black beans and doubanjiang is already there from fermentation. When they are stir-fried, their flavour spreads into the oil, and the red of the doubanjiang spreads with it; see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/). The finished dish has glossy slices of pork, chilies that are just cooked, and sauce that clings to the meat.

## Steps

1. Remove the skin from the pork belly and cut into thin slices of even thickness. Add the starch and all of the dark soy sauce, mix until there is no dry starch, and marinate in the fridge for 30 minutes. Hold back the salt, and adjust it at the end by tasting how salty the black beans and doubanjiang are.
2. Cut the green onions into sections, crack the garlic, and cut both kinds of chili on the diagonal. Lightly chop the fermented black beans and finely chop the doubanjiang, and set them out separately, so you do not have to leave the pan to look for them when frying them until fragrant.
3. Heat the pan, pour in 15 ml of oil and add the pork belly. Stir over medium heat and spread it out. Wait for the surface to change colour first, then keep frying until the fat turns slightly translucent, clear pork fat collects at the bottom of the pan, and the edges of the lean meat take on a little colour.
4. Remove the pork and leave a thin layer of fat in the pan to coat the bottom. If a lot of fat has been released, spoon some out, so the garlic and sauces do not sit in a thick pool of oil.
5. Turn to medium-low heat, add the garlic and stir-fry until fragrant, then add the fermented black beans and toss. When you smell the beans, add the doubanjiang and stir-fry until the oil turns red and the paste loosens. If the bottom of the pan begins to blacken, lower the heat first.
6. Return the pork belly to the pan and toss until the sauces cling evenly. Break open a thicker slice to look; if it still looks raw, keep stir-frying until cooked.
7. Add the bird's eye chilies, chaotian chilies and green onion sections, turn to high heat and stir-fry for about 40 seconds, until the chilies are bright and the raw taste has gone and the green onion has just softened. Taste, add salt as needed, toss and plate at once.

## Key points

- **Slices are greasy and not fragrant**: they were taken out as soon as they changed colour, before the fat had rendered enough, and the surface had not had a clear [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Stir off the steam first, and take the pork out once the pork fat has rendered and the slices have taken on a little colour.
- **Sauce is burnt black and bitter**: finely chopped doubanjiang burns easily over high heat (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Stir-fry over medium-low heat until the red oil comes out; the paste does not need to brown the way the pork slices do.
- **Chilies are limp, garlic is bitter**: the aromatics and vegetables were heated too long (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the fresh chilies after the pork is cooked through, add the fermented black beans as soon as the garlic smells fragrant, and take the pan off the heat when the chilies are just cooked.
