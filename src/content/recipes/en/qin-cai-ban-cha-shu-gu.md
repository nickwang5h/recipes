This cold dish puts the crisp tenderness of celery together with the chew of seasoned tea tree mushrooms. The celery is first [blanched](/principles/blanching/) briefly to take the rawness off, then cooled quickly so the cooking stops while it is still crisp. It is drained before tossing, so the sauce clings in a thin layer.

The tea tree mushrooms are a seasoned, ready-to-eat product that already carries oil and saltiness. Celery stalks and leaves differ in thickness, so they go into the water separately; cutting the thick stalks of regular celery into thin strips lengthwise shortens the distance heat has to travel to the inside (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

## Steps

1. Pick off the yellow and old leaves of the celery and wash each stalk to remove grit between the stalk and leaves. Keep the tender leaves and set them aside separately; if the outside of a thick stalk has obvious tough strings, pull them off first, and old tough leaves can be discarded.
2. Cut the celery stalks lengthwise into strips about 2–3 mm wide, then into pieces about 4 cm long. Cut tender thin celery into pieces as well, keeping stalks and leaves apart so the leaves are not overcooked while the thick stalks cook through.
3. Bring enough water to a boil to cover the celery. Add the stalks, spread them gently apart, keep the water boiling, and cook about 20 seconds, until the colour has brightened and the thin strips bend slightly; if the thick, tough strips still do not bend, extend the time a little.
4. Add the tender leaves, and after about 5 seconds lift out all the stalks and leaves. Cool immediately in drinkable cold water, then drain thoroughly in a sieve. Before tossing, pick up a handful; no water should drip continuously.
5. Put the celery in a mixing bowl, add the toasted sesame oil, oyster sauce and light soy sauce, and toss gently. Let the seasonings spread first so one spot is not salty and another bland, and do not knead the crisp stalks hard.
6. Add one jar of ready-to-eat tea tree mushrooms; decide from the package whether to bring in the original seasoning liquid, starting with a little, and after tossing, taste the celery and mushrooms together. Add a little salt only if needed, plate, and eat soon.

## Key points

- **Celery stalks are tough but the leaves are mushy**: stalks and leaves differ in thickness and need different heating times (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut thick stalks thinner first and add the leaves to the water last.
- **Colour is dull and the crispness is gone**: it was blanched too long, or residual heat kept softening it after lifting out (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Lift it out by texture, cool quickly, and drain.
- **Water pools at the bottom of the bowl and the flavour is weak**: the celery was not drained after cooling, and the seasoning was diluted by water (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Pour off the surface water before tossing.
- **Too salty after tossing**: the seasoning liquid of the ready-to-eat mushrooms plus oyster sauce and soy sauce stack up the salt (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Toss in the mushrooms first and then judge the salt, and add the mushroom liquid in portions too.
