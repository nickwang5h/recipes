*Pasta e fagioli* cooks beans and pasta into a thick soup, and the bean variety, pasta shape and meat differ from region to region. This one uses borlotti beans, pork ribs and beef stock, and finishes with a little butter and Parmigiano. The Romano beans in the dried goods aisle in Canada are usually this kind of borlotti bean. Do not confuse them with the long, thin Romano green beans.

The soup's thickness comes from two sources. Crushed cooked beans carry fine solids into the soup, and the short pasta cooked in the same pot releases starch that thickens it further (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The onion, carrot and celery are slowly sautéed in oil first, and then the stock catches their flavour (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The pasta keeps absorbing water, so the soup should still flow when it goes into the bowls.

## Steps

1. Cover the borlotti beans with plenty of cold water, cover and soak in the fridge for 8–12 hours, then drain off the soaking water. Cover with fresh water, bring to a boil, reduce to low heat and cook about 45–60 minutes or longer, until a bean can be crushed against the tongue with no hard centre. Leave them in the cooking water.
2. Put the olive oil and onion in a soup pot and sauté over medium heat until pale golden, then add the carrot and celery and stir until coated in oil. Add the ribs and keep cooking for about 10 minutes, turning from time to time, so the meat and vegetables develop their aroma.
3. Add the tomatoes and their juice, reduce to low heat and cook about 20 minutes, until the tomato is soft and the juice slightly thick. Do not let the bottom of the pot burn dry; add a small spoonful of bean cooking water if needed.
4. Add the drained cooked beans, stir gently and cook about 5 minutes, then pour in the beef stock, cover and bring to a gentle simmer. Scoop out about a small bowl of beans, mash to a purée, return it to the pot and season with salt and black pepper.
5. Add the short pasta to the soup, stirring up from the bottom often and gently. First add the reserved hot water, adjusting the soup so there is enough liquid to cook the pasta, and cook until the pasta is tender but still has bite and the soup flows slowly. If it is still too thick, keep adding hot water a little at a time.
6. Stir in the butter and cheese, turn off the heat, rest about 10 minutes and ladle into bowls. The meat can be taken off the ribs and returned to the soup, but not the bones.

## Key points

- **The pasta is cooked but the bean centres are still hard**: dried beans take longer to absorb water and soften, so cook the beans through before adding the pasta (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep cooking the beans, and continue only once they crush easily.
- **The soup is like batter and sticks to the bottom**: the bean purée and the starch released by the pasta thicken it together, while the pasta is still absorbing water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Lower the heat, stir gently from the bottom and add hot water in stages to keep the soup flowing.
- **The vegetables taste scorched and bitter**: they were cut fine and cooked too hard, so the aroma turned to scorch (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the next ingredients once the onion is pale golden, and do not scrape blackened bits into the soup.
