Braised beef shank has a deep soy-sauce flavour, and cut it comes in whole thin slices. The shank is cooked in a savoury, spiced broth, then left overnight to take up the flavour. The braising liquid has fermented soybean paste, doubanjiang, oyster sauce and fermented bean curd in it. The collagen in the shank gradually turns to gelatin (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)), so the sinew is tender when hot, and once chilled the meat is easier to cut into whole thin slices.

The caramel colour gets its brown-red from [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/), and the saltiness comes from the soy sauces and the pastes together. Taste the braising liquid before adding any [salt](/principles/salt/), and again whenever you reuse it.

## Steps

1. Soak the beef shank in cold water in the refrigerator for at least 3 hours, changing the water along the way. Cut the onion into chunks, smash the ginger and garlic, snip the dried chilies into pieces and put the spices in a heatproof spice bag.
2. Spread the sugar in a small heavy-bottomed pot and heat over low heat until it melts and turns amber to brown-red. Take it off the heat right away, add 150 ml of hot water slowly in portions, keeping your hands and face away from the pot, then warm gently until the caramel dissolves.
3. In another pot, cover the shank with cold water, bring to a boil over high heat and blanch for 2–3 minutes. Lift out, rinse off the foam, pour away the blanching water and wash the pot.
4. Bring the water for the braising liquid, the spice bag, onion, ginger, garlic and chilies to a boil, then add the four sauces, the caramel colour, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and beer. Stir, bring to a boil again and taste a small spoonful. Savoury enough is enough. If it is already salty enough, add no salt.
5. Add the beef shank and top up the liquid to cover the meat. The total fill and liquid must follow the pressure cooker's manual. Lock the lid and, once it reaches working pressure, time 25–30 minutes.
6. Turn off the heat and release the pressure naturally as the manual says, and open the lid only when the pressure has fully released. Test with a fork: it is done when the sinewy meat is pierced easily and comes apart with a light twist. If it is still tough, keep heating until tender.
7. Divide the beef and enough braising liquid for soaking into shallow containers and refrigerate as soon as the steam has gone, keeping it at room temperature for no more than 2 hours in total. Refrigerate overnight, take out the beef and cut thin slices across the grain.
8. Strain the remaining braising liquid, bring it to a boil, divide it into shallow containers to cool quickly and refrigerate. Use it within 2–3 days, or freeze it for longer keeping. Before braising again, bring it to a boil and top up water and seasonings according to the actual taste.

## Key points

- **The meat is cooked but hard to chew**: the sinew has not softened through (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Keep heating gently. Stop when it is tender, and do not stew until the muscle fibres fall apart.
- **The slices crumble at a touch**: the meat is still warm, or was braised too long. Chill it until the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) set to a jelly before cutting, use a sharp knife, and do not press back and forth as you cut.
- **The braising liquid gets saltier each time you use it**: water evaporates but the [salt](/principles/salt/) stays in the pot. Taste every time, and do not add all the salt and soy sauce again in the original proportions.
- **The caramel colour is bitter**: the sugar was cooked to black (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Make the caramel colour again.
