The Osaka way to make *okonomiyaki* mixes the cabbage into the batter and pan-fries the whole mass as a thick pancake, and the common buta-tama is topped with pork belly. Gaps are left between the cabbage pieces, and the grated yam helps the batter bind. When cooked, it is fluffy inside with a fragrant crust.

The flour [starch gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) as it heats, and the egg helps it set. Mixing only needs to get the cabbage coated in batter: beating too long develops [gluten](/principles/gluten/) and makes the cabbage release water, and the pancake turns heavy. The base sets first, and after the flip, covering the pan and heating gently lets the centre of the thick pancake catch up with the surface.

## Steps

1. Cut the cabbage into coarse pieces about 5 mm, cut the hard core more finely and drain it well. Peel and grate the yam, drain and chop the red pickled ginger, and lay out the pork belly in thin slices.
2. Mix the cake flour, dashi, grated yam and a small pinch of salt into a batter with no dry flour. It should fall slowly and be thick enough to cling to the cabbage. Do not add the eggs to this base batter yet.
3. Mix one pancake at a time. Take half the cabbage, base batter, green onion and tenkasu, and add one egg and half the red pickled ginger. Fold gently with a spoon from the bottom and stop as soon as the cabbage is coated in batter, then put it in the pan right away.
4. Heat a frying pan over medium heat and wipe in 1 tsp of oil. Add the mixture and shape it into a round about 15–16 cm across, with the centre slightly lower than the edge. Cook for about 3 minutes, until the underside is golden and the edges can hold their shape, then lay half the pork belly on top.
5. Support the whole pancake with two spatulas and flip it so the meat side is down. Cover the pan, turn the heat to low and cook for about 4 minutes. Do not press the pancake; the steam under the lid helps the centre heat up (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
6. Lift the lid, flip back to the cabbage side and cook for about 1 more minute to drive off the steam on the surface. Insert a thermometer from the side into the centre of the pancake, and take it out only when it reaches 74°C (165°F) and the pork is fully cooked; if it hasn't reached that temperature, cover again and keep heating on low.
7. Brush the top with 1 tbsp of okonomiyaki sauce, then squeeze on mayonnaise and sprinkle with bonito flakes and aonori as you like. Mix the second pancake only when it goes into the pan, cook it with the remaining 1 tsp of oil, and eat it as soon as it is done.

## Key points

- **The pancake is dense, like dough**: it was mixed too long, or you kept adding flour to fix a watery batter. Drain the cabbage and mix pancake by pancake, putting it in the pan as soon as it is coated, with little stirring (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **It falls apart when flipped**: the base hasn't set, or the cabbage pieces are too big. Wait until the spatula can support the whole underside before flipping, and support it with two spatulas together (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Burnt on the surface, wet and sticky in the centre**: the heat is too high or the pancake was spread too thick. Set it over medium heat first, then cover and cook on low; confirm doneness by the centre temperature, since pressing only squeezes out moisture and air pockets (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
