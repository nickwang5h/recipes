The crunch in sweet and sour pork comes from the fried crust, and the sweet and sour comes from the sauce that coats it at the end. When the starch on the outside of the pork pieces is heated, it loses water and forms a thin, crisp shell. Frying first to cook through and then frying again separates cooking the centre from drying the surface (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Tossing with the sauce last keeps softening to a minimum.

This version uses a tomato sauce with pineapple, with plenty of sauce and on the sweet side. The starch in the sauce thickens by [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and the [acid](/principles/acid/) in the white vinegar and pineapple balances the sugar and oil. The sauce and the crust each have a job: the sauce should thinly coat the meat, and the crust should stay intact.

## Steps

1. Pat the pork shoulder dry and cut into pieces of about 2 cm, close to the same size. Toss with half the salt and marinate for 20 minutes. Cut the bell pepper into small pieces, and drain the pineapple and cut it into chunks.
2. Stir the ketchup, white vinegar, minced garlic, light soy sauce, sugar, the remaining salt and 180 ml of water together. Mix the thickening starch separately with 20 ml of cold water and keep it for the end.
3. Put the pork pieces one at a time into the dredging starch, press lightly so the starch sticks, then shake off the loose starch. An even thin layer is enough; do not rub all the leftover dry starch into the meat.
4. Heat the frying oil in a deep pot to about 160–170°C (320–340°F) and add the pork in batches so the pieces do not stick together. Fry over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the surface has set and started to turn yellow. Check that the centre of the thickest piece has reached 71°C (160°F), then lift out and drain.
5. Raise the oil to about 180–190°C (355–375°F) and return the pork for a second fry of about 1 minute. When the crust is hard and golden, lift it out and spread it out so the steam can escape. If the oil starts to smoke, lower the heat first.
6. In another pot, add the oil for the sauce and pour in the sweet and sour sauce. Cook over medium heat for about 3 minutes, then add the bell pepper and pineapple and cook for about 2 minutes. If the sauce is still thin like water, cook it a little longer to reduce.
7. Stir the starch slurry again and drizzle it into the boiling sauce in portions, pushing as you pour. Stop when the sauce turns glossy and thinly coats the back of a spoon, and do not cook it for long after thickening. If there is still a lot of sauce, take some out for another use.
8. Add the fried pork and toss quickly until the surface is thinly coated with sauce, then plate and eat at once. Do not let a pool of sauce collect at the bottom of the plate, or the crisp crust will go soft.

## Key points

- **Dark brown outside, centre not cooked**: the pieces are too big or the oil is too hot (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Cut evenly, cook the pork through on the first fry, and leave the second fry only to dry the surface.
- **Goes soft as soon as it is tossed in sauce**: the sauce is too thin or too much, or it stood for a long time after tossing. Thicken the sauce thinly by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), then toss briefly, take it out and eat it right away.
- **A thick sauce turns thin again**: a thickened sauce containing [acid](/principles/acid/) was cooked too long and the starch chains gradually broke down. Cook the vegetables and reduce the sauce first, and thicken last.
