These are egg tarts with a lot of cream: ready-made pastry shells with an egg yolk and cream filling, a crisp shell and a tender custard. When the proteins in the yolks heat, they link into a network that holds the water from the milk and cream, so the filling turns from liquid into custard; baked too long, the network tightens, and the filling goes coarse and weeps water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

The pastry has to dry out in a hot oven, while the filling must stop just as it sets, so watch the shell and the centre together. The pan, the rack position and the preheat all change the speed of [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). This filling is enough for about thirty small shells, two per person for fifteen servings; if the shells are bigger there will be fewer, so leave room for the filling to rise when you pour.

## Steps

1. Put 450 ml of whipping cream, 200 ml of milk and 80 g of sugar in a bowl and stir gently until the sugar dissolves. Separate the yolks from eight eggs and keep the whites aside; add the yolks to the cream mixture and stir until the colour is even, taking care not to beat in foam.
2. Strain the filling to remove the yolk membrane and small lumps. Skim off any foam on the surface, or let it stand a while; the filling surface is finer when the liquid is smooth with no thick bubbles.
3. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F) for about 10 minutes, and put the shells in only when it has reached temperature. Place the shells on a baking tray at least about 0.5 cm apart; whether frozen shells need thawing follows the product instructions.
4. Divide the filling among the shells, leaving about 0.5 cm below the rim. Thirty may not fill exactly; portion by the capacity of each shell, and don't fill to the brim just to reach the count.
5. After they go in, turn the oven down to 200°C (400°F) and bake for about 25 minutes. If one tray can't hold thirty shells, bake in batches, and keep the waiting filling in the refrigerator. Watch them near the end: the pastry golden and the filling surface set.
6. Shake the tray gently; the centre may tremble slightly but must not ripple like water. If there is still liquid, keep baking; once set, take them out and cool briefly on a wire rack, and don't put hot tarts into a closed container, where they will go soft.

## Key points

- **Filling has honeycomb holes and a coarse texture**: too much air was stirred in, or the proteins tightened from heating too long (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Stir gently, strain, skim the foam, and take them out promptly once set.
- **Soft shell bottom but the filling is already done**: the preheat was not enough, or crowded shells slowed the heating (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Leave gaps between the shells and bake in batches; they are right when the shell bottom is golden and dry and the centre is set.
- **Filling spills over and sticks to the rim**: filled too full, and it rose past the edge of the shell as it heated. Leave about 0.5 cm of room; if you switch to different shell sizes, portion again by capacity.
