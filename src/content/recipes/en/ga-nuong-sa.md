*Gà nướng sả* is Vietnamese lemongrass roast chicken. Fish sauce, sugar, garlic and shallot give a salty-sweet base, and lemongrass gives a bright citrus aroma. The tender part of the lemongrass is minced fine, because once the cells are broken, the aroma passes easily into the oil in the marinade (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Skin-on, bone-in thighs can be roasted until the surface takes colour while the inside stays tender.

In the oven the moist chicken skin first loses water, and then the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) gives the golden-brown colour and roasted aroma. The sugar also helps the surface colour, and minced garlic and lemongrass burn more easily than the meat. Leave only a thin layer of marinade, and leave gaps between the pieces, so the hot air can carry the steam away.

## Steps

1. Prepare the lemongrass. Cut off the root end and the dark green tough leaves and peel off the dry, hard outer layers. Slice the tender part thin across the stalk, then chop it fine. Mix with the garlic, shallot, fish sauce, sugar, oil and pepper into a wet marinade that spreads.
2. Marinate the thighs. Pat the thighs dry with paper towels and cut 2 shallow slashes in the thick part of the meat. Rub the marinade over the skin, the underside and the cuts, cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes, until the surface is evenly coated and the sugar grains have mostly dissolved.
3. Preheat the oven. Set it to 200°C (400°F) and preheat for about 10–15 minutes, until the preheat indicator shows a steady temperature. Put an oven-safe rack on a baking sheet to catch the juices. Arrange the thighs skin side up, scraping off clumps of lemongrass and garlic but leaving a thin layer clinging, with about 2 cm between the pieces.
4. Roast. Roast on the middle rack for 30–40 minutes, until the skin is golden brown with slightly charred spots at the edges. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part, away from the bone. At 74°C (165°F) the chicken is done. At the 20-minute mark, check the colour. If the skin is already deep brown but the inside is not cooked, cover loosely with a sheet of foil and keep roasting, measuring again every 5 minutes.
5. Rest. Move the thighs to a clean plate and rest for 5 minutes, until the steam on the surface eases and less juice runs out when cut. Serve whole, or cut the meat off along the bone in slices, and eat with white rice. The knife and board used to cut the chicken should be separate from those used for raw chicken.

## Key points

- **A mouthful of tough lemongrass strands**: the dark green leaves and outer fibres are too coarse and don't soften even when cut fine (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Use only the tender part, slice across first and then chop, and make the marinade as fine and even as you can.
- **The skin is burnt but the meat near the bone is not cooked**: the surface colours faster than heat reaches the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Scrape off thick marinade, roast on the middle rack, and cover with foil if needed. Measure the centre with a thermometer, and don't judge by the colour of the skin.
- **Liquid pools on the pan and the skin is soft and pale**: the meat sits in the juices, and the surface can't dry (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Pat raw chicken dry, roast it on a rack with gaps, and don't pour the leftover raw marinade from the bowl onto the pan.
