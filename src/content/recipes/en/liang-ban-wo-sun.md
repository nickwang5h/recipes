Cold celtuce salad is about the crispness of the tender stem. The strips are salted to draw out a little water, rinsed of surface salt, cooked briefly and cooled, then tossed with ginger, garlic and chili oil. [Salt](/principles/salt/) draws out part of the water, and a short [blanch](/principles/blanching/) takes away the raw hardness. To keep the crispness, lift the strips out in time and do not cook them soft and limp.

Ginger, garlic and chili are heated in oil first to carry their fragrance onto the celtuce (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The daikon is optional. If you add it, it goes through the same salting, rinsing, cooking and cooling. Cut both to a similar thickness so they cook to the same texture in the same pot.

## Steps

1. Wash and peel the celtuce, then peel off the pale fibrous layer under the skin, until the flesh is a moist, even tender green. Cut off a small slice and taste it: there should be no tough strings that will not bite through. Then cut into small strips of even thickness.
2. If you use daikon, wash and peel it and cut it into strips about the size of the celtuce. Put both in a large bowl, mix in 5 g of salt and leave for about 10 minutes, until a little water collects at the bottom and the strips turn slightly pliable.
3. Pour off the water that came out and rinse the strips with clean water 1–2 times. Drain. Taste a piece: only a light saltiness should be left. If it is clearly salty, rinse once more, so the salting salt does not all stay in the vegetables.
4. Bring enough water to cover the strips to a boil. Add the celtuce and the optional daikon, spread them out and cook briefly for about 1 minute. When the raw taste is gone and the centres are still crisp, lift them out and put them straight into drinkable cold water to cool.
5. Once cooled, lift them out and drain thoroughly, then spread them in a clean large bowl. Cut the ginger into fine shreds and the garlic into thin slices, and slice the bird's eye chilies into rings. Cut small, they release fragrance quickly in a short stir-fry and are easy to pick up.
6. Add the vegetable oil to a small pan and, over medium-low heat, fry the ginger, garlic and bird's eye chilies for about 30–45 seconds. Take it off the heat when you smell the fragrance and the edges of the garlic slices are just about to colour. Pour the oil and aromatics together over the drained strips.
7. Toss until the strips are evenly glossy with oil, taste and plate. If you want it cooler, cover and refrigerate briefly. Toss once more before serving to bring the aromatic oil from the bottom of the bowl back onto the strips.

## Key points

- **Stringy bits when you bite**: the white fibrous layer was not peeled off, and a short [blanch](/principles/blanching/) will not make it tender. When peeling, look at the colour and texture of the flesh and keep peeling off any tough outer layer.
- **Soft strips with no crunch**: they were salted too long, or not cooled promptly after cooking (see [salt](/principles/salt/) and [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Follow the short times above and lift the strips out while the centres are still crisp.
- **The oil tastes burnt and bitter**: the garlic slices or chili were cooked too long (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Use medium-low heat and take it off the heat as soon as the fragrance rises. Drain the strips first, so hot oil does not spit when it meets water.
