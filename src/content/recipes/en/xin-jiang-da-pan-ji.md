In Xinjiang big plate chicken, the chicken is well seasoned, the potatoes are floury, and the thick sauce is for tossing with wide noodles. Bone-in chicken, potatoes and pepper pieces braise in the same pot. The sugar is cooked into caramel first, then the chicken goes in to take on colour. The sugar turns from clear to amber through [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/), and the chicken goes in before it blackens, which keeps a lightly sweet, toasty flavour.

The potatoes go in late and soften while absorbing liquid in the braise. A little starch on their surface disperses into the liquid and thickens it (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). If you are serving it with wide noodles, leave more sauce when you reduce.

## Steps

1. Pick any bone fragments off the chicken pieces and blot the surface juices with paper towels. Peel the potatoes and cut into chunks about 4 cm across with a rolling cut. Cut the large green onion into 4 cm lengths, cut the green and red bell peppers into pieces, crush the garlic, and snip the dried chilies into sections. Use separate cutting boards for raw chicken and vegetables.
2. Add the oil and sugar to the wok and push the sugar around over low heat so it melts slowly. When the caramel turns amber and fine bubbles appear, add the dried chicken pieces and stir-fry. Keep your hand close to the surface and add the chicken in several batches to avoid splattering, then toss right away.
3. Stir-fry until the chicken is evenly coloured and no obvious liquid pools. Add the Sichuan peppercorns, bay leaves, dried chilies and garlic and stir-fry briefly until the chilies brighten and smell fragrant. Move on the moment the colour changes (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
4. Add the salt, light soy sauce, oyster sauce and Shaoxing wine, then pour in 1000 ml of water. Bring to a boil over medium heat, skim off the new foam, turn the heat to medium-low, cover and braise for about 15–20 minutes, keeping the liquid at a gentle roll until the chicken is gradually cooked through.
5. When the liquid has dropped until the tops of the chicken pieces start to show, lay the potatoes on top, cover and keep braising for about 15 minutes. Don't turn the potatoes over much at first. If the water gets too low, add a little hot water so there is always flowing liquid at the bottom.
6. When the edges of the potatoes are soft and a chopstick goes in but the centre still has a little resistance, add the large green onion and bell peppers. Gently spoon the liquid from the edge of the pot over the potatoes and keep braising for about 5 minutes, until the potato centres are floury and the chicken bites apart easily. Insert a thermometer into the largest piece of chicken; it is cooked at 74°C (165°F).
7. Uncover and turn gently so the potatoes and chicken are coated in the thick sauce. When a spatula pushed through leaves sauce that still flows back, turn off the heat. For noodles, cook the wide noodles in a separate pot, drain, and toss them into the big plate chicken, so you don't have to reduce the sauce dry.

## Key points

- **Chicken skin blackened, whole pot bitter**: the caramel went too far (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Add the chicken as soon as it is amber, and if the caramel has blackened, start over in a fresh pot.
- **Potatoes stuck to the bottom, sauce sticky**: too little water or too much stirring, so a lot of surface starch comes off the potatoes (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add the potatoes late, turn them gently and keep flowing liquid.
- **Chilies black, harsh smell**: the spices stayed in the hot oil too long (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the seasoning liquid as soon as you smell the aroma, and don't chase a deep colour.
