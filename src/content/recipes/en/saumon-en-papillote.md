Salmon en papillote (*saumon en papillote*) is tender and juicy, and opening the parcel releases the smell of lemon and herbs. The zucchini and tomatoes under the fish soak up plenty of the juices.

The parcel keeps the fish and vegetables together, and the steam produced by the heat stays inside to help keep the fish moist. When the fish is cooked, its proteins set and the flesh separates easily into flakes; baked too long, it dries out (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F) convection or 200°C (400°F) conventional. Slice the zucchini thinly, halve the cherry tomatoes and slice the lemon thinly.
2. Cut two sheets of parchment paper. Divide the zucchini and tomatoes between them, in the centre of each sheet, and sprinkle with a little salt and black pepper.
3. Put a piece of salmon on each portion of vegetables and sprinkle with salt and black pepper. Put 2–3 lemon slices and two sprigs of thyme on each piece of fish, and drizzle each with 1 tbsp of olive oil.
4. Fold the edges of the paper tightly, leaving some room inside, and put the parcels on a baking sheet. Bake for 15–20 minutes; pieces of different thickness need different times.
5. Open the parcels carefully, away from the steam. When the thickest part of the fish is opaque and flakes when pressed lightly with a fork, serve it with the vegetables and juices.

## Key points

- **Fish is dry**: it baked too long, so the proteins tightened and squeezed out water ([protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Start checking at the shorter time, especially with thin pieces.
- **Fish is not cooked through**: pieces of different thickness need different times for heat to reach the centre ([heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Seal the parcel again and keep baking until the thickest part is cooked through.
- **Juices run out onto the baking sheet**: the parcel was not sealed tightly. Fold the edges over several times and press the seam down firmly.
