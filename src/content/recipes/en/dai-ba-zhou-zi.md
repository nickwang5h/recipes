Dai ba zhou zi is a whole-shank dish from Dali in Shaanxi. The "ba" is the trotter attached to the shank. It comes to the table in one piece, and a nudge of the chopsticks separates the skin from the meat, with the skin and tendon soft and sticky. That softness comes from [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/): collagen turns into gelatin over a long steam. The sauces supply salt and savouriness, and the steam carries heat all the way to the thickest part.

Dark soy sauce and light soy sauce replace the red and white soy sauces here. The shank sits in a deep dish that catches the juices and is steamed over water, and the water in the lower part of the steamer is used only to make steam. The fermented tofu and sweet bean sauce are mixed through first so no one piece of meat ends up especially salty (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)).

## Steps

1. Take the front shank the butcher has prepared, scrape off any leftover hairs, wipe the surface clean, check where the bone was cut and remove any visible bone fragments. At the leg bone you can separate the skin and meat on three sides ahead of time and leave one side attached. Keep the skin of the trotter intact so it can be curled into a round.
2. Put the shank in a pot, add water to cover, bring to a boil over high heat and skim off the foam. Turn to low heat and simmer for about 40 minutes at first, until the skin tightens and the meat can bend, then lift it out. This step only sets the shape. Steaming follows right after, so there is no need to check whether the inside is cooked.
3. Blot the skin dry with paper towels and, while it is still warm, rub on part of the dark soy sauce. Put the star anise and cassia bark at the bottom of a deep heatproof dish, lay the shank in skin side down and curl the trotter along the edge of the dish. Pick a fairly deep dish so it can hold the juices that come out in steaming.
4. Mash the fermented tofu and mix it with the 50 g of sweet bean sauce for steaming, the rest of the dark soy sauce, the light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, minced ginger and sliced garlic. Spread the green onion for the steaming seasonings over the shank, cover with a layer of clean food-safe cheesecloth and spread the mixed sauce on the cloth.
5. Bring the water in the steamer to a boil, put in the deep dish, cover and steam for about 3 hours. Keep the steam going, and when the water runs low, add hot water from the side as the steamer requires (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Once the steam stops, heat can't reach the meat, and that time doesn't count as steaming.
6. Lift off the cheesecloth and test the thickest part of the skin and tendon with chopsticks. When they go through easily and the meat separates with a slight nudge, take it out. Hold the dish steady with the whole plate when carrying it, so the shank doesn't fall apart. Remove the cheesecloth and green onion, pick out the spices, turn the shank out onto a plate, spoon over a little of the steaming juices, and serve with green onion pieces and sweet bean sauce on the side.

## Key points

- **Surface is soft but meat near the bone is still tough**: the thickest part needs more time. Keep steaming until the collagen softens (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). The colour of the skin doesn't show whether the inside is done.
- **Steaming juices overflow, or the pot runs dry**: the dish that catches the juices and the water making steam were used together, or water was added too late (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a deep dish for the juices and add hot water when the level is low.
- **Too salty**: fermented tofu, soy sauce and sweet bean sauce all carry salt, and the steaming juices concentrate (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Don't add salt before steaming, and take the dipping sauce in small amounts.
