*Bánh flan* is a Vietnamese caramel custard, so tender it melts as soon as you press it with your tongue, with a layer of slightly bitter caramel underneath. At home it is often made in a steamer. Whole eggs, milk and unsweetened evaporated milk are mixed into a custard, caramel is poured into the mould first, and the custard is chilled and turned out to serve. Heat links the proteins together and holds the milk's water in a soft network (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

The caramel and the custard need different heat. The sugar has to be cooked down far enough to colour, which gives the aroma of [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/); the custard has to heat slowly so the outer ring does not set hard first. Keep the water in the steamer at a bare simmer so heat reaches the mould and the centre of the custard gradually (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Once cooked, the custard still needs about 1 hour to cool and at least 3 hours in the refrigerator before it will turn out whole.

## Steps

1. Make the caramel. Put 70 g sugar and 30 ml water in a small pot and cook over medium heat for 5–6 minutes, until the syrup is clear and then turns amber. Swirl the pot gently so the colour is even, take it off the heat and pour it at once into a heatproof mould about 18 cm across and 5 cm deep. Tilt the mould to cover the bottom. Leave it for 5 minutes, until the caramel sets into a hard layer.
2. Mix the custard. Gently whisk the 4 whole eggs, 110 g sugar, vanilla extract and a pinch of salt, then add the evaporated milk and the milk and whisk until there are no obvious strands of egg white. Keep the whisk close to the bottom of the bowl so you do not beat the mixture into foam.
3. Fill the mould. Pour the custard through a fine sieve onto the caramel layer and skim off any foam on the surface. The lid drips, so cover the top of the mould with aluminum foil.
4. Steam the custard. Put 1000 ml water in the bottom of the steamer and heat over medium heat for 5–8 minutes, until it starts to boil, then put in the mould. Cover, turn the heat to low and steam for about 60 minutes, with the water only barely bubbling. Check the water level at 45 minutes and, if needed, add the reserved 500 ml hot water from the side of the pot so it does not boil dry.
5. Check for doneness. Shake the mould gently: the outer ring should be set and the centre should tremble as one piece, with no liquid rippling. The centre temperature should reach at least 74°C (165°F). If it is not set, steam for 5 more minutes; once it is set, take the mould out.
6. Cool and unmould. Remove the foil and cool on a rack for about 1 hour, until the mould is only slightly warm, then cover and refrigerate for at least 3 hours, until the centre is completely cold. Run a knife around the edge, set a plate with a rim on top and flip it over, and the caramel sauce will run over the custard.

## Key points

- **Honeycomb holes, coarse and firm texture**: the steam was too fierce or the custard was steamed too long, so the protein network tightened (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Keep the water at a bare simmer and take the custard out as soon as it has set.
- **Caramel turns black with a sharp bitter smell**: the sugar stayed in the hot pot after it coloured, and the residual heat kept pushing [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/). Have the mould ready beforehand and take the pot off the heat to pour as soon as the caramel is amber.
- **Set outside, still liquid in the centre**: the mould is deep and the centre heats slowly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Steam 5 minutes longer each time and check the centre again; do not turn up the heat to rush it.
