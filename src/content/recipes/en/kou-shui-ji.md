Mouth-watering chicken is plainly poached chicken and a numbing, spicy red chili oil sauce, each made separately. The first taste in the mouth is the peppercorn fragrance in the oil, then the chicken and the crunchy peanuts. Tenderness depends on gentle poaching and cooling in time: the protein in chicken contracts as it heats, and the longer it cooks the more water it loses (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The ice water stops leftover heat from cooking the chicken further, and gives the skin and meat a cleaner texture.

The red chili oil draws the aroma out of the chili and Sichuan peppercorns with hot oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Powders scorch more easily than whole spices, so control infusing the oil and pouring it separately. The chicken itself is lightly seasoned, so at the end taste a piece of chicken together with the sauce to know whether the salt, acidity and numbing heat are right.

## Steps

1. Slice the ginger and tie half the green onion in a knot; take 15 Sichuan peppercorns. Put the chicken in a pot, add clean water to cover, add the ginger, green onion knot and peppercorns, heat until the water boils, and skim off the foam.
2. Turn to medium-low so the water only bubbles gently, and cook for about 20 minutes. Check the thickest part of the thigh and breast with a thermometer probe, away from the bone, and lift the chicken out only when both centres reach 74°C (165°F); if not, keep poaching gently.
3. Move the chicken into clean drinking-water ice water and cool until safe to handle, then lift out, drain and blot off the surface water. Chop into pieces of similar size and arrange on a plate; if you won't dress and eat it right away, refrigerate it first.
4. Chop the roasted peanuts, cut the remaining green onion into sections, and mince the garlic. Put the chili flakes in a large, dry, heatproof bowl and moisten them first with a little of the oil from the recipe, so the hot oil doesn't only scorch a small patch on top.
5. Heat the remaining vegetable oil over low heat, add the green onion sections, the 15 Sichuan peppercorns and half the minced garlic, and stir until clearly fragrant and the edges of the garlic are slightly yellow, then take off the heat and strain. The oil should not keep smoking, and the garlic and peppercorns should not turn black.
6. First spoon a little hot oil onto the chili flakes as a test and stir right away. It should bubble and smell of peppercorns; if it smokes fast or black specks appear, stop and let the oil cool. When it behaves, pour in the rest of the oil in several additions, stirring as you pour, then let it cool and strain out the red oil.
7. Add the remaining garlic, light soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, toasted sesame oil and MSG to the red oil and stir. Add half the ground Sichuan peppercorns, dip a piece of chicken in the sauce and taste, then add the rest of the ground peppercorns and a little salt.
8. Pour the red oil sauce over the chicken, and sprinkle with chopped roasted peanuts and cilantro. Let the sauce run between the pieces, and toss lightly just before eating so each piece touches the sauce.

## Key points

- **The chicken is dry and the skin is wrinkled**: it was kept at a hard boil, or left waiting in hot broth after cooking (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Poach gently until the centre is cooked through, lift it out, and cool it promptly.
- **The red oil is bitter and the chili has black specks**: the oil was poured too hot and the fine powder heated too fast (see [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Test with a little first, then pour in several additions and stir; make a scorched batch again.
- **The chicken is bland and there is water at the bottom of the plate**: ice water carried onto the plate and diluted the sauce. Drain and blot dry first, then pour on the red oil sauce.
