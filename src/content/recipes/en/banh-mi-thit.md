*Bánh mì thịt* puts a thin, crisp baguette together with a savoury meat filling, sweet-sour pickled radish and fresh cilantro. Common fillings include roast pork, and also liver pâté with Vietnamese cooked pork roll; this recipe makes the latter, with cooked ham. The bread should bite through easily and have room inside for the vegetables, since a thick hard crust squeezes the filling out.

The pickled radish is first rubbed soft with salt and a little sugar to draw out some of the water, then soaked in a vinegar and sugar liquid. It stays crisp this way and carries less water into the bread (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). The vinegar's acidity makes the richness of the pâté and the mayonnaise feel fresher (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the daikon and carrot into thin strips about 5 cm long. Toss with the salt and 1 tsp sugar and rub for about 3 minutes, until a daikon strip can bend until its ends touch without breaking and liquid has collected at the bottom of the bowl.
2. Put the strips in a sieve, rinse off the salt and sugar with cold water and press lightly to drain. Stir the 50 g sugar, white vinegar and warm water until no sugar grains remain and pour into a narrow container holding the strips. The liquid should cover the strips. Soak for about 1 hour, until they taste sweet and sour and are seasoned inside and out.
3. Prepare the meat and fresh vegetables. Keep the pâté cold but spreadable, and slice the cooked pork roll and the ham thin. Remove the watery seed core from the cucumber, drain the cilantro and chilies, and drain the pickles in a sieve, using about 4 tbsp for each sandwich.
4. Put the bread in an oven preheated to 160°C (325°F) and toast briefly, about 3–5 minutes, until the crust is thin and crisp again. Take it out and let it cool a little, then split it along the long side, leaving one side attached. If the crumb is thick, scoop out a little to leave a shallow channel for the filling.
5. Spread mayonnaise evenly on the cut sides, drizzle each with 0.5 tsp light soy sauce, then spread on the pâté. Lay in both kinds of meat, the cucumber, pickles, chilies and cilantro, close the bread lightly, cut it crosswise into two pieces and eat right away. Refrigerate the remaining pickles in their liquid.

## Key points

- **Soggy bottom of the bread**: the pickles were not drained, or the fresh vegetables carried wash water. Rinse and press after rubbing with salt, drain again before assembling, and fill and eat right after spreading the sauces (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **Radish both salty and sour**: the salt used to draw out water was not rinsed off, or the pickling liquid was out of balance. Draw out water and soak in vinegar as separate steps; the liquid should taste both sweet and sour (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)).
- **Hard to bite and the filling falls out**: the crust is too thick or toasted too long. Choose thin small baguettes and toast only until the crust is crisp again; lay the meat slices thin and spread the vegetables out to leave room to close the bread.
