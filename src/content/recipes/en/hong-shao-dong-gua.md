The softness of red-braised winter melon comes from sustained moist heat. Pan-frying first gives the surface a little colour, then adding water and simmering lets heat travel from the outer layer to the centre of each piece (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Only when the winter melon is translucent at the edges and a chopstick passes through it easily is it ready to finish.

Winter melon on its own will not turn the liquid into a thick sauce. The final starch slurry thickens by [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and makes the sauce cling to the surface. Taking the pieces out before thickening means less stirring and fewer broken pieces; the sauce only needs to coat the melon thinly, not set into a gel.

## Steps

1. Peel off the hard skin of the winter melon, scoop out the seeds and the soft inner flesh, and cut into pieces no more than 2 cm on a side. Cut them as evenly as you can and drain off surface water; mince the ginger and chop the green onion.
2. Add 50 ml of oil to the pan and heat over medium heat, then add the winter melon spread out. Turn gently and fry for about 2 minutes, until the sides touching the pan turn pale golden. If there is no colour you can go a little longer, but do not let the outside blacken.
3. Transfer the winter melon to a bowl, pour off the extra oil and leave only enough to wet the bottom of the pan. Add the minced ginger, light soy sauce and oyster sauce and stir-fry briefly for about 15 seconds, just until the sauce begins to bubble and the ginger smells fragrant.
4. Return the winter melon and toss for about 30 seconds, then add boiling water along the edge of the pan to just cover the pieces. Cook over high heat for about 10 minutes, checking the amount of liquid partway; if the bottom of the pan is nearly showing and the centres are still firm, add more boiling water and keep cooking.
5. Add the dark soy sauce in several small amounts and stop once the pieces are coated reddish-brown. Keep cooking until the edges are translucent and a chopstick meets almost no resistance in the centre. Taste a cooked piece with some liquid, then decide whether to add a little chicken bouillon powder.
6. Add the Shaoxing wine and chopped green onion, toss and cook for about 30 seconds, then gently lift the pieces out. Leave in the pan enough liquid to pour over all the winter melon; if there is too much, reduce some of it first, and do not use a lot of starch to make up for a thin sauce.
7. Mix 10 g of starch with 30 ml of cold water, and stir up the settled starch just before using. When the liquid boils, pour the slurry in a little at a time, pushing as you pour; each time, wait until the liquid turns glossy and thick before deciding whether to add more. When the sauce is a thin glaze, turn off the heat and pour it over the winter melon.

## Key points

- **Soft outside, hard inside**: the pieces are too big, or the pot ran short of water partway, so the centre did not get enough heat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut evenly, add boiling water, and cook until a chopstick passes through.
- **Glaze clumps and looks like paste**: the starch was not dispersed in cold water, or too much went in at once (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add a little at a time and stop at a thin glaze; do not pour in all the leftover slurry.
- **Dark and salty**: too much dark soy sauce, and the salt in the light soy sauce, oyster sauce and bouillon powder adds up (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Use dark soy sauce only in small amounts for colour, and taste before adding bouillon powder.
