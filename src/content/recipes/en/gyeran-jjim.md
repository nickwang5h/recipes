*Gyeran-jjim* is the Korean home-style steamed egg, often heated directly on the stove in a small earthenware pot and eaten with rice. The egg is fluffy and still moist when scooped open, and the fragrance of green onion and sesame oil comes through clearly. Without an earthenware pot, a small heavy-bottomed pot of similar capacity works too.

With water added to the eggs, the proteins still link into a network when heated, but the water stretches the network thin, so the texture is soft (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Over direct heat the bottom of the pot warms first and the centre last, so use low heat and a lid to let the heat move in slowly; after the pot comes off the heat, its stored heat keeps setting the egg (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Beat the eggs in a cold small pot, add the water, fish sauce and green onion, and mix with a fork or whisk until no streaks of egg white trail from the tool when you lift it and the fish sauce is dispersed.
2. This mixture fills about two thirds of a 700 ml small pot, leaving room at the top for the egg to rise once covered. If using an earthenware pot, first confirm it can go directly on the stove.
3. Heat slowly over medium-low heat with the lid on the whole time; don't keep lifting it and letting the steam out. Once a little steam keeps coming from the edge of the pot, hold the heat gentle so the centre cooks through slowly.
4. At about 12–13 minutes for an earthenware pot, or about 8–10 minutes for a heavy metal pot, lift the lid for a look, keeping clear of the steam: the egg is puffed, the surface is set, the centre jiggles slightly when you shake the pot, and no pools of raw egg run out.
5. Insert a probe into the centre and take the pot off the heat at 74°C (165°F). If the centre is still loose, cover it again and keep heating on low.
6. Drizzle with the toasted sesame oil and serve right away while soft, with the hot pot on a trivet. The egg keeps firming up in the pot, so scoop it with a spoon and eat it with rice.

## Key points

- **Burnt on the bottom, raw in the centre**: the heat is too high, so the bottom warms far faster than the centre. Use a heavy-bottomed small pot and medium-low heat, and split into two pots if the liquid is too deep. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Coarse, hard egg with water underneath**: the set proteins keep tightening and squeeze out water. Take it off the heat once the centre is cooked through, and don't leave the hot pot on the stove to keep warm. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Stays runny and won't hold shape**: too much water, or the pot is large and the heat too low, so the centre never heated through. Measure the water and check the centre temperature, and avoid turning off the heat just because the edge has set. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
