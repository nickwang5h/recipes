Chairman Mao's red-braised pork is the Hunan way of red-braising pork. It uses skin-on pork belly with dried chilies, star anise and cassia, and the colour comes mainly from caramel colour. When sugar is cooked to a reddish brown, a caramel aroma joins the sweetness (see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). The dish first gets the caramel colour right, then relies on a slow braise to soften the meat, and only at the end reduces the sauce until thick.

The skin and connective tissue of pork belly need moist heat and time to turn from tough to soft (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). When done, a whole piece should lift out intact, bite apart easily and carry a thin layer of glossy sauce. If the meat is cooked through but the skin is still hard to bite, keep braising over low heat. Reduce the sauce only after the skin is soft, or you get meat with a handsome colour that cannot be chewed.

## Steps

1. Bring the blanching water to a boil, about 8–10 minutes, until the surface is rolling steadily. Add the whole piece of pork belly and boil over high heat for 1–2 minutes, until the water returns to a boil, then cook another 3–4 minutes, until the outside turns white and foam gathers. Lift out and drain for 3 minutes, until the surface no longer drips.
2. Cut the meat into pieces about 3 cm across, each with skin, fat and lean. Have the ginger, green onion and spices ready, and keep the braising water at hand.
3. Put the oil and sugar in the pot and stir over low heat for 3–5 minutes, until the sugar melts. Move to medium-low heat and cook another 1–2 minutes, until the syrup is reddish brown and giving off fine bubbles. As soon as that colour arrives, add the pork and toss for 1 minute, so every side picks up the caramel, then pour in the Shaoxing wine.
4. Add 600 ml water slowly down the side of the pot, then the ginger, star anise, dried chilies and cassia. Cook over medium-high heat for 3–5 minutes, until the liquid boils, then turn to low, cover and braise for 40–50 minutes, until a chopstick pierces the skin and meat fairly easily and the centre of the pieces reaches 71°C (160°F).
5. Turn the pieces every 15 minutes and glance at the bottom of the pot; the liquid should still flow. If the meat is still firm and the liquid is nearly gone, add 50 ml hot water and braise another 10 minutes over low heat, then test whether the meat is soft.
6. Once the meat is soft, uncover, add the light soy sauce and reduce the sauce over medium-high heat for 5–8 minutes, turning as it reduces, until the sauce coats the meat in a thin layer and a little flowing sauce is left at the bottom. Taste a piece of meat before adding salt. Add the green onion and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the leaves have just softened, then plate.

## Key points

- **Deep colour but bitter**: the caramel was cooked too far (see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Add the pork as soon as the reddish brown appears; caramel that has already gone black should be remade.
- **Lean meat is cooked, but the skin is still springy**: not enough time in moist heat (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Add hot water and keep braising over low heat, and reduce the sauce only after the skin is soft.
- **Sauce is sticky but the meat is dry and hard**: the liquid was reduced too early. Let the collagen turn to gelatin first (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)) and uncover only for the finish; turn off the heat when the sauce coats the meat, since it thickens more as it cools.
