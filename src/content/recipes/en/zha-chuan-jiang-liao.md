Fried skewer sauce is numbing, hot and richly fragrant, with chili, cumin, Sichuan peppercorns and sesame all steeped in a layer of oil. Brushed on fried vegetable or meat skewers, it brings a toasted aroma. One batch lasts several uses, and a thin brush of it is enough. Powders release their aroma fast and also scorch easily, so stir at once after the hot oil goes on (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The sesame oil, Sichuan peppercorn oil and soy sauce go in after the oil has been poured, so the closing aroma is clearer. The salt, chicken bouillon, mala seasoning, light soy sauce and oyster sauce in the sauce are all salty, and together they get very salty, so use less salt on the skewers it goes with. Start with 350 ml of vegetable oil; powders differ in coarseness and so does the thickness, and once stirred it just needs to brush on thinly.

## Steps

1. Put all the dry ingredients in a dry, deep heatproof bowl and mix well, pressing out any lumps in the chili flakes and the bouillon. Leave room in the bowl for the oil to foam, so the hot oil does not spill over.
2. Heat the vegetable oil in a small pot for about 2–3 minutes, to about 110–120°C (225–250°F), then take it off the heat. The powders are fine, so first test with a pinch of the dry ingredients and a little hot oil: fine bubbles and a fragrant smell are right; if it turns black at once or gives off sharp smoke, the oil is too hot.
3. Pour the oil in three times, about one third each time. Stir up from the bottom of the bowl as you pour, so all the dry powder touches the oil, and wait for the big bubbles of the last pour to settle before the next. Do not keep the hot oil in one spot.
4. When the vigorous foaming has stopped, mix in the toasted sesame oil, light soy sauce, Sichuan peppercorn oil and oyster sauce, and stir until there are no dry lumps and the sesame seeds are evenly spread. Soy sauce contains water and will still spatter on hot oil, so pour slowly.
5. Brush a little of the sauce onto a fully cooked fried skewer, taste, and see how thick a brush works. Cool the rest and keep it in a clean container in the refrigerator, stirring from the bottom before use; do not dip a brush that has touched raw meat back into the whole jar.

## Key points

- **Bitter taste, black specks or sharp smoke**: the oil was too hot and the fine powder scorched in spots (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Test with a small pour first; sauce that has already turned bitter and burnt must be remade.
- **Oil on top but dry lumps at the bottom**: only the top layer was stirred, and the oil never reached the spices at the bottom of the bowl (see [spice aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Lift the bottom with each pour of oil, and stir again before use.
- **Very salty once brushed on**: the salt from several seasoning powders and the soy sauce adds up (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Brush thinly and taste first; the skewers themselves need no more salt.
