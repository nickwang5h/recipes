Pan-fried dumplings are soft on top with a golden, crisp shell underneath. Water and steam first cook the wrapper and filling through, then the water is cooked off so the dumplings on the bottom of the pan fry directly against hot oil (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The wrapper takes up water and [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) happens, and only once the water is gone does it fry to a crisp bottom. The golden colour and toasty aroma come from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).

Frozen dumplings do not need to be thawed first. Arrange them in one layer so each one touches the pan bottom and leaves a channel for hot water and steam. The amount of water depends on the width of the pan: a wide pan needs more water, and the same hundred-odd millilitres reach a different level in different pans, so go by the water line. A golden bottom does not mean the filling is cooked. Dumplings with raw meat inside need the centre of the filling measured.

## Steps

1. Pour the oil into a nonstick skillet with a lid and swirl it into a thin layer. Arrange the frozen dumplings in the cold pan, each standing on its flat base with gaps between them. If some have stuck together, do not pry them hard apart, and do not put the whole clump in the pan.
2. While the pan is still cold, slowly add the water. Pour most of the amount listed first, then look at the water line and add until it reaches about halfway up the dumplings. Cover and bring to a boil over medium-high heat. Adding the water first and then turning on the heat means no water hits hot oil and splatters.
3. Once the water boils, adjust the heat so it keeps a steady bubble, cover and cook for about 8–10 minutes, or as the package directs. Check near the end: if the water has dried up early and the filling is not cooked, add a little hot water around the edge of the pan and cover again.
4. Measure the filling in the largest dumpling: at least 71°C (160°F) for pork filling, at least 74°C (165°F) for poultry or shrimp filling, and also meeting the package directions. If not, keep cooking in water. Vegetable fillings also have to be hot through, so do not go by the wrapper turning translucent alone.
5. When only about 2 mm of water remains, turn the heat to medium and take off the lid. Wait for the water to cook off and the sound to change from a bubbling to a fine, dense sizzle. Gently shake the pan so the bottoms pick up the oil evenly, and do not scrape under a crisp bottom that has not set.
6. Fry for about 1–2 minutes more and lift one to look at the bottom. When the bottoms are evenly golden and the edges crisp, turn off the heat. If you are using the sesame seeds and green onion, sprinkle them on at the end. Lift the dumplings out at once and lay them on a plate, and do not cover the pan again, which would steam them soft.

## Key points

- **Burnt bottoms and filling not cooked**: the water dried up too early, so the pan bottom was already dry-frying while the heat had not reached the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add hot water, cover and keep cooking, and measure meat fillings with a thermometer.
- **Wrappers soft and torn**: too much water, or they were cooked in water too long, and the wrapper kept absorbing water after [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Add water by the water line, and uncover and cook it dry once done.
- **Bottoms white and soft with no crisp shell**: water is still evaporating in the pan and the bottom temperature cannot climb. Wait until the water is gone, then fry, and let the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) colour the bottoms golden. Do not stack the dumplings after they come out.
