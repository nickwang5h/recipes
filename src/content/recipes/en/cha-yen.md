*Cha yen* is strong Thai tea with sugar, condensed milk and evaporated milk, served over a full glass of ice. Thai tea leaves are usually black tea with vanilla scent and food colouring, which turns orange once milk is added. When buying, look for dry leaves sold as Thai Tea Mix; the leaves are already flavoured, so brewing them directly gives the base taste of this drink.

The key is to brew it strong first, then cool it. Hot water draws flavour and [aroma out](/principles/aromatics/) of the leaves, and when time is up you strain the leaves out to fix the strength. Condensed milk adds both sweetness and milk flavour, and evaporated milk adds unsweetened milk richness. Pour hot tea straight over ice and the heat quickly [passes into the ice](/principles/heat-transfer/); the ice melts a lot and dilutes the tea. Cool it first, then serve over plenty of ice to keep the flavour of the strong tea.

## Steps

1. Bring the water to a boil, about 4–6 minutes, turn off the heat, and pour it straight into a heatproof pitcher holding the Thai tea leaves. Stir gently so all the leaves are wet, cover and steep for 5 minutes, until the tea is deep reddish brown and smells of tea.
2. Strain the tea through a fine-mesh strainer into another pitcher, taking about 1–2 minutes, until the tea drips through and the leaves stay in the strainer. If there are still obvious tea fines at the bottom of the pitcher, strain again through a layer of fine cloth. Move the leaves away after straining to stop the steeping.
3. While the tea is still hot, add the white sugar and salt and stir for about 1 minute, until no crystals remain at the bottom. Leave the lid off and let it cool for about 30 minutes, until the tea no longer steams and the side of the pitcher feels close to room temperature.
4. Measure out four portions of 180 ml tea base. Stir 1.5 tbsp condensed milk into each, mixing until the colour is even and there are no thick white streaks at the bottom of the glass. Keep any leftover strained tea base for another use.
5. Put 200 g ice in each of four large glasses, pour in the sweetened tea, and drizzle 30 ml evaporated milk over each. The evaporated milk leaves pale streaks as it runs down the ice. Stir before drinking and serve right away.

## Key points

- **Tastes thin, and a lot of water collects in the glass quickly**: the tea was poured over ice while still hot, and the melting ice watered down the flavour. Cool it until the side of the pitcher is near room temperature before pouring into the iced glass, and keep the ratio of strong tea to ice. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Tea is bitter and astringent, aroma is heavy**: it steeped too long, and the leaves left in the pitcher kept releasing flavour. Strain after timing 5 minutes; for a stronger tea, keep the amount of dry tea and do not lengthen the steeping. See [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Thick white clumps in the drink, sweetness differs from top to bottom**: cold condensed milk is thick and does not mix easily when poured over ice. Stir it thoroughly into the tea base without ice first, then pour into the iced glass; use the tea base at room temperature. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
