Qishan minced pork noodles are thin noodles in a hot, sour and spicy soup, with a minced pork topping carrying a layer of red chili oil, and egg crepe and chives floating on the soup. The tofu, wood ear and daylily buds are cut small so they come into the mouth together with the noodles. The bowls are filled with little noodle and plenty of soup, and refilled when you finish a small bowl; the soup must stay hot throughout.

The vinegar sets the sourness of the whole soup and cuts the richness of the pork belly (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). The chili flakes are heated briefly in the pork fat, so their colour and aroma pass into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Thin noodles need some strength to resist turning mushy (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)); at home, fresh thin noodles can replace hand-rolled ones, still cooked to order and served in small bowls.

## Steps

1. Rehydrate the dried ingredients. Soak the wood ear and daylily buds separately, each in 300 ml cold water, for 30–40 minutes, until the wood ear has opened out and the daylily buds can be bent. Rinse, remove the hard stems, and cut into small pieces and short lengths.
2. Fry the minced pork topping. Put 50 ml oil and the fat in the pan and stir-fry over low heat for 8–10 minutes, until the fat is translucent and there is clearly more oil. Add the lean meat and 20 g minced ginger and stir-fry over medium heat for 5 minutes, until all the meat has changed colour.
3. Simmer the topping. Add the vinegar for the topping, the salt and the five spice powder, turn to low heat, cover and simmer for 5–6 minutes, until the meat is tender and the bottom of the pan still has oil and juices. Uncover, sprinkle in the chili flakes and stir-fry over low heat for 5 minutes, until the oil turns red and smells of chili. Remove from the pan.
4. Make the egg crepe. Beat the egg. Put 0.75 tbsp oil in a flat-bottomed pan, pour in the egg and spread it thin, and fry over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until the surface is set. Flip and fry 10–20 seconds more, until neither side has liquid egg. Remove and cut into small diamonds.
5. Stir-fry the vegetables. Put the remaining 1.5 tbsp oil and 10 g minced ginger in the pan and stir-fry over medium heat for 30 seconds, until the ginger is fragrant. Add the carrot and potato and stir-fry for 5 minutes, until the edges are slightly translucent, then add the tofu, wood ear and daylily buds and stir-fry for 3 minutes, until everything is heated through.
6. Make the sour soup. Pour 80 ml vinegar down the side of the pan, add 1900 ml water and all the minced pork topping, and boil over high heat for 5 minutes, until the soup is at a full boil. Turn to low heat and cook for 10 minutes, until the potato dice crush easily. Taste the soup and add salt, then add the egg crepe and chives.
7. Cook the noodles and ladle on the soup. In another pot, heat the noodle-cooking water over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until at a full boil. Cook the fresh thin noodles in batches for 2–3 minutes, until there is no raw starch core and they are still springy when bitten; if the package gives a different time, follow the package. Lift out a small portion of noodles for each bowl and immediately ladle over the hot soup and vegetables.

## Key points

- **Soup tastes greasy and the sourness is faint**: the vinegar was diluted by the large amount of soup. Add the vinegar for the topping and the vinegar for the soup separately, and taste the finished soup (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
- **Red oil turns black and the chili tastes bitter**: the chili flakes were fried too long in a dry hot pan (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the chili only after the meat is tender and the heat is low, and stop as soon as the oil turns red.
- **Noodles go soft and have no spring by the second half of the bowl**: they were overcooked or the bowl was too full, so they keep absorbing soup as you eat slowly. Choose thin noodles, cook them in batches and refill small bowls (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
