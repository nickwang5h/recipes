Green onion oil is fragrant without being greasy: the aroma of green onion, ginger and onion is slowly infused into the oil, to toss with noodles, mix into fillings, or add fragrance to cooked dishes. When green onion and onion are cut and heated, their water gradually leaves and the aroma passes into the oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lower heat lets the ingredients colour slowly, so the outside does not burn while the inside is still wet.

Adding dried shrimp gives the oil a layer of seafood [umami](/principles/umami/); leave it out for a cleaner flavour. The wine is only for soaking the dried shrimp, so skip it if you skip the shrimp. A batch of oil can be used over several meals. The solids can be mixed into cooked food the same day; strain them out before storing so the oil is easy to use.

## Steps

1. For the dried shrimp version, soak the dried shrimp in warm water at about 50°C (120°F) with the Shaoxing wine for 10 minutes. Lift them out, drain, and blot the surface dry with paper towels; discard the soaking water.
2. Wash the green onion and cut into 5 cm lengths, slice the ginger and cut the onion into strips. Dry the surface of the vegetables and leave them spread out for a while, until no obvious water drops can be seen. Use the onion raw in strips, without cooking it first.
3. Put all the oil and the vegetables in a cold pot, then add the drained dried shrimp. Heat over medium-low heat and stir until everything is coated in oil, then adjust to a heat that keeps fine bubbles going and colours the ingredients slowly.
4. Cook for about 20 minutes, stirring from time to time to lift the ingredients that stick to the bottom. Turn off the heat when the onion is golden, the green onion pieces are starting to turn brown and the foam has clearly died down. If the thin green onion leaves darken first, pick them out first; there is no need to wait until all the ingredients are the same colour.
5. Strain out the oil before the solids turn black, so the residual heat does not keep toasting them. Put the oil in a clean heatproof container and refrigerate promptly, with the fridge at 4°C (40°F) or below, and use it within one week; for longer storage, freeze it in small portions.

## Key points

- **Oil tastes bitter and green onion leaves are black**: the heat was too high and the thin leaves scorched before the onion did (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Go by the part that changes colour fastest, and lift the solids out in stages.
- **Oil spits and foam suddenly increases**: the soaked dried shrimp or the vegetables carry water. Blot the surface dry and add them to cold oil so the water evaporates slowly; do not add the shrimp soaking water to hot oil.
- **Weak green onion flavour and onion still white and soft**: there is still a lot of water and it has not been heated enough (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Keep frying over low heat until golden, and do not rush it with high heat.
