Mung bean soup is a sweet home-style soup of whole mung beans cooked soft and sweetened with sugar. The skins split, the centres fall apart under pressure, and you can still spoon up beans from the soup. Use mung beans with their skins for this bean flavour and texture. Hulled yellow-green mung beans cook down into a fine purée more easily.

The beans take up water first, and as they heat, their starch gradually [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the centre goes from hard to soft and floury. As they keep cooking, the beans that fall apart make the soup slightly thicker. The heat controls how hard the water boils and evaporates (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). However hard the soup rolls, the centres do not soften any faster.

## Steps

1. Pick out any broken or insect-damaged mung beans and rinse the rest clean. Soak the beans in 1000 ml of water, covered, in the refrigerator for 8 hours, until the beans are clearly swollen and the skins are taut. Pour off the soaking water and drain.
2. Put the mung beans and the 950 ml of water for the soup in a pot of at least 3 L capacity. Heat over medium-high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the surface bubbles steadily, and skim off the foam.
3. Turn to low heat, leave the lid slightly open and cook for about 50 minutes. Keep the surface gently moving and push lightly from the bottom of the pot every 10 minutes. By now most skins should have split, and the centres should break apart under the back of a spoon.
4. Cook for 10 minutes more, until the centres are completely soft and floury, with no hard bits when tasted and some broken-down bean in the soup. If the centres are still hard, cook 10 minutes more at a time and taste again. For a thicker soup, rely on cooking the beans soft and do not crush beans that are still hard.
5. Add 55–75 g of white sugar and stir over low heat for 2 minutes, until the sugar has fully dissolved. The soup should flow, with soft beans in it. Serve in bowls while warm.

## Key points

- **The skins have split but the inside is still hard**: the beans are old or were not soaked through, and split skins do not mean the centres are cooked (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep cooking on low heat and taste one bean each time. Do not turn off the heat while there is a hard core.
- **The soup turns into a pot of thick bean purée**: it was cooked too long and the beans were crushed over and over, releasing more starch (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Start tasting when the beans burst open, keep some whole beans and only push lightly at the bottom.
- **The bottom is scorched and the soup tastes burnt**: the bean paste settled and kept heating (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use low heat and run the spoon along the bottom when stirring. If a scorched layer has formed, ladle the top of the soup into another pot and do not scrape the scorched layer in.
