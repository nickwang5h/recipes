Northern Shaanxi braised green beans is a pot of long beans, potatoes and tomatoes cooked down together: the potatoes are soft and floury, the beans are tender, and a little sour, fragrant thick sauce is left at the bottom. The order matters. The [acid](/principles/acid/) in tomatoes slows the softening of vegetable tissue, so cook the potatoes and beans through first and add the tomatoes after, and you get both tenderness and sourness.

The [starch in the potatoes absorbs water when heated](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and turns soft, and the small amount of starch released from the cut surfaces also thickens the broth. Beans that have turned green have only just started to heat and must be cooked thoroughly. Once a potato can be pierced through, taste a bean separately as well.

## Steps

1. Prepare the aromatics. Chop the green onion, shred the ginger and mince the garlic. Snap the ends and strings off the beans, wash them, cut them into sections of similar length and drain off surface water. Peel the potatoes and cut them into small pieces of about 1 cm; peel the tomatoes and cut them to the same size.
2. Prepare the chilies. If using long green chilies, remove the stems and seeds, cut them into thin strips and set aside to go in with the tomatoes. The beans and potatoes cook first; the acid and chili come in the later stage.
3. Fry the aromatics. Add 15 ml of oil to the pot and heat over medium heat until it flows and spreads easily. Add the green onion, ginger and garlic and stir until fragrant. If the oil starts to smoke, take the pot off the heat to cool it first, so the garlic does not turn black in overheated oil.
4. Fry the main ingredients. Add the beans and stir-fry until every piece is coated in oil and the colour looks bright, then add the potatoes and keep stir-frying for about 30 seconds. The beans are not cooked at this point; boiling water will cook them through next.
5. Simmer the beans and potatoes soft. Pour in about 800 ml of hot water, just enough to barely cover the ingredients. Bring to a boil, cover the pot, keep it boiling, and cook until the beans are completely soft with no hard core and a chopstick pierces the potatoes easily. Add hot water if it runs short along the way.
6. Cook the tomato sauce. Add the tomatoes, chilies, light soy sauce, oyster sauce and half of the five spice powder and keep cooking until the tomatoes break down into a sauce. Push gently from the bottom now and then so the potatoes do not stick; once the broth thickens, taste, then decide whether to add salt or the rest of the five spice powder.
7. Leave a layer of thick sauce at the bottom that can be spooned up, with the potato pieces mostly whole and the beans soft. Sprinkle with the cilantro, stir gently and turn off the heat; do not cook the liquid down until dry.

## Key points

- **Potatoes stay hard after long cooking**: the tomatoes went in too early, and the acidic environment slowed the softening of the tissue. Following the relationship between [acid and vegetables](/principles/acid/), cook until a chopstick pierces through, then add the tomatoes.
- **Beans look a lovely colour but have a hard core when bitten**: they were not heated long enough (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep cooking in boiling water until cooked through; undercooked beans can cause poisoning.
- **Broth sticks to the pot and the potatoes fall apart**: once the starch is released the broth thickens, and strong heat or hard stirring makes it stick. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); when it thickens, lower the heat and push gently, and add hot water as it gets close to dry.
