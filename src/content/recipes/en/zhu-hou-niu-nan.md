The base of this dish is the bean aroma of chu hou paste with ginger and garlic. The brisket is cooked until the tendon is soft and the sauce is smooth. Choose a cut with tendon: only once the [collagen in it softens](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) do you get this texture. After blanching, cut the meat into even large pieces so the whole pot softens at the same time.

This version adds chili bean paste, Sichuan peppercorns and dried chilies, so it is a spicy variation. The red fermented bean curd, oyster sauce and the optional char siu sauce are already salty and sweet, so the light soy sauce is held until the end (the reason is in [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). The main method uses an electric pressure cooker; a regular pot needs longer.

## Steps

1. Cut the ginger into pieces about 1.5 cm, crack the garlic, and cut the Thai chili into sections. Tie the bay leaves, Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, dried chilies, cloves and the optional licorice into a spice bag. Mash the red fermented bean curd, and mix the chu hou paste, oyster sauce, dark soy sauce and the optional char siu sauce together separately.
2. Put the whole piece of brisket in a pot of cold water. Cook until it boils and the foam gathers at the top, then lift the meat out, rinse off the impurities, cut it into pieces about 4 cm, drain and pat dry. Pour away the blanching water; the later stewing uses fresh water.
3. Add the oil to the pot, stir-fry the ginger, garlic and Thai chili over medium heat until fragrant, then turn to medium-low heat and turn the spice bag in the pot for about 30 seconds. Add the mashed red fermented bean curd, then the chili bean paste, and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until the oil turns red and you can smell the sauce.
4. Add the rock sugar and the brisket and stir-fry until the sugar slowly dissolves and the meat is evenly coated in sauce. Turn off the heat first, drizzle in the Shaoxing wine and the optional baijiu, then turn the heat back to medium and stir. When the wine boils, add the mixed sauce and stir-fry until the bubbles are small and dense and all the pieces are coated.
5. Transfer everything to an electric pressure cooker and pour in 800 ml of hot water, then top up until it just covers the brisket, staying above the cooker's minimum liquid level and below its maximum fill line. Choose the high-pressure meat stew program, time 40 minutes once it comes up to pressure, and release the pressure as the manual says before opening the lid.
6. Pierce the thickest piece with a fork. It is done when the fork goes in easily and the tendon is soft; if it is still tough, keep stewing. If you use a regular pot, cover and braise on low heat for about 3 hours, topping up with hot water along the way so the meat stays in the liquid.
7. Lift out the brisket and the spice bag, and reduce the liquid uncovered. Spices steeped for a long time get harsher (see [spice extraction](/principles/aromatics/)), so take them out before reducing. Taste the reduced sauce, then add the light soy sauce in portions to adjust the saltiness.
8. Return the brisket to the thick sauce and cook until heated through, then toss and plate. Skim the floating fat from the surface and keep a sauce that can be spooned over rice.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but the tendon is still rubbery**: the pressure time is only a starting point. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Pierce the tendon of the thickest piece, and keep stewing if it is still tough.
- **Too salty once the sauce has reduced**: several sauces stack up, and the saltiness concentrates as the water evaporates. See [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/). Keep the light soy sauce until after reducing and taste in portions.
- **Mouthful of clove and medicinal flavour**: the spices braised for a long time and were reduced along with the liquid. See [spice extraction](/principles/aromatics/). Bag the spices, lift them out once the flavour is enough, and the licorice can be left out.
