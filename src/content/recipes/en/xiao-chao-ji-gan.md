Stir-fried chicken liver is boiled first and then stir-fried briefly, which makes it soft and tender, with garlic shoots that keep a little crunch. When the liver heats up with cold water, the proteins that seep from the surface gather as scum, and the ginger, leek and Shaoxing wine carry off the gamey smell; see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/). The liver is already cooked through in the first pot, so the stir-fry is only for adding aroma and mixing in the seasoning.

Chicken liver can also carry harmful bacteria inside, so the centre must reach 74°C (165°F). Once it is cooked, do not stir-fry it for long. That is safer and gives a better texture: with more heat the [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) more tightly and the liver turns hard and powdery. For the vegetable, use green garlic, adding the stalks first and the leaves after, so the finished dish keeps a garlic aroma and a little crunch.

## Steps

1. Check the liver, remove the sinew and any obviously dirty parts, and blot off the water that seeps from the surface with paper towels. Wash the green garlic, drain, and cut into sections, keeping leaves and stalks separate. Set out two portions of leek and ginger.
2. Put the liver in a pot, cover with cold water, and add 100 g of leek sections, 70 g of ginger slices and the Shaoxing wine. Bring to a boil over high heat, turn to medium, skim the foam, and keep a gentle boil so the liver does not knock against the pot.
3. Once the water boils, cook for about 5–10 minutes. Cut open the larger pieces one at a time; lift each out when the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), and keep cooking those that have not. Clearing the foam is enough; the water does not have to run completely clear.
4. Drain the liver in a clean strainer, and once it has cooled a little, slice it on a board for cooked food. Remove any scum clinging to it, blot the surface dry, and stir-fry at once; there is no need to soak it in cold water.
5. Heat 30 ml of oil in a wok over medium heat, and when it flows smoothly add 50 g of sliced leek and 50 g of diced ginger. Stir-fry until the leek is slightly soft and fragrant. Add the liver and turn it gently, until the steam on the surface has gone and the edges are slightly yellow.
6. Add the garlic stalks and stir-fry until just soft, then add the leaves, for about 30 seconds in all. When the leaves are bright green and the raw taste is gone, turn to low heat and add half the salt, the five spice powder and the pepper, plus the chicken bouillon powder and ground cumin (optional). Mix gently.
7. Turn the heat to high and toss quickly for about 30 seconds, and turn off the heat once the spices coat the liver. Taste, and add the remaining salt if needed. After plating you can sprinkle on toasted white sesame seeds; do not leave them in the pan to keep heating.

## Key points

- **Liver is hard and powdery**: it was already cooked through in the first pot, then stir-fried for a long time in the second, and the [proteins denatured and coagulated](/principles/protein-coagulation/) more tightly and lost water. Boil only to the centre temperature, then lift it out, and in the stir-fry wait only until the garlic shoots are just cooked.
- **Water in the pan and a strong gamey smell**: the scum was not cleared, or the liver was not drained after lifting out (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Skim first, blot after lifting out, and do not cover the smell by adding more spices.
- **Spices are bitter and the liver breaks into crumbs**: the powders burnt on the hot bottom of the pan, and the stirring was too rough (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lower the heat before seasoning, let the powders mix into moist ingredients, and push gently with the spatula.
