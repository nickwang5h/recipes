For garlic broccoli, the small florets are first cooked to the right tenderness, then dressed with a savoury garlic sauce. Boiling water gets around the stalks and the heads and heats each floret evenly; see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/). The garlic is fried separately over low heat, which keeps its aroma and keeps it from burning while you wait for the broccoli to cook through; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

The part to look after is the stalk below each floret. The head softens easily, but the centre of a thick stalk can still be raw. Cutting the florets to similar sizes and splitting thick stalks lets them finish together; see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/). The broccoli is dressed straight after blanching and not returned to the wok, so it has to be cooked to an edible tenderness in the water.

## Steps

1. Break the broccoli into florets of similar size at the stalks. Split overly thick stalks lengthwise and peel off any tough outer skin. Keep the heads intact, rinse and drain.
2. Peel and mince the garlic. Stir the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and the 30 ml of water for the sauce together and set it by the wok, so you do not have to measure while the garlic is frying.
3. Add 1000 ml of water to a pot and bring to a steady rolling boil over high heat. Add the broccoli and loosen it gently so the water surrounds every floret. If there is too much for one go, cook it in batches.
4. Keep it boiling for about 2–3 minutes. Check a thicker floret: a chopstick should go into the stalk, it should have no raw firmness when bitten, and the head should be intact and bright in colour. If you like it crisper, lift it out at this point without waiting for the full time.
5. Drain the broccoli in a colander, shake out the water trapped between the heads, then arrange it on a plate. Do not soak it in cold water, and pour on the sauce right away. Do not let blanching water collect on the plate.
6. Pour out the blanching water and wipe the pot dry. Add 10 ml of oil and heat the garlic over low heat. When you smell the garlic and the edges just begin to turn pale gold, pour in the mixed sauce.
7. Cook the sauce until it bubbles across the whole pot and the sugar dissolves, then turn off the heat right away and spoon it evenly over the broccoli. The sauce should flow, and the heads only need to be coated in garlic.

## Key points

- **The heads fall apart and the stalks are still hard**: the florets and stalks differ too much in thickness (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Split thick stalks thinner first, check the thickest part while blanching, and do not judge doneness by colour alone.
- **A plate of watery liquid**: the heads carried blanching water, which thinned the sauce a little (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Drain well after lifting, shake the colander lightly before plating, and pour the sauce on as soon as it boils.
- **The garlic sauce is bitter or too salty**: the garlic burned, or the sauce was cooked too long and reduced too far (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the sauce as soon as the garlic is fragrant and stop the heat once it boils. Do not aim for a thick coating.
