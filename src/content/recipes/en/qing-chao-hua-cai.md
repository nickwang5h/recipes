Simple stir-fried cauliflower is about the tender softness of the florets and a little bite in the stems. [Blanching](/principles/blanching/) first lets hot water into the gaps between the florets, and draining before it goes into the pan makes it easier to stir-fry to an even doneness. The blanching goes only until slightly soft, because stir-frying and covered cooking follow; cooking it through the first time would leave the finished dish too soft.

The florets are loose and the thick stems are dense, so they heat at different speeds. Slicing the thick stems thin and dividing the small florets into similar sizes narrows that difference (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). The small amount of water at the end boils in the pan and helps the centre of the cauliflower keep heating; uncovering to drive off excess liquid keeps the garlic aroma and the salt from being diluted.

## Steps

1. Wash the cauliflower and divide it along the small branches into florets of similar size. Slice the thick stems thin, removing any obviously hard, old outer layer; peel the garlic and cut it into slices of even thickness.
2. Put about 1000 ml water in a pot and bring to a boil over high heat. Add the cauliflower and blanch for about 2–3 minutes; lift it out when the florets are still whole and the stems are slightly soft but still bite back. If you prefer it crisper, lift it out earlier once it reaches that state.
3. Drain the cauliflower well in a colander. Drain between the florets too, and begin stir-frying only once shaking the colander gently no longer produces continuous dripping, so you do not carry a ladle of blanching water into the oil.
4. Heat a wok, add 15 ml oil, and add the garlic slices; stir-fry until you smell the garlic and the edges just start to colour. Immediately add the cauliflower, turn to medium-high heat and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, so the florets and stem slices all touch the bottom of the pan.
5. Add the salt, toss, then drizzle 50 ml water down the side of the wok. Cover and cook for about 1 minute so the stems cook through; stay clear of the steam when you lift the lid.
6. Uncover and stir-fry quickly. When no obvious water is left at the bottom of the pan, taste a small piece of a thick stem; if there is no hard raw core, take it out. If it is still hard, keep covering briefly; if it is already soft and tender, take it out right away.

## Key points

- **Florets crumble while the thick stems are still hard**: the cutting was uneven (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Slice the thick stems thin and keep the florets similar in size; do not wait for the florets to cook to mush while waiting for the stems.
- **Soft and limp in the mouth**: the cooking effects of blanching and the brief covered cooking add together (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). When lifting it out the first time, leave some bite; if you like it crisp-tender, shorten the blanching rather than using up the full time at each stage.
- **Water pooling on the plate, faint garlic**: the florets carried in blanching water, or the extra water was not reduced after uncovering (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain thoroughly, and finish by stir-frying uncovered until the liquid only lightly clings.
