Stir-fried spinach and eggs is a home-style dish with soft spinach, tender egg curds and a little broth. Spinach and eggs are handled separately: the spinach is first [blanched](/principles/blanching/) until soft and then drained of surface water; the eggs are cooked into large curds and taken out, so they are not heated again for the time being. Combining takes only a short toss, so the eggs do not keep tightening and turn dry and hard (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

This version adds a little plain water at the end to leave some broth. Drain off the water left after blanching, or with the water added for stir-frying there will be too much broth and a bland taste. The 100 ml water has to come back to a boil after it goes in; watching whether the pot is boiling is more reliable than counting the last ten seconds (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Remove the roots from the spinach and wash off the sand where the leaves meet the stems. If stems and leaves differ a lot in thickness, separate them, putting the thick stems in boiling water first and then the leaves; blanch in batches so the water returns to a boil quickly.
2. Lift the spinach out once the leaves collapse and the stems bend, and drain in a colander. Do not set the colander in a pool of water; cut long stems into bite-sized pieces and do not squeeze the tender leaves into a crushed ball.
3. Crack the two eggs into a bowl and beat until the white and yolk are completely blended. Put 10 ml oil in the pan and heat over medium heat; pour in the eggs when they sizzle lightly on contact with the bottom of the pan, and spread into an omelette.
4. After about 15 seconds, start watching the egg: when the bottom and edges have set and no runny raw egg is left on top, break it into pieces with a spatula. If the omelette is thick, wait a little longer until it has set inside too before taking it out.
5. Transfer the egg pieces to a plate and do not wash the pan. Clear out any burnt bits, add 5 ml oil and put in the drained spinach, stir-frying over high heat for about 15 seconds, until the greens are evenly coated in oil and hot again.
6. Return the egg pieces to the pan, toss gently and add 100 ml drinking water. Once the pan is clearly boiling again, toss for about 10 seconds more and check that the egg is cooked through. Once the spinach and egg pieces are hot, the water does not need to be reduced away.
7. Pick up a piece of spinach with egg and taste, adding a little salt, then toss and plate at once. Serve with a little broth and do not leave the greens in the hot pan to keep softening.

## Key points

- **Hard egg pieces and dry texture**: the egg kept heating for a long time after it set, so the protein contracted and lost water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take it out first and combine only after the spinach is hot.
- **Pan looks like soup and the dish tastes bland**: water left after blanching was not drained, and with the water added at the end it diluted the flavour (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Drain the spinach first, and add water by the amount in the ingredients when combining.
- **Omelette cooked on the surface but runny inside**: the omelette is too thick and the centre heats slowly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Go by how it looks when set; cut open thick pieces and keep heating, and do not take it out only by the timer.
