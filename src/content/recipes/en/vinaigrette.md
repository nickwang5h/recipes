*Vinaigrette* is the most common dressing for French salads: sharp, fresh, and coating the leaves in a thin layer. Wine vinegar, oil, salt and pepper are enough, and fresh herbs are added by season. The classic starting point for vinegar and oil is a volume ratio of 1:3. With a mild vegetable oil the vinegar stands out more; with olive oil the dressing takes on the oil's own fragrance. It suits both lettuce and warm cooked vegetables.

Whisking breaks the oil into fine droplets that are temporarily dispersed in the water of the vinegar (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). There is no emulsifier such as egg yolk in the dressing, so it separates again on standing; shake or whisk it up before using. The vinegar's [acid](/principles/acid/) makes the fat taste fresh, and the salt goes into the vinegar first so that it dissolves. The leaves must be dried too, because water on the leaves dilutes the dressing and lets it run to the bottom of the bowl.

## Steps

1. Put the wine vinegar and a little salt in a bowl and whisk until no salt grains are visible. Taste a drop to make sure there is saltiness within the sourness, then start adding the oil.
2. Pour the oil in slowly while whisking. Start with a thin stream, and speed up gradually once the liquid turns cloudy and the oil droplets are fine, each time letting the newly added oil be broken up.
3. Add freshly ground pepper and keep whisking until the colour is even and only fine oil droplets show on the surface. You can also put the ingredients in a jar with a lid, screw it tight and shake hard for about half a minute until it is an even, cloudy dressing.
4. Dip a dried leaf into the dressing to taste and add a little salt to your taste. If using parsley, stir it in now. If the dressing has stood for a while, whisk it again before tasting.
5. Just before serving, pour the dressing over the leaves and toss gently until every leaf is thinly glossy. Use part of it first and add more if it is not enough after tossing; there should be no pool of dressing at the bottom of the bowl.

## Key points

- **It separates into two layers in the jar**: oil droplets slowly merge together (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Shake it up just before using so every spoonful has both oil and vinegar.
- **The leaves have no flavour and the bottom of the bowl is sour**: there was too much water on the leaves, or the dressing had already separated, so the oil and vinegar did not coat the leaves together. Drain and dry the leaves first, whisk the dressing again, and toss in small portions.
- **Only sharp sourness or greasiness in the mouth**: the acidity of the vinegar and the flavour of the oil differ, and 1:3 is only a starting point (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). When too sour, add oil a teaspoon at a time; when too greasy, add vinegar a teaspoon at a time, tasting with a leaf each time.
- **Grains of salt and uneven saltiness**: salt dissolves poorly in oil (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Dissolve it in the vinegar first; if you need more salt after tossing, dissolve it in a little vinegar before adding it.
