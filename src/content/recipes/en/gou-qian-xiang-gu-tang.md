Silky shiitake soup is a small bowl of thickened soup: fresh shiitake are sliced and cooked briefly, and potato starch thickens it at the end. The mushroom fragrance stays in the broth, a little chicken bouillon adds [umami](/principles/umami/), and green onion finishes it. The thickness is right when the soup scoops up smoothly and thinly coats the back of a spoon.

Potato starch only disperses in cold water and settles to the bottom if left. In hot soup the granules take up water and swell, and that is [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Potato starch thickens quickly, so it goes in last. Take the soup off the heat once it turns glossy and reaches the right thickness; long boiling or vigorous whisking weakens the thickening that has already formed.

## Steps

1. Rinse the fresh shiitake quickly, remove the tough stems and slice about 0.5–1 cm thick. Drain and pat off the surface water, and chop the green onion; do not soak the slices in salted water for long.
2. Mix 10 g of potato starch with 50 ml of cold water and stir until no dry starch is visible. Set the slurry beside the stove with a small spoon, and stir it again before it goes into the pot.
3. Add 10 ml of oil to the pot and heat over low until it spreads, then add the shiitake slices. Cook each side for about 10 seconds, until the surface is coated in oil and starts to give off fragrance; when water seeps out, stop trying for deep brown edges.
4. Slowly pour 300 ml of boiling water down the side of the pot, turn to medium and cook for 3–5 minutes. When the surface is at a gentle roll, the shiitake have softened and you can smell the mushrooms, add part of the bouillon powder and stir in.
5. Stir the slurry again. Keeping the soup at a gentle simmer, drizzle it in thinly while pushing gently with a ladle. Add it in several portions, and wait until the last portion has turned even and glossy before deciding whether to keep pouring.
6. Stop adding slurry when the soup thinly coats the back of a spoon and the liquid around the shiitake slices flows a little more slowly. Taste, then add more bouillon or a little salt. Sprinkle with green onion, take off the heat as soon as the soup bubbles lightly again, and ladle out right away.

## Key points

- **White lumps with raw starch inside**: dry starch met the heat directly, or the slurry settled and was not stirred up (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Disperse it in cold water first, stir right before pouring, and drizzle it in portions.
- **Thick at first, then thin again**: the potato starch was boiled for a long time after gelatinizing, or stirred hard again and again (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cook everything else first, thicken last, and take it off the heat when the thickness is right.
- **Umami turns clearly salty**: bouillon already contains salt and the table salt adds on top (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add both in portions; the 3 g of salt is a reserve and does not have to all go in.
