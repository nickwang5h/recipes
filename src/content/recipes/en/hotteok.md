*Hotteok* is a common Korean street sugar pancake: soft yeasted dough encloses brown sugar, cinnamon and walnuts, pressed flat as it fries, with a golden crust and hot syrup with bits of nut inside. Ordinary all-purpose flour is enough; the dough should be soft and sticky, able to enclose the filling without leaving a crack.

The yeast makes gas as the dough rises, and the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) network holds the gas in, giving the pancake its fluffy feel. Once in the pan, the [starch in the flour absorbs water and gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the crust gradually sets. The outside browns fast while the centre heats slowly, so at the end turn to low heat and cover, letting heat reach the sugar filling and letting the sugar dissolve into syrup with the water from the dough. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).

## Steps

1. In a large bowl, stir together the warm water, sugar, yeast, salt and the oil for the dough, add 240 g of flour and mix until there is no dry flour and you have an even, sticky, soft dough. You can gather it with a spatula, but it will still stick to the bowl; it does not need to be kneaded dry.
2. Cover the bowl and let it ferment at room temperature for about 60 minutes, until about double in volume with visible bubbles. If the room is cool and it has not risen, check every 10 minutes more; keep the bowl covered so a skin does not form on the surface.
3. Fold and press with a spatula to deflate the big bubbles, cover again and let it rise another 10–20 minutes, until the dough puffs up again with fine bubbles inside. While waiting, mix the brown sugar, cinnamon and chopped walnuts and divide into 8 portions.
4. Dust the board with some of the flour for preventing sticking, turn the dough out, knead lightly to deflate until it gathers into a soft mass, and divide into 8 portions. With a thin coat of flour on your fingers, flatten each portion to the size of your palm, put a portion of sugar filling in the centre, and pinch the dough around it together to seal. Use the dusting flour in portions, and do not let the seal pick up dry flour or sugar grains.
5. Preheat a nonstick pan over medium heat for about 1 minute, until hot, and add 0.5 tbsp of the frying oil, spread in a thin layer. Put in 2 dough balls per batch, seam side down, and fry for about 30 seconds, until the underside is pale golden and they lift out whole. Flip, and slowly press each with a wide spatula into a round about 12 cm across, leaving a gap between the two.
6. Keep frying over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the second side is golden. Flip again, turn to the lowest heat, cover and fry for 1 minute, until the pancake puffs up and is still springy to the press. Break open one from the first batch to look: it is done only when the centre has no wet, sticky raw dough and the sugar filling is flowing; if there is still raw dough, cover and fry 1 minute more each time.
7. Fry the remaining 3 batches the same way, adding 0.5 tbsp of oil to each batch, pressing flat after about 30 seconds, then 1 minute each for colour and for covering to cook through, until both sides are golden and there is no raw dough in the centre. Let them sit for 2 minutes after leaving the pan, until they no longer give off vigorous steam, and break a small opening before eating, since the syrup is still very hot.

## Key points

- **Dough is dry and stiff, cracks when filled**: too much dusting flour was kneaded in and the ratio of water to flour changed. Keep the dough soft and sticky; dusting flour is only to help with handling. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Crust dark brown, centre still raw dough**: the heat was too high or the pancake pressed too thick, so the surface cooked first. Press to an even thickness, and after both sides have colour, cover over low heat. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Sugar leaks from the seal, bottom of the pan scorched and bitter**: the seal picked up dry flour or filling, so the dough did not stick together. Pinch a clean edge tight, and press the pancake with gradually increasing force without squeezing the middle through. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
