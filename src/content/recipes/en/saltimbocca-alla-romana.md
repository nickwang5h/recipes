*Saltimbocca alla romana* is a thin veal cutlet from Rome: sage is sandwiched between the meat and the prosciutto, held with a toothpick and quickly fried in butter. This recipe keeps the early classic flat method, with only half a sage leaf on each piece of meat, so the aroma is clear without overpowering the delicate flavour of the veal.

The thinner and more even the veal, the faster heat reaches the centre (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Take the meat out of the pan as soon as it is cooked, so the muscle proteins do not keep contracting and squeezing out water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The prosciutto especially cannot take long frying, because a thin slice turns hard quickly.

## Steps

1. Divide the veal into 12 small slices, put them between sheets of parchment paper and pound lightly to a thickness of about 4 mm. Trim off the tough sinew at the edges and dry the surface, so the slices are close in thickness. Do not pound so hard that the meat fibres tear.
2. Sprinkle the meat with a little black pepper and a very small amount of salt. Put half a fresh sage leaf on each slice, cover with a small thin slice of prosciutto and pin it with a toothpick through the middle, keeping the slice flat.
3. Put part of the butter in a wide, flat-bottomed pan and heat over medium until the foam begins to subside and the butter is still pale yellow. Add as many slices as will lie flat in a batch, meat side down. The pan should make a gentle, steady sizzle, and the butter must not smoke.
4. Fry for about 2 minutes, until the meat side turns light golden and the edges are cooked, then turn it gently onto the prosciutto side for only ten-odd seconds, so the prosciutto sticks to the meat and warms through. Turn back to the meat side to check doneness: insert a thin probe from the side into one of the thicker slices, and take the meat out promptly when the centre reaches 63°C (145°F).
5. Finish the remaining slices in batches, adding the reserved butter to each batch as needed. If the butter has black, burnt bits, wipe the pan clean before the next batch so the bitterness does not build up. Put the meat on a warm plate and do not stack it in a thick pile, so it does not keep cooking in its own steam.
6. Pull out all the toothpicks, spoon the pan butter that has not yet burnt bitter over the meat and serve right away. Each portion is four small slices, which you can serve with separately prepared vegetables. When you pick up a slice, the meat should be tender and the prosciutto should still bend.

## Key points

- **Edges curl up and some parts are still raw**: the thickness is uneven or the tight sinew at the edge keeps the meat from lying flat on the pan. Pound it evenly, trim the tough edges and fry in a single layer in batches (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Prosciutto is as hard as leather**: the prosciutto side was fried too long and lost a lot of moisture. Let the meat side do most of the heating, and the prosciutto side only touch the pan briefly. Take the veal out as soon as it is cooked (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Sage is bitter and the butter is black**: the heat is too high and the herb and the milk solids in the butter are burning. Use fresh leaves, half a leaf per piece, keep the butter pale yellow and replace burnt butter promptly (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
