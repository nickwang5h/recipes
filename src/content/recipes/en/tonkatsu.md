*Tonkatsu* is the breaded pork cutlet of Japanese *yoshoku* cooking: pork loin coated in flour, egg and bread crumbs, deep-fried in hot oil, cut into strips and served with raw shredded cabbage and a sweet-sour tonkatsu sauce. The pork keeps a thickness of about 1.5 cm, so while the crust fries crisp, the inside still has the springiness and juice of the meat.

The coarse flakes and air gaps of panko make a loose coating. The oil passes heat to the crumbs, water evaporates to form the crisp crust, and heat then moves on toward the centre of the meat (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). This dish needs two conditions met together: a golden, dry crust and a fully cooked centre. Judging by colour alone easily misses the doneness of thick meat.

## Steps

1. Cut the cabbage into fine shreds, soak briefly in cold water, then lift out and drain thoroughly. Keep the board and knife for the shreds separate from those used for raw pork, and pile the shreds loosely on the plate just before serving.
2. Cut a few shallow slits in the border between fat and lean on the edge of each cutlet, to sever the sinew that shrinks easily. Pat the thick parts lightly so both cutlets are the same thickness, and sprinkle thinly with salt and black pepper.
3. Put the flour, beaten egg and panko in three separate dishes. Coat each cutlet first in a thin layer of flour and pat off the loose flour, then dip in egg and let the drips fall off, and finally cover it fully with panko, pressing gently until it sticks; cover the edges too.
4. Heat the oil in the deep pot to 175°C (350°F). Add one cutlet at a time so the oil temperature does not drop too much, and fry for about 5–7 minutes, turning once or twice. The crust should turn an even golden gradually, not shift straight to deep brown.
5. Insert a thermometer from the side of the cutlet so the tip reaches the centre of the meat, and take it out only at 71°C (160°F). If the crust is already golden but the centre is short, lower the heat and keep frying, rather than burning the surface chasing the colour. Lift out and drain, blot briefly to take off surface oil, then set on a wire rack to let the steam escape.
6. With a clean knife, cut the cutlet into strips about 2 cm wide and arrange them on the plate in their original shape. Add the drained shredded cabbage, and serve the tonkatsu sauce on the side; to keep the crisp crust longer, dip as you eat.

## Key points

- **Cutlet curls and browns unevenly**: the sinew at the edge shrinks in the heat and lifts the meat away from where it would heat evenly. Slit the border between fat and lean before breading, and do not pound the meat thin on one side and thick on the other. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Coating is soft and greasy**: the oil temperature dropped too far after the food went in, or the pieces were stacked after frying and trapped steam. Fry one cutlet at a time and let the oil recover between batches; spread them out to drain. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Burnt outside, raw in the centre**: the oil was too hot or the cutlet too thick. Choose the heat to suit the thickness and measure the centre with a thermometer; colour only judges the crust. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
