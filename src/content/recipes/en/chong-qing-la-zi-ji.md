Chongqing-style chili chicken buries fried small pieces of chicken in dried chilies. Each bite is firm meat, crisp skin and the numbing, spicy aroma of the peppers. The Geleshan version is known for small bone-in pieces, so the bones stay in and the chicken is chopped small and even. If chopping through bone is not practical at home, bone the legs and cut the skin-on thigh meat to the same size.

The chicken is fried once to cook it, then fried again to drive off the surface water (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The chilies and Sichuan peppercorns are stir-fried separately over low heat and only combined with the chicken at the end. Their fragrance has to come out slowly over gentle heat, and the high temperature of frying chicken would scorch them (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). At the table, pick out the chicken pieces to eat and leave the dried chilies and peppercorns on the plate.

## Steps

1. Blot the chicken dry and mix it with the ginger, green onion, light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, salt and white pepper for marinating. Refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the surface is evenly coated with the marinade. Pick out the ginger and green onion and drain the liquid from the bottom of the bowl.
2. Cut the dried chilies into pieces of about 2 cm, sift out the loose seeds, and soak them in water for 15 minutes. Slice the ginger and garlic, and chop the green onion for stir-frying. Spread out the chili pieces and check that there are no water droplets before they go in the pan.
3. Pour the vegetable oil into the frying pot and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the thermometer reads 160°C (320°F). Fry the chicken in 3 batches, about 3 minutes per batch, until light golden. Insert a probe into the largest piece, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) it is cooked. Lift out and drain for 30 seconds, until the oil no longer runs off in a stream.
4. Heat the oil again for about 2 minutes, until it reaches 180°C (355°F), and return the chicken in batches to refry for 30–60 seconds, until the skin is golden and the edges are crisp. Immediately lift onto a wire rack and spread out, draining for 1 minute, until there are no flowing oil droplets on the surface.
5. Leave 1.5 tbsp of the frying oil in a wok. Over medium-low heat, add the ginger, garlic and Sichuan peppercorns and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the ginger and garlic are fragrant but not yet brown. Add the dried chilies and keep stirring for 1–2 minutes, until the skins are bright red and clearly fragrant. Do not let the chilies sit on the bottom of the pan.
6. Return the chicken to the wok, add the light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and sugar for the final stir-fry, and stir-fry over medium-low heat for 2 minutes, until there is no wet liquid on the bottom and the chicken is coated with the chili oil. Sprinkle on the toasted sesame seeds and chopped green onion and toss for 15 seconds, until the green onion is evenly spread, then plate.

## Key points

- **The chicken smells of pepper but tastes bland**: it was not marinated through, and a short final stir-fry cannot add saltiness to the inside. Marinate for the full 10 minutes, and chop the pieces to the same size and thickness (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Greasy chicken with soft skin**: too much went in at once and the oil temperature dropped too low, or the pieces were piled together after leaving the oil and softened. Fry in batches, and spread the pieces out to drain after the second fry (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **The whole plate tastes scorched and bitter**: the high temperature of frying the chicken was used straight away to fry the chilies. Turn to medium-low before the final stir-fry and add the chicken once you smell the fragrance. Pick out any blackened chili pieces (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
