Lettuce with black beans and canned dace is salty, savoury and good with rice. The leaves are soft but not mushy, and the stalks stay crisp. The black beans in the can already had a salty, savoury, mature flavour from fermentation; a short stir-fry spreads that flavour through the dish (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). The dace is already cooked, so it only needs to be heated through with the lettuce at the end, and the pieces still have some chew.

Lettuce holds a lot of water. Stir-fry it until soft and pour off the liquid it releases, then return it to the pan to pick up the black bean and garlic oil, so the finished dish does not sit in a plate of water. If the water is not drained, the pan is too full or the cooking runs too long, the dish turns from stir-fried to boiled (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The can is already salty, so wait until everything is mixed before deciding how much light soy sauce to add (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).

## Steps

1. Pick off the tough base and any bad leaves from the lettuce. Separate the leaves and wash them clean, especially the grit at the base of the leaves. Drain, then cut into lengths that are easy to stir-fry, shaking apart the thick stalks and thin leaves as much as you can.
2. Open the can of dace with black beans and set the fish, beans and oil aside separately. Break the fish into small pieces, and check for and remove any main bones that are still hard or sharp. Peel and mince the garlic.
3. Heat the pan and add 15 ml of vegetable oil. Add the lettuce and stir-fry over high heat. If there is too much lettuce, cook it in batches, until the leaves have just wilted, the stalks are still crisp and liquid starts to seep out at the bottom of the pan.
4. Transfer the lettuce to a colander and drain off the liquid it released. Do not leave it in the hot pan to wait for the liquid to boil away, or the leaves will keep going limp.
5. Reheat the pan. Take enough oil from the can to cover the bottom of the pan in a thin layer, turn the heat to low and add the minced garlic. When you smell the garlic, add the black beans and turn them gently, so the beans and oil spread evenly.
6. Add the fish pieces and turn gently until the fish is heated through and coated in black bean and garlic oil. Push and fold with the spatula, so you do not crush the fish into a fine paste.
7. Return the lettuce to the pan, sprinkle in the sugar and toss quickly to combine. First taste a bite with fish and black beans in it, and add light soy sauce in stages only if it is clearly bland. Stop when the saltiness is right, and add no extra salt.
8. Toss until the lettuce is evenly coated and the whole pan is hot, then plate. If there is only a little sauce at the bottom of the pan, serve it with the lettuce; do not cook the lettuce until it is completely shrivelled.

## Key points

- **The lettuce sits in a plate of water**: the washing water was not drained, or the liquid from the first stir-fry was poured back into the pan (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain the leaves first, drain the liquid again after the first stir-fry, and keep the final combining time short.
- **One bite is very salty with black beans and another bite of lettuce is bland**: the fermented beans were not mixed in (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Spread the beans in the garlic oil first, then add the fish and lettuce; when tasting, take a little fish, beans and lettuce together.
- **Salty and greasy overall**: the can already holds a lot of salt and oil, and you used all of the light soy sauce and all of the oil in the can (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Take only enough oil to fry the garlic, and add light soy sauce after tasting; if it is too salty, dilute it with stir-fried leafy greens cooked without salt.
