*Pad kee mao* (ผัดขี้เมา) is a stir-fried rice noodle dish with the aroma of chili, garlic and holy basil, usually made with shrimp, pork or chicken. The fresh noodles soak up the salty, savoury sauce, the edges pick up a few charred spots, and the chili and garlic smell strongly. The chili-garlic paste is fried in the oil first and the holy basil goes in last so its fresh fragrance is kept (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). This version uses shrimp, gai lan and carrot.

The key is that the noodles must touch the surface of the pan. Cooking one portion at a time lets the pan recover its heat quickly, and the noodles take up the sauce fast (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Fresh rice noodles are already cooked, so they only need to soften, take on the sauce and heat through. Cold noodles turn hard, so warm them before separating and they are less likely to break. Holy basil has a very distinct peppery aroma, and another herb will taste different.

## Steps

1. Divide the refrigerated noodles into small bundles and microwave at about 70% power for 2–3 minutes, turning every 1 minute, until the sheets are warm and pliable and can be peeled apart gently. Then divide them into two portions.
2. Pound the Thai chilies first, then add the garlic and red bell pepper and pound to a coarse paste, keeping small pieces. Mix the oyster sauce, light soy sauce, Golden Mountain sauce and fish sauce. Divide the noodles, shrimp, chili-garlic paste, vegetables, basil, sauce and sugar into two portions each and set them beside the stove.
3. Preheat a heavy wok over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is hot but not smoking continuously. Add 2 tbsp of the oil for the first batch and half the shrimp. Spread them out and sear for about 1 minute, flip and cook for about 1 minute more, until the shrimp are opaque and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Remove them.
4. Take the wok off the heat and add half the chili-garlic paste, then cook over medium heat for about 30 seconds, until the small bits of garlic are just starting to turn golden and the chili aroma is strong. Add half the gai lan stems and carrot, turn the heat to high and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the surface is glossy and they are still crisp.
5. Add one portion of noodles, half the sauce and 1.25 tsp sugar. Toss gently for about 1 minute, until the noodles are evenly coloured and no sauce pools on the bottom. Return this portion of shrimp, spread everything out and leave it for about 15 seconds, until a few charred spots show on the bottom. Flip and leave for another 15 seconds.
6. Add half the gai lan leaves and the holy basil, turn off the heat, and toss in the heat of the noodles for about 15 seconds, until the leaves are just wilted and the basil aroma rises. Plate right away. Cook the second portion in the same order and for the same times.

## Key points

- **The noodles break into pieces and also stick together in a lump**: they were pulled apart while cold and stiff, or pushed constantly with a spatula once in the pan. Warm them to soften first, separate them and stir-fry in portions. Refrigerated noodle sheets harden because the starch has [retrograded](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Sauce pools at the bottom and the noodles taste only of sauce**: the pan was too full and the steam could not escape. Cook one portion at a time, cook the shrimp first and remove them, and leave the surface of the pan to the noodles. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Bitter garlic and basil with no aroma**: the chili-garlic paste burned in a pan that was too hot, and the basil went in too early. Add the paste off the heat, then fry briefly over medium heat, and add the basil last. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
