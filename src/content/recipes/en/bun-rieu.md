*Bún riêu* is a traditional rice field crab noodle soup from northern Vietnam: soft crab clumps float in a sour, savoury tomato broth, over thin round rice noodles. The crab is pounded and mixed with water, and as soon as the strained crab water is heated, its proteins join together and gather into the clumps that float on top of the soup (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Secure the crab clumps first, then deal with the tomato broth, and the finished bowl has both a clear broth and tender crab.

Tomatoes and Vietnamese fermented rice vinegar, giấm bỗng, give the broth a clean [sourness](/principles/acid/), and a little fermented shrimp sauce, mắm tôm, brings out the crab's savour (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). This pot is mainly about the crab curds; prepare the noodles, shredded lettuce and chili separately and assemble the bowls just before eating.

## Steps

1. Prepare the crabs. Wash them, remove the belly flap and lift off the shell, and scrape the roe out of the shell and set it aside. Remove the gills from the body, add 1/2 tsp of the salt, and pound in a mortar to a fine slurry, until no whole leg segments can be seen. Thaw frozen whole crabs in the refrigerator for about 12 hours beforehand, until there are no hard lumps of ice in the centre, before working with them.
2. Extract the crab water. Mix the water into the crab slurry in three portions; each time, work it loose and let it stand for 30 seconds, until the coarse shell settles, then slowly pour off the upper crab water. Finally check the crab water and pick out any shell bits; the fine crab meat stays in the liquid, and the coarse shell residue at the bottom is thrown away.
3. Set the crab clumps. Heat the crab water over medium-low heat for 10–15 minutes, gently pushing along the bottom with a spoon at first so it does not stick; once you see the fine crab meat start to gather, stop stirring and turn to low heat. When the clumps float up, the broth around them has cleared, and the centre of a clump reaches 74°C (165°F), lift them gently into a bowl, and pour the liquid that drains out back into the pot.
4. Fry the crab roe. Take half the lard and half the shallot and stir-fry over medium-low heat for 2 minutes, until the shallot is translucent and fragrant; add the crab roe and 1/2 tbsp of the fish sauce, and stir-fry for 2 minutes, until the roe turns orange-yellow and the fat looks glossy. Scoop it out and lay it over the crab clumps.
5. Fry the tomatoes. Chop two of the tomatoes and cut one into wedges. Stir-fry the remaining lard and shallot for 2 minutes, until you smell the shallot; add the chopped tomato and stir-fry for 5–7 minutes, until soft and juicy and the oil turns red. Pour in the crab broth, add the tomato wedges, the rest of the fish sauce, the salt and the shrimp sauce, and cook over low heat for 5 minutes, until the edges of the wedges are soft but still hold together. Turn off the heat and stir in the fermented rice vinegar.
6. Cook the noodles. Cook them in a separate pot of boiling water for 4–6 minutes, until there is no hard core in the centre, then rinse in cold water and drain; if the thickness varies, cook 1 minute longer at a time and check. Dip them in hot water for 30 seconds before filling the bowls, until the noodles are hot through, then divide among four bowls.
7. Assemble the bowls. Put the crab clumps on the noodles, pour the hot crab broth and tomatoes along the edge of the bowl, and sprinkle with green onion rings. Put the shredded lettuce, lime and chili on the table to add as you eat; do not use the ladle to crush the crab clumps.

## Key points

- **The crab meat breaks into fine crumbs**: the clumps were stirred while still forming, or the soup boiled hard and broke them up (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Stop as soon as the meat gathers, and lift out the clumps after they set on low heat.
- **You bite into grit or shell**: the coarse shell in the crab slurry did not settle out (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Heating wraps the shell bits into the clumps, so check for them and remove them before the crab sets.
- **The sourness covers the crab flavour**: too much fermented rice vinegar or rice vinegar was added (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Add half first and taste before adding the rest; the acidity differs between bottles.
