*Fagioli all'uccelletto* is a home-style Tuscan white bean dish. Cooked cannellini beans are coated in tomato sauce, with sage and garlic as the base in olive oil; the beans keep their shape and are creamy inside. It can be a side dish, or a simple meal with bread. Dried beans have to be soaked ahead, so start the day before.

The order of this dish comes from [acid](/principles/acid/): the acidity of tomato keeps beans from softening, so the beans are cooked soft in plain water first and the tomato goes in afterward. The sage goes into the oil first so its aroma spreads through the pot (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The final stewing is only for flavour and reduction.

## Steps

1. Pick out any broken beans, wash the rest and cover them with plenty of cold water. Soak in the refrigerator for 12 hours. When the beans have swelled, pour off the soaking water and rinse again, then transfer to a pot and add the water from the ingredient list.
2. Bring to a boil over high heat until the whole pot is rolling and keep it there for at least 10 minutes, then turn to low heat and cook until completely soft, which takes about 50–80 minutes more. The water should always cover the beans; if it drops too far, add hot water. Taste one: the beans are done only when there is no hard core in the centre and a light press makes a paste while the skin stays whole.
3. Keep 250 ml of the bean cooking water and lift the beans out with a slotted spoon. In a wide, heavy-bottomed pot, put the olive oil, garlic and sage and heat over low heat until the garlic is pale golden and the sage gives off its aroma, then take out the garlic and leaves and keep the flavoured oil.
4. Pour the crushed tomatoes with their juice into the flavoured oil and cook over low heat for about 15 minutes, until the pulp breaks down and the sauce thickens. Add the beans and the reserved cooking water and gently turn from the bottom with a spoon, so every bean is coated in tomato.
5. Keep cooking over low heat for about 10 minutes, shaking the pot gently from time to time. The sauce should cling to the beans and still flow slowly when you lift a spoonful. Taste and add salt and black pepper, then serve hot in shallow bowls.

## Key points

- **Stewed for a long time and the beans still have a hard core**: tomato went in before the beans were fully soft. Next time cook them in plain water until they crush with a press; old beans may take longer, so do not go by time alone. See [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/).
- **The skins fall apart and the pot looks like bean mash**: soft beans are broken up by vigorous boiling or frequent stirring. Lower the heat after the full boil and fold gently or shake the pot when combining. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The sage tastes bitter and the garlic smells burnt**: the oil was too hot and the aromatics burned. Take the garlic out on low heat when it is pale golden, and add the tomato in time to bring the temperature down. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Dried beans only warm-cooked in a slow cooker**: slow cooking at low temperature does not replace the boiling needed to remove lectins. After soaking, change the water and first keep a hard rolling boil for at least 10 minutes, then finish the rest of the cooking. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
