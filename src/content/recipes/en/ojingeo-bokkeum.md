Ojingeo bokkeum is a Korean home dish of raw squid and vegetables stir-fried in a sweet and spicy sauce. Gochujang gives a thick sauce base, chili flakes add heat, and the onion, carrot and zucchini keep a little crunch. The sauce clings to the squid, and a little liquid can remain at the bottom of the plate, which suits eating with rice.

Squid turns white and curls quickly in the pan, which shows its proteins changing with heat. If you keep stir-frying, the squid tightens and squeezes out water, and turns tough (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook the vegetables until slightly soft first, then leave room for the squid to touch the hot pan directly, so that the two finish at nearly the same time. A wide pan with nothing stacked works better than simply extending the reducing time (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Remove the squid's innards, the clear quill and the hard beak in the centre of the tentacles. Rinse, drain and pat dry. Cut the bodies into rings about 2 cm wide and the tentacles into pieces about 5 cm long. The meat shrinks, so don't cut the pieces too small. If you use pre-cleaned bodies, still check inside for leftover quill.
2. Stir the gochujang, chili flakes, soy sauce, sugar, garlic, ginger, sesame seeds and black pepper together, and toss with the squid. Refrigerate for about 10 minutes, until each piece is evenly coated in red sauce, and prepare the vegetables while it marinates.
3. Slice the onion. Cut the carrot and zucchini into thin slices about 5 cm long, and cut the green onion into pieces of the same length. Cut the carrot a little thinner than the zucchini so the firmer vegetable also softens in a short stir-fry (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
4. Preheat a wide frying pan over high heat for about 1 minute, until hot, then add the vegetable oil. It should spread quickly without smoking. Add the carrot first, then the onion, zucchini and green onion, and stir-fry quickly for about 2 minutes, until the edges of the onion turn translucent and the vegetables have just begun to soften but still hold their shape.
5. Push the vegetables to the sides of the pan and pour the squid in the middle with all the sauce from the bowl. Stir-fry over high heat for about 2 minutes, until the squid rings tighten, the tentacles curl and the flesh goes from translucent to opaque. Turn up any sauce that sticks to the bottom of the pan, and don't let the sugary sauce sit in one place and burn.
6. Toss the vegetables and squid together and stir-fry for about 1 more minute, until the sauce coats both evenly and the thick parts of the squid are opaque too. Drizzle with the toasted sesame oil, turn off the heat and plate. A little liquid at the bottom of the plate is fine; don't keep stir-frying to reduce it.

## Key points

- **The squid is hard and rubbery**: it stayed in the pan to reduce the sauce after it was cooked, and the proteins kept contracting. Move to the finish once all of it has turned white, and eat it after plating without keeping it warm in the hot pan (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **The pan turns into a pool of soup**: the squid carries water, or the pan is small and the food piled thick. Dry the main ingredient and use a wide pan; for a double batch, cook in two pans (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The carrot is hard and the squid is overcooked**: the vegetables are cut too thick or went in together. Slice the carrot thin and stir-fry it first, and add the squid last (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
