Cantonese poached chicken has crisp skin and smooth meat, and it is very lightly seasoned: the first taste is the chicken and its skin, then the salty fragrance of ginger and green onion picks it up. The method is poaching: once the chicken is cooked, you chop it into pieces with the skin on, serve the ginger and green onion sauce in a separate dish for dipping, and this is the Cantonese way of letting the chicken keep its own flavour.

The breast and thigh of a whole chicken differ in thickness; the outer layer cooks first, and the heat still has to travel all the way to the part next to the bone. Letting hot water fill the cavity and keeping a gentle simmer lets all parts heat more evenly; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Put the chicken into an ice bath as soon as it is cooked, so the residual heat does not keep cooking the meat tough, and the skin is springier. Whether the meat is tender depends on whether [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) has gone too far.

## Steps

1. Take out the bag of innards from the cavity, trim off excess fat and blot the surface dry with paper towels. Choose a narrow, deep pot that fits the whole chicken, pour in 4000 ml of water and 1 tsp of salt, and heat over high heat for about 15–20 minutes, until the surface of the water is boiling all over; heat the separate 1000 ml of water in another pot for about 5 minutes, until boiling.
2. Support the chicken with long tongs and a skimmer, and slowly lower it into the water, then lift it out and let the water drain from the cavity; repeat 3 times, about 1 minute in all, until large bubbles no longer come steadily from the cavity. Put the whole chicken in the pot with the water covering the back; if there is not enough, add 250 ml at a time from the reserve boiling water until it is fully submerged.
3. Cover and cook over medium-high heat for about 5 minutes, until the water starts to move at a boil again. Turn to low heat and poach for 35–40 minutes, keeping small bubbles at the edge of the pot and the surface moving gently. Insert a probe into the thickest part of the thigh joint and the breast, avoiding bone, and take the chicken out only when the centre of the whole chicken reaches 82°C (180°F); if it has not, poach another 5 minutes and measure again.
4. While the chicken cooks, make the ginger and green onion sauce. Put 75 ml of oil in a pot and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the surface of the oil shows slight ripples. Add the ginger and stir-fry for 15 seconds, until the ginger is clearly fragrant; add the green onion white and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the mince has just softened and is still pale. Transfer to a bowl and mix in the toasted sesame oil and the dip salt.
5. Put 2000 ml of cold water and 500 g of ice cubes in a large basin. When the chicken is cooked, lift it out and drain over the pot for 30 seconds, until water no longer pours from the cavity, then put it straight into the ice bath for 5 minutes. Turn it over once midway so every side of the skin is soaked in cold water, until the skin has cooled and is still springy.
6. Lift out the chicken and drain for 2 minutes, until it no longer drips. First separate the wings and legs along the joints, then take the breast off with the skin, and chop each part with a cleaver into pieces about 2–3 cm wide. If you do not have a cleaver, cut apart along the joints, remove the bones and slice, and plate the skin and meat together with the ginger and green onion sauce.

## Key points

- **The breast is dry and the skin splits**: the water kept boiling hard and the outer layer stayed overheated, so [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) contracted the meat and squeezed out its water. Turn to low heat as soon as the water returns to a boil, and put the chicken in the ice bath right after it is cooked.
- **The part next to the bone is undercooked**: air was left in the cavity, or you went by skin colour and ignored [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) at the centre. Drain the air out of the cavity when dipping, and in the end measure the doneness of the whole chicken with a probe, rather than judging by the colour near the bone or the juices.
- **The ginger and green onion sauce is bitter**: fine mince burns quickly in hot oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the ginger first and the green onion after, and take it out when it smells fragrant and the colour is still pale.
