*Mỡ hành* is a Vietnamese scallion oil eaten together with the green onion pieces. Chopped green onions go into hot oil for a short time and come off the heat the moment they wilt. The result has bright green pieces in clear oil, and it is spooned over [Cơm Tấm Sườn Nướng](/recipes/com-tam-suon-nuong/), and often over grilled eggplant, grilled corn and thin Vietnamese rice noodles.

Hot oil heats the cut green onion quickly and carries its aroma into the fat (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The time is short, so the green onion's sharpness mellows while soft pieces remain. A thick pan keeps heating after it leaves the burner (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)), so move the pieces to a bowl as soon as you see them slump, and the colour and texture stop changing.

## Steps

1. Wash the green onions. Trim off the roots and any yellowed outer leaves, rinse out the grit between the white layers, drain for 3 minutes, then wipe the surface dry with paper towels. Dry the surface well or it will spatter in the pan; the water inside the green onion will make the oil foam, and that is fine.
2. Chop the green onions. Cut the white and green parts together into short pieces about 5 mm long, put them in a small bowl and toss with 0.25 tsp of fine salt. Halve the thicker white parts lengthwise before cutting, so the pieces in one batch are a similar size. Use medium-thick, bright green, upright green onions and cut off any tough leaf tips.
3. Heat the oil. Put 4 tbsp of oil in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 1–2 minutes, until the oil flows quickly and a small piece of green onion dropped in sizzles at once with fine bubbles around it. The oil should stay clear. If it keeps smoking, move it off the heat for about 1 minute until the smoke clears before adding the green onion.
4. Wilt the green onions. Tip the rest of the green onion and the salt into the pot and stir for about 30–60 seconds so every piece touches the oil. When the pieces go from upright to soft and draw together, with fine bubbles in the pot and a green onion smell, turn off the heat at once. The white parts should be slightly translucent and the green parts still bright green, with no brown at the edges.
5. Transfer and use. Pour the green onion and oil together into a small heatproof bowl away from the hot pot and stir for about 15 seconds, until the pieces are spread evenly through the oil. Stir again before spooning it on, so each serving gets both green onion and oil, rather than only oil from the top. Use the finished oil the same day, over freshly cooked rice and grilled dishes.

## Key points

- **Green parts turn brown and the oil tastes scorched**: the oil was too hot or heated too long, and once the water in the green onion is gone it starts to burn (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Test the oil with a small piece, then watch how the whole batch slumps after it goes in, and take it off the heat in time.
- **The green onion is still firm in the bowl and tastes only sharply raw**: the oil was not hot enough, or the white parts were cut too thick (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut the pieces evenly, add them when the oil gives off fine bubbles, and stir until the white parts turn slightly translucent.
- **Bright green when the heat goes off, dull once served**: the heat left in the thick pan keeps cooking the pieces (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Pour them into a heatproof bowl right after turning off the heat, so they leave the bottom of the pan.
