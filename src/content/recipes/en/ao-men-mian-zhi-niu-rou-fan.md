The meat is loose, the sauce is a thin layer clinging to the rice, and an egg with a yolk you can break sits on top: this is the "wet" version of Macanese minced meat rice. The traditional combination is minced meat, onion, fried potatoes and egg; here it uses ground beef, ketchup and a starch-thickened sauce, with more sauce. The starch in the meat juices [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat and thickens the liquid, thick enough to cling to the rice but still flowing.

The ground beef has to be broken up before the sauce thickens to stay as loose small crumbs; a sauce that is too thick will glue the meat into lumps, so the starch slurry goes in a little at a time and you do not have to use all of it. The white and yolk of the fried egg set through [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and a broken yolk mixed into the rice makes it silkier.

## Steps

1. Mix the ground beef with 5 ml of light soy sauce, the sugar and 2 g of black pepper, and marinate in the refrigerator for about 10 minutes. Finely dice the onion; the green onion is optional and kept separate.
2. Stir the remaining light soy sauce, the dark soy sauce, ketchup, Shaoxing wine and the remaining black pepper into a sauce. In another bowl, stir the starch into 100 ml of cold water until no lumps are visible.
3. Add the frying oil to a small frying pan, crack in the egg and fry over low heat until the white sets, then flip and fry until the yolk is set too. Remove and set aside.
4. In a wok, melt the butter over medium-low heat, add the diced onion and stir-fry until translucent with slightly yellow edges and a sweet smell. If the butter gives off heavy smoke, take the pan off the heat to cool it down.
5. Add the ground beef, break up large clumps with the spatula and stir-fry with the onion until it separates into fine crumbs. Once the meat changes colour, do not pour in the sauce yet; cook it through first: the centre must reach 71°C (160°F), and browning alone does not count.
6. Pour in the mixed sauce and stir-fry until the raw harshness of the wine is gone. Stir the starch slurry again, pour in half, and keep stirring as it cooks, until the sauce bubbles again and turns glossy, then decide whether to add more.
7. When the sauce thinly coats the meat crumbs and, after the spatula has drawn a line, slowly flows back into the gap, stop adding slurry and discard the rest. If the sauce is too thick, add a little hot water. Taste last; no more salt is needed.
8. Pour the beef and sauce over hot rice, top with the fried egg, sprinkle with green onion, and mix and eat right away.

## Key points

- **The sauce turns to jelly and won't mix into the rice**: too much starch or too little water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Thicken in portions to a thin sauce, and if it is too thick, add water and bring it back to a boil.
- **The meat is tough and in large lumps**: the ground beef was not broken up in time and some of it was heated too long (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Break up the clumps as soon as it goes into the pan, and add the sauce once it is cooked.
- **The saltiness overpowers the meat flavour**: the marinade, soy sauce and ketchup are all salty, and reducing concentrates the salt further (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Keep the sauce wet and lightly thickened; do not cook it down until only meat fat is left.
