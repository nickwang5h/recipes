Kalguksu (*kalguksu*) rolls a wheat dough thin, folds it, cuts it into strips with a knife and cooks them in broth. Common broths are chicken, dried anchovy and clam; below uses chicken broth, with torn chicken and zucchini. The noodles vary a little in thickness and have a soft chew in the mouth.

Kneading builds the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) into a continuous network, and resting spreads the water evenly and relaxes the tension, so the sheet rolls out easily. The loose flour used for cutting [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when it gets into the broth and thickens it; cooking the noodles for half their time in plain water first, then moving them into the chicken broth, keeps the broth fluid.

## Steps

1. Mix 500 g of flour, the salt, the oil and 180 ml of water into crumbs, then knead in the remaining 120 ml of water in stages, until there is no dry flour and the dough is on the stiff side but holds together. Knead for about 5 minutes, until the surface is fairly smooth, then cover tightly and rest for 10 minutes, until a pressed dent no longer springs back strongly right away.
2. Put the chicken pieces, the onion for the broth, whole garlic cloves, ginger and green onion whites in a pot and add 3840 ml of water. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat in about 10–15 minutes, until foam rises, and skim off the foam; cover, turn to low heat and cook for about 60 minutes, until the meat pulls easily away from the bone and the thickest part of each piece reaches 74°C (165°F).
3. Toss the zucchini strips with a little salt and leave for 5–10 minutes, until water collects at the bottom of the bowl and the strips are soft, then squeeze out the water. Take the chicken out and cool it for about 10 minutes, until you can hold it steadily, then bone it and tear it into shreds, and toss with the minced garlic, toasted sesame oil, salt and pepper. Strain the broth back into the pot and remove the scum and large pieces of floating fat.
4. Knead the dough for another 2–3 minutes, until the surface is smooth, and divide it into two. Dust the counter thinly with flour and roll out each piece into a sheet about 1–2 mm thick; the middle and the four edges should feel equally thin, and flip it over from time to time to keep it from sticking.
5. Dust both sides of the sheet thinly with flour, fold loosely into 3–4 layers, and cut into strips about 6 mm wide. Unfold them straight away, shake off the extra loose flour, and arrange them in loose small bundles; the noodles should lift out one strand at a time, with no thick clumps stuck together at the folds.
6. Add the soup soy sauce and the sliced onion to the chicken broth and cook over medium heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the broth is boiling again and the onion begins to turn translucent. In another pot, boil water over high heat for about 5–10 minutes, until it is at a rolling boil, then add the noodles, separate them and cook for 2–3 minutes, until they float and the surface has softened but the centre is not fully cooked.
7. Transfer the noodles into the boiling chicken broth, add the zucchini, and cook another 2–3 minutes, until the centre has no raw flour taste, the zucchini is soft, and the broth can still be ladled smoothly. Taste the broth and add salt and pepper, divide among bowls, top with the shredded chicken and the green onion greens, and eat right away.

## Key points

- **The dough shrinks back as soon as you roll it**: the gluten tension hasn't relaxed. Cover and rest another 10 minutes, until the sheet unfolds easily, and don't pull it hard. See [Gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Noodles stick together and cook into thick clumps**: there wasn't enough dusting flour before folding, or the strips were not unfolded right after cutting. Dust thinly, fold loosely, and separate the cut strips one by one. See [Gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Broth as thick as batter**: too much loose flour, and all of it gelatinized in the broth. Shake off the loose flour and cook in separate pots as in the steps; a clear broth must first have the chicken's savoury flavour cooked out. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and [Umami](/principles/umami/).
