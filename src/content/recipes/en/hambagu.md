*Hambagu* is the hamburger steak of Japanese home-style yoshoku, usually served with rice. The beef supplies meaty flavour, the pork brings fat and a soft texture, and cooked onion, milk and breadcrumbs keep the patty moist when cut open. Mix the meat until it turns sticky first: salt helps some of the muscle proteins dissolve out, so the ground meat binds into one mass (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).

Frying and braising each have a job. The surface loses water and browns first, picking up the aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/); after a little wine goes in, the pan is covered so heat keeps travelling to the centre. The brown juices left on the pan bottom are then reduced with ketchup and tonkatsu sauce and spooned over the patties.

## Steps

1. Put 1 tbsp of the oil and the onion in a pan and cook over medium heat for about 6–8 minutes, until the onion is translucent and soft. Spread it in a large bowl to cool for about 15 minutes, until it no longer feels warm; don't mix hot onion straight into cold ground meat.
2. Mix the breadcrumbs with the milk and leave for 2 minutes, until the crumbs have soaked up the liquid. Add the cooled onion, both ground meats, the egg, nutmeg, salt and black pepper, and mix for about 2–3 minutes, until the mixture is sticky and holds together in a ball.
3. Divide into 4 portions. Toss each lightly between your hands 5 times, then press into an oval patty about 1.5 cm thick and neaten the edges. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes, until the patties are cold and hold their shape; before they go in the pan, press a shallow dimple in the centre.
4. Add the remaining 1 tbsp oil to a frying pan and preheat over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the oil spreads smoothly. Arrange the patties with space between them; fry 3 minutes first, until the bottom is browned and golden and can be lifted whole, then flip and fry 3 minutes more, until the other side has colour too.
5. Pour the 45 ml of red wine for braising down the side of the pan, turn to low heat, cover and cook 5–7 minutes, until the centre of the patties is cooked through. Insert a thermometer from the side into the thickest part, and take them out only at 71°C (160°F); if not there yet, cook 1 more minute at a time.
6. In the same pan, add the butter, ketchup, tonkatsu sauce, the remaining red wine and the water, and stir over medium heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the brown bits on the pan bottom have dissolved into the sauce and a spatula drawn through leaves a brief trail. Spoon over the patties and serve hot.

## Key points

- **Patty cracks as soon as it's flipped**: the mixture wasn't mixed until sticky, the edges have cracks, or it was moved before the bottom set. Mix until it holds together, press out large air gaps when shaping, and fry a whole bottom before flipping. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Burnt outside, uncooked centre**: the patty is too thick or the heat was high throughout. Make the thickness even, turn to low heat and cover once it has coloured; go by the centre temperature, not the colour of the juices. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Sauce is scorched and bitter**: there are already black bits on the pan bottom, or the sauce was reduced too dry after going in. Brown juices can melt into the sauce, but black burnt bits should be wiped out first; take it off the heat once the sauce can cling to the patty. See [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
