The fragrance of sesame oil tossed noodles comes from roasted sesame oil. When the noodles have just been cooked and are still warm, the oil mixes in easily and the aroma is easy to smell. The sesame oil is added last, in the bowl, so it is heated once less and loses less aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Instant noodles are used here, dark soy sauce gives the colour, and pepper only supports the flavour.

Cook the noodles until the cake has loosened and the centre is soft but still springy. Noodles whose [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) is already complete turn softer and stickier if they stay in hot water. So have the seasoning ready, lift the noodles out and toss them while hot. A film of moisture on the strands lets the soy sauce spread evenly. Instant noodles are often salted already and the dressing adds soy sauce (see [salt](/principles/salt/)), so cook them in plain water.

## Steps

1. Bring the noodle water to a boil. Take out the noodle cake and check the cooking time and serving size on the package; set the seasoning packet aside and do not pour it into the pot. Put the sesame oil, dark soy sauce and a little white pepper into the serving bowl first.
2. Add the noodle cake and cook for about 3 minutes, or follow the package time for that product. Do not force the cake apart as soon as it goes in. Wait until the edges loosen, then nudge it gently with chopsticks so water gets inside.
3. Taste one strand. When the centre is soft with no hard core and the bite still has a little spring, turn off the heat. If the package suggests a shorter time, taste early instead of waiting until the noodles swell and turn mushy.
4. Scoop out a little of the noodle water and keep it. Drain the noodles until they no longer run in streams and tip them straight into the seasoning bowl. Lift and drop them with chopsticks, tossing repeatedly, until the noodles are an even light brown from top to bottom.
5. If soy sauce sticks in patches and the noodles will not separate, add a little of the reserved noodle water, mixing as you add, until the noodles are moist and no liquid pools at the bottom of the bowl. Do not pour in a lot of water at once.
6. Taste, then decide whether to add the optional light soy sauce and more pepper. Eat right away, once the bowl has no separate pool of oil. If the noodles cool and tighten, loosen them with a little warm water first, then see whether more seasoning is needed.

## Key points

- **Pepper's heat covers the sesame oil**: finely ground pepper releases its flavour fast and is hard to take back once too much goes in. Sprinkle in a little at a time, as in [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/), mix and taste; pepper should only support the sesame aroma.
- **Noodles too salty**: both the noodle cake and the soy sauce carry salt, and salting the cooking water on top stacks it. Count the seasoning already there (see [salt](/principles/salt/)), and add the optional light soy sauce last.
- **Noodles clump and soy sauce coats unevenly**: the surface dried too fast, or the noodles were overcooked and left standing. Judge the end point of [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and toss as soon as you lift them out. If they clump, a little warm noodle water loosens them more easily than adding more oil.
