This lychee pork pairs pineapple with fried pork as a home-style variation. The pork pieces are coated in a bright red sweet and sour sauce, crisp outside and still juicy inside when they come off the heat. Frying twice has two purposes: the first fry cooks the pork, and the second fry dries the crust further so it turns crisp; see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).

The sauce thickens through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). As soon as the pork meets the sauce its crust starts to soak up liquid, so cook the sauce first and let the pork go back in the pan only briefly. The pineapple brings in some juice, so coat evenly and take it off the heat.

## Steps

1. Cut the lean pork into pieces 2–3 cm across and wipe off any surface moisture. Cut the pineapple into pieces, drain off the juice that runs out, and mince the ginger fine.
2. Add the egg, 50 g starch, 3 ml light soy sauce and the optional 2 g bouillon powder to the pork, and mix until every piece has a thick coat of batter. Add 5 ml oil and mix again. The batter should cling to the pork, with no runny egg at the bottom of the bowl.
3. Mix the ketchup, sugar, vinegar, the remaining light soy sauce, minced ginger and the optional 3 g bouillon powder with 200 ml water. Take a little of the cold sauce and stir 10 g starch into it to make a starch slurry, and keep it aside.
4. In a deep pot, heat the frying oil to about 170°C (340°F), measured with an oil thermometer. Add the pork pieces one at a time and fry in batches so they don't stick together. Once the outside has set, turn about every 30 seconds until pale golden. Lift out when the centre of the largest piece reads 71°C (160°F), and drain.
5. Let the pork steam off for about 1 minute while the oil rises to about 190°C (375°F), then fry again briefly until the crust is crisper and darker, and lift out. Spread the pieces to drain; don't pile them in a bowl to steam.
6. In another pot, bring the sweet and sour sauce to a boil, stir the starch slurry and pour it in a little at a time, pushing the sauce as you add it. After about 30 seconds, when the sauce looks glossy and thinly coats the back of a spoon, stop adding slurry.
7. Add the pork and pineapple and toss quickly until the surface is evenly coated, plate at once and scatter with toasted sesame seeds.

## Key points

- **Thick, gummy crust that tastes floury**: the batter is too thick, or the crust did not dry out enough (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Mix the egg and starch together first, and don't keep adding starch by feel.
- **Burnt outside and raw inside**: the pieces are too large or the oil too hot (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Measure the centre of the largest piece.
- **Soft soon after tossing with the sauce**: the sauce is too thin, or it was tossed too long (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Reduce the thin glaze first, and return the pork to the pan only once to coat it.
