*Poulet au vinaigre* is a home-style chicken dish from Lyon: browned chicken pieces finished with a wine vinegar sauce. This is the creamy vinegar sauce common in local bistros. The vinegar goes in first and the cream later, so the sourness is distinct and the sauce still has a rich chicken flavour.

Dry the chicken skin first, so the heat in the pan can drive the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) easily. The vinegar then dissolves the brown bits from the bottom into the sauce. Reducing takes away water and some of the acetic acid, so the sourness softens but does not disappear; the remaining acid is balanced by the stock, cream and meat juices (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Breast and legs cook at different speeds, so judge them separately.

## Steps

1. Pat the chicken pieces dry and season with salt and black pepper. Melt the butter in a pan over medium heat, and when the foam subsides add the chicken in batches, skin side down, leaving gaps at the bottom of the pan.
2. Brown all sides of the chicken until golden and the skin releases from the pan fairly easily, about 8–10 minutes. The butter should stay a brown gold and must not turn black. Take the chicken out; the centre may still be raw.
3. Pour off the excess fat, leaving about 1 tbsp. Add the shallots and stir over low heat until soft and translucent, without browning.
4. Pour in the red wine vinegar, bring to a boil over medium heat, and scrape up the brown bits on the bottom with a wooden spatula. Reduce to about 3–4 tbsp, slightly thickened, with sauce still in the pan; do not let it boil dry.
5. Add the chicken stock and the legs and wings, cover, and cook over low heat for about 10 minutes. Add the breast pieces and the cream and simmer gently. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part of a piece, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) it is done. The breast is done first and can be taken out first.
6. Move the cooked chicken to a warm plate and cover loosely to keep warm. Reduce the sauce uncovered until it lightly coats the back of a spoon; nudge the bottom of the pan to keep the shallots and cream from sticking.
7. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper, and stir the juices from the plate back into the sauce. Spoon the vinegar sauce over the chicken, sprinkle with parsley if you like, and serve at once.

## Key points

- **The sauce is sharply sour**: the vinegar was not reduced enough, or the stock is too weak. Reduce the vinegar to a small volume first, then add the stock and cream (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
- **The skin has no flavour and the sauce is pale**: the surface of the chicken was wet, or too many pieces went in at once, and the pan temperature dropped so the chicken boiled. Dry it and brown in batches, keeping the golden-brown bits on the bottom (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **The breast is dry and the legs are not cooked**: different cuts were cooked together for a long time, and the lean meat lost water first. Add the breast later and judge each piece by its centre temperature, taking it out when done; do not go by the colour of the browned skin (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
