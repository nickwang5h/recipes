Salt and pepper corn is sweet and juicy, with a thin crisp coating. The corn surface has to stay a little damp for the dry starch to stick. In the pan the starch absorbs water, [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with the heat, and then the water is driven off, which forms the crisp shell. If the starch coating is thick, or the surface is wet, it first turns into a wet, sticky paste.

While frying, let the bottom layer set first, then turn gently. If too much goes in at once, the oil temperature drops and the kernels pile on one another, so the shell sticks together and falls off easily; see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/). The pepper salt and toasted sesame seeds are saved until the corn comes out, falling directly onto a dry surface, and you can taste before adjusting the saltiness.

## Steps

1. Take the frozen corn out of its packaging, cook it according to the directions on the bag, and drain it in a colander after cooking.
2. Spread the corn on a plate lined with paper towels and shake it gently so the paper absorbs the surface water. When the paper is soaked, change to dry paper, and repeat until the kernels have no visible drops of water and still feel slightly damp to the touch. Do not wipe them so dry that the starch will not stick.
3. Sprinkle in the starch in stages and shake gently so each kernel is evenly coated. Start with 40 g and add more if it does not cover, up to 70 g in total. Finally shake off the loose dry starch and keep the kernels as separate as possible.
4. Pour oil into the pan to cover the bottom, and heat over medium heat until the oil runs freely. Fry a small amount of the coated corn first as a test; there should be steady fine bubbles at once. If nothing happens, keep preheating; if the oil is already smoking, take the pan off the heat to cool.
5. Spread the corn in a single layer in batches and fry for about 30 seconds without turning. Once the starch shell on the bottom has set, lift from underneath with a spatula and gently spread the kernels apart. Do not stir the starch layer while it is still wet.
6. Keep turning gently for about 3 minutes, until the shell is dry, slightly yellow and the kernels come apart, then take it out. Spread it on paper towels to absorb the extra oil, then move it to a dry plate at once, and do not pile it in a thick layer.
7. Sprinkle on part of the pepper salt and toasted sesame seeds, mix and taste, then add more, using 3 g of pepper salt as the starting point. Serve hot; if left standing, the moisture in the corn gradually softens the crisp shell.

## Key points

- **The coating sticks in big lumps**: the corn surface still had drops of water, or the starch coating was too thick. Before [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) happens, wipe the surface until only slightly damp, coat thinly and then fry.
- **Starch falls off and the corn is not crisp**: it was stirred hard right after it went in, or the pan was too full, so the [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/) dropped. Let it sit and set first, then turn gently, and fry in batches in a single layer.
- **Pepper salt is too salty**: salt content differs from brand to brand. Following the idea in [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/), sprinkle in stages, mix and taste, and you do not have to add all you prepared.
