*Tomates farcies* are French home-style stuffed tomatoes: the tomato is soft, tangy and juicy, the filling inside is rich and gives off juices when cut, and the shell still holds it. The filling is often pork, beef or a mix of the two. This is the beef version, with onion, garlic and parsley for fragrance, and an egg that binds the loose ground meat into one piece once baked.

Scoop out the seeds and liquid, then salt lightly and drain, so no water collects in the baking dish (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The filling sets through [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) as it heats. When the tomato skin wrinkles, the centre of the filling is not necessarily cooked, so measure with a thermometer.

## Steps

1. Cut the tops off the tomatoes to use as lids, and scoop out the seeds and soft pulp with a small spoon, leaving a wall about 0.5 cm thick. Do not scoop through the bottom, and keep the lids intact so you can lift them out together after baking.
2. Sprinkle a little salt inside each tomato and set them upside down on a rack to drain for about 15 minutes. Put the scooped-out pulp in a separate bowl and strain off the seeds and thin juice. Keep the firm flesh for another tomato sauce, and do not pour the bowl of liquid back into the filling.
3. Cook the onion in 1 tbsp of olive oil over low heat for about 8 minutes, until translucent and with no raw sharpness. Stir in the garlic, then scoop into a bowl and spread out, and let it cool until no longer warm before it touches the raw meat.
4. Mix the ground beef, egg, onion and garlic and parsley, and season with salt and pepper. Mix only until it holds together in a mass; do not beat it over and over until tight and sticky. The filling should be soft with no free liquid.
5. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Lightly oil a baking dish with the remaining olive oil and arrange the tomatoes open side up. Spoon in the filling gently without packing it too tightly, mound it into a small dome on top, put the lids on and rub them with a little oil.
6. Bake on the middle rack for about 40 minutes, until the tomato skins wrinkle and the filling has set. Insert a thermometer from the side into the centre of the thickest part of the filling, and take them out at 74°C (165°F). If the lids brown first, cover loosely with foil and keep baking.
7. Take them off the hot baking dish and rest for about 5 minutes, so the juices stop running out in quantity, then lift them with a wide spatula, supporting the bottom. Spoon a little of the liquid from the dish back over the tomatoes, leaving the floating fat behind; rice or bread goes well with them.

## Key points

- **The dish is full of water and the filling is loose**: the seed sacs and thin juice were not removed, so there is too much liquid in the filling. Drain the tomatoes first and mix only dry, cooked onion into the filling (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Tomatoes split and fall apart when lifted**: the walls were scooped too thin, or the tomatoes were overripe. Choose large firm fruit and keep the bottom intact; heat keeps softening the shell (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Filling is dry and hard, yet the centre cooks slowly**: the meat was too lean, packed too tightly, or baked too long while waiting for the centre. Use ground meat with some fat, fill lightly and measure the centre temperature (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
