Shiitake mushroom chicken has silky chicken and a sauce with the savouriness of chicken and dried shiitake cooked together. When the savoury compounds in meat and mushrooms meet, the flavour gets fuller; see [umami](/principles/umami/). The soaking liquid carries a lot of flavour from the mushrooms, so strain out the grit and add it to the pan, and the shiitake aroma is not left only inside the mushrooms.

The surface of the chicken needs to be fairly dry for the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/), but the longer the meat heats, the more tightly its [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and the more water is squeezed out. So small pieces, laid flat and taken off the heat in time, keep the chicken silkier than chasing a deep golden colour.

## Steps

1. Rinse the dust off the dried shiitake and soak them in warm water until the stem bends and a cut piece has no hard core. Lift them out and cut into quarters. Let the soaking liquid settle, strain it, and measure 150 ml, topping up with clean water if needed.
2. Bone the chicken legs and cut the meat into small pieces of similar size. Cut the ginger into small pieces and halve the garlic. Keep the white and green parts of the green onion separate, and save the green part for the end so it does not simmer for the whole time.
3. Put the chicken pieces into boiling water and blanch for about 1 minute, until the surface turns white. Lift out, drain, and pat the surface dry with paper towels.
4. Toss the chicken with the Shaoxing wine, 15 ml of the light soy sauce, the salt and the dark soy sauce. If there is liquid at the bottom of the bowl, lift the meat into the pan with tongs and keep the liquid to cook with the mushroom liquid later.
5. Heat the vegetable oil in the pan over medium heat until it flows freely but does not yet smoke. Lay the chicken pieces flat in the pan and wait until the side touching the pan takes on a little colour before turning. Pan-sear until the surface has pale golden patches, then remove.
6. In the oil left in the pan, stir-fry the white part of the green onion, ginger and garlic until fragrant, then add the shiitake and toss over high heat for about 20 seconds. When you smell the mushroom, return the chicken.
7. Add 150 ml of the mushroom liquid, the remaining 15 ml of light soy sauce, the sugar and the liquid left in the bowl. Once it boils, cook uncovered over medium heat for about 2 minutes. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece; at 74°C (165°F) the chicken is done. If it is not there yet, keep cooking, and add a little hot water if the liquid is too low.
8. Once the chicken is cooked, reduce the sauce until it thinly coats the pieces with a little flowing sauce left at the bottom of the pan. Drizzle with the toasted sesame oil, sprinkle with the chopped green part of the green onion, then turn off the heat at once and plate.

## Key points

- **Chicken is dry**: blanching, searing and reducing added up to too long a cook, and the [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) too tightly, squeezing water out of the meat. Sear only until there are pale patches, and take the chicken out as soon as it is cooked.
- **Chicken does not brown and the pan is full of water**: the surface was not dried, or too much went in at once, and the steam blocks the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Dry the chicken first, and sear in batches if the pan is small.
- **Sauce is salty and very dark**: the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and salt all concentrate as the sauce reduces (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Use the 30 ml of light soy sauce in two parts, and once the flavour is right, leave some sauce rather than reducing it dry.
