For sliced pork with garlic sauce, pork with both fat and lean is boiled plain until cooked, sliced thin and tossed with garlic paste, sweetened soy sauce and chili oil. Sichuan often uses fatty hind leg, and at home a leaner skin-on pork belly is also common. The garlic hits first, then the soy sauce flavour, then the sweetness and the heat.

Plain boiling has to control how much the meat contracts; see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Lift it out once it is cooked through, and do not keep it at a hard boil. Tenderness also depends on the thin slicing in [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/): fibres in each bite are short, and the garlic sauce can cover the whole slice. The spices for the sweetened soy sauce are cooked first and the raw garlic is pounded last, so each keeps its own layer of aroma; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

## Steps

1. Put the star anise, bay leaf, cassia, fennel seed, clove, tangerine peel and tsaoko in 250 ml of hot water and soak for 30 minutes, until the spices have taken up water and smell fragrant. Drain off the soaking water.
2. Put the soaked spices and the rest of the sweetened soy sauce ingredients in a small saucepan. Heat over medium for 3–5 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and the edges bubble. Turn to the lowest heat, half cover and cook for 30 minutes, until the sauce is slightly thick and smells of spice. Turn off the heat, cover and steep for 1 hour, until the liquid is no longer hot to the touch, then strain out the solids. Measure out 2 tbsp and refrigerate the rest in a clean container.
3. Put the pork belly, the ginger and green onion for boiling, the Shaoxing wine and the Sichuan peppercorns in 1500 ml of cold water. Heat over medium for about 10 minutes until it boils, and skim off the foam. Turn to low and cook for 30–40 minutes, with the surface barely bubbling, until a chopstick goes into the thickest part and the centre of the meat reaches 71°C (160°F).
4. Lift the pork onto a clean plate and let it cool for 10–15 minutes, until it is no longer hot to the touch and the surface is a little firmer but the inside is still warm. Keep 1 tbsp of the cooking broth and cool it in a small dish for 10–15 minutes, until no longer hot. Do not leave the pork soaking in the hot broth.
5. Pound the garlic to a fine paste and let it sit for 5 minutes, until the garlic smell is strong. Stir in the ginger paste, minced green onion, vinegar, toasted sesame oil and 2 tbsp of chili oil from the garlic sauce group, then add the 2 tbsp of sweetened soy sauce and the 1 tbsp of broth you kept. Stir into a sauce that flows.
6. Smash the cucumber, cut it into pieces and spread them over the bottom of the plate. Cut the pork belly across the lean grain into slices about 1–2 mm thick, with skin, fat and lean joined in each slice. Spread the slices out as you cut so hot meat does not pile up and keep cooking.
7. Lay the slices over the cucumber and spoon the garlic sauce evenly over them, then drizzle on the remaining 1 tbsp of chili oil. When you lift a slice, both sides should be coated in sauce. At the table, toss the cucumber lightly so the dressing at the bottom of the plate reaches every bite.

## Key points

- **The lean meat is dry and hard**: it was boiled hard, or left soaking in hot broth after cooking, so the muscle kept contracting and losing water. Keep it at a gentle simmer, check the thickest part for doneness and lift it out once it is cooked through. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **The slices are thick and taste greasy**: cutting while the meat is still hot and soft, or with a dull knife, gives thick slices, and the fat in a thick slice is greasier to chew. Let it cool until slightly firm, then draw the knife across the grain to slice thin, keeping skin and meat joined. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **The garlic is weak and the sauce is only salty-sweet**: the garlic was only roughly chopped, or was heated with the sauce. Pound the garlic to a fine paste and mix in the sweetened soy sauce only once it has cooled to warm, so the raw garlic aroma stays. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
