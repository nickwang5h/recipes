Stir-fried rice cakes are soft, chewy and springy, coated in soy sauce and green onion oil, and can take egg and baby bok choy. Rice cakes are steamed or boiled into shape and turn hard when cold, because the starch retrogrades; heating softens them again slowly (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). So boil the rice cakes soft first, then put them in the pan; the stir-fry is short and they are less likely to stick to the bottom.

The green onion whites are fried slowly in oil to golden so the aroma goes into the oil. Take the green onion oil out, and toss it back in at the end off the heat, and the green onion flavour is clearer (see [spices and aromatic extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Drain the rice cakes before stir-frying, or the water washes the soy sauce and green onion oil into a thin soup.

## Steps

1. Break the rice cakes into single slices, and cut thick pieces into thin slices first. Bring a pot of water to a boil, add the rice cakes and stir gently to keep them from sticking, and cook for about 2–3 minutes, until the slices bend and the centres are soft and springy. Different packages soften differently, so start tasting at the time on the package, and do not cook until the edges fall apart.
2. If the cooked rice cakes cannot go straight into the pan, put them in a bowl of clean water so they do not pile up and stick fast. Just before stir-frying, drain them in a colander; do not let the rice cakes go into the pan with a bowl of water.
3. Separate the green onion whites and greens and cut them into short sections. Wash and drain the baby bok choy (optional), cutting the stems thin and the leaves into short sections; beat the egg (optional).
4. Put 30 ml oil in the wok, add the green onion whites over medium-low heat, and stir-fry slowly for about 3–5 minutes, until most are light golden and fragrant, then immediately lift out the whites and the oil together. The residual heat of the pan will keep colouring the whites, so do not wait until they are dark brown before scooping them out.
5. Add the remaining 20 ml oil to the pan. If using egg, pour it in first, push it gently into curds, and take it out as soon as it sets; use the oil left in the pan to stir-fry the vegetables and rice cakes.
6. If using baby bok choy, stir-fry the stems until slightly soft, then add the leaves and stir-fry until wilted. Add the drained rice cakes, lift and toss gently, spreading them over the bottom of the pan; if they stick, do not pull them off by force.
7. Add the light soy sauce and toss until the surface of the rice cakes is evenly coloured, then return the egg to the pan. Taste a slice, then decide whether to add salt; turn off the heat when the sauce has just coated the slices and the rice cakes are still soft and whole.
8. Off the heat, toss the green onion oil and whites back in, sprinkle in the green onion greens, toss and plate right away; eat while hot.

## Key points

- **Rice cakes soft outside, hard inside**: the centres of thick slices are not yet heated through (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cut them thin, boil until the centres are soft, then stir-fry briefly.
- **Green onion oil is bitter, whites are black**: chopped green onion scorches easily, and if it comes out late the residual heat deepens the colour (see [spices and aromatic extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Take it out once it is light golden and fragrant.
- **Rice cakes are salty, only thick sauce is left at the bottom**: the salt in the soy sauce concentrates as it dries (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Do not add salt at first and stop heating as soon as the sauce coats them; if they stick, add a little hot water and gently push them apart.
