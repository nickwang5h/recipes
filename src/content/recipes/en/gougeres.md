*Gougères* are savoury cheese puffs from Burgundy, often served as a small bite with drinks. The Gruyère goes straight into the paste, and once baked the shell is thin and crisp with cavities of different sizes inside; the cheese aroma is clearest while they are still warm. The paste makes about 40 bite-sized puffs.

The flour is cooked first so its [starch gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); the paste turns sticky and can hold water vapour. In the oven, the water turns to steam and pushes the paste open, and the [proteins coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) in the egg and flour to set the shape. Before they come out, the steam inside has to be let out, so the hollow does not collapse and the shell does not go soft.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment, grate the cheese, crack the 4 eggs into small bowls one by one and keep the egg for the wash separate.
2. Bring the water, butter, a little salt, the white pepper and a small pinch of nutmeg to a boil, until all the butter has melted. Take off the heat, pour in the flour all at once and stir with a wooden spoon until no dry flour is visible.
3. Return the pot to medium heat and keep turning and pressing the dough for about 1–2 minutes. Stop when the dough comes together in one mass and leaves the sides of the pot, with a thin film on the bottom; do not cook the film brown.
4. Take off the heat and spread the dough out to cool a little, until it no longer gives off lots of steam. Beat in the eggs one at a time, mixing each until the paste is smooth again before adding the next. The finished paste should be glossy and, when you lift the spatula, fall slowly and leave a thick triangular tail.
5. Mix in the cheese, then pipe or use two small spoons to portion about 40 mounds, leaving space between them to puff. Press down the pointed tips with a brush dipped in egg, and brush the surface thinly with egg wash, taking care it does not run onto the sheet.
6. Do not open the oven door during the first half. Bake for about 20 minutes, until clearly puffed and golden on the surface and the sides feel firm. Poke a small hole in the side of each puff with a small knife, turn the oven off, leave the door ajar and let them dry for about 10 minutes to vent the steam, then move to a rack.

## Key points

- **The paste spreads into flat discs**: the paste was not cooked dry enough, or it was already too thin when the eggs went in. Cook until the thin film forms on the bottom of the pot, and let each egg absorb fully; beat the last egg and add it in portions, stopping when the tail looks right. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Golden outside, damp and sticky inside**: not baked long enough, and the steam inside has not escaped. After poking the holes, keep using the residual heat to dry them, and cut one open to check; the centre should have a hollow, with the inner wall slightly soft but not raw. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **They collapse soon after coming out**: the shell cooled before it set. Bake until the sides are firm before opening the door to vent; the cheese adds weight, so the finished puffs are normally shorter than plain choux. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
