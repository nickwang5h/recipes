*Moo ping* is a salty-sweet grilled pork skewer from Thai streets, usually served with [Thai Sticky Rice](/recipes/khao-niao/). The marinade has the fragrance of garlic, cilantro roots or stems and pepper, and the coconut milk, palm sugar and soy sauce turn the thin slices a glossy brown on the grill. The slices are layered into flat skewers with fat and lean together, the edges slightly charred and the centre still tender.

This dish starts with the cut. Slicing against the grain about 5 mm thick shortens the muscle fibres and lets heat reach the centre easily (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Marinating in the fridge lets the salt slowly penetrate the slices (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). On the grill, the surface water has to dry off first before the browning and grilled aroma of the [Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) appear; the sugar in the marinade burns easily, so the heat cannot be too fierce.

## Steps

1. Cut the pork: remove the tough silverskin and keep the fine fat. First cut along the grain into strips about 4 cm wide, then slice across the grain 5 mm thick. Keep the thickness even, or in one skewer the thin slices will dry out before the thick ones are cooked.
2. Pound the marinade: pound the garlic, cilantro stems and both peppers for about 3 minutes, until they form a fine wet paste. Add the palm sugar and pound for about 2 minutes more, until no hard lumps of sugar remain, then stir in the oyster sauce, both soy sauces, oil, coconut milk and 45 ml of water to make an even brown liquid.
3. Marinate the pork: pour in all the marinade, separate the slices and massage until coated, cover and refrigerate for 8 hours, making sure every slice is coated with marinade.
4. Soak the skewers: put about 24 short bamboo skewers in a shallow dish and soak in 1000 ml of water for 1 hour, until each skewer is wet through. While you wait, sprinkle the cornstarch over the marinated pork and toss until no white clumps remain.
5. Thread the skewers: thread 4–5 slices on each skewer, passing the skewer through the middle of each slice. Lay them flat on a tray and press the slices into a flat strip with the slices overlapping slightly like roof tiles. Cover and refrigerate for 1 hour, until the slices hold together and do not slump when you lift a skewer.
6. Preheat the grill: preheat over medium heat for about 10 minutes, until the temperature is steady and the grate is well heated. Leave one side at a lower temperature, and put folded aluminum foil under the exposed bamboo. Put the skewers on the hot zone and grill each side for 1.5–2 minutes, until there are brown grill marks and the edges sizzle and release fat.
7. Check doneness: insert a thin probe from the side of the skewer into the thickest slice, and take them off only at 71°C (160°F). If not there yet, move them to the cooler zone and grill 1 more minute per side, then test again, so the sugar does not blacken first. Grill in batches, and serve with warm sticky rice.

## Key points

- **Chewy skewers, cooked outside and raw inside**: the pork was cut with the grain, or thick pieces were hidden among the thin slices. Find the grain first and cut slices of even thickness against it, and keep the skewers flat (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
- **Black on the surface and not cooked inside**: the sugary marinade got too much heat. When the flames flare continuously, move the skewers to the cooler zone and cook the centre with steady heat (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Unusually salty after marinating**: the Thai black soy sauce was replaced with saltier Chinese dark soy sauce while the light soy sauce was kept at full amount. Check which one the bottle says, and count the saltiness of both soy sauces and the oyster sauce together (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
