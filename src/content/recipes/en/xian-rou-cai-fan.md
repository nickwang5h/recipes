Cured pork and greens rice cooks cured pork, greens and rice together in one pot. Once the rice grains have absorbed water and cooked through, a little lard is mixed in to give a sheen. How soft or firm the rice is depends on the water available (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)); greens also release water, so drain the washed greens well and do not pour the extra vegetable juice from the pan into the rice.

Treating the stems and leaves separately lets the stems soften along with the rice while the leaves keep their fresh aroma. The cured pork is already salty, so this rice gets no added salt. Cured pork varies a great deal in saltiness; while prepping, cook a small slice and taste it, which is easier to deal with than finding out the rice is too salty after it is cooked.

## Steps

1. If using fresh winter bamboo shoot, peel and slice thin, put it in cold water, bring to a boil and cook for about 10 minutes, then lift out and drain. For canned bamboo shoot, just rinse and drain.
2. Cut the cured pork into small dice of about 1 cm. Wash the bok choy leaf by leaf and drain; cut the stems into small pieces of about 0.5 cm and the leaves into small pieces of about 2 cm, and keep them separate.
3. Put 10 g of lard, the cured pork and the optional winter bamboo shoot in a cold pan and fry over medium-low heat until the fatty part of the pork turns slightly translucent and small bubbles appear at the bottom. Add the optional Shaoxing wine and sugar and stir-fry until the raw smell of the wine is gone.
4. Add the stems and stir-fry until the colour is bright and the surface slightly soft. Turn off the heat and take out the ingredients; if there is a clear amount of vegetable juice at the bottom, leave it in the pan and do not pour it over the rice.
5. Wash the rice, drain it and put it in the rice cooker with 310 ml of water, adding another 20 ml if you use winter bamboo shoot. Spread the cured pork and stems over the rice without disturbing the rice underneath, and start the normal rice programme.
6. If your rice cooker lets you open the lid midway, quickly spread in the leaves and close the lid about 10 minutes before the programme ends. For models that cannot be opened midway, stir-fry the leaves separately until just wilted and drain off the liquid, then spread them over the rice once it is cooked and keep warm with the lid on for 5 minutes.
7. When the rice finishes cooking, let it rest for 5 minutes, then taste a grain; it is ready only if the centre has no hard core. Add the remaining 5 g of lard and the optional white pepper, and gently fluff from the bottom, mixing until the vegetables and meat are evenly distributed.

## Key points

- **Rice is wet and sticky and the grains are broken**: the greens brought in too much water and were stirred repeatedly in hot rice (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Drain the greens, leave the vegetable juice behind, and fluff the rice with a lifting motion.
- **Rice is too salty**: the salt in the cured pork enters the rice grains as it cooks (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Dice heavily salted cured pork, soak it in clean water and change the water, then drain it before use.
- **Leaves are dark yellow and the rice has a hard core**: the leaves were heated too long, or the lid was open too long and let steam escape (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add the leaves later and open the lid quickly; rice varieties differ, so fine-tune the water by the actual texture next time.
