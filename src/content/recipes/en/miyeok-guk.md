*Miyeok guk* is a Korean soup made with wakame: once soaked, the fronds are thin and soft, and cooked with beef they are silky in the mouth. In Korea it is also a common birthday soup. The beef is stir-fried before simmering, so the broth carries tiny drops of oil and stays pale in colour.

The beef and the soy sauce together carry the [umami](/principles/umami/), while the wakame adds a seaweed aroma and a soft texture. Thin slices shorten the time the beef needs to turn tender (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Soup soy sauce is salty and light in colour, so measure it and use it in portions. Adjust with [salt](/principles/salt/) only at the end, and the soup is less likely to turn dark and too salty.

## Steps

1. Put the dried wakame in a large bowl, add 1000 ml of cold water and soak for 30 minutes, until the fronds are fully open and the stems bend. Rinse twice, drain and squeeze out the excess water gently, then cut into pieces about 4 cm long. Discard the soaking water.
2. Slice the beef against the grain into bite-size pieces about 2 mm thick. Mix in the minced garlic, 1 tbsp of soup soy sauce and black pepper. If you are using light soy sauce instead, use only 0.5 tbsp here and keep the rest for the wakame.
3. Add the toasted sesame oil to a heavy-bottomed pot and stir-fry the beef over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until the slices separate and the red colour on the surface is gone. Use a spatula to pull apart slices that are rolled together, so each one touches the bottom of the pot.
4. Add the wakame and the remaining 1 tbsp of soup soy sauce and stir-fry over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, until all the fronds are coated in oil, darker in colour, and the seaweed aroma is strong. If you are using light soy sauce instead, add the remaining 0.5 tbsp now. Keep the bottom of the pot moist, so the fronds do not dry out.
5. Pour in 3840–5760 ml of water and heat over medium-high heat for about 10 minutes, until the whole pot boils. Skim off the grey foam on the surface, turn the heat to medium-low, cover and cook for 40 minutes, then cook 10 minutes more, until the beef bites through easily, the thick wakame stems have softened, and the broth is slightly milky.
6. Ladle out a spoonful of soup, let it cool and taste, then adjust with salt and black pepper. If it is too salty or savoury, add 100 ml at a time from the reserved hot water, simmer over low heat for 2 minutes until the soup bubbles evenly again, then taste once more. Ladle into bowls and serve with white rice.

## Key points

- **The beef is chewy even though the soup is done**: the slices were cut with the grain or at uneven thickness. Find the grain first and slice thinly against it. Cook thick slices 10 minutes longer each time, until they bite through easily (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
- **Fronds crowd the pot and it looks like a bowl of greens**: the soaked weight was treated as the dry weight. Dried wakame swells a lot, so weigh it dry and cook it in the stated amount of water. Too little water makes the flavour too concentrated (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)).
- **Dark soup colour and a harsh saltiness**: ordinary soy sauce was swapped in for soup soy sauce in equal amounts, and salt was added too early. Halve the substitute amount as the ingredient note says, and taste and add salt only at the end (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
