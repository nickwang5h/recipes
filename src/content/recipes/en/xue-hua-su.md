Snowflake crisp has a thin coat of milk powder on the outside like white frost, a soft, sticky inside, and a crunch of cookie and nuts when you bite in. The method melts marshmallows into a thick, sticky mass, stirs in butter and milk powder, then folds in the cookies and nuts. Marshmallows contain sugar, water and gelatin; heat softens them and breaks up the foam structure, and as they cool they bond into one block that can be cut.

The key is gentle, brief heating. If the mass is heated too long, water keeps evaporating and it sets harder as it cools, and spots on the bottom of the pan can scorch. Low heat with constant stirring keeps [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) even, and you move to the next step as soon as the marshmallows have just melted evenly.

## Steps

1. Break the larger cookies into pieces about the size of small coins, keeping them as whole chunks. If the cooked nuts are large, break them smaller too. Weigh out two portions of 20 g milk powder, and have a mould or a small tray lined with parchment paper ready.
2. Put 20 g of unsalted butter in a nonstick pan and heat over low heat until just melted, then swirl the pan so the bottom is coated. The butter should not brown or smell burnt. Once it has melted, add 75 g of marshmallows.
3. Stir constantly with a heatproof spatula, from the edge of the pan to the centre, until the marshmallows melt into one even, thick mass. When there are no unmelted lumps and the butter no longer floats separately, add the first 20 g of milk powder.
4. Stir quickly until there is no dry milk powder, then turn off the heat right away and move the pan off the burner. While the mass can still flow, add 60 g of nuts and 75 g of cookies all at once and fold from the bottom so every piece is coated.
5. When the mixture has cooled enough to handle and won't burn your hands, fold and press it gently so the dry ingredients are evenly distributed. A spatula is enough. If you use your hands, wear food-safe gloves and knead as little as possible so you don't crush the cookies to powder.
6. Press into the mould while still warm, packing the corners firmly and levelling the surface, with straight edges. Keep the thickness as even as possible and leave no obvious gaps inside; once the mass cools and hardens, it is hard to press loose pieces back in.
7. Leave at room temperature until fully cool and the block releases from the mould in one piece (about 30 minutes), then cut into small pieces. Dust the remaining 20 g of milk powder thinly over every side, pat off any loose powder, then store airtight so the cookies don't absorb moisture and go soft.

## Key points

- **Squares are so hard they are difficult to bite**: the marshmallows were heated too long and the mass lost too much water. Melt over low heat, move the pan off the heat as soon as the milk powder is stirred in, and don't let residual heat keep cooking it (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Milk powder clumps and the dry ingredients don't hold**: the milk powder wasn't stirred in while the mass was still soft, or the dry ingredients went in too late. Weigh everything first, and add the nuts and cookies right after the milk powder is mixed in.
- **Cut pieces are all crumbs, with few crunchy chunks**: the cookies are too crumbly, or mixing was too rough. Use firm small cookies and distribute them by folding and light pressing; don't pull at the mixture for long.
- **Loose corners, pieces shed bits when cut**: the mass had already hardened when it was shaped, or pressing left gaps. Press firmly while warm and cut only when fully cool.
