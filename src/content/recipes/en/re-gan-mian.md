Hot dry noodles are alkaline noodles coated in rich sesame paste and tossed while hot, with only a little sauce left at the bottom of the bowl. Sesame paste holds oil and also fine sesame particles, so loosen it with warm water added in stages until the oil, water and particles disperse into a sauce that clings to the noodles (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). At the right thickness the sauce falls from the spoon in a continuous stream and coats the noodles without clumping.

The noodles are pre-cooked, steamed ones whose starch was already [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), so a brief dunk to reheat is all they need before lifting out. If you bought raw alkaline noodles, cook them according to the package until there is no white core; the 25 seconds here is not enough for them. The minced pork is cooked and used as a topping, with meat broth to go with it.

## Steps

1. Dice the pickled long beans and dried pickled radish. Taste a small piece of the radish first, and if it is too salty, rinse it and drain. Chop the green onion; mash the garlic and stir it into the drinking water to make garlic water, and set aside.
2. Using the oil for the pork, break up the ground pork and cook it through, until there are no pink clumps and the liquid has cooked off, then remove it. Bring the meat broth to a boil, then keep it warm. Once all the toppings are ready, dunk the noodles.
3. Stir the sesame paste from the bottom of the jar and spoon it into a bowl. Add the warm water in several portions, first the initial third. Stir after each addition until the water and paste come together before adding the next. It is normal for it to thicken partway through; then loosen it with a little more water.
4. Stir the light soy sauce, warm broth and garlic water into the sesame paste. Lift the spoon to look: the sauce should fall smoothly and stay thick. If hard clumps remain, thin it with the rest of the warm water in stages, not all at once.
5. Bring plenty of water to a boil, add the pre-cooked noodles, separate them and dunk for about 25 seconds. Lift out one strand to try; once it is hot through, quickly lift out and drain.
6. Put the hot noodles in a bowl and add the noodle sauce, cooked pork, pickled long beans, dried radish, green onion and a little white pepper. Lift from the bottom and toss until every strand is coated in sauce, then add the optional chili oil, bouillon powder and salt to taste.

## Key points

- **The sesame paste is a hard lump and won't toss through the noodles**: too little water, or it was not stirred in stages, so the oil, water and particles did not disperse (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Stir in a little warm water until it flows, then toss with the hot noodles.
- **The centre of the noodles is hard**: raw alkaline noodles were treated as pre-cooked and dunked for only a few dozen seconds (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Check the package, and cook raw noodles through, strand by strand.
- **Liquid pools at the bottom of the bowl and the sauce won't cling**: the noodles were not drained, and all the spare loosening water went into the bowl (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Thin with the meat broth and garlic water first, and add warm water only if you need more.
- **Too salty, and the pepper covers the sesame aroma**: pickles, light soy sauce, bouillon powder and extra salt stack up (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Leave the salt to the end and add it after tasting, and use only a little pepper rather than a spoonful.
