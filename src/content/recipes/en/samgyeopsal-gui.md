Samgyeopsal gui is a common Korean grill of plain pork belly. The slices are fried until the fat renders and the outer edges turn slightly crisp, then wrapped in lettuce with garlic, onion and [ssamjang](/recipes/ssamjang/) to make one bite. The pork belly supplies its own fat, and the seasoning goes in the dipping sauce, which keeps the separate flavours of the meat and the vegetables.

Success depends on letting the fat melt out slowly while the surface browns from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). If the pan is crowded with meat or holds a pool of water and fat, the meat is hard to brown evenly. Slice the meat to an even thickness and cook it in spread-out batches, so heat reaches the centre fairly quickly and you do not need long cooking to make up for thickness (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Blot the pork belly dry, cut it into slices about 6 mm thick and return it to the fridge, taking it out only when it is that batch's turn. Cut the onion into thick slices and the garlic into slices; wash and dry the lettuce and the optional perilla leaves and put them on a clean plate.
2. Divide 4 tbsp of ssamjang among small dishes. Keep a separate plate and tongs for the cooked meat, and keep the raw-meat plate and tongs apart, so wrapping does not touch raw meat juices.
3. Preheat a heavy flat-bottomed pan or griddle over medium for 3–5 minutes, until the thin film of sesame oil starts to flow and the meat sizzles the moment it goes in. Lay down one layer of slices, leaving gaps between them, with no lid.
4. Cook the first side for 3–4 minutes, until the fat turns translucent and the underside is golden brown. Flip and cook 3–4 minutes more, until both sides have colour and the edges are slightly crisp. Insert a thermometer from the side of the thickest slice to the centre and take the meat out at 71°C (160°F); if it is not there yet, cook 1–2 minutes more.
5. Use the pork fat in the pan to cook the onion and garlic for about 3–5 minutes, turning until the edges of the onion are browned and the centres are soft, and the garlic slices are golden. If the garlic reaches its colour first, lift it out first and put it at the edge of the cooked-meat plate.
6. Spoon the extra pork fat into a heatproof container and leave a thin layer on the pan. Before the next batch goes in, preheat for 1–2 minutes, until the meat sizzles at once, then cook it for the same times. Each batch takes about 8–10 minutes; eat one batch as the next cooks.
7. Cut the cooked meat with clean scissors into pieces about 3 cm across. On a lettuce leaf, put a perilla leaf if using, then meat, garlic and onion, with about 0.5 tsp of ssamjang on each, and fold it into one bite. Dip in the rest of the sauce to taste.

## Key points

- **Meat stays pale and the plate is full of liquid**: the pan is too full and the moisture cannot escape. Cook less meat per batch and spoon off the pooled fat, so the meat sits directly on the hot pan (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Surface is charred but the centre is raw**: the heat is too high or the slices are uneven. Cut them all to about 6 mm, lower the heat a little after they take colour, and confirm doneness at 71°C (160°F) in the centre (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Lean meat is dry and tough, and the fat has lost its softness**: the cooked meat stayed on the hot pan, and the residual heat kept pushing water out. Take it off the heat as soon as it is done and cook only the next batch (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
