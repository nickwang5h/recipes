This noodle soup is clean-tasting, with chewy noodles and the savoury fragrance of shiitake in the broth. The vegetables can be changed, but the order they go in depends on the ingredient: the shiitake and tofu go into the broth first, the noodles are cooked next, and the greens go in last, so each keeps its texture. This bowl uses shiitake, firm tofu and baby bok choy. The stock and shiitake make the base flavour, and a little light soy sauce adds saltiness and savouriness (see [umami](/principles/umami/)).

When noodles absorb water and heat, their starch gelatinizes and the hard core in the centre gradually disappears (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Thin dried noodles and thick noodles take different times, so there is no single number of minutes. The soup must be enough to cover the noodles, with some spare for evaporation and for the noodles to absorb; cooking the noodles in a separate pot and then adding them to the broth keeps the soup clearer.

## Steps

1. Remove the hard stems from the shiitake and slice them thin; cut the tofu into small pieces about 2 cm. Wash the baby bok choy leaf by leaf, separate the stems from the leaves, and cut thick stems lengthwise into thin strips.
2. Put the stock and 200 ml of water in a small pot, bring to a boil over high heat, then turn to medium heat and keep the surface at a gentle bubble. Add the shiitake and cook until the slices are soft and the broth smells of mushroom.
3. Add the tofu and push it gently apart, and keep cooking gently until the centre of the tofu is hot with no cold core. Push with the back of a spoon along the bottom of the pot, so you don't break the cubes.
4. In another pot, bring enough noodle water to a boil, add the noodles and gently separate them right away. Start timing by the package's suggested time, keep a light boil with the lid not fully closed, and turn down the heat promptly when the foam is about to rise over the edge.
5. When the noodles are nearly done, add the bok choy stems to the soup pot first and cook until they bite through easily, then add the leaves. The leaves only need to have just wilted with the colour still bright; don't cook them long.
6. Pick out a noodle from the noodle pot to check; lift them out and drain when the centre has no dry hard core and they are still springy. If the package says about 5 minutes, start testing as that time approaches.
7. Put the noodles in a large bowl, add the light soy sauce to the soup pot, taste, and then add salt and white pepper if needed. Turn off the heat and drip in the toasted sesame oil. Pour the hot broth, tofu and greens over the noodles and eat while hot, so the noodles don't go soft.

## Key points

- **Noodles are soft outside and hard inside**: the centre of the noodle hasn't absorbed enough water to gelatinize (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep cooking and taste; thick noodles can't use the time for thin ones.
- **Soup keeps shrinking and the noodles clump**: the noodles absorb water while the water in the pot keeps evaporating (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cook the noodles and the soup in separate pots, add hot water when the soup runs low, and taste the saltiness again.
- **Soup is bland or too salty**: stocks differ in strength and salt content (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). Start with a lighter stock as the base and leave the seasoning until the vegetables are cooked, so you don't keep adding soy sauce and darkening the soup.
