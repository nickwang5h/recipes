Tonjiru (*tonjiru*) is a Japanese home-style soup with pork belly, root vegetables and tofu. The sweetness of soft-cooked roots, the flavour of the pork and the miso come together, so the soup is heartier than plain [miso soup](/recipes/miso-shiru/). Kombu and bonito dashi and miso together supply the [umami](/principles/umami/), so no soy sauce is added.

First stir-fry the pork and root vegetables until they are coated in oil, then add the broth and cook them through; the miso is dissolved last to keep its fermented fragrance. Daikon and carrot are cut thin and taro a little thicker, so they all soften at nearly the same time in one pot (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The finished soup can have a few droplets of oil on the surface, the taro stays in whole slices, and there are no undissolved lumps of miso in the soup.

## Steps

1. Freeze the pork belly for 15 minutes before slicing. Slice the onion thin, cut the daikon and carrot into fan-shaped slices about 3 mm thick, and the taro into slices about 8 mm thick. Lightly scrape the burdock skin and shave it into thin slices, rinse off the surface debris with water as soon as it is cut and drain; cut the negi on the diagonal.
2. Cut the konnyaku into short slices about 3 mm thick. Heat 1000 ml of water in a small pot over high heat for about 5 minutes, until the surface keeps rolling. Blanch the konnyaku for 3 minutes, until the slices are hot through and the alkaline smell has faded, then lift out and drain.
3. Put the aburaage in the same pot of boiling water and blanch for 1 minute, until the surface oil disperses. Lift out and squeeze off the water, then cut into thin strips. Discard the blanching water; cut the tofu separately into pieces about 2 cm across.
4. Heat the sesame oil in the soup pot over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows quickly. Spread out the pork belly and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the slices separate, the surface is no longer pink and a little rendered fat appears at the bottom of the pot.
5. Add the onion and stir-fry for 2 minutes, until the edges are translucent. Add the daikon, carrot, burdock and taro and stir-fry for another 2 minutes, until each piece looks glossy on the surface; add the konnyaku, aburaage and negi.
6. Pour in 720 ml of [dashi](/recipes/dashi/) and heat over medium heat for about 4 minutes, until bubbles start to form on the surface. Skim off the foam, cover, turn to low and cook for 10–15 minutes, until a bamboo skewer slides easily through the thickest slice of taro, and the pork is cooked through, reaching 71°C (160°F) in the centre.
7. Turn off the heat. Use a ladle of hot soup to dissolve 2.5 tbsp of miso into a thin paste, pour it back into the pot, taste and then decide whether to add the remaining 0.5 tbsp. Stir in the grated ginger and the tofu, and warm over low heat for 2 minutes, until the tofu is hot through and the soup steams without boiling, then ladle into bowls and sprinkle with green onion.

## Key points

- **Taro is still hard while the daikon has fallen apart**: the root vegetables are not cut to the right thickness, or the heat is too high. Prepare them by slice thickness, judge by whether the thickest taro slice can be pierced, keep the heat low and stir little. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Miso fragrance is faint and the soup keeps getting saltier**: the miso went in early and the soup was boiled for a long time, and evaporation concentrated the salt. Dissolve the miso only after the roots are cooked, and just warm it. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Only saltiness and a thin broth**: the dashi is too weak, and adding more miso only adds salt. Build full flavour in the dashi first, then add miso in portions to adjust the saltiness. See [umami](/principles/umami/).
