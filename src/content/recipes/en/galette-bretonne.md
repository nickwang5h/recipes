*Galette bretonne* is the savoury buckwheat crêpe of Brittany. The crêpe is made of only buckwheat flour, water and salt. It is thin and nutty, slightly crisp at the edge, and is eaten with butter or wrapped around a savoury filling.

Buckwheat does not form gluten the way wheat does, so the crêpe sets through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when heated. The batter rests in the refrigerator for a day so the flour absorbs enough water, and then spreads into an even thin layer. The heat of the pan sets the underside and evaporates water, and the edge slowly turns crisp (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). An ordinary frying pan makes about 8–10 galettes.

## Steps

1. Whisk the buckwheat flour, salt and 200 ml of water into a thick batter with no dry flour. Beat hard with a whisk until the batter is smooth and tiny bubbles appear on the surface.
2. Gently pour 50 ml of water over the surface of the batter, cover, and refrigerate for at least 24 hours. This layer of water keeps a skin from forming on top, so do not stir it yet.
3. Take out the batter before cooking, add the remaining 200 ml of water and stir well. First stir up the flour that has settled at the bottom of the bowl. When you lift the whisk, the batter should run off in a continuous stream, and the marks where it falls back into the bowl should vanish quickly.
4. Preheat a frying pan over medium-high heat and brush it with a thin layer of oil. Pour in a small ladle of batter and tilt and swirl the pan at once so it spreads into a thin layer; on a cast-iron crêpe pan, spread it with a dampened crêpe spreader.
5. When the surface loses its wet shine and the edge curls up, loosen all the way round with a thin spatula, then support the whole galette and flip it. Cook the second side for about 1 minute, until there is no wet batter and brown spots show on the underside.
6. While hot, spread on a little butter and salt, fold, and plate. Keep cooking the rest of the batter, giving it a light stir before each one and brushing on more oil as needed; stack any galettes you are not eating right away and cover them so they do not dry out.

## Key points

- **Cracks as soon as you flip**: buckwheat galettes are fragile, and flipping while the surface is still wet tears them. Wait until the starch has set with heat and the edge can be lifted in one piece, then support it and flip (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Turns into a thick clump as soon as it is poured**: the batter has settled after absorbing water, or the pan is too hot and the swirl too slow. Stir it even, test one galette, then adjust the heat; the batter should be spread before it sets (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Pale colour and soft edge**: the pan is not hot enough, or too much batter went in at once. Use less batter each time and let the pan recover its heat before the next one; take it out once the edge is clearly dry (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
