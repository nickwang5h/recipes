Wensi tofu is a soup of finely shredded tofu from Yangzhou. Silken tofu is cut as fine as hair and floats in a clear savoury broth with fine shreds of chicken, ham, shiitake and bamboo shoots. The dish is said to be named after a Yangzhou monk called Wensi. Cut all the ingredients to a similar fineness first, so that no single mouthful has a coarse piece standing out (see [knife work and heat](/principles/knife-work/)). At home you can cut the tofu into shreds of about 1 mm, slicing it thin first and then cutting into shreds in the same way.

The smoothness of the soup comes from a light thickening: starch absorbs water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), making the soup slightly thick enough to hold up the light, fine tofu shreds. The chicken stock, ham and shiitake give the soup [umami](/principles/umami/). Season lightly so the bean flavour can come through. The finished soup flows, and the strands are distinct when you spoon it up.

## Steps

1. Turn the tofu out onto a damp, flat cutting board and trim off any uneven edges. Dip the knife in water and slice the tofu into very thin sheets in one go, then fan the sheets slightly and stack them evenly and cut them fine into shreds. Cut all the way through and keep the same direction; do not saw the knife back and forth through the tofu.
2. Lift the tofu shreds on the flat of a wide knife and slide them onto the surface of a bowl holding 1000 ml of clear water. Swirl the bowl gently so the shreds spread out. Drain through a fine sieve and keep the shreds intact; do not squeeze them with your hands. Cut the chicken, ham, shiitake, cooked bamboo shoots and spinach leaves into fine shreds separately.
3. Put the chicken stock, shiitake shreds, bamboo shoot shreds and ham shreds in a pot and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes until it boils. Turn to low heat and cook for 3 minutes, until the shiitake are soft and the soup smells of mushrooms and ham.
4. Add the cooked chicken shreds and tofu shreds and heat over low heat for 2 minutes, until the soup comes back to a light bubble. Push with the back of the ladle from the bottom of the pot forward so the shreds spread evenly. Do not stir the way you would when stir-frying.
5. Mix the cornstarch with 30 ml of cold water and drizzle it in along the edge of the pot in two additions, nudging gently for about 30 seconds each time, until the soup turns bright before continuing. Add the spinach shreds and cook for 1 more minute, until the shreds are soft and the soup coats the back of a spoon thinly. Taste, add salt and serve.

## Key points

- **The tofu turns into crumbs**: the knife was not sharp, the thin slices were not cut through, or the tofu was squeezed while being moved. See [knife work and heat](/principles/knife-work/). Wet the blade before cutting, hold the shreds in water after cutting, and only nudge gently in the pot.
- **White lumps of starch in the soup**: dry starch hit the hot soup directly, and its outer layer [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) first. Mix it with cold water, stir it again just before pouring, and add it in portions to soup at a gentle simmer.
- **The shreds sink or the soup is like paste**: too little thickening cannot hold up the shreds and too much covers the light, soft texture. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Stop when the soup still flows and a thin film coats the back of a spoon, and watch the thickness as you add the second pour.
