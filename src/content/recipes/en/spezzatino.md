The Italian beef stew *spezzatino* is soft enough to press apart with a fork. The sauce is thick and smooth, and the potatoes and peas sit in the tomato sauce, with the potatoes soaked in the meat juices.

Beef chuck comes from a hard-working muscle with plenty of fascia and connective tissue, which suits slow stewing. Collagen has to stay in hot liquid long enough to turn into gelatin in quantity: the meat loosens, and the gelatin makes the broth thick and smooth (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). If the heat is too fierce, the muscle contracts first and squeezes out moisture, and the meat is cooked but still tough, so keep the liquid at a gentle bubble.

Browning the meat dark first gives the stew a layer of roasted flavour from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/), and the brown bits on the bottom of the pot dissolve into the stew with the stock. The gelatin in the stew sets to a jelly when it cools and melts again when reheated.

## Steps

1. Cut the beef into large chunks, sprinkle with salt and black pepper, coat with flour and shake off the extra. Heat the butter and olive oil in a pot, brown the meat on all sides over high heat until deep brown, leaving space between the pieces, then take it out.
2. Add the tomato paste to the same pot and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, then add the chopped carrots, celery, potatoes and onion and stir-fry until the tomato paste turns darker and the edges of the vegetables have taken colour.
3. Return the beef to the pot, add the peas and passata, and cook over high heat for about 10 minutes. Meanwhile heat the beef stock.
4. Pour in the hot stock to cover the meat and vegetables, and add the bay leaves. Cover and simmer over low heat for about 1 hour, with only small bubbles slowly rising, until the beef is tender and a fork slides easily into the thickest piece.
5. Taste, add salt and black pepper if needed, and serve hot.

## Key points

- **The beef is tough to chew**: it has not stewed long enough, and the sinew has not softened. Keep the liquid at a gentle bubble and keep stewing until the meat is tender. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **The meat comes out grey with no browned crust**: the meat was wet, or too much went in at once, the pan temperature dropped and the meat was boiling rather than searing ([Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Pat the pieces dry, add them in batches and brown them before turning.
- **The bottom of the pot scorches**: the pot is too hot after browning the meat, and tomato paste burns easily. Watch the bottom while stir-frying and add the vegetables once the colour darkens.
- **For lunch boxes**: refrigerate after packing, and reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) in the centre.
