*Gratin dauphinois* comes from the Dauphiné in France. Thin potato slices bake soft in dairy until a thin golden layer forms on top. This version uses milk and butter and no cheese, so it tastes rich and creamy while the potato slices stay distinct.

The starch on the surface of the potatoes [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) in the hot milk and thickens the liquid between the slices. So put the slices straight into the dish after cutting, and do not rinse them. Choose a yellow-fleshed potato such as Yukon Gold that holds together when cooked. Cut evenly and layer shallow so the centre cooks along with the top (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). Get a stovetop-safe heatproof metal baking dish about 25 cm across and about 5 cm deep. Rub the inside all over with the cut side of half a garlic clove, then grease it with 15 g of butter.
2. Peel the potatoes, wash them whole and dry them, and cut into rounds about 3 mm thick. Layer them into the dish as you cut. Do not rinse or soak the cut slices.
3. Lay half of the potato slices overlapping in an even layer, sprinkle with salt and pepper and dot with half of the remaining butter. Add a second layer of potatoes, season it the same way and top with the remaining butter. Do not pile all the potatoes in the middle.
4. Heat the milk until it is just boiling and pour it in along the side of the dish. Set the dish on the stove over low heat until fine bubbles appear at the edges, then move it straight to the upper part of the oven. The milk only needs to be at the bottom and between the layers; it does not have to cover the top potatoes.
5. Bake for about 20–30 minutes, until a knife tip slides from the centre to the bottom with no firm resistance, the milk no longer forms a flowing pool and the surface is golden. If the top has coloured but the centre is still firm, cover loosely with a sheet of foil and keep baking.
6. Take it out and let it rest briefly, until the vigorous bubbling at the edges stops, then serve. A spoonful should lift up soft stacked slices, and what is left at the bottom of the dish is rich, creamy liquid. For a bigger batch, use a wider dish and keep the layer depth the same.

## Key points

- **The bottom of the dish is like milk soup**: the surface starch was washed off after slicing, or the potatoes were not baked through. Do not rinse after slicing, and bake until the knife tip slides to the bottom before taking it out (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Top is browned but the inside is still firm**: the slices are too thick or uneven, or the dish is piled too deep. Shield the coloured surface and keep baking; next time cut evenly, and when scaling up add dish area (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Potatoes break into a mush**: a floury potato that falls apart in cooking was used, or the dish was stirred repeatedly after baking. Switch to a variety that holds its shape, and serve by the layer to keep the stacked slices.
