Garlic scapes with ground pork uses a little pork belly to add richness to the scapes. The meat is cut fine, so the fat renders out easily and spreads among the scapes. The minced garlic is fried briefly in the oil first, which carries its aroma into the base oil; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). This is a small stir-fry with lots of garlic scapes and the meat as an accent.

Garlic scape stems are tougher than leafy vegetables. They are fried until fragrant first, then braise-fried with a little water, and the water and steam carry heat inside; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). When you stop the heat, the skin should be slightly soft, still crisp when bitten, and the water at the bottom of the wok mostly gone.

## Steps

1. Wash the garlic scapes, cut off the dry, tough ends and the flower buds, and cut them into pieces about 5 cm long. Keep pieces of clearly different thickness apart, and add the thicker ones to the wok first so the thin pieces do not go soft first.
2. Cut the thin slices of pork belly into fine mince, so fat and lean are mixed together. Smash and mince the garlic. Hold them in separate dishes, and clean the board and knife used on raw meat before handling the vegetables.
3. Add 10 ml of oil to the wok and heat over medium until the oil flows smoothly. Add the garlic and stir-fry for about 10 seconds. When you smell the garlic and it is still pale, go to the next step.
4. Add the pork mince and break it up with the spatula, then add 5 ml of light soy sauce. Stir-fry over medium heat for about 30 seconds, until all the mince has changed colour and the fat starts to turn translucent. Do not leave the garlic at the dry, hot edge of the wok.
5. Add the garlic scapes and the remaining 10 ml of light soy sauce, and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until every piece is coated in the base oil and soy sauce.
6. Add 20 ml of water and braise-fry over medium heat for about 5 minutes, stirring now and then. Watch for the scape skin to gradually soften and the colour to even out, and taste one piece for doneness. If it is still hard, cook longer, and if the bottom of the wok dries out first, add a little water.
7. Once the scapes are cooked through, taste for saltiness and decide whether to add salt. Stir-fry for a final 30 seconds, until only a thin film of oily sauce is left at the bottom of the wok and the scapes are still whole and crisp, then plate right away.

## Key points

- **The garlic is scorched and the whole dish is bitter**: finely minced garlic releases its aroma fast, and was fried dry for too long (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the pork as soon as you smell the garlic. Do not wait for the garlic to turn deep brown.
- **The scapes are scorched outside and hard in the centre**: the wok surface heats fast and the inside has not caught up (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Finish with a braise-fry using a little water, and adjust the time to thickness and the crispness you want.
- **The soy sauce fragrance is there but it is too salty**: salt was added by a fixed amount after the light soy sauce (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Taste the cooked scapes first and add only what is missing.
