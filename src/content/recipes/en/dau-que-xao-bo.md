*Đậu que xào bò* has tender beef and green beans that are cooked through with a little snap left, all coated in a garlic and oyster sauce. The green beans are blanched first and then stir-fried, and the beef goes into the pan on its own for a quick fry, so the meat never waits in the pan for the beans (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

## Steps

1. Mix the beef with half the garlic, half the salt, black pepper, cornstarch and water, then mix in 1 tsp of the oil. Set it aside to marinate while you prepare the other ingredients.
2. Bring a pot of water to a boil, add the green beans and carrot, and blanch for about 2 minutes. Lift them out, rinse briefly under cold water and drain.
3. Heat a skillet over medium heat, add enough oil to cover the bottom and stir-fry the remaining garlic until fragrant. Add the green beans and carrot and the remaining salt, and stir-fry for 3–4 minutes, until the beans are cooked through with a little snap left. Remove from the pan.
4. Keep the pan on medium-high heat. Spread the beef in a single layer and sear briefly, then add the oyster sauce and stir-fry until the meat is still slightly pink, 1 minute at most.
5. Return the green beans and carrot to the pan and stir-fry until the beef is cooked through. Drizzle in the fish sauce and toss, then plate and sprinkle with black pepper.

## Key points

- **Beef is tough and grey**: the pan was too full, so the meat gives off water and does not brown ([Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Spread the beef out to sear it, and return the vegetables last.
- **Green beans taste raw**: they were only stir-fried briefly and not cooked through, so the centres stay hard ([heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Blanch first, then stir-fry, and taste one to confirm they are done before removing them.
- **Lunch box**: pack into a shallow container and refrigerate promptly once it stops steaming; reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) in the centre.
