*Spaghetti alla puttanesca* is a salty, savoury, slightly hot red sauce for long pasta: tomatoes, black olives, capers and garlic, and traditionally anchovies. Stories about the name vary and have nothing to do with the method. Let the salted fish dissolve in warm oil first, then cook the tomatoes, so the flavours of these pantry ingredients end up in one pot of sauce.

Anchovies are cured and strong, so a small amount gives the tomato sauce a deep [umami](/principles/umami/). Garlic and chili are heated in the oil first so their aroma moves into the fat (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Capers, olives and anchovies all carry salt, so rinse them, go light on salt in the pasta water, and taste the pasta with the sauce after it has reduced to judge the saltiness.

## Steps

1. Rinse and drain the capers, pit the black olives and break them into small pieces, and crush the tomatoes in a bowl. Finely chop the parsley leaves, drain the oil from the anchovy fillets, and lightly smash the garlic, keeping the cloves whole.
2. Put the olive oil, garlic and dried chili in a wide frying pan and heat over medium-low heat until the garlic is pale gold and fragrant. If the garlic starts to turn dark brown, lower the heat right away.
3. Add the anchovies and press and stir them gently with a wooden spoon until the fillets break down into a fine paste mixed with the oil. Remove the garlic so it does not keep browning while the sauce reduces.
4. Pour in the tomatoes and their juice, and add the olives and capers. Cook uncovered for about 10–15 minutes, crushing the tomatoes now and then, until the liquid has thickened and there is no ring of watery juice at the edge of the pan. If the canned tomatoes are thin, cook a little longer.
5. In a separate pot, boil water with only a little salt and cook the spaghetti until the centre still has a slightly firm core. Before draining, set aside a cup of the pasta water. Do not rinse the pasta with cold water.
6. Put the pasta straight into the sauce pan and toss over medium heat until the pasta is cooked to your liking. If the sauce is too dry, add pasta water a spoonful at a time and toss until the tomato sauce spreads in a thin layer along the strands and there is no obvious pool of oil at the bottom.
7. Turn off the heat and stir in the parsley. Taste a forkful of pasta with sauce, and add salt only if it is clearly bland. When plating, divide the capers and olives evenly among the servings, and eat right away.

## Key points

- **So salty it covers the tomato flavour**: the capers were not rinsed, or salt was added to the pasta water as for an ordinary tomato pasta. Reducing concentrates the salt further. Next time deal with the cured ingredients first and season last; if it is already too salty, simmer in some unsalted tomatoes. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Bitter garlic and anchovies**: the oil was too hot, the garlic has burned black, and the anchovies are frying dry. Take out the garlic when it is pale gold, and add the tomatoes as soon as the anchovies have broken down. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Oil all over the plate but dry pasta**: the sauce was reduced too far, or the pasta and sauce were not tossed together enough. Add a little pasta water and keep tossing to disperse the fat into the moist tomato juice; if you added too much water, reduce briefly. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
