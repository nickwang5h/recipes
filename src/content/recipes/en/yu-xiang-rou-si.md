In fish-fragrant shredded pork, the pork is slick and tender, the carrot and bell pepper stay crisp, and a thin sauce carries salty, sweet, sour and spicy at once. The pork is first loosened in oil and returned to the pan, and cooked only briefly. The vinegar in the bowl sauce goes into the pan last, and its [sourness](/principles/acid/) makes the deep flavour of the doubanjiang clearer.

The pork is slick and tender because of the egg white and starch coating and the short cooking time. [Starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) thickens the coating on the pork, so the bowl sauce can cling to the vegetables quickly. If you keep stir-frying pork that is already cooked through, you only squeeze the water out of it and the meat dries, so have the vegetables and bowl sauce ready before anything goes in the pan (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

## Steps

1. Soak the dried wood ear in clean water in the refrigerator for about 4 hours, until fully open with no hard core. Trim off the base, rinse, cut into shreds and use soon after soaking.
2. Cut the tenderloin against the grain into shreds of similar thickness, mix well with the marinade ingredients and marinate in the refrigerator for 15–30 minutes. A thin coat on the shreds is enough; if there is a lot of egg white liquid in the bottom of the bowl, lift the shreds out when they go in the pan and do not pour the liquid in with them.
3. Cut the carrot and bell pepper into thin shreds, mince the ginger and garlic, and cut the green onion into short pieces. Blanch the carrot briefly in boiling water until it bends a little but is still crisp to chew, then lift out and drain. Drain the wood ear too.
4. Stir the light soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, salt, starch and water of the bowl sauce together, until the sugar dissolves. The starch sinks to the bottom, so stir again just before it goes in the pan.
5. Heat the pan, add 15 ml of oil, and when the oil spreads and does not smoke, add the pork. Break the shreds apart with chopsticks or a spatula and stir-fry until the surface turns white and the shreds no longer stick in big clumps, then remove at once.
6. Add 5 ml of oil to the pan, turn the heat to medium, and stir-fry the green onion, ginger, garlic and doubanjiang until fragrant. When the doubanjiang breaks up and the oil turns red, add the carrot and stir-fry for about 20 seconds, then add the bell pepper and wood ear and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, until the vegetables are cooked but still crisp.
7. Return the pork and toss quickly for about 20 seconds. Cut open the thickest shred to look at the centre: if it is all white, it is done; if any is raw, stir-fry a little longer.
8. Stir the bowl sauce and pour it in along the side of the pan, pushing and stirring quickly for about 15 seconds. When the sauce boils, turns from cloudy to glossy and thinly coats the pork, turn off the heat and plate at once. Do not leave it in the hot pan.

## Key points

- **Pork shreds clump together**: too much coating liquid, or the shreds were not broken apart soon enough after going in the pan (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Lift the pork into the pan, spread it out, and do not use the leftover marinade as stir-fry liquid.
- **Dry, tough pork**: both the first stir-fry and the return to the pan went on too long, squeezing water out of the meat (see [protein shrinkage](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take the pork out first, and return it once the vegetables are cooked.
- **Glaze clumps or tastes of raw starch**: the starch sank to the bottom of the bowl and was not stirred in, or the sauce did not come to a boil (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir the bowl sauce before it goes in, and turn off the heat when the sauce turns glossy and clings to the ingredients.
