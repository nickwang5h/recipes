*Ribollita* is a Tuscan bread and vegetable soup, and the name means "boiled again". Lacinato kale, savoy cabbage, Swiss chard and white beans are first cooked into a soup, then layered with day-old bread, chilled overnight and reheated the next day. Allow for the bean soaking and the overnight chilling before you start.

More than half the beans are puréed and the rest left whole, so the soup has both body and some chew. The starch in the bread was already [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when it was baked; here it mainly takes water up again and breaks apart, drawing the bean broth into one soft whole. The finished soup mounds on a spoon and still has a moist sheen.

## Steps

1. Drain the soaked beans and add 2000 ml of fresh water. Bring to a boil, then turn to low and cook until the beans crush at a touch and have no powdery hard centre, about 1–1.5 hours; add hot water as the level drops, and keep all the bean broth.
2. In a heavy-bottomed pot, heat the olive oil for cooking the vegetables, add the onion, carrot and celery, and cook over medium-low heat until soft and fragrant. Add the tomatoes, potatoes and the three leafy greens, and stir until the leaves wilt.
3. Lift out about two thirds of the cooked beans, push them through a sieve or mash them, and stir them back into the bean broth. Pour the bean purée and broth into the vegetable pot. The liquid should roughly cover the vegetables; add hot water if there isn't enough.
4. Simmer over low heat for about 1 hour, stirring from the bottom now and then, until the potatoes and chard stems are soft. Add the remaining whole beans and season with salt and black pepper. The soup must still be moist enough for the bread to absorb.
5. In a shallow container, lay down a layer of bread, pour a layer of the vegetable and bean soup over it, and alternate until everything is used. Pause until the steam has dispersed, then refrigerate promptly and cover once cold; no more than 2 hours should pass between leaving the pot and entering the fridge. Overnight lets the bread soak up the soup.
6. The next day, move the portion you will eat to a heavy-bottomed pot and heat over low heat for about 10 minutes, stirring. If it is too thick, add hot water, and cook until the whole pot bubbles and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), still thick enough to scoop with a spoon. Drizzle the remaining olive oil over each bowl.

## Key points

- **Beans with a hard core**: tomatoes went in before the beans were cooked through, and the acidic environment slowed softening. Make sure the beans crush to a fine purée before combining. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
- **The bread turns into a sticky, sweet paste**: soft sweet sandwich bread has a fine, dense crumb that loses its support once wet. Choose day-old country bread with a firm crumb, and keep some whole beans and vegetable strips. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Scorched at the bottom and still cool on top when reheating**: thick soup moves slowly and the bottom heats first. Use low heat, keep lifting from the bottom, and add hot water in stages; chill the overnight soup promptly in a shallow container, and reheat only the portion you will eat. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
