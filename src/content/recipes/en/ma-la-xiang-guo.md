Mala xiang guo is a pot where each ingredient is cooked separately and everything is coated in sauce at the end. The bok choy, pork, tofu sheets and instant noodles are pre-cooked one by one by thickness, using the method in [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/), then drained, returned to the pan and stir-fried with the dry pot sauce until every piece is seasoned. Only the noodle cake is used; the separate seasoning packet would stack more saltiness on top.

The main ingredients come to about a kilogram, and a home stove piled full loses pan temperature easily. Too much water turns the pan into a steamer and dilutes the sauce, so draining, working in batches and constant tossing are all about managing [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). The chilies are fried briefly in the oil first to put their fragrance into it, and the oil then carries it to the surface of the ingredients.

## Steps

1. Wash the bok choy and cut the stems and leaves into sections separately. Cut the pork into thin slices of similar thickness and the tofu sheets into wide strips. Set out a few colanders or plates for the pre-cooked ingredients, and do not reuse a plate that held raw meat.
2. Bring plenty of water to a boil. Add the stems first, then the leaves. Lift them out when the stems are slightly soft and the leaves turn bright and wilt. A short blanch is enough, so do not boil the tender leaves for 5 minutes. Spread them out to drain and keep a little crispness.
3. Put the tofu sheets in the boiling water and cook until the strips are hot through, soft and not sticky, then lift out and drain. Add the pork slices and separate them with chopsticks. After about 4 minutes, start checking the temperature, and lift them out when the centre of the thickest piece reaches 71°C (160°F).
4. Cook the noodle cake in a fresh pot of water for about 2 minutes, until the strands loosen and the centre still has some chew. Drain well. The original seasoning packet is not needed, and do not carry noodle water into the wok.
5. Pour out the cooking water, wipe the wok dry and add the stir-frying oil. Heat over medium heat until the oil flows easily but is not smoking. Add the dry pot sauce and stir-fry for about 20 seconds until it loosens, then add the dried chilies and stir-fry for about 10 seconds. When you smell fragrance, add the ingredients.
6. Add the pork and tofu sheets and toss over medium heat for about 3 minutes, so every piece is coated in sauce. If the bottom starts to stick and scorch, lower the heat first. If the wok is too small, split the sauce and ingredients into two batches instead of piling them high.
7. Add the greens and stir-fry until the surface is coated in red oil and there is no pool of water at the bottom, then move on. The greens are already pre-cooked, so this step is just to toss them in; leafy greens do not need a fixed 3 minutes.
8. Add the noodles and toss, loosening them with chopsticks, for about 1 minute, until noodles, greens and meat are evenly mixed and hot all the way through. Serve hot. The sauce should stay on the ingredients, with only a little oily liquid left on the plate.

## Key points

- **Tastes like a pot of spicy soup**: the pre-cooked ingredients were not drained, and the water diluted the sauce (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain each batch well after lifting it out; noodles especially carry cooking water.
- **Leafy greens mushy, noodles gluey**: pre-cooking and stir-frying times stack up (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Blanch the greens briefly and add them late. Cook the noodle cake only until it loosens, and leave the last minute for tossing with the sauce.
- **Bitter base or blackened chilies**: the oil was too hot, and the [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/) burned before the main ingredients went in. Fry the sauce over medium heat and watch the state of the oil, not just the heat setting.
