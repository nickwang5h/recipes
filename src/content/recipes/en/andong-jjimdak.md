Andong jjimdak (*안동찜닭*) braises chicken with soy sauce, syrup and whole chilies; the result is sweet and salty with some heat, and the sauce is a brown colour. The chicken, potatoes, vegetables and sweet potato glass noodles finish in the same pot, and when you serve, you scoop up the noodles together with the chicken pieces, which makes a full meal.

The noodles go in last. They soak up the stewing liquid and turn clear and chewy because starch absorbs water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Stew the chicken and potatoes until done first, and leave enough flowing liquid for the noodles, so you get both cooked chicken and well-textured noodles. The whole chilies provide the heat and fragrance, and the sauce keeps a clear soy sauce flavour.

## Steps

1. Put the sweet potato noodles in a large bowl, cover with 1000 ml of warm water and soak for 30 minutes, until the whole bundle bends and the strands separate, then drain. Prep the other ingredients while they soak; all the prep takes about 40 minutes.
2. Cut the potatoes into pieces about 3 cm, the carrot into slices about 1 cm thick, and cut the onion and shiitake into pieces and the green onions into lengths. Pat the surface moisture off the chicken pieces. Stir together the soy sauce, oyster sauce, mirin, brown sugar, rice syrup and black pepper.
3. Put the chicken pieces in a wide lidded stewing pot and pour in the sauce and 710 ml of water. Heat over high heat for about 5–7 minutes, until the whole pot boils; keep cooking uncovered for 10 minutes and skim off the foam, until the surfaces of all the chicken pieces have turned white.
4. Add the potatoes, carrot, onion, shiitake, dried chilies, garlic and ginger, toss, cover and stew over medium-high heat for 8–10 minutes, until a chopstick pierces the potatoes and the centre of the thickest chicken piece reaches 74°C (165°F), measured away from the bone. If it is not done, stew 2–3 minutes more and measure again.
5. Stir the soaked noodles, the fresh chili and the green onion lengths into the same pot and cook uncovered for 3 minutes, until the noodles are clear with no hard core in the centre and the potatoes pierce easily. Turn gently with tongs. If the noodles are not cooked and the bottom of the pot is nearly dry, add the spare 120 ml of water 30 ml at a time, keeping a flowing sauce at the bottom of the pot.
6. Turn off the heat, stir in the toasted sesame oil and sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds. You should be able to pick up whole chicken pieces and potatoes, with the noodles coated in glossy sauce and a layer of sauce left at the bottom of the pot; serve straight from the pot.

## Key points

- **The noodles clump together and break when you lift them**: they went in too early and then cooked too long after absorbing water. Soak them soft first, add them when the chicken and potatoes are nearly done, and turn gently rather than scraping repeatedly. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The sauce keeps getting saltier**: evaporation concentrates the salt in the soy sauce and oyster sauce. Do not rush to reduce it dry before the noodles go in; keep a flowing sauce, and if the bottom is too dry, add water as in the steps rather than more seasoning. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **The potatoes fall apart while the chicken is not cooked**: the chicken pieces are too large, or the potatoes are cut too small. Chop the chicken small and keep the potatoes at about 3 cm; check the centre temperature of the chicken before the noodles go in, so you are not relying on longer noodle cooking to finish the chicken. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
