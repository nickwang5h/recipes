Sausages with lentils has soft lentils, carrots and smoked pork sausage, and each spoonful tastes of salty bacon. The lentils, meat and vegetables simmer together in one pot, so no rice is needed on the side.

The bacon is browned in the pot first, and the browned flavour on its surface comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The lentils soften slowly in the broth, and the starch inside them absorbs water and swells (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

## Steps

1. Rinse the lentils and drain. Put the diced bacon in a heavy-bottomed pot and dry-fry over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the fat renders and the surface turns slightly golden.
2. Add the onion and cook for about 5 minutes until softened, then add the carrots and garlic and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
3. Pour in the lentils, then add the cloves, bay leaf, thyme and cold water. Lay the whole sausage on top of the lentils without piercing it.
4. Bring to a gentle bubble over medium heat, then turn the heat to low, cover with the lid left slightly open, and stew for about 40 minutes. If the water is about to drop below the lentils, add a little hot water. The dish is done when the lentils are soft through but still whole and the centre of the sausage reaches 71°C (160°F).
5. Lift out the sausage and cut it into slices about 1.5 cm thick. Remove the cloves and herbs. If there is too much liquid in the pot, ladle some out and keep a little broth.
6. Off the heat, stir in the mustard and red wine vinegar. Taste, then add black pepper and as much salt as needed. Return the sausage slices and sprinkle with parsley.

## Key points

- **Sausage skin splits and the meat turns dry**: the broth boiled too hard. Keep the heat low so the surface only bubbles gently, and add the sausage whole without piercing it.
- **Lentils still hard but the pot is dry**: the water evaporated too fast. Add a little hot water while the lentils are hard, and keep stewing until they are soft through.
- **Lentils turn to mush**: they cooked too long or the heat was too high. Taste one near the end and turn off the heat once it is soft.
- **For lunch boxes**: pack in a shallow container and refrigerate promptly, then reheat in the microwave the next day until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
