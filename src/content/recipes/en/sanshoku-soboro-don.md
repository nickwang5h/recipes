Sanshoku soboro don (three-colour soboro rice bowl) lays brown ground chicken, yellow scrambled egg and green peas in separate sections over rice. Here, "soboro" is freshly cooked fine crumbs of ground meat, moist and easy to pick up. The chicken is sweet and salty, the egg is slightly sweet, and the peas add a fresh bite to the bowl.

The key to the fine crumbs is the order in the pan. The ground chicken is first broken up with the seasoning liquid in a cold pan, then stirred as it heats. If it is fried into one block first, the proteins have already bound into a tight lump and are hard to break apart afterward. The egg also starts in a cold pan: stir each layer apart as it sets (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The rice is soaked thoroughly before cooking (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

## Steps

1. Rinse the short-grain rice gently in several changes of water, pouring off the cloudy water, and drain. Put it in a small heavy-bottomed pot with a tight lid, add the 200 ml of cooking water and soak for 30 minutes, until the grains have absorbed water and changed from translucent to milky white.
2. Cover the pot with the lid slightly ajar and heat over medium for 4–5 minutes, until the water boils. Close the lid tightly, turn to the lowest heat and cook for 12–13 minutes, until the rice has absorbed the water and small holes show on the surface; if any water is still visible, cook 1 minute more. Take it off the heat and steam covered for 10 minutes, until the centre of the grains has no hard core, then fluff.
3. While the rice cooks, put the ground chicken, grated ginger, sake, mirin, soy sauce and the sugar from the chicken group in another small pan. Without turning on the heat, break the chicken apart with chopsticks so the seasoning is spread evenly.
4. Turn on medium-low heat and keep stirring in circles and breaking up the meat with a few chopsticks for 3–4 minutes, until all the meat has changed colour. Cook 2–3 minutes more, until there is no flowing liquid left on the bottom of the pan and the crumbs are still glossy. Gather some of the meat together and check that the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), then remove from the pan.
5. In another small cold pan, beat together the eggs, the sugar from the egg group and the salt. Turn on medium-low heat and keep scraping up the egg that sets on the bottom and breaking it apart, stirring for 4–5 minutes, until all the egg is set into crumbs with no liquid egg left. Gather some together and check that the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), then take it out right away.
6. Add the 300 ml of water for boiling the peas to a small pot and heat over medium for 2–3 minutes, until it comes to a boil. Add the peas and cook 2–3 minutes, until bright green and hot in the centre, then lift out and drain. Divide the rice between two bowls, lay the chicken on one side and the egg on the other, and arrange the peas down the middle.

## Key points

- **Chicken forms big clumps**: the pan was hot, so the outside set too early. Break the meat up with the seasoning liquid in a cold pan first, and keep stirring it loose as it heats (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Egg crumbs are dry and hard, and the edge of the pan is browned**: the heat was too high and the egg stayed in the hot pan after setting. Use medium-low heat and take it out as soon as it is cooked through (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Rice is wet and sticky in a lunch box**: the chicken juices were not cooked off and the peas were not drained, so moisture soaks back into the rice (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Reduce the juices dry and drain the peas before packing, spread each out thin to let the steam escape, and chill promptly; the next day reheat to 74°C (165°F) at the centre.
