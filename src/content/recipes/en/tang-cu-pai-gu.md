Sweet and sour pork ribs have a little chew on the outside, tender meat next to the bone, and a glossy sweet-sour coat. A small amount of ketchup in the sauce makes the colour redder and adds a touch of tomato sweet-sourness. The ribs are fried and then braised; in the braise the fried surface softens again, so the gloss has to come from reducing the sauce at the end.

The membrane and tendons on the ribs only loosen once the [collagen turns to gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/); frying them golden only means the surface has coloured. The sugar and vinegar balance the flavour (see [acid](/principles/acid/)); the vinegar goes in twice, the first addition setting the sourness for the whole pot and the last keeping the vinegar aroma.

## Steps

1. Put the ribs and ginger slices in cold water, with the water covering the meat. Bring to a boil and skim the foam, keep at a gentle boil for 2–3 minutes, lift out and rinse off the scum, then drain well and blot the surface dry with paper towels.
2. Heat the frying oil to about 170°C (340°F) and add the ribs in batches. Fry for 3–5 minutes, until the surface is slightly golden, then lift out and drain. Wait for the oil to recover between batches; don't pile all the ribs into the pot at once.
3. In a clean pot, heat the 50 ml of water and the sugar over low heat, stirring until the sugar is completely dissolved. The sugar water only needs to stay clear; don't cook it to a colour.
4. Add the ribs and stir for about 30 seconds, then add 20 ml of the vinegar, light soy sauce, oyster sauce, ketchup, five-spice powder and the optional bouillon powder, and stir for about 30 seconds. Pour in the hot water for braising, bring to a boil, then add the dark soy sauce.
5. Cover, turn to low heat and braise for about 25 minutes, keeping the surface at a gentle simmer. Turn the ribs once or twice along the way; if the water is nearly gone and the meat is still firm, add hot water and keep braising until a chopstick goes into the meat and the meat next to the bone loosens with a light nudge.
6. Uncover and reduce the sauce over medium-high heat, turning as it reduces. When the sauce turns to large bubbles and thinly coats the ribs, turn to low heat, add the remaining 5 ml of vinegar, mix well and turn off the heat. Sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds; a little flowing sauce should remain at the bottom of the pot.

## Key points

- **Golden on the surface but the meat near the bone is still firm**: frying only coloured the surface, and the tendons haven't softened (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Braise until the meat is tender before reducing the sauce; if the liquid dries fast, add water instead of using strong heat to hurry it.
- **Oil splatters violently when the ribs go in**: the blanched ribs still have water on the surface, which turns to steam quickly (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Blot dry, add in batches, and leave enough room in the pot.
- **Very sweet, weak vinegar aroma**: too much sugar and not enough acid, and long cooking also carries off some of the vinegar aroma (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Keep part of the vinegar for the end; when tasting, take a small piece of meat rather than only the thick sauce.
- **Sauce sticks into a hard crust with a burnt taste**: the sugar sauce was reduced too dry and kept heating, causing [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) or even burning. Turn off the heat once it coats the meat; if it is slightly too thick, take it off the heat and loosen it with a little hot water, and if it is bitter and burnt, the sauce has to be replaced.
