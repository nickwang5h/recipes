*Godeungeo gui* is the salt-grilled mackerel found on Korean tables. The fish is rich in fat, so when it is salted and grilled the skin picks up a roasted flavour while the flesh stays moist, and it is usually served with rice and side dishes. At home, the oven's top-element Broil setting heats the skin directly and lets you watch it change from silvery blue to blistered golden brown.

Salt sprinkled ahead of time dissolves in the moisture on the surface of the fish, then spreads into the flesh (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Still dry the surface before grilling: evaporating water uses up heat, and the aroma from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) comes more easily once the surface is dry. The skin changing colour and the flesh being cooked through are two separate things, so check the thick part on its own (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Check the fillets, pull out any fine bones you can feel, and pat dry with paper towels. Cut a few shallow slashes in the skin, cutting through the skin only and not the flesh. Sprinkle salt evenly on both sides.
2. Refrigerate for 30 minutes, until the salt has dissolved and a little moisture beads on the surface. Pat dry again with paper towels and shake off any salt grains left. The skin should show no sheen of water.
3. Set the oven to high Broil and preheat for about 5 minutes, until the top element glows red or the oven signals it is ready. Use a metal pan that can take Broil and line it with aluminum foil. Adjust the rack so the surface of the fillets is about 10–15 cm from the element.
4. Brush the oil thinly on both sides of the fillets and set them skin side up with gaps between them. Broil for 2–3 minutes, until the skin forms small blisters and turns golden brown at the edges, and the edges of the flesh go from translucent to opaque. Watch the colour as it cooks, and move the pan before anything blackens.
5. Support each fillet with a wide spatula to turn it, and broil for 1–2 minutes more, until even the thickest part is opaque and flakes easily with a fork. If the thick part is still translucent, move the pan down one rack level and broil another 1 minute, until the flesh flakes.
6. Lift the fillets onto plates, skin side up, and serve hot with rice. If you like some sourness, add lemon juice at the table, so the grilled skin stays dry.

## Key points

- **Pale skin, weak grilled flavour**: the fish was not dried after salting, so the heat went first into evaporating water. Pat dry again before it goes in the oven, and leave gaps between the fillets. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Skin charred but flesh still raw**: the fish is too close to the element, or the fillets vary in thickness. Move down one rack level and keep grilling, checking that the thickest part is opaque and flakes. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Too salty**: you used pre-salted fish and added salt as well. Read the package first; the salt here is for unsalted raw fillets. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Dry, crumbly flesh**: the fish kept heating after it was cooked through, so the protein tightened and lost water. Take it out as soon as it flakes easily, and support the whole fillet with a wide spatula. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
