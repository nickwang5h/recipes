*Tôm rim* is the salty-sweet Vietnamese shrimp eaten with white rice. Small shrimp keep their thin shells, and fish sauce, sugar, green onion and garlic cook down into a thick glaze that clings to the surface, so the shells shine and the edges have a little toasted flavour. The meat is firm and the thin shell gives a crisp bite; large shrimp have thick shells, and it is easy to throw out the glaze along with them when peeling.

The dish cooks first in the liquid the shrimp and marinade release, and then the water is cooked off. While there is a lot of water, sugar only dissolves and concentrates; only when the liquid at the bottom of the pan has nearly dried up and the temperature keeps rising does clear [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) begin. Red shells only show that the shrimp are cooked, and whether the sugar has coloured is judged from the sauce, so at the end watch the liquid and the colour of the sugar together.

## Steps

1. Prepare the shrimp. Snip off the sharp rostrum and the legs, and remove the heads, keeping the shells and tails. Use a toothpick to pick the vein out between the shell segments, and blot the surface dry. Thaw frozen shrimp in the fridge for 8–12 hours, until the body bends and there is no ice core in the centre, then prepare them the same way.
2. Marinate the shrimp. Toss the shrimp with the fish sauce, sugar, the optional chicken bouillon powder, half the pepper, half the minced garlic and half the minced shallot. Cover and refrigerate for 15 minutes, until the sugar has melted into the marinade and every shrimp is coated.
3. Sizzle the aromatics. Put the oil in a wide, flat-bottomed pan and heat over medium heat for 1 minute, until the oil flows freely. Add the remaining garlic and shallot and the optional minced green onion white, and stir-fry for 15 seconds, until fragrant and the garlic has not yet browned. Immediately add the shrimp and all the marinade, spread them out, cover and cook over medium heat for 2 minutes, until the shells begin to turn red and liquid appears in the pan.
4. Reduce the sauce. Uncover and stir-fry over medium heat for about 8 minutes, until the shrimp are opaque, the shells are shiny, and the liquid has gone from a thin layer to a thick glaze clinging to the shells. Keep stirring during the last 1–2 minutes; when almost no flowing liquid is left at the bottom of the pan, the sugar glaze turns amber-brown and the edges of the shells are slightly crisp, turn off the heat at once. Once the bottom of the pan starts to colour, do not wait until the minced garlic turns black.
5. Plate. Stir in the remaining pepper and the optional green onion greens, and scrape out of the pan immediately. Serve with white rice together with the bits of green onion and garlic stuck to the shrimp; do not leave them in the hot pan to dry out further.

## Key points

- **Shrimp are red but the sauce is still watery**: the shells have changed colour, but the sugar glaze has not reduced enough to colour (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Spread them in a wide pan and reduce uncovered; do not add dark soy sauce to fake the caramel colour.
- **Shells are black and the green onion and garlic are burnt and bitter**: once the liquid is gone the pan temperature rises fast, and the sugar and fine mince burn easily (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Keep stirring as it nears dry, and take it off the heat and out of the pan when you see amber.
- **Shrimp shrink into small hard curls**: continued heating drives water out of the meat (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose small thin-shelled shrimp and stop once the glaze clings tightly; peeled large shrimp are not suited to being stir-fried and dried out for this long.
- **Glaze is too salty to eat**: the salt in the fish sauce and bouillon powder concentrates as the water evaporates (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The bouillon powder can be left out, do not add salt at the end, and judge the flavour together with the white rice.
