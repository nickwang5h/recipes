This dry-fried chicken takes the sweet and spicy route, with potato and green pepper, a variation that fries first and coats in sauce afterwards. The starch on the outside of the chicken pieces sets with heat first (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)) and then loses water to become a thin crust. Frying the chicken and potato separately lets each reach its own doneness, and a brief toss together then coats them in the sauce.

The doubanjiang is fried open in oil first so the red oil and the savoury fermented aroma spread through the whole pan (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). The sugar melts into a little water and forms a thick sweet and spicy sauce with the doubanjiang. Finish reducing the sauce first and return the fried ingredients last, so their crust does not absorb too much liquid.

## Steps

1. Cut the boneless chicken thighs into 2 cm pieces and mix in the salt, pepper, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and Shaoxing wine; if using chicken bouillon powder, add it now. Toss until slightly tacky, then mix in the starch and 5 ml of oil and marinate in the refrigerator for 30 minutes.
2. Cut the potato into 2 cm pieces, boil in boiling water for 5 minutes, then lift out and spread them to drain and blot dry. Seed the green pepper and cut into pieces of the same size, and slice the garlic. Before the potatoes go into the oil, check that their surface has no water droplets.
3. Put the frying oil in a small deep pot, leaving plenty of room against overflow, and heat to 180°C (350°F). Fry the potatoes in batches for about 3 minutes, until pale golden on the surface and set at the edges, then lift out and drain. Wait for the oil to recover between batches (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
4. When the oil is back to 180°C (350°F), fry the chicken in batches for about 2 minutes, lift out to rest briefly, then return to the same oil temperature and fry again for about 1 minute. Check that the centre of the largest piece reaches 74°C (165°F); the times are only a starting point, so if it is not cooked through keep frying, and once done, spread the pieces out to drain.
5. Fry the potatoes again in 180°C (350°F) oil for about 1 minute, until golden and a chopstick goes through the centre, then lift out and drain. Leave the hot oil in the original pot to cool down.
6. Put 5 ml of oil in a separate wok and stir-fry the green pepper over medium heat until bright in colour and just cooked, then take it out. Turn to low heat and fry the garlic until light yellow, add the finely chopped doubanjiang, and fry until the oil turns red and smells savoury, without letting the bean bits burn black.
7. Add the sugar, ground Sichuan peppercorns and 40 ml of water and stir constantly until the sugar melts and the sauce is thick enough that a spatula drawn across briefly shows the bottom of the pan. Return the chicken, potato and green pepper, toss quickly, and plate as soon as the surface is coated in sauce.

## Key points

- **Dark crust but not cooked inside**: the chicken pieces are uneven, or they were taken out by the clock only. See [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/); cut evenly, fry in batches and check the centre temperature.
- **Sauce tastes bitter**: the doubanjiang and sugar burned in a dry pan. See [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/); fry gently until fragrant and add water in time so the sugar dissolves in the liquid.
- **Chicken goes limp with a pool of sauce on the plate**: the pieces were tossed together before the sauce had reduced enough, and the fried crust took water up again. The fried crust absorbs water (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)), so reduce the sauce thick first, then toss briefly to coat.
