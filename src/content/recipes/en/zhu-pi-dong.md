Pork skin jelly cooks the collagen in the skin into gelatin that disperses in hot water. As it cools, the gelatin links into a network that traps the broth (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Hot, it is a broth; chilled, it can be sliced; heated again, it melts. It sets only if the gelatin is concentrated enough and chilled thoroughly.

Scrape the fat from the inside of the skin completely, and keep the simmer to small bubbles. If a lot of fat is left and the broth boils hard, oil droplets get stirred into the liquid and the jelly turns cloudy (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). This version adds light and dark soy sauce, so the jelly is a soy-brown colour. The skin soaks overnight and the cooked jelly chills overnight, so start a day ahead.

## Steps

1. Cut the pork skin into pieces no larger than 10 cm, put them in a lidded container, cover with cold water and soak in the refrigerator for 12 hours. Put the skin in a pot of cold water with 10 g of ginger and the Shaoxing wine, bring to a boil and blanch for 5–10 minutes, then lift into cold water.
2. Lay the skin on a cutting board and scrape off any leftover hairs, and scrape away all the white fat and any attached meat from the inner side. Cut into thin strips no wider than 3 mm, as even as you can, so the pot does not hold both soft and hard pieces.
3. Add the white vinegar and 5 g of scrubbing salt, rub firmly for about 3 minutes, then wash clean in fresh water. Rinse until there is no sour smell and the skin no longer feels slippery with oil. Pour away all the blanching water and the scrubbing brine (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)).
4. In a clean pot put 4000 ml of water, the skin, the green onion and the remaining ginger slices. Put the star anise and Sichuan peppercorns in a spice bag and add it. Bring to a boil and skim, then simmer over low heat for about 90 minutes. Keep it to occasional small bubbles, until a strip of skin snaps easily between tongs.
5. Remove the green onion, ginger and spice bag, and skim off the floating fat. Add the light soy sauce and dark soy sauce, and add salt in portions. Use either MSG or chicken bouillon powder, adding a little at a time until the taste is right. There is no need to use the full amount.
6. Spoon a little of the broth into a small dish and refrigerate it to see whether it sets. If it is still runny when fully cold, simmer over low heat to reduce and test again. If the broth is already very salty, do not rely on reducing to make up the concentration.
7. Divide the skin and broth among clean shallow containers. Once they stop steaming, put them in the refrigerator, lid loose at first, then cover tightly once fully cold, and chill overnight before slicing. Do not leave it at room temperature to wait for the whole pan to set. Chilled pork skin jelly counts as cooked food; eat it within 2–3 days.

## Key points

- **Fully cold but not set**: not enough gelatin has dissolved out, or there is too much water (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). First confirm the skin is cooked soft, then use a small chilled sample to decide whether to reduce.
- **Cloudy jelly, thick white fat on top**: leftover fat, or too hard a boil (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Scrape off the fat, skim the floating fat, and simmer gently.
- **Off smell, tough strips**: blanching and scraping were not finished, or the skin had not softened (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Clean it properly before simmering, cool it in shallow containers, and do not leave it at room temperature for long.
