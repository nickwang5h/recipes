A cup of bubble milk tea has the aroma of black tea, the rich smoothness of milk and the springiness of tapioca pearls. Brew the tea strong first, then add the milk, and finally add black tapioca pearls that have been cooked and soaked in syrup. The first thing you taste is tea, then milk, and the pearls at the bottom of the cup are soft and chewy.

The pearls soften by absorbing water through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The outside cooks first and the centre later, so floating is not enough; bite one open to check for a hard core. The tea base draws its flavour out with hot water (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)), and milk dilutes the tea, so use less water for steeping than usual. The syrup gives the pearls a sweet surface, and once in the cup they are stirred together with the tea and milk.

## Steps

1. Put the brown sugar and 30 ml of hot water in a small heatproof bowl and stir for about 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves and no grains are left at the bottom. If there are still grains, stir over a bowl of hot water for 1–2 minutes, until the syrup is even.
2. Put 1000 ml of water in a small pot and boil over high heat for 4–6 minutes, until the surface keeps rolling. Pour in the pearls and stir gently for about 30 seconds, until they separate and begin to float up.
3. Turn to low heat and cook for about 5 minutes, keeping the water at a gentle roll and stirring from time to time. Bite one open: the centre should be soft and springy, with no chalkiness or hard core. For quick-cook pearls, follow the package for the cooking and resting times; if the centre is still hard, cook 1 minute more each time and bite again.
4. Pour the pearls into a sieve and rinse with cold water for about 15 seconds to wash off the surface gumminess, drain and stir into the syrup. Soak for 10–15 minutes, until the surface is coated in syrup and tastes sweet.
5. While the pearls soak, put the tea bags in a heatproof pitcher, pour in 240 ml of boiling water and steep for 10 minutes, until the tea is a deep reddish brown with a clear tea aroma. Remove the tea bags and keep the tea.
6. Divide the pearls with their syrup between two cups, divide the tea and the milk equally, and stir until the whole cup is an even light brown. For a cold drink, add 60 g of ice cubes to each cup, stir and drink at once.

## Key points

- **Pearls soft outside and hard inside**: the starch in the centre has not absorbed enough water and gelatinized. Add them to boiling water, follow the package for cooking and resting times of the pearls you bought, and bite one open at the end; see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Pearls turn hard as they sit**: once cooled and left standing, the starch realigns and no longer stays soft and springy. Make the tea base ahead, cook the pearls just before drinking, and put them in the cup once they have soaked in syrup; see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Only milk, with very faint tea**: too much water was used for steeping, so after the milk goes in it is thin. Brew a strong tea by the amounts given, take out the tea bags when the time is up, and do not strengthen it by long boiling; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
