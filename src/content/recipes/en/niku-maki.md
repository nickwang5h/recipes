*Asparagus niku-maki* has a thin slice of lightly browned pork on the outside and crisp, tender asparagus inside, wrapped in a glossy, sweet and salty thin glaze. It also packs well in lunch boxes. The surface of the rolls is dusted thinly with potato starch, and once the rolls are cooked, soy sauce and mirin are added and reduced.

The pork slices should be thin and overlap little where they wrap, so that heat reaches the inner layers more easily (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). The asparagus is blanched briefly first and then cooked with the pork, so the outside is not done while the centre of the vegetable is still firm. The small amount of starch on the surface [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when it meets the sauce, and once the sauce thickens it clings to the meat.

## Steps

1. Cut off the woody ends of the asparagus and peel the tougher lower parts thinly, then cut the spears into pieces about 5–6 cm long. Spread out the pork slices and pat the thicker spots lightly to thin them. Stir the soy sauce and mirin together in a separate bowl.
2. Put 1000 ml of water in a small pot and boil over high heat for 4–5 minutes, until it boils. Blanch the asparagus for 1 minute, until bright green and able to bend slightly but still firm. Lift out and spread it out for 2 minutes, until the steam on the surface eases, then blot dry.
3. Divide the asparagus into 8 bundles. Sprinkle each slice of pork with black pepper, lay a bundle across it and roll tightly from one end. Press the seam underneath, and avoid layering the meat into a thick lump.
4. Dust the surface of the rolls thinly with potato starch and turn them until they are evenly coated all around, shaking off extra dry starch. Preheat a frying pan over medium heat for 1 minute, add the vegetable oil, and wait until it spreads easily.
5. Put the rolls in the pan seam side down, spaced apart. Cook for 2 minutes first, until the underside has set and can be turned whole. Keep turning and cook for another 4–5 minutes, until pale golden all around and the centre of the thickest meat layer reaches 71°C (160°F).
6. Pour in the soy sauce and mirin mixture and boil over medium heat for 1 minute, rolling the rolls gently, until the sauce forms fine bubbles and coats the surface thinly, and the trail from a spatula dragged across the bottom of the pan closes up only after a moment. Take off the heat immediately.
7. Serve the rolls whole, or cut each in two on a cutting board. The asparagus should bite through easily, and the meat layer should cling to the centre.

## Key points

- **The seams come undone and the asparagus falls out**: the vegetable surface is too wet, or you turned the rolls as soon as they went into the pan. Blot dry after blanching, roll tightly but not thickly, and start by cooking the seam against the pan until it sets (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The outside is dry and tough while the inside is raw**: the pork slices are too thick or overlap too much. Choose whole thin slices, lay them flat before rolling, and turn the rolls as they cook so every side gets heat (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
- **The sauce forms a thick paste or tastes bitter**: the starch coat is too thick, or you kept cooking after the sauce had reduced. Leave only a thin layer of dry starch, and take the pan off the heat as soon as the sauce clings, since residual heat will still thicken it (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Liquid collects in the box after packing**: there was too much loose sauce, and the steam condensed into water. Let the sauce cling tightly before packing, spread the rolls out to cool for about 10 minutes, until they stop steaming noticeably, then refrigerate promptly. Reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) at the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
