Moo shu pork (*mu xu rou*) is a northern Chinese home stir-fry. The sliced pork, golden egg curds, black wood ear and cucumber each have their own texture, and the sauce only lightly coats the surface. The eggs are taken out first and returned after the pork and vegetables are done, which keeps the curds soft and the cucumber crisp.

A little starch on the pork gelatinizes in the heat and helps the seasoning cling (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The tenderness of the egg and meat depends on controlling [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/): take them out of the pan as soon as they are cooked, rather than heating them again and again. Cucumber cut into slices of even thickness heats through together in the short stir-fry (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

## Steps

1. Soak the wood ear in 500 ml of cold water for about 30 minutes, until the fronds are fully open and no hard core can be felt. Snip off the hard base, wash away the grit in the folds, tear into small pieces and drain.
2. While the wood ear soaks, slice the pork tenderloin against the grain and toss it with the marinade's light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, cornstarch, toasted sesame oil and the minced ginger. Refrigerate to marinate for 20 minutes, until the slices have a thin coating and there is no pool of marinade at the bottom of the bowl.
3. Halve the cucumber lengthwise, scrape out the soft seedy core, and slice on the diagonal about 3 mm thick. Beat the eggs with 1 tsp of Shaoxing wine and the salt. In another bowl, mix the 2 tsp of Shaoxing wine for the stir-fry, the light soy sauce, oyster sauce and 30 ml of water.
4. Add 1 tbsp of oil to the pan and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows freely. Pour in the egg and push it around for about 1 minute, until it sets into soft large curds with no liquid raw egg, then take it out at once.
5. Add the remaining 2 tbsp of oil to the same pan and heat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until faint ripples appear on the surface of the oil. Spread out the pork and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the slices separate, the edges take on a little colour, and the centre of the thickest piece reaches 71°C (160°F). Add the green onion and stir-fry for 15 seconds, until the aroma comes out.
6. Add the wood ear and cucumber and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the wood ear is hot through and the edges of the cucumber turn slightly translucent. Pour in the sauce and stir-fry for 30 seconds more, until the sauce coats everything evenly and no obvious liquid pools at the bottom of the pan.
7. Return the eggs and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until the curds are hot through and coated with a thin layer of sauce; plate once the centre of the curds reaches 74°C (165°F). Leave the curds in their natural uneven pieces and just toss them through.

## Key points

- **Pork slices are tough and stuck in clumps**: the slices were cut too thick, or the thin coating was not mixed in evenly (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Slice evenly against the grain, separate the pieces quickly once they are in the pan, and add the vegetables as soon as the meat is cooked.
- **Dry, crumbly egg**: the curds stayed in the pan while the pork cooked, and repeated heating tightened them and squeezed out water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take them out first and return them at the end just to heat through.
- **A pool of water on the plate**: the wood ear was not drained, or the cucumber was cooked too long. Extra water dilutes the thin coating (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Drain the vegetables beforehand, keep the amount of sauce fixed, and do the last few steps without stopping.
