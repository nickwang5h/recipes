*Bibimbap* is rice mixed with separately seasoned vegetables, beef and chili paste. The stone pot version is called dolsot-bibimbap: the pot keeps up its [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) from underneath, first driving off the water on the surface of the rice, then forming a thin crisp rice crust with a toasty fragrance. Handling the vegetables one at a time is what keeps each one's colour and texture.

Cooked beef and a fried egg are also common toppings. Fernbrake and bellflower root bring the texture of mountain vegetables and can be left out if you cannot buy them; soybean sprouts, spinach and bell pepper can still carry a bowl of bibimbap. The stone pot must be one the maker says can go directly on a stove.

## Steps

1. Mix the beef with the soy sauce, honey, minced garlic and sesame oil from the beef group, plus 1 tsp sesame seeds, cover and refrigerate. Sprinkle the zucchini and cucumber each with a little salt, leave for a few minutes and wipe off the liquid that comes out.
2. Cook the soybean sprouts with 60 ml water, covered, over high heat for about 5 minutes, until the beans are cooked through and have no raw bean smell. Drain, and toss with salt and 0.25 tsp sesame oil; if the pot dries out first, add a little water.
3. Blanch the spinach in boiling water for about 30 seconds, until the leaves collapse, lift out and rinse in cold water, squeeze dry, cut short and toss with salt and 0.25 tsp sesame oil.
4. Stir-fry the vegetables separately: the bell pepper with 1 tsp oil for about 30 seconds; the zucchini and the cucumber with 0.5 tsp oil each, for about 1 minute and about 30 seconds respectively. Toss the zucchini with the remaining 0.25 tsp sesame oil. Take each out as soon as it is just soft and still holds its shape.
5. If using bellflower root, rub it with salt for about 1 minute after soaking, rinse clean, drain, and stir-fry in 1 tsp oil over medium heat for about 3 minutes until soft. Cut the cooked fernbrake short and stir-fry in 0.5 tsp oil for 3–5 minutes, then add the rest of the seasonings from the mountain vegetable group and toss.
6. Spread the beef in a hot pan and stir-fry until all of it has changed colour and the edges are lightly browned, then take it out. Use the remaining vegetable oil to fry four eggs, turning them over or covering the pan so the whites and yolks set and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
7. Grease the bottom of the stone pot with a little sesame oil, divide the hot rice among the pots and press it down lightly; arrange the vegetables, beef and fried egg on the rice and add 1 tbsp chili paste to each pot. Heat over medium heat until the bottom makes a continuous light crackle and the bottom layer of rice is golden, then sprinkle with sesame seeds and a little sesame oil.
8. Set the pot on a trivet, mix while hot and gently scrape up the thin crisp crust. If you do not have a stovetop-safe stone pot, make one shared pot in a heavy-bottomed cast iron pan, and when serving, share out the crust too.

## Key points

- **Soggy bottom of the pot**: the vegetables carried water, so the bottom layer of rice kept steaming. Squeeze the spinach dry, wipe the salted vegetables dry and turn the rice as little as possible before the crust forms. See [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Bitter, burnt crust**: the bottom of the pot is heating faster than the top of the rice. Lower the heat when you smell toastiness and see a pale gold colour; if there is already a burnt smell, take it off the heat at once. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Too salty in each bite**: every vegetable was seasoned too heavily, and the chili paste adds more salt on top. Use only a little salt on the vegetables and taste again after mixing. See [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/).
