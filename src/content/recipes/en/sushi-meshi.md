Sushi rice (*sushi-meshi*) is white rice seasoned with vinegar, sugar and salt: glossy, separate grains, a light sweet-sour taste first and then the taste of rice. Nigiri, rolls and chirashi all use it.

The vinegar goes in while the rice is still very hot. Freshly cooked rice has its starch fully [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and a moist surface, so it soaks up the vinegar most easily; once the rice cools, the surface starch firms and the vinegar only clings to the outside. The vinegar gives the rice its sweet-sour base (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). At home you can't measure how acidic the rice is, so treat it like any cooked rice: use it within 2 hours at room temperature. This cooks about 660 g of rice, enough once seasoned for four rolls.

## Steps

1. Wash the rice: put it in a large bowl, add water, swirl twice and pour it off at once, then rinse once more. Add a little water and rub the rice gently in circles with your palm for 10–15 seconds, then rinse and pour off; repeat the rubbing and rinsing two or three times, until the water runs mostly clear. Keep the whole wash under 3 minutes.
2. Drain the rice in a fine sieve, then put it in the rice cooker bowl or a heavy pot with the 360 ml of water and the wiped kombu, and soak for 20–30 minutes.
3. Cook on the rice cooker's white rice setting. On the stove, cover and cook over high heat until steam escapes from the lid and it bubbles audibly, about 5 minutes; remove the kombu, cover and boil 1 minute more, then turn to the lowest heat for about 14 minutes, and turn off the heat and let it steam 9 minutes, without lifting the lid in between.
4. While the rice cooks, make the sushi vinegar: heat the vinegar, sugar and salt in a small pot over medium heat, stirring, until the sugar has dissolved and it is about to bubble, then take it off the heat; do not let it boil.
5. Wet a wooden sushi tub (*hangiri*) with water and wipe it dry with a cloth; without one, use a large shallow bowl or a lightly dampened baking sheet.
6. As soon as the rice has steamed, remove the kombu, turn the whole pot of hot rice into the tub at once and spread it out with a rice paddle. Pour the sushi vinegar evenly over the rice, letting it run down the paddle.
7. Hold the paddle at an angle and slice through the rice as if cutting it, breaking up clumps, while fanning with a fan or a piece of cardboard. Every few strokes, gently turn the rice from the bottom up; do not stir or press. It is ready when the grains are glossy and feel about body temperature.
8. Cover with a damp cloth, keep at room temperature, and use it within 2 hours. Divide leftovers into small portions while warm, and once cool, freeze them in airtight containers and use within a month.

## Key points

- **Rice clumps and turns mushy**: the grains were broken by rough washing, or the rice was stirred and pressed while seasoning, squeezing out gelatinized starch that glues the grains together (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Rub gently, wash briefly, and only cut through the rice, never stir.
- **Vinegar sits on the surface, grains are hard**: the rice cooled before the vinegar went in and could not absorb it. Have the vinegar ready and turn the rice into the tub the moment it is done.
- **Sharply sour, or too sweet and salty**: seasoned sushi vinegar was used and sugar and salt were added as well (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Use unseasoned rice vinegar; if you use bottled sushi vinegar, use the same amount and add no sugar or salt.
- **Refrigerated sushi rice is dry and hard**: chilling makes the starch in cooked rice retrograde (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep sushi rice at room temperature and eat it within 2 hours; freeze any extra in small portions rather than refrigerating it.
