*Nam phrik nam pla*, also often called *prik nam pla*, is a fish sauce and chili condiment on the Thai table. Fresh chilies are cut into small rings and steeped in fish sauce, and at the meal it is spooned over fried rice, fried eggs or basil stir-fried meat. The basic version is only fish sauce and fresh chilies, with garlic slices added to taste; the result is a clear brown liquid with chili rings floating in it.

This sauce is salty and savoury. As fish ferment, their proteins break down and release free amino acids including glutamate, which is [umami](/principles/umami/); the salt in the fish sauce goes onto the food with each spoonful, so a small spoonful can change a whole bite (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). When the chilies are cut, the cut faces sit in the fish sauce, so both the liquid and the rings carry heat; the garlic slices give a raw garlic aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the chilies. Wash and dry them, remove the stems, and slice crosswise into small rings about 3 mm thick, keeping the white ribs and seeds inside. Put them in a small bowl.
2. Add the garlic slices. Peel the garlic, slice it crosswise into thin slices that bend, and put them in the bowl.
3. Pour in the fish sauce and stir lightly so the chilies and garlic are all coated. It can go to the table now; for a softer chili texture, cover and refrigerate for 2–3 hours until the chili rings soften a little, then use.
4. Spoon into a small dish and serve with the meal. Add a little to one bite of rice to taste first, then decide how much to spoon over the whole plate. Scoop up the rings if you want them, or take only the liquid for less heat.

It goes with [Pad Kra Pao](/recipes/pad-kra-pao/). Taste the saltiness of the stir-fried meat itself first, then add the sauce. Use a clean small spoon to take it, and keep the rest covered in the fridge. Lime is often served alongside too; if you want more sourness, squeeze it straight over your own rice, where the fresh aroma is clearer.

## Key points

- **One spoonful makes it too salty**: too much sauce was poured on. Fish sauce is salty to begin with, so taste with one bite of rice and stop when the saltiness is right; if you have poured too much, eat more of the unseasoned rice or dish to spread it out (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **Garlic overpowers the fish sauce**: the garlic was cut into a fine mince, so its flavour came out too fast, or too much was used. Cut thin slices and follow the amount; if you dislike raw garlic, leave it out rather than adding more fish sauce to cover it (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Too hot to bear**: bird's eye chilies are very hot, and the steeped liquid is hot too. Try a little on rice first; if it is too hot, use fewer chilies and mix a fresh small bowl (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
