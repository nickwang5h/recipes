The shiitake are braised until soft and well seasoned, the bok choy is green and crisp, and a glossy sauce goes over the top. Braise the mushrooms first, and blanch the bok choy separately to lay underneath. Choose baby bok choy with plump stems and round, spread leaves. Dried shiitake and oyster sauce bring [umami](/principles/umami/), and the soaking liquid is kept for the sauce.

Cooking the two main ingredients separately means the bok choy does not have to wait for the mushrooms to take on flavour. The greens are blanched briefly in boiling water ([blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)), lifted out and drained at once, and sauced last. Mix the slurry first, then add it to the boiling liquid; [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) makes the sauce glossy and lets it cling.

## Steps

1. Rinse the dust off the dried shiitake and put them in a heatproof bowl with 500 ml of hot water, pressing them under with a small plate. Soak for 60 minutes, until the caps swell, bend softly, and a pinched-open centre has no dry, hard layer. If still hard, soak another 30 minutes; braise only once they are soaked through.
2. Take out the mushrooms, squeeze off extra water gently and cut off the hard stems. Let the soaking liquid settle for 2 minutes until the grit sinks, then measure 250 ml from the top and leave the sediment in the bowl. Halve the bok choy lengthwise and rinse between the leaf stalks; cut large heads into quarters.
3. Bring 1500 ml of water to a boil over high heat for about 6–8 minutes, until it is at a steady rolling boil, and add the 0.5 tbsp of oil for blanching and the salt. Blanch the bok choy for about 0.75 minutes, until the leaves have just collapsed and the stems still stand firm, then lift into a colander at once.
4. Drain the bok choy cut side down for 2 minutes, until it no longer drips continuously, then arrange around the edge of a plate with the stems outward. Pour off the water that has run onto the plate, so no blanching water gets into the sauce.
5. Add 1 tbsp of oil to a wok and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows smoothly. Add the garlic and stir-fry for about 0.25 minutes, until it smells of garlic but is still pale, then add the shiitake and stir-fry for about 20 seconds, until coated in oil.
6. Pour in 250 ml of the soaking liquid, then add the oyster sauce, dark soy sauce and sugar. Cook over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until it boils, then turn to medium-low and cook uncovered for 5 minutes, until the mushrooms are soft and the sauce has reduced a little. Taste, and add salt if it is bland.
7. Stir the cornstarch with 2 tbsp of cold water and pour half into the boiling mushroom sauce, stirring it in and cooking for about 0.5 minutes, until the sauce turns glossy. If it still will not cling to a spatula, add the rest of the slurry and cook for about 0.5 minutes more, until the sauce thinly coats the mushroom caps. Stir in the toasted sesame oil and spoon over the bok choy while hot.

## Key points

- **The dish floods into a plate of water when served**: the bok choy was not drained, and the water on the plate thinned the glaze (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Let the greens drip cut side down, pour off the water after plating, and sauce only at the end.
- **Glaze is gluey with white lumps**: the starch settled at the bottom of the slurry and was not stirred, or too much went in at once (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir the slurry again before adding it, add it in parts, and stop when the sauce turns glossy and clings to the mushroom caps.
- **Mushrooms are soft outside, hard inside, and gritty**: they were not soaked through to the centre, or the sediment from the bottom of the soaking liquid went in. Soak through before removing the stems, and use only the clear liquid from the top, keeping the mushroom aroma and [umami](/principles/umami/).
