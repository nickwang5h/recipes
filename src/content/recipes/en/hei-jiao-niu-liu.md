Black pepper beef strips get their lift from the spicy fragrance of black pepper; the beef is tender, and the onion and bell pepper are only a small side. It is seasoned only with salt, black pepper and starch, with no black pepper sauce: dry and fragrant. The pepper goes in twice: once when marinating the meat as a base, and again just before serving for another layer of aroma.

Beef tenderloin has little connective tissue and suits a short stir-fry. [Cutting the strips against the grain](/principles/knife-work/) shortens the fibres, and the thin coating [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat, forming a slick layer on the surface of the meat. The meat comes out first and the vegetables are cooked after, so the beef is not left in the heat with the onion and dried out.

## Steps

1. Find the direction of the beef's fibres and cut across them into strips about 5 mm thick and 1 cm wide, keeping the length within 8 cm. Cut off any obvious tough sinew, and keep each strip about the same thickness.
2. Toss the beef with 2 g of the salt, 3 g of the black pepper, the 15 ml of water and the starch until the water is absorbed and the surface has a thin coating. Then mix in 5 ml of the oil and refrigerate for 20 minutes, leaving no dry powder at the bottom of the bowl.
3. Cut the onion and bell pepper into strips, removing the stem and seeds of the pepper, then rinse and drain. Measure out the remaining pepper and salt in advance; once the beef is in the pan, the whole dish is done in a few minutes.
4. Put 20 ml of oil in the wok and heat over medium-high heat until the oil spreads quickly and has not yet started to smoke. Spread the beef in one layer and let the side touching the pan set first, then break it apart with a spatula; don't stir it back and forth and break it up before the coating has set.
5. Stir-fry about 2 minutes, until the surface has changed colour and the coating is no longer wet and sticky, then take the beef out. For thicker strips, cut one open to check the centre.
6. Add the remaining 5 ml of oil to the pan, add the onion and bell pepper, and stir-fry over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes. When the edges of the onion turn translucent and the bell pepper still keeps its shape and crispness, return the beef.
7. Add the remaining salt and pepper and toss quickly for about 30 seconds, until the meat and vegetables are all hot through. Cut open the thickest strip; if it is not done to your liking, stir-fry a little longer; if the vegetables are still hard while the meat is done, take the meat out and cook the vegetables on their own.
8. Plate right away when the onion is slightly soft and the strips look glossy. There should be only a little thin sauce on the plate; if the pan already has pooled water, next time stir-fry the meat in batches and drain the vegetables well.

## Key points

- **Beef strips are hard to chew**: cut too long with the grain, or with very uneven thickness (see [knife work](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut short strips against the grain, remove the tough sinew, and make the thickness even.
- **Meat turns dry and hard**: the beef was stir-fried with the vegetables too long, so the [proteins contract](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and squeeze out the juices. Take the meat out after stir-frying, and return it when the vegetables are nearly done.
- **Coating is thick and gummy, and floury lumps form in the pan**: the starch wasn't dispersed, or was coated on thickly without water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Toss the water and starch together first, then add the oil, and keep the coating thin.
- **Pepper is bitter rather than fragrant**: it was stir-fried too long at high heat, so the aroma was lost and the pepper scorched (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The pepper added at the end is only stir-fried briefly, and the oil should not be heated until smoking.
