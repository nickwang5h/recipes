The dry fragrance of dry pot cauliflower comes from two different kinds of heat: the cauliflower is briefly blanched, and the pork belly is then fried until it renders its fat, so the edges of the slices pick up the toasty flavour of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). When the cauliflower goes back in, its surface should be as dry as possible, so that it stir-fries in the pork fat instead of continuing to soften in water pooled at the bottom of the pan.

Garlic and chili perfume the oil. Smashed cloves are easier to control than fine mince; once you smell them, add the next ingredients so the cauliflower picks up this layer of flavour (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Keep the florets close in size and slice thick stalks thin lengthwise; the tips and the thick stalks heat at different speeds, so do not judge the whole piece by the tip going soft.

## Steps

1. Soak the cauliflower florets face down in light salted water for about 20 minutes, then wash under running water. Break it into florets of similar size with a small knife and slice the thick stalks thin; open out the branches while rinsing to wash out any dirt hidden in them.
2. Blanch the cauliflower in boiling water for about 1 minute, and lift it out when the surface has heated but the stalk is still firm. Rinse at once in cold water, then drain thoroughly in a colander and shake out the water hidden between the florets.
3. Cut the pork belly into thin slices, smash the garlic cloves and cut the red chili into pieces. Do not cut the slices into wedges thick on one side and thin on the other; keep the fat and lean parts joined as much as you can, so they cook through together later.
4. Put 10 ml of oil in the pan, add the smashed garlic and stir-fry until fragrant and not yet darkened on the surface. Add the pork belly at once, spread it out over medium heat and fry, turning, until all the slices have changed colour.
5. Keep frying the pork until the fat turns fairly translucent, the amount of oil in the pan increases and the edges of the slices are slightly golden. If the garlic has already turned golden, push it on top of the pork so it does not keep scorching against the bottom of the pan.
6. Add the red chili and the drained cauliflower, toss a few times and drizzle in the light soy sauce. Add the sugar and a little salt, turn to high heat and keep stir-frying for about 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves and the surface of the cauliflower is coloured with no liquid pooling at the bottom of the pan.
7. Check the largest floret: the stalk should be cooked but still have some bite. Toss in the remaining seasoning, turn off the heat, cover the pan and let it sit for 1 minute, then uncover and plate at once so the residual heat does not keep steaming the cauliflower soft.

## Key points

- **Cauliflower is limp and lacks dry fragrance**: it was blanched too long, or the water between the florets was not drained out. [Blanching](/principles/blanching/) is only preparation; drain thoroughly after it has cooled, and do not cook it for long once it goes back in the pan.
- **Pork is not fragrant and the pan is full of meat juice**: the surface was not given time to lose water and colour. Spread out the pork and fry out its fat first, then add the cauliflower, leaving conditions for the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The whole dish tastes bitter**: the garlic cloves were fried too long in plain oil. Add the pork as soon as the garlic smells fragrant (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)), and take out any garlic that has already burnt black.
