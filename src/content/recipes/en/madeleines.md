*Madeleines* are small shell-shaped cakes, ridged on the bottom, and the back often puffs a small hump in the middle. Lemon zest, vanilla and lightly browned butter give the aroma. The batter has no baking powder, and the recipe makes about 24, 3 per serving.

Chilled batter is thick. In a fully preheated oven the edges heat first and set while the centre is still expanding, which is what pushes up the hump (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Protein coagulation and starch gelatinization then fix the shape. Mix the flour in only until combined, because beating it again and again strengthens the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) and the little cakes lose their softness.

## Steps

1. Mix the flour for the batter with the sugar, add about three quarters of the beaten egg and stir with a wooden spoon into a thick paste. If it is too dry to stir, add more from the remaining egg a little at a time until no dry flour is left. Rest for 10 minutes and keep the remaining egg.
2. Put both portions of butter in a small pot and heat over medium-low heat. White bubbles rise first, then the foam turns fine. When the milk solids at the bottom turn light brown and smell nutty, take it off the heat at once. Take out about 1.5 tbsp of the butter and mix it with the 8 g of flour for greasing the pan.
3. Set the pot with the remaining butter in a bowl of cold water and stir as it cools, until it is lukewarm but still flows. Stir the remaining egg into the batter, then gradually stir in the butter. Add the salt, vanilla, lemon zest and a few drops of lemon juice, and stir until even and glossy.
4. Cover the batter and chill for at least 1 hour, until clearly thicker and a spoonful holds in a small mound. Brush the butter-flour paste thinly over the grooves of a metal madeleine pan, brushing out any that pools at the bottom, and chill the pan too.
5. Preheat the oven fully to 190°C (375°F). Put about 1 tbsp of batter in each mould, filling no more than three quarters, and leave it as a mound so it spreads out by itself in the oven. Keep the rest of the batter chilled and bake in batches.
6. Bake on the middle rack for about 12–15 minutes, until the edges are golden, the middle is domed, the top springs back when touched with a fingertip and a skewer comes out without wet batter. After removing from the oven, tap the pan to release the cakes and move them to a rack. They are best while the surface is slightly crisp and the inside is still warm and soft.

## Key points

- **The hump is not obvious**: the batter was still thin, or the oven was not fully preheated, so the edges and centre did not set at different rates enough. Chill the batter until thick and put it in a hot oven; the depth of the mould also affects the rise. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Chewy, like a small bread**: the batter was beaten again and again after the flour went in, and too much gluten developed. Stop when it is even; do not try to whip it. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Dry hard edges and dry centre**: baked too long, and the proteins keep losing water after they set. Check when the edges colour and the centre springs back, and look earlier with dark pans. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
