Seaweed egg drop soup is light. The laver gives the savouriness, and the egg forms soft, thin sheets. The flavour of the laver cooks out into the water, and a little dried shrimp makes it more savoury still (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). The egg [coagulates](/principles/protein-coagulation/) in the heat, and how you pour and stir decides whether you get sheets of egg ribbons or fine egg crumbs.

The amount of water suits splitting among several bowls to go with rice. Buy unseasoned laver made for soup. Thin laver softens as soon as it cooks, and if you wait until its flavour has come out before pouring in the egg, the ribbons do not need a long time at a boil.

## Steps

1. Tear the laver into bite-sized pieces, pick out any debris, soak in clear water for about 15 minutes, then lift out and drain. If the package says it can be cooked directly, skip the soaking. If the laver is sandy, rinse it several times, and do not pour the soaking water into the soup pot.
2. Beat the eggs until the white and yolk are mixed. Chop the green onion. If using dried shrimp, rinse them quickly first, then drain and set aside.
3. Put 1500 ml of clear water and the vegetable oil in a pot. After it boils, add the laver and the optional dried shrimp. Cook for about 3 more minutes, until the laver has opened out and softened and the soup has a clean seaweed savouriness.
4. Turn to low heat so the soup stays at a bare simmer. Hold the beaten egg up and pour it in a thin stream over different spots on the surface. Pouring it all in one place makes thick lumps. Wait until the egg that has fallen sets, then push it apart slowly once with a spoon. Stirring fast the whole time beats the ribbons into fine crumbs.
5. After all the egg is in, keep heating on low heat for about 30 seconds, until all the egg ribbons have set with no clear liquid egg. If the egg has gathered into thick sheets, cook a little longer until cooked through.
6. Sprinkle in the green onion and cook on low heat for about 20 seconds more. Taste the soup first, then add salt in portions; the dried shrimp are salty themselves, so you do not need to use all the salt. Off the heat, drizzle in the toasted sesame oil, stir lightly and serve right away.

## Key points

- **Egg is in fine crumbs and the soup looks cloudy**: the egg was stirred apart before it had [set](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Keep the soup at a bare simmer, pour in a thin stream, wait a moment, then push once gently.
- **Sandy laver, or the soup tastes bitter and salty**: the grit in the dried laver was not washed off, or seasoned snack seaweed was used. Use unseasoned laver made for soup, not snack seaweed. The [savouriness](/principles/umami/) comes from the laver itself, so rinse out any grit first.
- **No smell of green onion or sesame oil**: green onion and sesame oil cooked together for a long time lose their volatile [aroma](/principles/aromatics/). Cook the green onion only briefly, add the sesame oil off the heat, and serve once the seasoning is right.
