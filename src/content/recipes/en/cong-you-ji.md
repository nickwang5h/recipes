Green onion oil chicken has tender meat, a clean green onion fragrance and the natural flavour of the chicken still there. The chicken is poached and soaked until cooked, and only at the end is it tossed with the green onion oil, soy sauce and a little of the poaching broth. The oil holds both the sweet aroma of fried green onion whites and the fresh aroma of the green parts and minced ginger lightly heated.

The chicken is cooked only with a gentle simmer and residual heat, so the outer layer is not boiled hard the whole time (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cooling it as soon as the centre is done stops the proteins in the meat from contracting further, so it does not turn dry (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The green onion oil carries the aroma of the green onion and ginger into the fat and coats the surface of the chicken evenly (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Arrange the drumsticks in a small pot and add the green onion pieces, ginger slices and wine for poaching, along with 1500 ml of water. Heat over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water boils and foam gathers on the surface, then skim off the foam.
2. Turn to the lowest heat, cover and cook for 12 minutes, with small bubbles at the edge of the pot and the chicken skin gradually turning white. Turn off the heat, cover and soak for 10 minutes so the heat keeps moving into the thick parts. Insert a probe into the thickest part of each drumstick, away from the bone; it is cooked only at 74°C (165°F). If not, return to low heat and cook 3 minutes at a time, testing each time.
3. Cool the drumsticks in the ice water for 10 minutes, until the surface is cold and they are not hot to hold. Drain, remove the bones and skin, and tear the meat into pieces of about 2–3 cm. Keep the poaching broth and take 2 tbsp for the dressing.
4. While the chicken is poaching, make the green onion oil. Put the green onion whites and 4 tbsp of oil in a clean small pot and fry over low heat for 5–8 minutes, until the pieces are soft, the edges are light brown and the oil clearly smells of green onion. Lift out the whites and turn off the heat.
5. Add the chopped green parts and minced ginger to the hot oil and stir for about 30 seconds in the residual heat, until the fine bubbles slowly die down and the green parts are slightly soft but still bright green. Divide the green onion and ginger oil into two portions; use one half for this plate, and spread the other half in a shallow bowl to cool for about 10 minutes, until no longer warm, then refrigerate.
6. Spread the chicken on a plate and drizzle over 2 tbsp of the poaching broth, the light soy sauce and this plate's share of the green onion and ginger oil, sprinkle on the white pepper and toss gently. The liquid should only moisten the pieces of meat, leaving a thin layer on the plate; eat as soon as it is tossed.

## Key points

- **Chicken is dry with parched fibres**: it was boiled hard the whole time, or left soaking in hot broth after it was cooked, so the proteins in the meat kept contracting (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook it with a gentle simmer and a covered soak, and cool it as soon as it is done.
- **Oil is bitter with black specks**: the onion whites were fried too far, or residual heat scorched the fine green parts. Lift the whites out when the edges are light brown, and add the green parts and minced ginger after turning off the heat (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Cooked outside but not next to the bone**: the drumsticks differ in size and the thick parts heat slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Test the centre of the thick part of every one and keep cooking any that are not done; the colour beside the bone is not a reliable guide.
