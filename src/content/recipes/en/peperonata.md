*Peperonata* is an Italian bell pepper stew in which peppers, onion and tomato are the main ingredients, and the herbs and accompaniments vary by region. This is the traditional Piedmont method: the peppers are roasted and peeled, then combined with the tomatoes in one pot for a short stew, and a little cured anchovy adds a savoury saltiness to the base. The result is soft and juicy, with the pepper flesh still thick, and it goes with roast meat, pan-fried fish or bread.

Roasting and stewing are done separately. Roast first, so the skin blisters and separates from the flesh; once everything is in one pot, the only job is to let the flavours blend (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Garlic, parsley and celery leaves go into the oil first to lay down a base aroma. Basil is kept for the end so its fresh scent does not escape (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Put the whole peppers on a baking sheet under the broiler and turn them until the skin on every side blisters and large patches blacken. Take them out into a bowl and cover, so the residual heat loosens the skin. When cool enough, peel them, remove the seeds and white ribs, and cut into wide strips. Keep the juices from the peppers and do not rinse them with water.
2. Score a cross in the bottom of each tomato and put them in boiling water. Lift them out when the skin at the cuts curls up, let them cool and peel. Halve them and remove the seeds, drain the flesh in a sieve to remove excess juice, then chop.
3. Put the olive oil, onion, garlic, parsley, celery leaves and anchovies in a heavy-bottomed pot and stir over low heat until the onion begins to soften and the anchovies break down. Add the tied rosemary and bay leaves, pour in the white wine right away and keep cooking until the onion is translucent and a thin layer of liquid remains at the bottom of the pot.
4. Add the pepper strips and tomatoes, fold gently and stew over low heat for about 10 minutes. When the tomatoes begin to break down into juice and the pepper flesh can be cut with the edge of a spoon but still holds its shape, stop the heat. Do not cook it to a mush.
5. Remove the herb bundle and stir in the basil. Taste a piece of pepper with its juice, then add salt, and let it rest for a few minutes before serving, with the juices from the bottom of the pot.

## Key points

- **Bits of skin are mixed into the dish and leave tough flakes in the mouth**: the pepper skin was not roasted until blistered, or was not steamed loose after coming out of the oven (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Roast every side thoroughly before peeling, and put any hard-to-peel spots back under the broiler.
- **The whole pot is watery**: the tomatoes were not drained after seeding, or the pot was too small and evaporation too slow (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lift out the strips that are already soft, reduce the liquid on its own, then stir them back in, so the pepper flesh does not fall apart as it cooks.
- **The herbs taste bitter and the garlic tastes scorched**: the fine mince stayed in the hot oil too long (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the wine as soon as you smell the aroma and add the basil off the heat. If the base has already blackened, start over.
