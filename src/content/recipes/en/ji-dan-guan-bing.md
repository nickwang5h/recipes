Egg-filled flatbread has a crisp outside and a tender egg in the layers. The dough round is cooked until it puffs, the top layer is opened, and the beaten egg is poured into the hollow and cooked until set. A hot flatbread spread with sweet bean sauce and wrapped around lettuce is a common breakfast. This recipe makes 4 small flatbreads, each filled with 1 egg, enough for two people to share.

The flatbread puffs into a full hollow because of two things: the oil paste keeps the top and bottom layers from sticking, and the steam produced by heat pushes them apart. The dough must be soft and stretchy so that the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) can be pulled open as the bread puffs. Once the egg is poured in, heat still has to pass through the dough to reach it (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cooking both sides in turn lets the [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) inside the layers set all the way through.

## Steps

1. Stir the flour, salt and warm water together for 2–3 minutes into a sticky, soft dough that still gathers into a ball. Coat the surface with 1 tsp of the coating oil, cover tightly and rest for 60 minutes, until the dough stretches slowly and is easier to open out than when it was first mixed.
2. Mix the 15 g flour and 2 tbsp oil for the oil paste into a thick paste and divide it into 4 portions. Divide the dough into 4 too. Lightly coat the board and rolling pin with coating oil, and press each piece of dough into a small round that is thick in the middle and thin at the edge.
3. Put a portion of oil paste in the centre of each round, lift the edges around it to enclose it, and pinch the seam shut, keeping the paste off the seam. Set it seam side down, cover and rest for 10 minutes, until a light press barely springs back. Then roll slowly into a round about 17 cm across, keeping the top and bottom layers intact so the paste does not press through.
4. Divide the green onion into 4 portions. Before cooking each flatbread, beat 1 egg in a small bowl with a spout and stir in one portion of green onion. Preheat a skillet over medium heat for 2 minutes, until oil spreads easily in the pan without smoking. Add 1 tsp of the cooking oil and put in one flatbread.
5. Cook the first side for about 2 minutes, until the bottom is set with light golden spots. Add another 1 tsp cooking oil, flip, and cook 1–2 minutes, until large blisters join across the surface. With a chopstick, open a slit about 2 cm long at the edge of the top layer, keeping clear of the escaping steam. Gently lift the opening and pour in the whole portion of egg in two additions, tilting the bread so the egg flows around the hollow.
6. Turn the heat to medium-low, cover and cook for 1–2 minutes, until the egg at the opening sets. Flip and cook another 1–2 minutes, until both sides are golden and the egg inside is fully set. Insert a thermometer through the opening into the egg layer: at 74°C (165°F) it is done. If not, cook 1 minute more on each side.
7. Spread each cooked flatbread with a quarter of the sweet bean sauce, and if you like it spicy, a quarter of the chili oil, then fold it around a piece of lettuce. Cook the other three the same way. Eat each flatbread as soon as it comes off the heat, because leaving it in the hot pan dries it out.

## Key points

- **The flatbread does not puff and has only scattered small blisters**: the oil paste was not sealed in as a continuous layer, or the dough tore while rolling. Close the seam cleanly, roll gently, and let the relaxed [gluten](/principles/gluten/) stretch it open. Dough rounds that have not gone in the pan can be re-wrapped and reshaped.
- **Egg leaks out as soon as you pour it in**: the chopstick pierced the bottom layer, or the egg went in before the hollow had puffed. Let the bread heat until it makes enough steam ([heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)) and open only the edge of the top layer. Cook any egg that leaks out until it is set.
- **The crust is burnt while liquid egg remains inside**: the heat is too high and the egg layer heats unevenly. Lower the heat, cover and flip, and wait until the [protein has coagulated](/principles/protein-coagulation/) before wrapping in lettuce.
