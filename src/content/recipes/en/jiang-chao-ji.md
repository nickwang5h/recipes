Ginger stir-fried chicken uses a lot of ginger. It is both a vegetable in the dish and the base for the whole pan of oil. The slices are stir-fried first, and the cut tissue releases its aroma, then the oil carries the aroma onto the chicken (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). In the finished dish you eat soft ginger slices, and the chicken is coated in a concentrated sauce.

The chicken is browned first and then braised. Driving off the surface water first builds the savoury flavour of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Then the beer loosens the browned bits on the bottom of the pan and keeps the meat next to the bone heating. Bone-in pieces vary in thickness, so judge doneness by the thickest spot.

## Steps

1. Prepare the chicken: pat the surface dry and pick out bone chips. Cut the thicker pieces smaller so the meat is about the same thickness throughout. Add all the light soy sauce and the Shaoxing wine, toss and refrigerate for 30 minutes.
2. Prepare the ginger slices, garlic and chopped chilies. Lift the chicken out of the marinade and drain off the liquid at the bottom of the bowl. Keep the marinade aside to go into the pan later with the beer and cook through.
3. Heat the pan, pour in the oil, and add the ginger when the oil flows freely and ripples on the surface. Turn the heat to medium and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until the ginger smells strongly and the edges of the slices soften.
4. Add the chicken, spread it out, and stir-fry for about 3 minutes, until the surface changes colour, the steam from the pan lessens and chicken fat begins to come out. If the pan is small, brown in batches and then return everything to the pan.
5. Add the chilies, pickled chilies, garlic and dark soy sauce and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, so the chicken takes on an even colour and the aroma of the garlic and pickled chilies goes into the oil.
6. Pour in the beer and the reserved marinade, bring to a boil, then turn to medium-low and braise. Braise for 2 minutes first, then insert a thermometer into the centre of the thickest piece, away from the bone: it is done at 74°C (165°F). If not, keep braising, and keep liquid in the bottom of the pan the whole time.
7. When the chicken is cooked through, turn the heat to high and reduce the sauce, tossing until it thinly coats the pieces with a little glossy sauce still in the pan. Taste, add salt and plate right away.

## Key points

- **Pale chicken and weak ginger aroma**: there is too much water in the pan, so the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) cannot happen on the surface. Drain the pieces before they go in, brown the chicken in batches, and add the beer after browning.
- **Blackened ginger and bitter garlic**: the aromatics overheated in spots (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Stir-fry the ginger over medium heat and add the garlic later. If the oil keeps smoking, take the pan off the heat to cool.
- **Cooked outside, raw next to the bone**: a quick stir-fry only sets the [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) at the surface. After braising for 2 minutes, measure the centre temperature, and reduce the sauce only once it is cooked through. Do not force thick pieces in a dry pan.
