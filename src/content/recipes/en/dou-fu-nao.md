This is the northern style of tofu pudding with a savoury gravy: the tender pudding is sliced into the bowl with a flat spoon and covered with a gravy of wood ear, daylily buds, shiitake, pork and egg ribbons. The pudding tastes only of soybeans and all the saltiness is in the gravy, so each bite is a light, soft pudding with a rich, savoury gravy.

The proteins in the soy milk are first unfolded by cooking, and the calcium ions from the gypsum help them link into a network that holds the water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The gravy thickens by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Lift the set pudding out gently, and thicken the gravy so that it still flows, or it will crush the tender pudding.

## Steps

1. Soak each dried ingredient separately in 120 ml of warm water for 30 minutes, until the wood ear has unfurled, the centres of the shiitake are soft and the daylily buds bend. Rinse them. Trim the hard base from the wood ear and shred it, remove the stems from the shiitake and slice them, and trim the hard ends from the daylily buds and cut them into pieces. Strain the grit out of the shiitake and daylily soaking liquid and keep 240 ml in total from the two; discard the wood ear soaking liquid.
2. Set the container of chilled soy milk in 500 ml of warm water, without letting the water get into the soy milk, for 10–15 minutes, until it is close to room temperature. In a shallow heatproof bowl with a capacity of at least 700 ml, stir the gypsum with 2 tbsp of the soy milk into an even suspension. Pour in the rest of the soy milk while stirring, scraping up the powder from the bottom of the bowl. Mix well, skim off the foam and cover with a small heatproof plate to keep water from dripping in. The total amount of soy milk is still 400 ml.
3. Add 1000 ml of water to the steamer and bring to a boil over high heat for 5–8 minutes, until it boils steadily. Put in the bowl of soy milk, cover and steam over high heat for 10 minutes, until the surface has set and the whole pudding trembles as one piece when you shake it gently. If the centre still moves like liquid, steam for another 2 minutes and check again. Turn off the heat and let it rest for 5 minutes, until the gel is stable, then lift off the lid gently.
4. Cook the gravy ingredients. Add the oil to a pan and stir-fry the shredded pork belly over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until the shreds turn white and the fat starts to render. Add the dried ingredients and the white parts of the green onion and stir-fry for 1.5 minutes, until you smell the green onion. Add the Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, 0.25 tsp of salt, 240 ml of water and the 240 ml of soaking liquid you kept.
5. Cook the gravy. Bring to a boil, then turn to low heat and cook for 2 minutes, until the daylily buds are soft all the way through and the pork shreds are cooked through. Mix the cornstarch with 1 tbsp of water, stir it up again and drizzle it in a thin stream, push it through the gravy and cook for another 5–7 minutes, until the gravy is shiny and thinly coats the back of a spoon.
6. Drizzle the beaten egg in a fine stream into the gently simmering gravy and cook for 1–2 minutes, until the egg ribbons are fully set, the gravy bubbles again and the egg gravy reaches 74°C (165°F). Taste and add salt if needed, then scatter in the green parts of the green onion. With a wide flat spoon, slice the pudding into large pieces and scoop them into two bowls, then pour half of the hot gravy over each.

## Key points

- **The pudding does not set or is coarse and firm**: the soy milk concentration and the amount of gypsum do not match, or you used a soy drink that contains thickeners. The proteins cannot form a network (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose soy milk made from soybeans and water, and weigh the coagulant.
- **There are powder grains at the bottom and the top is still thin**: gypsum is a suspension and sinks. Keep stirring while you pour in the soy milk and scrape up the powder so that [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) is even throughout; do not wait for the powder to settle before it goes into the steamer.
- **The gravy has white lumps**: the starch slurry was not mixed well, or it was poured all at once into still liquid. The starch has to disperse before it is heated and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); drizzle it in a thin stream and push gently, and it will not clump.
