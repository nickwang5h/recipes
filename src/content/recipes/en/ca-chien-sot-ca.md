*Cá chiên sốt cà* is a Vietnamese home dish of fried fish with tomato sauce. The fish is fried through first, a tomato sauce with green onion and garlic is cooked separately, and then it is poured over the fish and eaten with rice. Fish sauce brings savour and sugar softens the acidity; canned crushed tomatoes form the sauce base, and fresh tomato keeps soft small pieces.

The fish fillets must be dry before they go into the pan, with only a thin coat of starch on the surface. The water has to evaporate first, and then the surface heats up easily and forms a golden aroma (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Take the fish out as soon as it is fried, so it does not keep cooking in the acidic sauce and lose water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Pour the sauce over only just before serving, and the surface of the fish keeps more crispness.

## Steps

1. Prepare the fish. Dry the fillets with paper towels, check for and pull out any remaining fine bones, and cut into pieces large enough to lift with one spatula. Sprinkle salt and pepper on both sides, pat on a thin coat of cornstarch, and shake off the excess. Thaw frozen fish in the refrigerator first for 8–12 hours, until there is no ice in the centre, then dry and season.
2. Fry the first side. Put 3 tbsp oil in a frying pan and heat over medium-high heat for 1–2 minutes, until the oil shows ripples and the fish sizzles steadily when it goes in. Leave gaps between the fillets and fry for 3–5 minutes, until the underside is golden, the edges are opaque, and the fish lifts easily with a spatula; do not push it back and forth before the surface has set.
3. Fry the second side. Turn it over and fry for 3–5 minutes more, until the other side is golden, and the flesh at the thickest part is opaque and flakes easily. If the centre is still translucent, lower the heat and fry 1 minute longer. Put it on a plate, pour off the leftover oil, and wipe out any black bits at the bottom of the pan first.
4. Make the sauce base. Put the remaining 1 tbsp oil in the same pan and stir-fry the shallot and garlic over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, until the shallot is soft and the garlic is fragrant. Add the canned crushed tomatoes and 80 ml water, and cook over low heat for 3 minutes, until the sauce bubbles evenly and still closes slowly after a spatula is drawn across the bottom.
5. Finish the tomato sauce. Add the sugar, fish sauce and fresh tomato and cook for 2–3 minutes more, until the sugar has dissolved, the fresh tomato is soft, and the sauce thinly coats the back of a spoon. Turn off the heat, spoon the sauce over the middle of the fish and leave a little of the golden surface showing at the edges, sprinkle with the optional green onion, and serve at once.

## Key points

- **The fish sticks to the pan and breaks when turned**: the surface was too wet, or the outer layer had not yet set (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Dry it thoroughly, add the fish only when the oil is hot, and turn it when the underside is golden and has loosened.
- **The fish flakes apart and is dry**: after frying, it was kept turning in the sauce and kept coagulating and losing water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Finish the fish and sauce separately, lift the fish with a spatula, and pour the sauce over after it comes out of the pan.
- **The tomato sauce is cloyingly sweet or sharply sour**: the acidity of the tomatoes and the canned tomatoes varies, and sugar can only hold the sourness down (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Use ripe tomatoes and unseasoned canned ones, stop when the sauce coats the back of a spoon, then taste it together with a little fish; do not keep reducing for seasoning, since the sourness only gets stronger.
