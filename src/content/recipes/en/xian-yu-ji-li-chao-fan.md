The flavour of salted fish and chicken fried rice comes from a small amount of Mui Heong salted fish: it is fried with shredded ginger in oil until fragrant, and when the rice goes in, each grain absorbs this savoury oil. The fish is cut into small pieces so the saltiness spreads evenly; the principle of drawing flavour out in oil is in [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

Rice that is cooked ahead and refrigerated promptly stir-fries into loose grains more easily (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The chicken and eggs are fried first and taken out, so the pan has room for the rice to lose its moisture. A home stove has less heat, so this rice is fried in two batches, and no liquid is left at the bottom of the pan in either batch.

## Steps

1. Cut the chicken breast into small pieces of about 1 cm, mix with the oyster sauce, cornstarch, 1 tbsp of water and white pepper, and refrigerate to marinate for about 10 minutes, until the surface has a thin coating and there is no pool of liquid in the bowl. Feel through each piece of salted fish for bones, pat dry and cut into small pieces of about 0.5 cm.
2. Break up the rice, cut the green onion, ginger and lettuce, and beat the eggs. Divide all the ingredients into two portions; each batch uses 1 tbsp of oil, of which 0.5 tsp goes to the eggs and 0.5 tsp to the chicken, 1.5 tsp to frying the salted fish, and 0.5 tsp is kept for the finish.
3. Preheat the wok over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is dry and oil spreads easily when added. Add the oil for the eggs, pour in half the beaten egg and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until the egg sets into moist small curds, then move them to a clean plate.
4. Add the oil for the chicken and half the chicken, and stir-fry over medium heat for about 1–2 minutes, until the pieces separate and turn white on the outside, then take them out first.
5. Add the oil for frying the salted fish, then half the ginger and the salted fish, and stir-fry over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the edges of the fish pieces are slightly dry and the fragrance is strong. Add half the rice, turn to high heat and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, breaking up clumps with the spatula, until the grains are separate and there is no moisture at the bottom of the pan.
6. Return this batch's eggs and chicken, add half the green onion and the white pepper, and stir-fry for about 2 more minutes, until the chicken is cooked through and the rice is hot through. The centre of the chicken and the rice are done at 74°C (165°F); if not, stir-fry 1 more minute. Taste first, and if it is bland, add half of the fish sauce in stages.
7. Add half the lettuce and the oil for the finish, and stir-fry over high heat for about 1 minute, until the shreds have just softened and still have some crunch, then plate right away. Fry the second batch in the same order; the finished rice has distinct grains with the small pieces of salted fish evenly scattered through it.

## Key points

- **One bite too salty, the next bland**: the salted fish was cut too large, or fish sauce was added without tasting (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Cut the fish small, stir it in evenly first, and you can leave out the fish sauce.
- **Rice clumps and there is water at the bottom**: the rice or lettuce is too wet and too much went into one batch (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Break up cold rice first, dry the lettuce and add it last, and fry in batches.
- **Salted fish tastes bitter and burnt**: the fish and ginger stayed in the pan too long (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry over medium heat until fragrant and then add the rice; do not wait for the fish pieces to turn black.
