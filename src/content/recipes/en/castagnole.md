*Castagnole* are small fried balls common at the Italian carnival, about the size of a walnut, coated in sugar outside and soft inside. Anise liqueur and lemon zest are a common aroma pairing. This is the version made today with butter and baking powder.

Baking powder releases gas when it meets water and heat, and the water in the dough turns to steam and puffs up the inside; hot oil dries and colours the surface; see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/). Knead only until the dough forms a ball. With too much kneading, [gluten](/principles/gluten/) grows strong and the soft little sweets turn tough.

## Steps

1. Mix the flour and baking powder. In another bowl, mix the sugar, lemon zest and vanilla seeds, then add the eggs, softened butter, anise liqueur and salt and stir until there are no large pieces of butter.
2. Add the dry ingredients and mix with a spatula until no dry flour remains, then move to a lightly floured counter and gently bring it together into a soft dough. It only needs to hold its shape and be slightly sticky; do not knead it again and again to build elasticity. Cover and rest for about 30 minutes.
3. Roll the dough into a thick rope and divide it into portions of about 12 g, then gently roll them round. This amount makes about 30–35 balls; keep each one as close in size as you can, and pinch shut any cracks on the surface so they do not burst open at the crack once in the pot.
4. Add oil to a deep pot, leaving plenty of space above it in the pot. Heat to 170°C (340°F), measuring with an oil thermometer with the probe kept off the bottom of the pot. Fry one test ball first, turning as it fries so it colours evenly all round.
5. Cut the test ball open once golden; the centre should be soft with fine holes, with no wet raw dough. After you confirm this, fry in batches, keeping the oil at about 170°C (340°F), turning the balls until the surface is evenly golden. Wait between batches for the oil temperature to recover before adding the next.
6. Lift onto absorbent paper, drain briefly and roll in white sugar while still warm. Spread them out to let the steam escape and eat them warm or at room temperature, so the crust is not softened by trapped steam.

## Key points

- **Dark brown outside, wet and sticky inside**: the oil was too hot, or the balls were rolled too large, and the outside coloured before the heat reached the centre. Fry a test ball and cut it open; make the next batch smaller and hold the oil steady at 170°C (340°F). See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Greasy surface, colour slow to change**: too many went in at once, and the oil took too long to recover after it dropped. Reduce the number per batch, drain promptly, and do not pile freshly fried balls together. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Dense inside, chews like bread**: too much extra flour was added or the dough was kneaded too long, so the gluten is too strong. Dust on only the thin layer of flour needed to stop sticking and stop once it comes together; the baking powder must also be fresh and not damp. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
