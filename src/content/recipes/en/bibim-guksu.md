Bibim guksu (비빔국수) is a Korean thin noodle dish eaten cold, with chili paste, vinegar and sugar mixed into a glossy red sauce and cucumber for crispness. It is served with cucumber and a hard-boiled egg, and you can leave out the egg. Mix the sauce and cut the vegetables first, cook the noodles last, and eat as soon as they are tossed.

Thin noodles cook through by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) in hot water and keep absorbing water and softening after they come out. Rinsing cools them quickly and washes off the sticky starch on the surface so the strands separate. The vinegar and sugar set the sweet-sour balance of the sauce (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Draining at the end matters just as much: water on the noodles dilutes that balance.

## Steps

1. If using an egg, put it in a small pot with water to cover by about 2 cm and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes until the surface bubbles continuously. Turn to low and cook 10–12 minutes, then cool in cold water for 2 minutes, until the shell can be held comfortably. Peel and halve; both the white and the yolk should be completely set.
2. Cut the cucumber into thin strips about 3 mm thick. Stir the chili paste, sugar, rice vinegar, soy sauce and garlic together; if using chili flakes, add them now. The sauce should be thick but run slowly from a spoon, with no undissolved sugar grains at the bottom.
3. In another pot, bring water to a boil over high heat for about 5–10 minutes, until it is rolling, then add the thin noodles and separate them at once with chopsticks. Cook according to the package time, about 3–4 minutes, and taste one before lifting them out: they should be springy with no hard core in the middle. Lower the heat when the foam rises, keeping the water boiling.
4. Pour the noodles into a sieve and rinse under running cold water for about 1 minute, turning gently as you rinse, until the whole handful has no warm spots and the surface is no longer slippery. Thin noodles break easily, so just loosen them without rubbing hard.
5. Shake the sieve a few times and drain for about 1 more minute, until the bottom of the sieve no longer drips continuously. The noodles should now be loose, with no pool of rinsing water at the bottom of the bowl; do not leave the mass in the sieve for long or it will press into a block.
6. Put the noodles in the bowl with the sauce, add the toasted sesame oil, and gently toss until every strand is evenly red. Divide into two bowls, add the cucumber, the hard-boiled egg and sesame seeds, and serve at once, so the cucumber only mixes with the noodles as you eat.

## Key points

- **Noodles stick in a clump**: they still had starchy film on the surface after cooking, or sat in the sieve too long. Rinse until fully cool and no longer slippery, drain and toss right away. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Water at the bottom of the bowl and a weak sauce**: the rinsing water was not drained off, or the cucumber sat too long after being mixed in. Keep the noodles and cucumber separate until the end, and do not add more chili paste to cover the watery taste. See [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/).
- **Soft outside, hard inside**: the thin noodles were lifted out before they were cooked through, and the hard core is more obvious after rinsing cold. Separate them when they go in, and taste one before lifting out to check that the centre is tender. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
