*Pot-au-feu* is a French family pot: beef, root vegetables and herbs cooked slowly together, with the clear broth eaten first, then the meat and vegetables. Beef chuck and bone-in short ribs are one lean and one fatty, and the [collagen in the sinew gradually turns to gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), making the meat tender and giving the broth a little silkiness.

The key is to keep it at a gentle bubble. Leave the meat in large pieces and let only an occasional small bubble break the surface; fine scum is then easy to skim, and the vegetables keep their shape. In Canada, look for chuck or blade roast at the meat counter for the whole piece of beef, and thick-cut bone-in short ribs.

## Steps

1. Tie the chuck with kitchen string and put it in a deep pot with the short ribs and marrow bones, then add the cold water and salt. Choose a fairly narrow pot that can still hold all the vegetables, and cover the meat with water without adding too much.
2. Heat over medium heat until bubbles start to break the surface, and skim off the grey scum along the surface with a spoon. Keep skimming as new scum appears, until the surface is fairly clean.
3. Lower the heat, leave the lid ajar, and keep a very gentle simmer for about 45 minutes. If the surface keeps rolling, lower the heat further; if the liquid drops and exposes the meat, top up with hot water to the original level.
4. Toast the cut side of the onion in a dry pan until brown and stick in the clove. Tie the celery, chervil, bay leaf and the optional garlic into an herb bundle, put the peppercorns in a spice bag, and add them to the broth with all the vegetables.
5. Keep simmering gently for about 3–3.5 hours. When a knife tip passes through a vegetable easily it can be lifted out early, keeping a little broth with it to stay moist; do not let them fall apart while waiting for the meat to soften. The meat is done when a fork goes easily into the thickest piece and a gentle twist separates it along the fibres.
6. Return the vegetables to the hot broth to warm through, lift out the beef and cut off the string. Strain the broth to remove the herbs and bits, skim off excess fat from the top with a spoon, then taste and adjust the salt. Cut the meat into thick slices, put it on a plate with the vegetables, and ladle over a little broth to keep it moist; serve the rest of the clear broth separately.

## Key points

- **The meat is cooked but still has tough sinew**: the muscle is done, but the collagen has not softened enough. Keep it at a bare simmer and check the thickest piece (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). If you use tenderloin in place of the shoulder, long cooking dries it out.
- **The broth is cloudy and the meat edges are ragged**: the boil was too hard, and fat and scum were stirred into the broth. Lower the heat first, then skim the fat and scum; do not turn the meat often (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The vegetables fall apart when picked up**: the root vegetables were cut too small, or already cooked through and kept simmering. Keep them in large pieces, check each separately and lift out whichever is done first; return them to the broth to warm before serving.
