The home filling for Korean dumplings (*mandu*) often combines meat, tofu and vegetables. The tofu is soft, the bean sprouts and garlic chives add crunch and fragrance, and the sweet potato glass noodles give the filling some spring. This recipe makes steamed pork and vegetable *mandu*, about 40 of them, enough for eight people to share. The pork is cooked first, the vegetables are drained first, and then everything goes into round wrappers.

The zucchini is drawn out with [salt](/principles/salt/), and the tofu and bean sprouts are also squeezed dry, so the filling does not soak the wrappers after wrapping. The glass noodles are cooked until [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) leaves them soft inside, then chopped, so no hard pieces remain in the filling. A drained filling should be loose and moist, hold in a heap when scooped, and leave no water at the bottom of the bowl.

## Steps

1. Toss the zucchini and onion with 1.5 g salt and let stand at least 15 minutes, until the shreds go soft and water collects at the bottom of the bowl. Squeeze out the liquid by the handful, keep the shreds, and do not pour the squeezed liquid into the filling.
2. Put the ground pork in a pan and stir-fry over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until the bits separate. Add the garlic, ginger, soy sauce and 0.5 tsp pepper and keep stirring for 3–4 minutes, until all the pork has changed colour and the centre of the largest clump reaches 71°C (160°F). Take off the heat, stir in 0.5 tsp sesame oil and move to a large bowl.
3. Take 30 ml from the 2000 ml of water and set it aside for sealing. Bring the rest to a boil over high heat, about 5–10 minutes, until the surface is rolling. Add the glass noodles and cook for 7–8 minutes, until translucent and soft with no hard core when bitten through. Lift out and rinse under cold water for about 1 minute, until there are no warm spots, then drain for 1 minute, until it no longer drips continuously. Cut into short pieces about 1 cm long.
4. Bring the water in the same pot back to a boil for about 1 minute, then blanch the bean sprouts for 1 minute, until the stems soften. Lift out and rinse under cold water for about 1 minute, squeeze dry and chop. Add the vegetable oil to a skillet and stir-fry the squeezed zucchini and onion for 1 minute, until the shreds are just cooked, then add to the pork bowl.
5. Squeeze the tofu in a clean cloth until no stream of water runs out, then crumble it into the pork bowl. Mix in the glass noodles, bean sprouts, garlic chives, the remaining salt, the remaining pepper and 1 tsp sesame oil. Spread the filling out loosely and cool for about 10 minutes, until it stops steaming and feels only slightly warm, then begin wrapping.
6. Put about 1 tbsp of filling in the centre of each wrapper. Moisten the edge with the reserved water, fold in half into a half moon, press out the air and pinch the seam tightly. The sealed edge should hold only clean dough. Leave space between the filled dumplings, and cover the unused wrappers so they do not dry out.
7. Line the steamer tray with perforated steaming paper and set the dumplings on it, not touching. Heat the steamer water over high heat for about 5–10 minutes, until steam is rising steadily, then add the tray, cover tightly and steam for about 10 minutes, until the wrappers are cooked and soft, the seam has no dry white band and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Steam in batches, and keep the remaining filling and dumplings in the refrigerator while they wait.
8. Mix the dipping sauce ingredients. Take the steamed dumplings out right away and eat them dipped in the sauce. Lift each one by supporting the bottom, so you do not pinch only the hot, soft seam.

## Key points

- **Filling leaks water and the wrapper bottoms split**: the vegetables and tofu were not squeezed dry. Draw out water with salt, blanch, then squeeze, and do not mix the vegetable liquid back in. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Hard noodle pieces in the filling**: the glass noodles were not cooked through, and the water in the filling is not enough to keep softening them while steaming. Cook until there is no hard core before chopping. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Wrapper cooked but centre still cold**: the dumplings are overfilled or crowded together, so heat reaches the centre more slowly. Keep gaps between them and check the centre temperature before taking them out. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
