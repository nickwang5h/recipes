A home-style griddle flatbread is soft and layered. The softness comes from the half-scalded dough and the layers from the oil paste rolled into it. Hot water lets part of the starch absorb water early ([starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)) and also changes how well the flour proteins form a network. The half mixed with cold water keeps more chew. Together they give a dough that rolls out well and a cooked flatbread that is not too hard.

Resting spreads the water evenly and lets the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) relax. The oil paste keeps the rolled layers apart, so the final rolling only needs to press the flatbread to an even thinness without crushing the layers. The paste is mixed with room-temperature oil, and the flour in it cooks as the flatbread is griddled.

## Steps

1. Divide the flour for the dough into two halves. Gradually add the hot water to one half and stir with chopsticks into shaggy flakes. Stir the cold water into the other half. Once the hot flakes are cool enough to touch, knead both halves into one even, soft dough. Cover and rest for 40 minutes.
2. While the dough rests, make the oil paste: stir the paste flour, salt and room-temperature oil into a spreadable paste with no dry lumps. If the oil and flour separate, stir again before spreading.
3. Press the rested dough gently flat and divide it into four pieces. Cover three and shape one into a round, then roll it out gradually into a thin sheet. If it shrinks back as soon as you finish rolling, cover it and let it relax a little longer.
4. Spread one quarter of the oil paste evenly over the sheet, leaving a border around the edge. Cut from the centre to the edge, then roll the sheet up in a spiral from one side of the cut to form a cone. Pinch the end closed so the paste does not leak out.
5. Stand the cone on its point, press it flat from the tip down, then roll gently from the centre outward to a flatbread about 3 mm thick. Keep the thickness as even as you can. Roll it just wide enough to lie flat in the pan; it does not need to reach the edge.
6. Preheat an electric griddle or a flat pan with a lid. Brush on a layer of oil, add the flatbread, brush a thin layer of oil on top and cover. Griddle over medium heat for 1 minute, then lift the lid and check the underside. When it has just set and has pale yellow spots, flip and griddle for about 1 minute more.
7. Lower the heat to medium-low and keep flipping as the colour builds, until both sides are golden and the flatbread puffs slightly. Cut the first one open to check: the centre should be cooked layers that separate, with no wet, sticky band of raw flour. Lift it out and griddle the rest in turn.

## Key points

- **The sheet keeps shrinking back**: the tension in the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) has not relaxed after kneading and rolling. Give it its full rest and shape gently after dividing. Kneading it again whenever it shrinks only makes it harder to roll.
- **The layers stick into one**: the oil paste was spread unevenly, or the last rolling was too thin and too heavy (see [Gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Roll the cone up tightly, close the end, and stop rolling once the thickness is even.
- **Browned outside and raw inside**: the surface colours fast before the centre is hot through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). After the first 2 minutes of flipping, lower the heat and keep griddling under the lid. It is cooked only when the cut centre has no wet flour.
