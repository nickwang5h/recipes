Stir-fried small anchovies (*myeolchi bokkeum*) are sweet, salty, dry and toasty, a side dish often packed into Korean lunch boxes, made with small dried anchovies. Pick the small, whole-fish stir-fry grade, which can be eaten in one piece, so the heads and guts do not need to be removed. Each person has a small pinch with rice.

The small fish are stir-fried in oil first and then coated with the sauce. Frying the fish first and coating after keeps them dry, and the syrup clings in only a thin layer. The residual heat of a hot pan is enough to make a little sugar sauce bubble (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)); take the pan off as soon as the fish are coated, because the sugar coating thickens as it cools and cooking it dry makes the fish stick into hard clumps.

## Steps

1. Put the small fish in a dry strainer and shake gently to sift out crumbs and powder, then pick out any foreign bits. Keep the whole fish, and neither soak nor rinse them in water, so they do not go into the pan wet.
2. Mix the soy sauce, water, sugar and rice syrup into a sauce, scraping up the sugar from the bottom of the bowl so it is evenly moistened. Keep the toasted sesame oil and toasted sesame seeds for the end.
3. Preheat a skillet with the vegetable oil over medium heat for 1 minute, until the oil spreads quickly. Add the minced garlic and stir-fry for about 10 seconds, until you can smell the garlic and it is still pale.
4. Add the small fish and stir-fry over medium heat for 3–4 minutes, until they are dry and turn a faint golden colour, with a toasty smell. Keep lifting them from the bottom of the pan, and move the fish at the edge to the centre so none blacken in one spot.
5. Push the fish to the side of the pan and take the pan off the heat, then pour the sauce into the empty space in the middle. Stir for about 0.5 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and the sauce forms fine bubbles. If the residual heat is not enough, heat over low heat for 0.5 minutes more, and turn off the heat as soon as it just bubbles.
6. Fold the fish into the sugar sauce and stir off the heat for about 0.5 minutes, until the fish are evenly glossy and there is no pool of sauce at the bottom of the pan. Add the toasted sesame oil and toasted sesame seeds and toss, then spread out on a plate at once, and do not leave them in the hot pan to keep heating.

## Key points

- **The whole dish is too salty**: dried fish already carry salt, so only a little soy sauce is needed for seasoning. Do not substitute the large soup-stock fish or add extra salt, and judge the saltiness along with the rice (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **Bitter, burnt taste in the fish**: crumbs or minced garlic scorched first and carried the bitterness through the whole dish. Sift out the crumbs first, keep the heat at medium while frying the fish, and add the fish as soon as the garlic is fragrant (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **A hard lump after cooling**: the sugar sauce was reduced too dry over the heat. Coat the fish off the heat and spread them out while they can still be pulled apart, rather than packing hot fish tightly into a small bowl (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The sugar coating turns damp in a lunch box**: steam was sealed in the box, or it was packed with a wet dish. Spread it in a shallow container, refrigerate promptly, cover tightly only when cold, and pack it in its own small compartment with the rice. The next day, reheat to 74°C (165°F) in the centre, stirring halfway to separate the fish so they heat evenly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
