Stir-fried broccoli with shrimp is seasoned with a garlicky white sauce. The shrimp are sweet, and the broccoli keeps clearly defined florets. A thin starch sauce carries the seasoning to the surface of both ingredients; the finished dish should only look glossy, not leave a pool of liquid on the plate. Mix the white sauce with cold water first, then cook it in the hot pan until it turns glossy (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

The thick stems of the broccoli and the shrimp heat at different speeds, so treat them separately first and then stir-fry them together briefly. The broccoli is blanched until just cooked through ([Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)), and the shrimp are scalded until the outside changes colour and finish cooking in the white sauce. This shortens the time the shrimp wait for the vegetable to cook.

## Steps

1. Get the main ingredients ready. Check the raw shrimp for veins, pat them dry and toss with the 1 tsp of cornstarch for coating, so the surface has an even, thin layer of starch. Wash the broccoli and cut it into small florets; peel the outer skin off the thicker tender stems and slice them thin, so stems and florets finish at about the same time.
2. Mix the white sauce. Stir together the 60 ml of water, the cornstarch, salt, sugar, chicken bouillon powder and Shaoxing wine from the white sauce until the starch is dispersed and there are no dry lumps. Set the minced garlic aside separately, and stir the sauce again before it goes into the pan, because the starch settles at the bottom.
3. Blanch the broccoli. Pour 1000 ml of water into the pot and heat over high heat for about 4–5 minutes until it boils steadily. Add the broccoli and blanch for 30 seconds, until the colour turns bright green and the outside of the stems is slightly soft but the centre still feels firm. Lift out immediately and drain.
4. Scald the shrimp. Bring the water back to a boil for about 0.5 minutes, until it is rolling, then add the shrimp and scald for 0.25 minutes, until the outside turns white and the centre is still translucent. Lift out at once. The shrimp are not cooked through yet; they finish cooking during the stir-fry. Pour out the blanching water and wipe the pan dry.
5. Bring out the garlic aroma. Add 2 tbsp of oil to the pan and heat over medium-high heat for about 0.5 minutes, until the oil spreads quickly. Add the minced garlic and stir-fry for 0.25 minutes, until you smell the garlic and it is still pale, then pour in the white sauce, stirred again.
6. Stir-fry together until cooked through. Cook the white sauce for about 0.5 minutes, until it bubbles and starts to turn glossy. Add the shrimp and broccoli and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the sauce thinly coats the ingredients and there is no liquid left at the bottom of the pan. The shrimp should be opaque all the way through and curved in a relaxed arc, with the centre of the thickest part at 74°C (165°F). Take it out of the pan right away.

## Key points

- **Shrimp are tight and hard**: they waited in the pan for the broccoli to cook, and the [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tightened more and more. Pre-cook the two separately and stir-fry them together at the end; take the shrimp out when they curve in a relaxed arc.
- **Sauce is thin or forms white clumps**: the broccoli was not drained, or the starch that settled was not stirred up again. Stir the white sauce before it goes in, and cook it until [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) makes it clear and clinging before finishing.
- **Florets go soft in a lunch box**: the first blanching was too long and reheating cooks them further. Before packing, keep the broccoli in shape with the tender stems slightly firm, and drain it before the final stir-fry (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Pack in shallow boxes, refrigerate promptly, and reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) in the centre.
