*Salsa verde* is a cold Italian sauce served with poached meat and fish. Parsley gives it the green colour and herb flavour, capers and anchovy add savouriness, and olive oil ties them into a sauce you can spoon. The version for fish uses lemon juice and a little mustard. Some regions add bread soaked in vinegar; this sauce is mostly chopped herbs and cured ingredients.

Done well, it tastes of fresh parsley, and you never bite into a lump of raw garlic. The extraction of flavour from [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/) starts with the chopping, so there is no need to fry anything. The anchovy supplies [umami](/principles/umami/), and the [acid](/principles/acid/) of the lemon keeps the oily sauce tasting bright. The finished sauce keeps its fine, small pieces, so it clings when spooned onto the fish, while a little oily liquid runs off.

## Steps

1. Wash the parsley, shake it thoroughly dry, pick off the coarse stems and chop it fine with a sharp knife. Measure after chopping; do not estimate by cramming a handful of wet leaves into a bowl.
2. Drain the capers and chop them fine. For salt-packed capers, soak and rinse them for about 5 minutes first, drain once the saltiness tastes much milder. Mash the anchovy with the back of a spoon until you can no longer see strips of fish and it spreads evenly into a paste.
3. Add the capers, anchovy, very finely minced garlic and mustard to the parsley and mix with a fork, then add the lemon juice. The base should now be a moist, finely chopped paste, with no separate clumps of fish or garlic.
4. Pour in the olive oil in several additions, stirring quickly with the fork as you pour, so the oil mixes thoroughly into the base. Turn up the parsley from the bottom of the bowl and mix until the colour and the pieces are evenly distributed through the bowl.
5. Put a small spoonful of the sauce on the cooked fish or vegetables you are serving it with and taste before deciding whether to add salt. Spoon into a small bowl and use right away. If you make it ahead, cover and refrigerate, stir again before using and take only what you need for the meal.

## Key points

- **It tastes like a mouthful of salt and the herb flavour disappears**: the capers or anchovy were not handled according to the type of packaging, and salt was added early on top. Desalt salt-packed capers, drain oil-packed anchovies, and taste with the main dish after mixing. The savoury base should support the main ingredient (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)).
- **Sharp garlic and the occasional hard lump**: the garlic was cut too coarse, so the flavour is concentrated in a few pieces. Measure the garlic as a fine mince and chop it a few more times. If the sauce is already mixed, pick out the large pieces, chop them fine and stir them back in (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **The sauce tastes dull, or is suddenly too sour**: you tasted the sauce alone without thinking about the main dish. Add the acid according to the recipe first; when adjusting, add a little at a time and taste each time with the fish (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)).
