In chicken and shiitake rice, the grains soak up the chicken juices, the chicken is tender and the mushroom slices are soft and juicy. The method is to lay chicken thigh and fresh shiitake, tossed in a thin starch coating, over uncooked rice and braise them together. Cut the chicken small and even so it cooks through at the same time as the rice.

The starch in the coating [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) as it heats and makes the surface of the chicken slippery; tenderness still depends on controlling [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Soak the rice first, spread the meat out flat and cover the pot tightly, so the rice and chicken finish at about the same time. The greens go in last, and you turn off the heat as soon as the stems are cooked through.

## Steps

1. Rinse the rice, add 600 ml of cold water and soak for 25 minutes, until the grains swell slightly and look white on the surface. Pour into a sieve and drain for 2 minutes, until the water no longer runs off in a stream. Do not use the soaking water to cook the rice.
2. Pat the chicken dry and toss with the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, dark soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, toasted sesame oil, white pepper and half of the salt, then mix in the cornstarch. Marinate in the fridge for 30 minutes, until the seasoning is absorbed and the pieces have a thin coat. The chicken can marinate while the rice soaks.
3. Remove the hard stems from the shiitake, slice them and mix into the marinated chicken. Wash the bok choy, rinsing out the grit at the cut ends, and drain for 2 minutes, until it no longer drips continuously. Shred the ginger and chop the green onions.
4. In a heavy-bottomed pot about 20 cm wide with a tight-fitting lid, mix the drained rice, 240 ml of low-sodium chicken stock, the vegetable oil and the rest of the salt, and spread it level. Spread the shiitake chicken evenly over the rice, with the chicken pieces overlapping as little as possible, and sprinkle with the ginger and the white part of the green onion.
5. Cover and heat over medium-high heat for about 3–4 minutes, until steam is coming out steadily at the rim and you can hear a gentle bubbling. Turn the heat to its lowest and braise for 25 minutes. The pot should keep a faint sound of steam and should never smell scorched.
6. Lift the lid. The rice should have no hard core. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece of chicken; at 74°C (165°F) it is done. If not, cover and cook 3–5 more minutes before adding the greens. Lay the bok choy over the rice, cover again and braise over low heat for 18–20 minutes, until the leaves collapse and the stems can just be bitten through.
7. Turn off the heat and move the pot off the still-hot burner. Leave it covered for 5 minutes so the rice surface absorbs the leftover water. Open the lid, sprinkle with the green part of the green onion, and gently fluff the rice from the edge of the pot. Divide the chicken, shiitake and greens between two portions.

## Key points

- **Rice is undercooked while the chicken surface is done**: the lid leaks or the rice was not soaked enough (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Soak and drain the rice as directed and spread the chicken out. If the rice has a hard core, add 30 ml of hot water along the edge, cover and cook over low heat for 3 minutes, then braise 5 more minutes and check.
- **Thick paste on the outside of the chicken**: the meat surface was wet and the starch did not form a thin coat (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Dry the meat before marinating, and do not pour extra water into the marinade.
- **Greens watery and rice sticky in a lunch box**: the greens were not drained, or braised too long (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). For a lunch box, stop the greens just as they are cooked; before boxing, fluff the rice, divide it into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly. The next day, stir and reheat until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
