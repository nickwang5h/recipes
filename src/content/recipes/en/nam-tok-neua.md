*Nam tok neua* is a grilled beef salad from northeastern Thailand. The steak is grilled for flavour, then sliced thin and tossed with lime, fish sauce, dried chili and toasted rice powder. It is usually eaten with [Thai sticky rice](/recipes/khao-niao/). The bowl is mostly meat slices and herbs, and the mint, cilantro and shallot bring out the grilled flavour of the beef.

The browned crust on the steak comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Slice across the grain to cut the long fibres short (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Toasted rice powder soaks up liquid, and cut herbs wilt and lose their fragrance quickly, so both go in just before serving (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Stir together the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, the marinade sugar, water, oil and black pepper. Coat the steak in the marinade, cover and refrigerate for 2 hours, turning it at 1 hour. Take the steak out at least 30 minutes before grilling.
2. Spread the raw glutinous rice in a dry pan and stir constantly over high heat for 3–4 minutes, until the grains turn evenly brown and smell like toasted rice. Spread it out to cool for 5 minutes, until it is no longer hot to the touch, then grind to a powder with a few fine grains left. Separately, dry-roast the chili flakes over low heat for 1 minute, until fragrant and slightly darker.
3. Preheat the grill for about 10 minutes, to about 200°C (400°F). Drain the extra marinade off the steak and put it on the grate. Grill for about 6–8 minutes, turning every 2 minutes, until both sides have brown grill marks. Insert a thermometer from the side into the thickest part and take the steak off the heat at 57.2°C (135°F).
4. Rest the steak on a clean plate for 5 minutes, until the juices run slowly and the centre reaches about 62.8°C (145°F). Keep the juices on the plate. Find the direction of the muscle grain and slice against it into pieces about 3 mm thick, then cut them into bite-sized pieces.
5. Put the meat and its juices in a mixing bowl. Add the fish sauce, lime juice, the dressing sugar, chili flakes, toasted rice powder and 1 tbsp water. Toss until the sugar dissolves and every slice is coated. If the rice powder has absorbed too much of the liquid, add a little more water.
6. Fold in the thin lemongrass rings, shallot, mint and cilantro, toss gently and serve. The meat slices should be moist, there should be a little dressing at the bottom of the bowl, and the herbs should still be bright green with their leaf shapes intact.

## Key points

- **The slices are like long rubber bands**: you cut with the grain, so the long muscle fibres are all still there. Slice thin against the grain; the grain can change direction within one steak, so change your knife angle with it (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
- **Gritty to bite, and the rice powder tastes bitter**: the rice was not ground fine enough, or a few grains burned in the dry pan. Keep stirring, take the rice off the heat once it is an even golden brown, and grind it after it cools (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Dry after tossing, and the herbs have wilted**: the rice powder has taken up the dressing, and the herbs sat too long in the sour, salty liquid. Keep the dressing, rice powder and herbs separate and toss them together only just before serving (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
