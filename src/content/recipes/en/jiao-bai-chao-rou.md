In stir-fried pork with water bamboo, the water bamboo is crisp-tender and the pork strips are soft. The pork gets a light starch coating first, and the starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat so the surface of the meat turns slippery. As soon as the pork is cooked it comes out of the pan, so it does not keep shrinking and losing moisture; see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).

Cut the water bamboo into slices about 5 mm thick and [blanch](/principles/blanching/) briefly so the inside gets close to crisp-tender, then it goes into the pan to pick up the fragrance of ginger and garlic. The pork and the water bamboo are cooked separately and combined at the end, where all they need is to be tossed with the seasoning and a thin sauce. Choose pale, tender water bamboo and peel off the outer husk and the coarse, fibrous old skin. Even thickness matters more than every slice having the same shape.

## Steps

1. Husk the water bamboo, peel off the old skin and cut into slices about 5 mm thick. Cut the lean pork across the fibres into strips 3–5 mm thick. Cut the ginger into fine shreds and mince the garlic, and set the stir-fry ingredients out separately.
2. Mix the pork strips with the Shaoxing wine, 1 g of the salt and the marinating water, working it with your hand until the liquid is mostly absorbed, then mix in the starch. The surface should have only a thin coating, with no loose starch water at the bottom of the bowl. Cover and set aside while you prepare the blanching.
3. Bring a generous amount of water to a boil in a pot, add the water bamboo and cook for about 60–90 seconds after the water returns to a boil. Taste a slice; when the raw taste has just gone and it still has a light crunch, lift it out and drain. Edges soften first, so check the centre of a slice too.
4. Wipe the wok dry, heat it, and add 15 ml of oil. Add the pork strips, spread and separate them quickly, and stir-fry for about 60 seconds, until the surface turns white and the strips no longer stick together. Transfer to a clean plate. It finishes cooking when everything is combined.
5. Add the remaining 15 ml of oil to the wok, turn to medium heat and stir-fry the ginger and garlic for about 30 seconds. When you smell the fragrance, add the water bamboo and stir-fry for about 30 seconds. Add the vegetables just as the minced garlic starts to turn golden, and do not wait until it blackens.
6. Return the pork and stir-fry for about 60 seconds, then add the water for combining and the optional chicken bouillon powder, and taste before deciding whether to add the rest of the salt. Keep stir-frying for about 60 seconds, until the pork is cooked through and the centre of the slices is still crisp-tender. Serve when the liquid has turned from cloudy to glossy and clings thinly to the ingredients.

## Key points

- **Pork strips stick in a lump and the surface is thickly coated**: too much starch, or pooled water at the bottom of the bowl, made the coating too thick (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Use a thin coating and separate the strips the moment they hit the pan. Next time let the pork absorb the water first, then mix in the starch.
- **Pork is tough while the water bamboo is only just done**: the pork and vegetable were stir-fried together too long, so the pork protein keeps tightening (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take the pork out first and combine only when the vegetables are nearly done.
- **Water bamboo is limp and there is water on the plate**: it was blanched too long or not drained (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Blanch only until the raw taste goes, drain before it goes into the pan, and add only a little water at the end.
- **Garlic tastes bitter and has black specks**: finely minced garlic scorches easily in hot oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Stir-fry briefly over medium heat and add the vegetables as soon as you smell the fragrance. If there is a burnt, bitter taste, clean the bottom of the wok and start the stir-fry again.
