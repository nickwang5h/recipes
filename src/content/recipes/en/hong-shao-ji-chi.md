Red-braised chicken wings are salty and sweet with a savoury depth, the wing skin coated in a glossy sauce and the meat braised soft and tender. The aroma of the caramel colour comes from [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/), and the tenderness of the meat depends on how far [proteins coagulate when heated](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Wing flats are small, so they do not need the long stewing that pig's feet do.

Make the caramel over low heat, keep the liquid at a light simmer while braising the wings, and raise the heat only for the final reduction. Caramel, braise and reduction each have a point to stop at: caramel is cooked to reddish brown and must not turn black; the wings are braised until cooked through at the centre; the sauce is reduced until it coats the wing skin and the bottom of the pan must not burn dry. If all three steps use high heat, the sugar aroma and the meat juices are lost first.

## Steps

1. Pat the surface of the wings dry with paper towels and cut two or three shallow slashes in the thick part. Slice the ginger, cut the green onion into sections, smash the garlic and set out the spices.
2. Put the wings in a pot of cold water with half the ginger slices and 20 ml of Shaoxing wine; this is [blanching to remove off-odours](/principles/blanching/). After it comes to a boil, simmer lightly for 2–3 minutes, skim off the foam, lift out the wings, rinse off the scum clinging to them, then drain and pat the surface dry.
3. In another pan, put the oil and crushed rock sugar and cook over low heat the whole time until the sugar melts and turns from golden to reddish brown. Take it off the heat at once, add the wings and toss to coat, then return to low heat and turn for 1–2 minutes so every side picks up the colour.
4. Add the bay leaf, star anise, chili (optional), the remaining ginger, the green onion sections and the garlic, and stir-fry for about 30 seconds until fragrant. Drizzle the remaining Shaoxing wine and the light soy sauce down the side of the pan and stir for about 10 seconds.
5. Pour in the hot water for braising, add the dark soy sauce, half the salt, the sugar and the pepper, bring to a boil, cover and simmer lightly over medium-low heat for 7–8 minutes. Insert a thermometer into the thickest wing, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) they are done. If not, braise a little longer.
6. Pick out the whole spices, green onion and ginger, uncover and reduce the sauce, turning the wings gently as it reduces; with little liquid left in the pan this takes about 1–2 minutes. Once the sauce is thick and coats the wing skin, taste; if it is bland, add the reserved salt. Turn off the heat and sprinkle on the roasted sesame seeds and chopped green onion.

## Key points

- **Bitter caramel, black spots on the wing skin**: high heat let the sugar burn in places first (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Cook over low heat and watch the colour, and take the pan off the heat before adding the wings; throw out burnt caramel and start again.
- **Cooked on the outside, still raw next to the bone**: wing flats differ in size and start at different temperatures, so time alone does not tell you whether the centre is done (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Choose the largest one and take its temperature with the probe away from the bone.
- **Dry meat, skin falling apart**: braising on after the meat is cooked through keeps shrinking the protein and squeezes out moisture (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). If there is a lot of liquid, lift the wings out first and reduce the sauce, then return them and toss to coat.
