*Mentsuyu* pours as freely as soy sauce and smells of smoky bonito, and tasted straight it is sweet and salty. It joins the savoury depth of dashi with the sweet-salty character of soy sauce and mirin: sake, mirin and soy sauce are simmered with kombu and bonito flakes, strained and stored, then diluted with water when used as a soba dipping sauce or a udon soup base.

Like [dashi](/recipes/dashi/), mentsuyu uses the glutamate in kombu and the inosinate in bonito flakes to build [umami](/principles/umami/). The soy sauce provides saltiness along with its fermented flavour, and the mirin provides sweetness. A short simmer is enough for thin bonito flakes to give up their flavour; boiling it down further would concentrate the [salt](/principles/salt/) along with it. Diluting by volume of mentsuyu to water is easier to control than guessing the saltiness as it cooks.

## Steps

1. Wipe the grit off the kombu with a clean, slightly damp cloth, leaving the white powder. Pour the soy sauce, mirin and unsalted sake into a small pot, add the kombu and bonito flakes, and gently push down the floating flakes so all the shavings are wet with liquid.
2. Heat over medium-low heat for about 5 minutes, until bubbles form continuously at the edge and the centre starts to simmer gently. Then turn to low and leave the pot uncovered, keeping the liquid at a bare simmer with only small bubbles on the surface and no splashing.
3. Cook over low heat for another 5 minutes, until the kombu has unfurled, the bonito flakes are soft and wet and the liquid has a clear sweet-salty bonito aroma, then turn off the heat. Do not reduce it until sticky; when you lift the spoon it should still pour freely.
4. Set the pot in an outer basin holding 1000 ml cold water, with no water getting into the pot, and cool for about 20 minutes, until the liquid stops steaming and the pot wall is only slightly warm. Strain through a fine sieve and let it drain naturally for 2 minutes, until it no longer drips continuously and the strained liquid has no obvious bits. Do not press hard on the bonito flakes.
5. Pour the concentrate into a clean, dry container with a lid and refrigerate. One batch gives about 480 ml, with some variation from evaporation and the liquid absorbed by the flakes, so add water according to the amount of mentsuyu you use.
6. For [Zaru Soba](/recipes/zaru-soba/) dipping sauce, mix 60 ml mentsuyu with 120 ml cold water to make 180 ml of sauce. For the hot soup of [Kitsune Udon](/recipes/kitsune-udon/), use 60 ml mentsuyu with 240 ml water and heat over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until it bubbles at the edge and the soup is hot through. Taste it with the noodles and adjust slightly.

## Key points

- **Still too salty after diluting**: it was reduced too far while cooking, or the sake itself contained salt. Use unsalted sake and simmer only briefly; dilute a small portion with extra water to taste, then adjust the portion you will use (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Sweet and salty but missing the bonito aroma**: too few flakes were used, or they were not pushed into the liquid. Weigh the bonito flakes, wet them through before cooking (see [umami](/principles/umami/)).
- **Cloudy mentsuyu with fine bits**: the bonito flakes were broken up, or pressed too hard when straining. Stir gently while heating and let it strain naturally after the heat is off; refrigerate the concentrate separately and dilute only what you need for the meal.
