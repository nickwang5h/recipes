This lamb soup is slightly milky in colour, the strips of meat come apart at one bite, and the sharp aroma of white pepper and green onion holds down the lamb flavour. The lamb is blanched first, so the foam and some of the off-odour go out with the water (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). It is then stir-fried and boiled hard with boiling water; a little fat is boiled into the soup, which is what turns it white (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

This uses boneless leg of lamb cut into thin strips, which can come off the heat as soon as it is cooked through. Bone-in meat, thick pieces of lamb belly and raw lamb offal need long stewing and are not suited to cooking this way. White pepper and green onion set the soup's tone; add the cumin and cilantro after ladling, and their fragrance is clearer.

## Steps

1. Trim the coarse sinew from the boneless leg of lamb and cut it across the grain into strips about 5 cm long and 0.5 cm thick, as even in size as you can. Cut the leek into short sections, wash and chop the cilantro, and set both aside in a clean bowl.
2. Put the lamb in a pot, cover with cold water, and add the Shaoxing wine and leek. Bring to a boil over high heat and keep cooking for about 2 minutes, until the foam rises and the surface of the meat changes colour. Lift out the lamb, rinse off the foam and drain. Discard the blanching liquid and the leek sections.
3. Wash the pot, add the vegetable oil and stir-fry the lamb over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the surface is slightly yellow and there is not much liquid at the bottom of the pot. Hot steam and spattering oil can burn your hands and face, so stand back and slowly add 1000 ml of boiling water along the edge of the pot.
4. Turn the heat to high and bring to a boil, then keep it at a clear rolling boil for about 5 minutes, until the surface of the soup goes from clear to slightly cloudy white. If the soup visibly evaporates, top it up with boiling water so the meat stays covered.
5. Add the white pepper and cook for about 5 more minutes. Lift out the thickest slice and cut it open: when it is no longer red inside and is soft to bite, it is cooked. If the meat has already gone tight, ignore whether the soup is white and turn off the heat at once.
6. Add the salt a little at a time, stir, taste the soup and turn off the heat. Ladle into bowls and stir in the optional cumin and cilantro. The soup is done when it has lamb flavour and peppery fragrance, the strips can be bitten through, and only a little fat floats on the surface.

## Key points

- **Clear soup with oil floating on top**: the soup did not boil hard, or the meat itself has little fat, so the fat does not disperse (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Keep the soup at a clear rolling boil.
- **Strong gamey smell and foam in the soup**: coagulated foam is still stuck to the meat after [blanching](/principles/blanching/). Discard the blanching liquid, rinse and drain the meat before stir-frying; green onion and wine can only damp the smell a little.
- **Dry, hard strips**: thick cuts or cuts with a lot of sinew were used and cooked too long. [Contracting muscle protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) keeps squeezing out water, so trim the sinew, slice thin, and stop once it is cooked through.
