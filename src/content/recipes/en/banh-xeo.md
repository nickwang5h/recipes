*Bánh xèo* is a thin, crisp Vietnamese rice flour pancake; the *xèo* in its name is the sizzle of batter hitting a hot pan. This version is the coconut milk, shrimp and pork pancake common in the south. Turmeric gives the batter a pale yellow colour, and inside are pork, shrimp and bean sprouts. Break off a piece, wrap it in lettuce and herbs and dip it in the sweet-sour fish sauce.

The batter first goes through [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with the heat so the thin pancake holds together; then the pan is left uncovered so the water evaporates and the bottom and edges crisp. A lid traps steam and can be used briefly to cook the bean sprouts, but it must come off at the end (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The batter makes 3 pancakes about 30 cm across.

## Steps

1. Mix the rice flour, cornstarch, turmeric and the batter's salt, pour in the coconut milk and 240 ml water and whisk for 1 minute, until there are no dry lumps. Rest for 20 minutes so the flour absorbs the water, then stir in the green onion rings. The batter should flow smoothly and coat the back of a spoon thinly.
2. Stir the lime juice, sugar and warm water from the dip group for 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves completely, then add the fish sauce, garlic and chili. Wash and drain the lettuce and herbs and arrange them on a plate.
3. Heat a skillet over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes, until it is hot enough that a drop of water evaporates quickly. Stir-fry in batches with the 1 tbsp oil for the filling: cook the onion for 2–3 minutes, until translucent but still crisp; cook the shallot and pork for about 3 minutes, until the meat has no raw colour and the centre of the thickest slice reaches 71°C (160°F), then add the fish sauce for the pork and the pepper. Take each out separately.
4. Return the shrimp to the hot pan and cook about 1 minute per side, until opaque inside and out and just beginning to curl, then sprinkle with the shrimp's salt and take out. Divide the cooked onion, meat and shrimp into 3 portions each and wipe the pan clean.
5. Heat the pan over high heat for about 1 minute, add 2 tsp of the pancake oil and swirl to spread it. Stir the batter from the bottom of the bowl, pour in one third, and immediately tilt the pan to spread it into a thin layer, until the batter sizzles and covers the bottom of the pan.
6. Cook for about 2 minutes, until the surface is set and the bottom begins to hold its shape, then put one portion of the cooked filling and 50 g bean sprouts on one half. Cover and cook 20–30 seconds, until the sprouts are hot all the way through and slightly softened. Uncover and drizzle the remaining 1 tsp of this pancake's oil around the edge.
7. Cook uncovered for another 2–3 minutes, until the edge is golden and lifting, a spatula slides under easily and the bottom is dry and crisp. Fold the empty half over the filling, slide it onto a plate and eat while hot. Make the other two the same way, stirring the batter from the bottom each time before pouring.

## Key points

- **Thick, soft pancake with no crisp edge**: too much batter, or the pan stayed covered so the water stayed in the pancake. Spread only a thin layer, and uncover and fry dry after the sprouts are cooked (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The first pancake is whole but the next one tears at a touch**: the flour has settled to the bottom of the bowl, so the batter poured out varies in thickness. Stir from the bottom each time and measure the amount into the pan (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Bottom is scorched but the middle is still wet**: the heat is too high, or the sprouts carried water. Drain the vegetables well, lower the heat slightly if the bottom colours too fast, and keep the pan uncovered to drive off moisture (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
