*Tarte Tatin* is an apple tart turned out upside down: apples coated in deep brown caramel sit on top of a crisp, flaky pastry. The apples, butter and sugar go in the bottom of the pan and the pastry covers them. The apple juice first dissolves the sugar, and only after the water evaporates does the concentrated sugar syrup colour near the bottom of the pan (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). So you have to judge by whether the juice has reduced, not just by the pastry turning golden.

The sweet pastry uses cold butter to coat the flour first, with little water and little kneading so that no [gluten](/principles/gluten/) develops, which is what makes it short and flaky after baking. The apples are sliced thin and packed tightly, and shrink into a firm layer when baked. Use an ovenproof pan with a solid base, 23–25 cm in diameter and about 5–6 cm deep, to leave room for the juice.

## Steps

1. Mix the flour, sugar and salt for the pastry, add the cold butter and rub it quickly into coarse crumbs with small lumps. Add the ice water in stages and stop when the dough can be pinched into a mass. Push it a few times with the heel of your hand to even it out, press it into a disc, wrap and refrigerate for about 2 hours.
2. Peel and core the apples and cut them into slices about 3 mm thick, tossing them with the 65 g of sugar. Put them in the pan right after slicing; don't let the apples sit in the juice they release.
3. Spread 30 g of soft butter thickly over the bottom and sides of the pan and sprinkle in 50 g of sugar. Pack a third of the apples tightly, and drizzle with a third of the melted butter; repeat for two more layers, sprinkle the remaining 50 g of sugar over the top and press the apples down lightly.
4. Roll the cold dough into a round about 3 mm thick and the size of the pan opening, lay it over the apples and tuck the edge against the inside wall of the pan. Poke four or five small vent holes so the steam from the apple layer can escape.
5. Bake on the lower rack of an oven preheated to 190°C (375°F) for about 45–60 minutes. When the pastry darkens, tent loosely with foil. Wearing oven mitts, tilt the pan gently: what runs from the edge should be a thick brown syrup, and the apples should be easily pierced, not a pale watery juice.
6. Take it out and set a rimmed heat-proof plate over the pan. Holding the plate and pan firmly with mitts, flip it over in one motion. While the caramel can still flow, lift off the pan, put back in place any apples left on the bottom of the pan, and let it cool to warm before cutting.

## Key points

- **Pastry is hard and shrinks back when rolled**: too much water was added or the dough was handled too much, so the gluten developed too strongly. Stop once the dough comes together and roll after chilling; if it shrinks back as you roll, put it back in the refrigerator to relax for a while. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **A puddle of liquid runs out when turned out**: the apple juice hadn't reduced, or the pan was too small or the apple layer too thick. Choose apples that keep their shape after baking, use a pan that is deep and wide enough, and judge doneness by the deep brown syrup. See [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/).
- **Pastry is burnt but the apples are still hard**: the top got too much heat and the centre hadn't caught up. Bake on the lower rack, and cover with foil once the pastry has enough colour, so the apple layer keeps softening. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Apples stick to the bottom of the pan**: it cooled for too long and the syrup set. Get ready to turn it out as soon as it comes from the oven, and take care that hot syrup doesn't run onto your hands; a few stuck apple slices can be put back. See [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/).
