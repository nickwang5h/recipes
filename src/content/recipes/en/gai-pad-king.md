*Gai pad king* is a Thai home-style stir-fry of chicken with shredded ginger. The chicken, wood ear and onion are coated in the savoury saltiness of fish sauce, and the ginger gives warmth and can also be eaten as a side vegetable.

Part of the ginger goes into the pan with the garlic first, so its aroma coats the chicken through the oil. The rest is stir-fried with the vegetables to keep the bite of the ginger strips (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Everything is cut ahead so it can go into the pan one after another (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Soak 8 g of dried wood ear in warm water for at least 5 minutes, until it has opened out. Remove the hard stems, tear it into small pieces, then rinse and drain. Cut the ginger into thin strips about 4 cm long, slice the onion, chop the garlic and slice the chicken.
2. Heat a wide wok, add the oil and set the heat to medium-high. Add the garlic and part of the ginger and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until fragrant and the edges start to colour.
3. Add the chicken and stir-fry for about 1 minute, so the slices are coated in oil.
4. Add the onion, the remaining ginger and the wood ear, toss, then add the fish sauce and sugar and stir-fry a few more minutes until the onion is soft. Cut into the thickest piece of chicken: it is done when the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Serve with rice.

## Key points

- **Ginger strips are coarse and tough**: ginger cut too thick is hard to chew together with the chicken. Cut it into fine strips and fry part of it first to bring out the aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Water pooling at the bottom of the pan**: the wood ear brought in too much water, or the pan was piled too thick. Drain it after soaking and stir-fry in a wide pan (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Dry, stringy chicken**: the chicken was stir-fried for a long time after it was cooked, squeezing out water. Cut it to an even thickness and take it out as soon as it is cooked through (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Water in the bottom of the lunch box**: when packing, lift the food away from the liquid left in the pan, spread it shallow to let the steam out, then refrigerate promptly and eat within 2–3 days. When microwaving, leave the lid loose, stir halfway, and reheat to 74°C (165°F) at the centre (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
