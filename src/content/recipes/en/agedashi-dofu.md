Agedashi tofu (*agedashi dofu*) is tofu lightly coated in starch and fried, put in a shallow bowl, covered with hot dashi, and served with grated daikon, grated ginger and green onion. Just-fried, the coating is slightly crisp; once it meets the broth it turns into a thin, soft, slightly sticky layer, with tender tofu inside. The potato starch first absorbs surface moisture and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat, then loses water and sets. Only a thin coating is needed; a thick one leaves a heavy, pasty layer of starch.

There are two kinds of water to control in this dish. The loose water on the outside of the tofu is drained off first, while the water inside is kept as far as possible. After frying, the broth is poured in along the edge of the bowl, leaving the top with a little dry coating. The classic [dashi](/recipes/dashi/) pairs kombu with bonito; for the umami, see [umami](/principles/umami/). Because the dashi contains bonito, this dish is not vegetarian.

## Steps

1. Take the tofu out of its package and put it on a plate lined with paper towels. Cover it with more paper towels and a light plate, and drain for 15 minutes, until the paper is clearly wet and the tofu is still soft and can be lifted. Do not press until it visibly shrinks.
2. While the tofu drains, grate the daikon, gently squeeze out the free-running liquid and keep the purée moist. Slice the green onion into thin rings and grate the ginger to get 1 tsp.
3. Put the dashi, soy sauce and mirin in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until small bubbles rise continuously at the edge. Turn off the heat, cover, and keep it as the hot sauce for pouring; do not reduce it until thick.
4. Pour 500 ml of oil into a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until a thermometer reads 160–170°C (320–340°F) and the oil is not smoking. Cut the tofu into 6 pieces of similar size, and gently blot each side with fresh paper towels to remove surface droplets.
5. Coat only the 3 pieces about to go into the pot with potato starch, holding each piece and shaking lightly to remove the excess. Slide them one at a time into the oil along the side of the pot and fry for about 2 minutes, turning once gently halfway, until the coating has set, is a pale golden colour and a spatula can lift each piece whole.
6. Lift the tofu onto a rack and drain for 1 minute, until it no longer drips continuously. Let the oil return to 160–170°C (320–340°F), which usually takes about 1–2 minutes, then coat the other 3 pieces, fry them for about 2 minutes in the same way and drain.
7. Divide the tofu between two bowls and pour the hot sauce in from the edge of the bowl, keeping the top coating dry. Add the daikon purée, grated ginger and green onion, sprinkle the bonito flakes on last if you are using them, and serve immediately.

## Key points

- **The coating is thick, pasty and falls off**: the tofu surface was too wet, or it sat too long after being coated, so the dry starch turned into a thick paste. Coat each batch only just before it goes into the oil, thinly, and shake off the excess. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The coating is greasy and the tofu breaks**: too many pieces went in at once, the oil temperature dropped and the coating was slow to set. Fry 3 pieces per batch, let the oil recover before the next batch, and do not press and pinch them with chopsticks. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **The sauce is salty and strong**: salted concentrated noodle soup base was used in place of unsalted dashi, or the sauce was cooked too long. Measure the dashi and the seasonings separately and heat only to a gentle simmer. See [umami](/principles/umami/).
