Honey roasted chicken wings have a sticky, glossy, lightly sweet skin and a savoury inside from the green onion, ginger, garlic and soy sauce marinade. What carries the flavour into the meat is the [diffusion of salt](/principles/salt/) during the long marinade; the honey stays in the outer layer and is the sweetness you taste first.

Once the wings are in the oven, the surface first loses water, then browns and develops a roasted aroma (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Honey is high in sugar, so brushing it on thinly and late is easier to control. Roasting the meat until nearly done and then brushing on honey in two coats keeps the outer layer sticky and moist, while leaving enough time for heat to reach the meat next to the bone.

## Steps

1. Pat the wings dry. Mix the light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, dark soy sauce, green onion, ginger, garlic, brown sugar, salt and black pepper, and rub it into the wings. Put them in a food bag, press out the air and refrigerate for 12 hours, turning the bag once partway through so every side stays in the marinade.
2. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F) with top and bottom heat, about 10 minutes, until the preheat signal. Line a baking tray with heatproof baking paper. Lift the wings out of the marinade, pick off the large pieces of green onion, ginger and garlic, and arrange the wings in a single layer with the thicker-skinned side down. Discard the leftover marinade.
3. Roast on the middle rack for 15 minutes, until the surface tightens, the edges show a little colour and juices begin to collect on the tray.
4. With a clean brush, thinly brush about 3 tbsp of honey over both sides of the wings. Turn them so the thicker skin is up and roast 10 minutes more, until the skin is golden brown and the honey layer has fine bubbles and starts to turn sticky.
5. Thinly brush the remaining 1 tbsp of honey over the top and sprinkle on the sesame seeds. Roast 5 minutes more, until the surface is sticky and glossy and the edges slightly darker. Insert a thermometer into the thickest drumette, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) the wings are done. If not, lay a loose sheet of foil over them and roast another 5–10 minutes.
6. Move the wings out of the juices on the tray onto a clean plate and rest for 5 minutes, until the honey layer on the surface stops running, then serve. Do not pour the juices from the bottom of the tray back over them.

## Key points

- **Honey layer burnt and bitter**: the sugar layer is too thick or went on too early, so the surface has overheated. Control the position as in [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/): stay on the middle rack and brush thinly. Cover the parts that have coloured and let the inside keep cooking.
- **Pale wings and a tray full of liquid**: the wings carried in too much marinade, or were stacked, so the heat went into steaming off water. Drain them and leave gaps so the surface dries before it [browns](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Honey layer runny after packing, with the meat sitting in liquid**: before packing, move the wings away from the juices on the tray, portion them into shallow boxes, refrigerate promptly and cover tightly only once cold; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) for how heat moves. The next day, reheat to 74°C (165°F) in the centre, keeping the sticky honey layer.
