Cold-eaten spicy rabbit is a numbing, spicy, dry-fragrant cold dish: rabbit cut into small pieces and coated in red oil made from chilies, Sichuan peppercorns and spices, then eaten cooled, dry and fragrant with some chew. Frying first dries the surface and turns it lightly golden, and in the second stage warm oil slowly draws out the [aroma of the spices](/principles/aromatics/). The meat and the spices need different heat, so handle them separately and the chilies are less likely to blacken.

Rabbit is lean, and to be dry and fragrant it still has to keep some moisture in the centre. [Proteins contract when heated](/principles/protein-coagulation/), so the meat tightens the longer it fries, and once fried hard it won't soften again however long it soaks in oil. So fry the meat first and the spices after, and let the flavour build overnight in the fridge.

## Steps

1. Cut the rabbit into small pieces of about 2 cm and remove loose bone fragments. Mix with the salt, Shaoxing wine and MSG and marinate in the fridge for 15 minutes. Before it goes in the pan, drain off the liquid that has come out and blot the surface dry.
2. Cut the dried chilies into sections of about 2 cm, rinse, drain and wipe dry. If you rinsed any whole spices, let them dry too. Chop the ginger and garlic and cut the green onion into sections.
3. Put the frying oil in a deep pot, leaving a good gap between the oil surface and the rim. Add the green onion sections over medium-low heat and fry until the fragrance is strong and the edges are slightly yellow, then lift them out. Don't let the oil smoke.
4. Turn to medium heat to bring up the temperature and test with one piece of rabbit first: the surface should keep bubbling and not darken too quickly. Fry the rabbit in batches until the surface is lightly golden. Keep the thermometer probe away from the bone and insert it into the thickest piece; lift out and drain at 74°C (165°F).
5. Carefully pour the frying oil into a heatproof container and leave only 60 ml in the wok. Turn to low heat and add the star anise, cassia, bay leaves, sand ginger, white cardamom and fennel seeds. Stir-fry until fragrant, then add the dried chilies and green Sichuan peppercorns. Keep stirring, and move on to the next step while the chilies are still red and glossy.
6. Return the rabbit, add the oyster sauce and stir-fry for a few minutes so each piece is coated in the spiced oil. Just before turning off the heat add the ginger and garlic and stir-fry until the raw smell fades. Off the heat, mix in the toasted sesame seeds and pick out the large spices such as the cassia, star anise and bay leaves.
7. Spread the rabbit in a shallow container and refrigerate once it stops steaming; leave it at room temperature for no more than 2 hours in total. Refrigerate overnight for the flavour to set. Take out only what you will eat, keep the rest chilled, and finish it within 2–3 days.

## Key points

- **The oil spatters violently**: the rabbit still carries marinade, or rinsed chilies and spices are still wet (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Dry them, add them in batches, and put in less if the pot is crowded.
- **Black outside, raw inside, and a hard texture**: the heat was too fierce and the surface cooked before the centre. Keep control of the [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/), stop frying at a light golden colour, and then measure the centre temperature.
- **Blackened chilies and a bitter dish**: the hot oil from frying the meat was not cooled, so the spices overheated (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Keep a little warm oil aside and bloom the spices over low heat; if you smell burning, redo that batch.
