Golden coin eggs are hard-boiled eggs cut crosswise into rounds, pan-fried until the edges are golden, then briefly stir-fried with chili and doubanjiang so they carry a salty, spicy, savoury sauce. Both the white and the yolk must first [coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and set so the rounds stand up when sliced. Letting the eggs cool before cutting breaks them less than cutting while hot.

The slices can take a thin coating of starch. It [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) on contact with surface moisture, then dries into a shell. A thick coating gives a floury taste and soaks up too much sauce. Chili and garlic add fragrance at the end, and the dish comes off the heat once a thin sauce coats the slices.

## Steps

1. Boil the eggs. Put the eggs in cold water to cover, bring to a boil over high heat, then turn to low and keep at a gentle boil for 8 minutes. Take one out and check: the white and the yolk should both be set. If the yolk is still wet and soft, keep cooking the rest.
2. Cool the eggs. Lift them into cold drinking water until they are cool enough to hold firmly, then peel off the shells. Cut the long green chili and the bird's eye chilies into small pieces, mince the garlic finely and chop the green onion. Stir the light soy sauce, oyster sauce and sugar together into a sauce.
3. Cut the egg slices. Use a sharp knife to cut crosswise, dividing each egg into 4 slices of similar thickness, and wipe the yolk off the knife after each cut. If coating in starch, dust every side thinly with cornstarch, then shake off the loose extra.
4. Fry the first side. Add 25 ml of oil to the pan, or 20 ml if not coating in starch, heat over medium-low heat and put the slices in in a single layer. Wait until the bottom has set and the edges are light golden, then turn them with a wide spatula.
5. Fry the second side. Add 10 ml of oil along the edge of the pan and fry until the second side is also slightly yellow. Gently push the slices to one side and put the long green chili, bird's eye chilies and garlic in the cleared space, and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until you smell the fragrance.
6. Fry the doubanjiang. Put the doubanjiang in the oily space and stir-fry for a few dozen seconds, until the oil turns red and the paste smells fragrant, then push the slices gently to spread the stir-fried ingredients evenly. Pour in the sauce as soon as the doubanjiang starts to stick to the pan.
7. Coat and serve. Do not keep tossing the slices until they break, and do not simmer them long in thick sauce: pour in the sauce, shake the pan lightly or lift from the bottom with a spatula so the slices pick up a coat of sauce. Sprinkle with green onion, turn off the heat and plate.

## Key points

- **The slices fall apart and the yolk runs out**: the yolk had not fully [set](/principles/protein-coagulation/), or the knife pressed too much. Cook the eggs through and let them cool, then cut crosswise with a sharp knife and wipe the blade clean.
- **The coating looks white and tastes of raw starch**: the coating was too thick and the inner layer did not absorb enough water to [gelatinize](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Leave only the layer that sticks to the slices and shake off all the loose starch.
- **Garlic and doubanjiang turn bitter**: the finely chopped aromatics stayed too long in hot oil. Following the key points of [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/), add the sauce as soon as you smell the fragrance to cool the pan, and do not add more salt, since the seasonings are already salty.
