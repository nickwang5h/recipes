Tomato beef puts thin slices of beef in a sweet and sour tomato broth, with enoki mushrooms that soak up the broth to eat alongside. It is a home-style main dish for rice; the broth has bits of tomato and the aroma of meat and still flows when you scoop it. The tomato, chicken stock and beef together provide [umami](/principles/umami/), tomato paste adds body and a little sugar softens the acidity.

Use hot pot style beef with fat marbling, in thin slices. Thin slices have a short distance from the surface to the centre and cook quickly in hot broth (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Soften the tomatoes and season the broth first, then add the beef, which shortens the time the meat waits in the pan; rolled beef also has to be loosened so the hot broth reaches every slice.

## Steps

1. Cut the tomatoes into dice of about 1 cm, mince the ginger and garlic, and trim and separate the enoki. Loosen the beef slices one by one, and put them back in the refrigerator when ready, to take out once the broth is boiling.
2. Add the oil to a pot and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily. Add the ginger and garlic and stir-fry for 0.5 minutes, until fragrant and the garlic is still light in colour.
3. Add the tomatoes and stir-fry over medium heat for 3 minutes, pressing them gently with the spatula as you go, until the edges of the tomatoes have collapsed and there is clear red juice at the bottom of the pot, with some small pieces still left.
4. Pour in the chicken stock and add the oyster sauce, light soy sauce, tomato paste and sugar. Stir to dissolve the tomato paste and cook over medium-high heat for 2–3 minutes, until the whole pot is boiling and the colour of the broth is even, with no dark red lumps left.
5. Add the loosened beef slices and gently spread them out with chopsticks. Cook over medium heat for 3–4 minutes, skimming off the foam, until all the slices have changed colour and no longer stick together in rolls, and the surface of the broth is clearly visible again. Keep it at a gentle simmer and do not break up the slices by stirring repeatedly with the spatula.
6. Add the enoki and submerge them in the broth for 2 minutes, until all the bunches are heated through and hang soft, and the broth bubbles again. Stir in the white pepper and green onion, turn off the heat and transfer to a deep bowl, and serve with the tomato broth.

## Key points

- **The beef rolls are cooked outside and cold inside**: whole clumps of meat went in at once, so the broth could not reach the centre. Separate the slices one by one before adding and put them in spread out; [heat has to reach the surface first before it can move inwards](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The beef gets tougher the longer it cooks**: thick lean slices were used in place of hot pot beef, or the meat kept boiling after it was cooked. [Proteins keep contracting and lose water](/principles/protein-coagulation/); finish the broth first, then add the thin slices, and take the dish out as soon as the mushrooms are cooked.
- **The broth is very salty but has no tomato flavour**: too much stock and seasoning sauce stacked up, and the tomatoes were not ripe enough. Choose ripe tomatoes and cook out their juice, and use measured tomato paste and low-sodium chicken stock; [umami](/principles/umami/) should come from the broth, not from adding more soy sauce.
