Tian provençal is soft and juicy. The eggplant, zucchini and tomato slices still show their shapes, the edges are lightly browned, and the onion underneath is baked sweet and fragrant.

Cut the vegetables into slices of an even thickness and stand them on end in the dish. The oven's hot air and hot walls heat them from all sides, and the heat slowly works to the middle (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). All three vegetables hold a lot of water, and while there is water on the surface the heat goes first into evaporating it, so bake long enough for part of the water to cook off and the flavour to concentrate.

Salt draws water out of the vegetable cells, softening the tissue and seasoning every bite (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The onion goes at the very bottom, softened first, and soaks up the juices that run down from the vegetables.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F). Slice the onion thinly and crush the garlic; cut the eggplant, zucchini and tomatoes into rounds of an even thickness.
2. Pour part of the olive oil into a pan and cook the onion and garlic until the onion is soft and translucent. Spread it over the bottom of a baking dish.
3. Stand the eggplant, zucchini and tomato slices on end in alternating rows over the onion, packing them tightly enough that the slices support each other and stay upright. Sprinkle with salt, black pepper and thyme leaves, and drizzle with the remaining olive oil.
4. Bake in the oven for about 45 minutes, until the vegetables are tender, the surface has a little browning and the juices at the edge of the dish bubble gently. If it browns too fast, cover with a sheet of foil and keep baking.

## Key points

- **Surface is browned but the middle is still hard**: the slices are uneven in thickness, so they heat at different speeds ([heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut them as evenly as you can, and cover with foil if they brown too fast.
- **Flavour is bland**: the salt was not spread evenly. Season evenly after arranging, taste before serving, and add salt to your liking ([salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Too much liquid at the bottom of the dish**: eggplant, zucchini and tomato hold a lot of water, and salt makes the cells give it up ([salt](/principles/salt/)). Salt only after the vegetables are arranged, not ahead of time, and bake until the juices bubble gently and reduce.
- **For lunch boxes**: pack in a shallow container and refrigerate promptly; reheat the next day until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F) before eating.
