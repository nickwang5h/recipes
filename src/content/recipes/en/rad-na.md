*Rad na* is pan-fried wide rice noodles in the bottom of a bowl, topped with pork, gai lan and a thick sauce. It eats toasty and slippery at once. The noodles are first pressed against a hot pan until they pick up a toasted flavour, and the sauce goes over them afterwards. Thai soybean paste gives the salty savoury taste and fermented aroma (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)).

The sauce thickens as tapioca starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) in the heat, thick enough to cling to the noodles yet still pourable from a spoon. The starch and egg white in the marinade also dissolve into the sauce, so pour in only half the slurry first and judge the thickness once the sauce returns to a boil. For four people, fry the noodles in two batches so every piece touches the pan.

## Steps

1. Mix the pork with all the marinade ingredients, then marinate in the fridge for 20 minutes, until the egg white and starch coat the slices evenly. If you are making the chili vinegar, soak the chilies in the white vinegar for 15 minutes, until the vinegar tastes of chili.
2. If the fresh noodles are cold and stuck in a block, separate them into small bundles on a large plate and microwave at about 70% power for 2–3 minutes, turning every 1 minute, until the noodle sheets are warm and soft and peel apart gently. Separate the noodles and toss them with the black soy sauce.
3. Heat a heavy-bottomed wok over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is very hot but not smoking constantly. For each batch, add half the frying oil and half the noodles, spread them out and fry for about 1 minute, until brown spots appear on the bottom. Flip and fry for about 1 minute more, then move them to bowls.
4. Add the sauce oil, garlic and soybean paste to the wok and stir-fry over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the garlic starts to turn golden and smells of the bean paste. Add the stock, light soy sauce, Golden Mountain sauce, sugar, toasted sesame oil and white pepper, and cook for about 4–5 minutes, until the liquid is at a rolling boil.
5. Add the pork and nudge the slices apart. Cook for about 2 minutes, until the surface turns white. Add the gai lan stems and cook for 1 minute, then add the leaves and cook for 1 minute, until the leaves are soft and the stems are still crisp, and the pork and egg-white coating are cooked all the way through, to 74°C (165°F).
6. Stir the thickening tapioca starch into the 60 ml of cold water. Stir it again, then pour in half of it to the boiling sauce, pushing as you pour. Cook for about 30 seconds, until the sauce bubbles again and turns glossy. If it is still thin, add the rest of the slurry in stages, cooking 30 seconds after each, until the sauce coats a spoon in a thin layer and still flows off it.
7. Divide the pork, gai lan and sauce evenly over the noodles. Serve the chili vinegar on the side, to add at the table.

## Key points

- **The sauce is gluey and the noodles won't loosen**: too much slurry went in at once, or the marinade was not counted and thickened the sauce too. After each addition wait for the sauce to bubble again and [gelatinize](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) fully, then judge the thickness, and stop once it is thick enough to coat the noodles.
- **The noodles break into pieces**: chilled noodle sheets were pulled apart before they softened, then stirred repeatedly. Warm them until they peel apart, then spread them flat to fry. Noodles turn hard in the fridge because the starch has [retrograded](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The noodles taste only of oil, with no toasty flavour**: too many went in at once and the pan temperature dropped. Fry in two batches, spread the noodles flat against the pan (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
