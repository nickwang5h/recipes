Sour plum drink gets its sourness from smoked plums and hawthorn. Smoked jujube adds a fruity note, and dried tangerine peel, licorice and a little red cardamom form the base aroma. Rock sugar softens the [sourness](/principles/acid/), though the smokiness of the plums stays. Dried fruit varies in acidity from batch to batch, so cook it by the recipe first, then taste for strength.

Two simmers bring the flavour of the dried fruit and spices into the water, and the brews are combined and chilled. Osmanthus has a delicate fragrance that long cooking drives off, so it goes in after the drink has cooled; see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/). The total water is 2100 ml: 1500 ml for soaking and the first simmer, and 600 ml for the second simmer. Some evaporates while it cooks.

## Steps

1. Quickly rinse the jujube, smoked plums, hawthorn, licorice, tangerine peel and red cardamom, and pick out any debris. Put them in a pot with 1500 ml of water, cover and soak for at least 2 hours. In hot weather, finish the soak in the refrigerator.
2. Heat to a boil with the soaking liquid, then turn to low, cover and simmer for 40 minutes. Keep it at a gentle boil until the dried fruit softens and the liquid darkens, and do not let a strong boil cook the pot dry.
3. Put the rock sugar in a heatproof container, strain the first hot brew over it and stir until the sugar is fully dissolved. Keep the fruit and spices in the strainer and return them to the pot.
4. Add 600 ml of water, bring to a boil, then turn to medium, cover and simmer for 20 minutes. Strain again and combine the second brew with the sugar brew, stirring well. Discard the fruit and the cooked spices.
5. When the combined liquid has cooled to about 60–70°C (140–160°F), add the dried osmanthus, cover and let the aroma infuse briefly. Then divide it into shallow containers to cool quickly, and move it to the refrigerator within 2 hours of making it. Do not leave the whole pot at room temperature until fully cool.
6. After chilling for at least 3 hours, strain out the osmanthus and taste a small cup for sweet-sour strength. If it is too strong, add drinking water a little at a time, stir and portion into cups. Drink within 2–3 days refrigerated and freeze the rest in portions.

## Key points

- **Sharply sour and very sweet too**: boiling evaporated too much, so the acid and sugar concentrated together (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Add drinking water first to bring back a fresh taste, then decide whether it needs adjusting for sweetness.
- **Faint osmanthus and a prominent bitterness from the tangerine peel**: the osmanthus went in too early and was heated too long, or too much tangerine peel was used (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Weigh the ingredients, and add the osmanthus after the drink has cooled.
- **It tastes salty-sweet like a snack liquid**: salted and sweetened preserved plums or hawthorn candy slices were used. Choose unsweetened smoked plums and dried hawthorn, and check the label before swapping ingredients.
- **The drink takes a long time to cool**: the centre of a large pot of liquid cools slowly. Portion it into shallow containers and refrigerate quickly. Use clean utensils to serve, and do not keep returning it to room temperature for long.
