The prawn meat in this dish is coated in garlic butter with a sharp hit of wasabi. The pan-fried prawns are stir-fried briefly and the sauce reduced, which keeps the butter fragrance and the pungent aroma of the wasabi; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The sauce is not thickened with starch. Its thickness comes from the oyster sauce itself and the liquid reducing.

The prawns are fried and taken out first so the garlic can be fried on its own. Once the garlic smells fragrant, the prawns and butter go back in, which keeps the garlic in the hot pan for a short time. The wine and cilantro at the end each have their own aroma; add them, toss, and take the dish off the heat, so the prawn meat sees less heat too.

## Steps

1. Snip off the sharp points of the head and tail, the feelers and the legs of the prawns, cut open the back shell and score the meat shallowly, and remove the vein and any visible sand sac. Rinse and pat dry, blotting up water in the head and at the cut on the back in particular. Mince the garlic, wash, drain and cut the cilantro into pieces, and cut the butter into small pieces beforehand.
2. Stir the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, wasabi and sugar together thoroughly. Hold back the extra salt; the saltiness of the sauces themselves will be strong once reduced. Measure out the wine and keep it at hand, so you do not have to stop to prepare it during the second stir-fry.
3. Add the vegetable oil to a frying pan, heat over medium heat, and spread the prawns in a single layer. Fry for about 2 minutes on each side, until the shell turns red on both sides and is golden where it touches the pan, then take them out. If there are many prawns, fry in batches and let the pan heat up again between batches; do not pile the prawns into a heap.
4. Turn the pan to low heat and add the minced garlic. Stir in the oil left from frying the prawns until you smell the fragrance and the edges have just turned slightly yellow; if the pan is already smoking, take it off the heat to cool before adding the garlic. Return the prawns and the butter, and toss gently until the butter melts.
5. Add the mixed sauce, bring to a gentle boil over medium heat, and keep tossing so every prawn gets coated. Look at the thick meat of the largest prawn, which must be completely opaque. If the sauce starts to stick to the pan while the prawns are not yet cooked, add a little water and keep cooking.
6. When the sauce can cling to the prawn shells, take the pan off the heat first and then pour in the wine. Return it to the stove and stir until the sauce has thickened slightly again, add the cilantro and toss twice, taste and add salt if needed, and plate at once. If using rum, also pour it in off the heat, and keep the alcohol away from an open flame.

## Key points

- **Garlic turns black and the sauce is bitter**: finely minced garlic and the milk solids in the butter were heated too long (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Take the prawns out first, lower the heat to fry the garlic, and move on once you smell the fragrance.
- **Prawn meat is tight and hard**: the prawns were already cooked through and stayed in the pan while the sauce dried (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Stop as soon as the sauce clings to the shells, and do not keep frying cooked prawns.
- **The sauce is too salty in one bite**: the salt in the light soy sauce and oyster sauce concentrated as it reduced (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Use the extra salt only to adjust the taste, and leave a little sauce in the bottom of the pan rather than reducing it to a dry paste.
