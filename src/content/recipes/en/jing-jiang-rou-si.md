Shredded pork in Beijing sauce is a sauce-fried dish from Beijing: tender shreds of pork coated in sweet bean sauce, served with raw green onion shreds and eaten rolled up in tofu sheets. The sweet bean sauce sets the savoury, sweet and salty base of the dish, so fry it first until thick and glossy, and the pork only needs to be tossed through it when it returns to the pan. The white of the green onion stays raw and crisp, so every bite has the different textures of meat, sauce and green onion.

Cut the shreds an even thickness so they cook through at the same time; see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/). Egg white and starch coat the pork so a thin layer forms around the surface when heated; see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The tenderness of the meat itself still depends on controlling the stir-frying time; see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). On a home stove, spreading the shreds out in a wide flat-bottomed pan works better than piling the meat on the bottom of the pan.

## Steps

1. Cut the tenderloin with the grain into shreds about 6 cm long and 4 mm thick. Mix with the marinating Shaoxing wine and egg white, then add the salt, minced ginger and starch, mixing until there is no dry starch left on the shreds. Marinate in the fridge for 15 minutes, until no obvious liquid is left at the bottom of the bowl.
2. Stir the sweet bean sauce, the Shaoxing wine for the sauce, light soy sauce, sugar, water and sesame oil together until the sugar and sauce are dispersed and no lumps of sauce remain. Cut the green onion for frying into pieces, cut the white parts of the serving green onion into fine shreds, and set them on a clean plate.
3. Cut the tofu sheets into squares about 8 cm across. Bring 1000 ml of water to a boil, which takes about 5–7 minutes, until the surface is rolling. Blanch the tofu sheets for 2 minutes, until hot through and soft. Lift out and drain for 2 minutes, until no more drops fall, then set them next to the plate of green onion.
4. Heat 1 tbsp of the oil in a wide flat-bottomed pan over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until the oil runs freely. Add the pork and separate it with chopsticks, then stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the outside has turned white and the shreds are separate. Take it out at once, then go on to fry the sauce.
5. Add the remaining 1 tbsp of oil and the green onion pieces to the pan and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the green onion is fragrant. Turn to medium-low heat, pour in the mixed sauce, and cook for 2 minutes, stirring, until the colour deepens and the track left by the spatula across the bottom closes up slowly.
6. Return the pork and toss for about 1 minute, until every shred has a thin coat of sauce and the centre of the thickest shred reaches 71°C (160°F). The sauce should cling to the meat with no pool of loose liquid in the bottom of the pan. Take it off the heat and plate at once when cooked through.
7. Put the raw green onion shreds beside the pork or spread on the bottom of the plate, and serve the tofu sheets separately. To eat, take a sheet of tofu, put pork and green onion shreds on it and roll it up, and eat while the meat is hot and the green onion is still crisp.

## Key points

- **Shreds vary in thickness, so some are dry and some undercooked**: uneven cutting (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut slices of even thickness first, then shreds of even width.
- **Shreds stick in a lump**: coated shreds were piled in the pan, and the [gelatinized starch](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) on the outside glued neighbouring shreds together. Spread them out in a wide pan and separate them as soon as they go in.
- **The sauce tastes good but the pork is dry and hard**: the meat was cooked in the sauce too long (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Reduce the sauce first, then return the pork and coat it, and take it out once it is cooked through.
