Ice jelly wobbles when you shake it and spoons out as a soft jelly, served with osmanthus syrup poured over. It is made by hand: the gum on the surface of a plant seed is rubbed into water, then left to set into a jelly. It uses Nicandra seeds, whose pectin is linked into a network by calcium ions that holds the water in. The calcium comes from food-grade slaked lime, used only to make a clear liquid; the white sediment at the bottom does not go into the finished dessert.

Nicandra seeds, aiyu seeds and instant ice jelly powder each use different amounts of water and different setting methods, so read the label when you buy. Steeping the osmanthus off the heat keeps its fresh fragrance (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)), and lemon juice balances the syrup's sweetness and acidity (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). The syrup should be fully cooled before pouring, so the jelly stays tender.

## Steps

1. Stir the food-grade slaked lime into 300 ml cooled boiled water for the setting liquid and leave for at least 30 minutes. Gently pour off about 220 ml of the clear liquid from the top, stopping as soon as it turns cloudy, and discard the white sediment.
2. Wrap the ice jelly seeds tightly in clean, fine-meshed cheesecloth and check that no seeds leak out. Rinse the seed bag quickly in cold water, then put it in a clean basin holding 1500 ml cooled boiled water.
3. Rub the seed bag in the water for about 8 minutes, squeezing and releasing it like washing cloth. The water gradually turns slippery and sticky with fine bubbles; when the bag no longer gives off obvious slime, take the bag out.
4. While stirring gently, pour in the clear lime water in several additions so it spreads evenly. Stop stirring as soon as the liquid starts to thicken, cover and leave for about 2.5 hours, until it sets enough to be spooned into soft pieces, then move it to the refrigerator.
5. Put the sugar and the 50 ml water for the syrup in a small pot and cook over medium heat for about 6 minutes, until the sugar has completely dissolved and the syrup is slightly thick. Turn off the heat, add the osmanthus, lemon juice and a small pinch of salt, steep for about 4 minutes, then pour out and let cool.
6. Using a clean spoon, scoop the ice jelly into bowls, pour over the cold syrup and stir gently. Keep it refrigerated and finish it the same day.

## Key points

- **Stays liquid and will not spoon into a jelly**: the wrong seeds, not enough gum released, or the setting liquid was left out. First confirm you used food-grade Nicandra seeds and weigh the ingredients accurately; no amount of chilling will make up for missing calcium.
- **Powdery or alkaline taste in the finished jelly**: the lime sediment was poured in, or too much setting liquid was added. Take only the clear liquid from the top and stir it in in stages; do not substitute quicklime, construction lime or tablets.
- **Weak osmanthus taste and a heavy, coarse syrup aroma**: the osmanthus was boiled too long in the pot and its fragrance evaporated (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Cook the syrup first, then steep the flowers briefly off the heat.
- **Sourness overpowers the osmanthus**: the lemon juice was not mixed evenly into the syrup (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Stir the syrup well first, then portion it into the bowls and taste.
