The red sauce of this fish is salty, savoury and mildly spicy, and the fish is tender; spoon some sauce over the fish as you eat. The whole fish is fried first and then braised: the skin is fried until set, so the fish does not fall apart in the liquid; the sauce carries the aroma of Pixian chili bean paste, fermented black beans, ginger and garlic, and comes out of the pot as a red sauce that still flows.

The chili bean paste has to be broken up and fried in the oil first, and the water goes in once the oil turns red and the paste aroma comes out. Fat carries the flavour, and the liquid then brings the flavour to the fish (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Frying and braising each do their own part: first set the surface by frying, then let the heat gently reach the part next to the bone (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Clean out any remaining blood in the belly cavity and cut 3 diagonal slashes on each side, down to the thick flesh without cutting through the backbone. Rub the fish with the marinating Shaoxing wine and the salt, and refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the slashes are coated with the marinade too; drain off the liquid and dry the body and the cavity.
2. Add 1 tbsp oil to a wide skillet and heat over medium heat for 1 minute, until the oil flows freely and is not yet smoking. Lay the fish flat in the pan and fry the first side until the skin is light golden and the fish comes off the pan with a nudge. Support the body with two wide spatulas and flip it, then fry until the other side is set in the same way, about 5 minutes for both sides together, and take it out.
3. Lower the heat to medium-low, add the remaining 1 tbsp oil and the chili bean paste, and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the paste breaks up and the oil is red and glossy. Add the fermented black beans, minced ginger, garlic and green onion whites and stir-fry for 30 seconds, then add the water once you smell the ginger and garlic.
4. Pour in 300 ml hot water, the braising Shaoxing wine and the sugar, and cook over medium heat for 3 minutes, until the liquid bubbles steadily and the chili bean paste aroma has blended into it. Use a spatula to loosen the light brown bits left from frying the fish on the bottom of the pan, but pick out any burnt black bits first.
5. Slide the fish gently back into the pan and braise over medium-low heat for 4 minutes, spooning the liquid over the exposed part of the fish every 1 minute. When the flesh at the thickest slash is opaque and flakes easily with a chopstick, with no translucent flesh next to the bone, lift the fish onto a plate.
6. Reduce the red sauce in the same pan over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until it thinly coats the back of a spoon and still flows when you tilt the pan. Stir in the green onion greens, pour over the fish, leave a ring of red sauce on the plate and serve at once.

## Key points

- **Skin sticks to the pan and tears when flipped**: the surface is too wet, or the fish was moved before the skin set. Dry it again after marinating and lift it only once a nudge frees it from the pan (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Sauce is salty and bitter and the red oil turns black**: the chili bean paste scorched in a dry pan, or the sauce was reduced too long after the fish was cooked. Add the water once the paste gives red oil; take the fish out first and reduce the sauce separately (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Surface cooked but flesh near the bone still translucent**: a whole fish is thick, and the colour from frying says nothing about the doneness inside. Open the thickest slash to check, and if needed keep braising over low heat for 2 more minutes (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
