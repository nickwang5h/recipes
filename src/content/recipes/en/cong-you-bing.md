Green onion pancakes have a crisp edge and, inside, soft thin layers that tear apart, with fine, dense chopped green onion and a lard-based oil roux that smells wonderful. The dough sheet is spread with the oil roux, scattered with green onion, rolled up and pressed flat, then pan-baked. Two servings make 4 small pancakes, which suit breakfast with congee.

The dough is made with hot water, so some of the starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the proteins in the flour change too, which is why the dough is soft and does not spring back. After a rest the moisture spreads evenly and the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) relaxes, so it rolls out easily. The oil roux sits between neighbouring dough sheets, so they can still separate after baking; the layers are already there from the rolling and coiling.

## Steps

1. Put 250 g of flour in a heatproof bowl and pour in 160 ml of freshly boiled water. Stir with chopsticks for about 1 minute, until there is no loose flour. Spread it out and let it cool for 5 minutes, until warm enough to handle, then knead into a ball. Cover tightly and rest for 15 minutes, until the dough is soft and does not crack easily when pressed.
2. Put the lard in a small pot and heat over low heat for about 1 minute, until fully melted, then take it off the heat. Mix it with the 15 g of flour for the roux, the salt and the five-spice powder into a paste with no dry specks that spreads thinly. Keep the green onion separate, as salting it first draws out water.
3. Knead the dough again for 1–2 minutes, until the surface is smooth. Brush the oil for rolling thinly over the dough, the work surface and the rolling pin, and roll the dough into a rectangle of about 43 × 33 cm, even in thickness and rolled out to the edges, with no more dry flour sprinkled on.
4. Spread all of the oil roux evenly over the sheet, then scatter on the green onion. Roll it loosely from the short side into a long log and cut it into 4 pieces. Stand each piece on its cut end, press it flat with your hand, then roll it into a round about 13 cm across and about 0.5 cm thick. Push the rolling pin lightly so you do not break the green onion through the outer skin.
5. Preheat a frying pan over medium heat for 2 minutes, until a small piece of green onion dropped in sizzles lightly. Add the oil for pan-baking in two portions, and cook 2 pancakes per batch. Cover and cook for 2 minutes, until the bottom is golden and the edges have set; flip, cover and cook for another 2 minutes, until the other side is golden too and the centre of the pancake has no wet, raw dough.
6. Move the pancakes to a rack for 1–2 minutes, until the steam has gone but the edges are still crisp, then cut into pieces or tear apart to eat. Cook the second batch the same way; if the pan starts to smoke, lower the heat first, or the next pancakes will scorch as soon as they hit the pan.

## Key points

- **Won't roll out and keeps springing back**: the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) is still tight. Cover and rest another 10 minutes, until the dough spreads with a light push, then continue rolling; pulling it hard tears the skin.
- **No layers, like one thick flatbread**: the oil roux was missed in places, the roll was too tight, or it was pressed hard repeatedly at the end, so the layers stuck together. Roll thin gently along the [gluten](/principles/gluten/), and spread the oil roux to the edges.
- **Burnt outside, wet and sticky inside**: the surface heats too fast before heat reaches the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Turn to medium-low, cover and cook 1 minute more on each side, until the centre is no longer wet and sticky.
