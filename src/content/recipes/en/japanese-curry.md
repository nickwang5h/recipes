The sauce of Japanese curry rice (*karē raisu*) is thick and slightly sweet, and goes over white rice with distinct grains. The home method uses curry roux blocks: the meat, onion, carrot and potato are cooked soft first and then the roux goes in. The fat, flour and spices in the block are already balanced, and the flour absorbs water in the soup and thickens it by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).

Take the pot off the heat before adding the roux, so the blocks can spread and dissolve evenly, then return it to low heat. The thicker the sauce, the more easily it burns on the bottom, so keep scraping the bottom as you stir. The rice is cooked separately to keep distinct grains, so the sauce has somewhere to rest when it is poured on.

## Steps

1. Rinse the short-grain rice gently several times and drain, add 650 ml of water for cooking the rice, and soak for about 30 minutes. Put it in a heavy-bottomed pot, cover tightly and bring to a boil, then turn to the lowest heat and cook for about 12–15 minutes, until the water is absorbed and small holes appear on the surface. Turn off the heat and rest covered for 10 minutes, then fluff with a rice paddle.
2. Cut the chicken, potato and carrot, keeping the thickness of pieces of the same ingredient as even as you can. Cut the onion into wide strips, so that it softens when cooked but keeps a little of its shape.
3. Add the oil to the pot and stir-fry the chicken and vegetables over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the surface of the chicken turns white and the edges of the onion are translucent. Add the 540 ml of water for the curry, bring to a boil, skim off the foam, cover, turn to low heat and cook for about 15 minutes.
4. Poke the potato and carrot with a bamboo skewer, which should pass through the centre easily; insert a thermometer into the thickest piece of chicken, and at 74°C (165°F) it is done. If the vegetables are still hard, keep cooking covered, and soften them before adding the roux.
5. Take the pot off the heat, break the roux into pieces and add them, stirring until fully dissolved, with no hard lumps when the spoon is drawn through the soup. Return to low heat and cook for about 5 minutes, scraping the bottom as you stir, until the sauce coats the back of a spoon and still flows slowly.
6. Spoon the hot rice onto one side of a plate and the curry onto the other. If the pot has become too thick, add 1 tbsp of hot water at a time, stir in and judge again; a sauce that suits rice spreads out and also stays on the surface of the grains.

## Key points

- **Potatoes are crumbly outside and hard inside**: the pieces differ too much in thickness, or the soup was thickened before the vegetables were cooked through. Cut evenly, check the centre with a skewer first, then add the roux. See [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Lumps in the sauce**: in boiling soup the outside of the roux thickens quickly while the inside has not dissolved yet. Add the broken roux off the heat and keep cooking only after stirring it smooth. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Scorched bottom, bitter sauce**: a thick sauce flows slowly, and the part touching the pot keeps getting hot. Lower the heat and scrape the whole bottom as you stir; do not scrape the burnt bottom into the sauce, and move the unburnt top part to another pot. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
