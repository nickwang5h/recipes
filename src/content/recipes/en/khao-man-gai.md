*Khao man kai* puts poached chicken, chicken fat rice and a ginger and chili dipping sauce on one plate. The rice soaks up the chicken stock, the fermented soybeans, vinegar and chili in the sauce add savoury depth and a sour heat, and cucumber and a small bowl of clear broth come alongside.

The chicken should cook slowly in water that only barely bubbles, so the outer layer is not held at too high a heat and does not squeeze out its juices (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The garlic for the rice is fried in chicken fat first, then the rice absorbs the stock and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The fat carries the aroma, and the liquid cooks the rice through.

## Steps

1. Put the chicken pieces and the water in a pot, add the salt for poaching and heat until small bubbles keep rising at the edge of the pot. Skim off the foam. Turn the heat to low and poach for about 30–40 minutes. Put a thermometer into the thickest part of the breast and of the leg, away from the bone, and lift the chicken out only when it reaches 74°C (165°F). Cover it to keep it moist.
2. Cut the fat you set aside and a little skin into small pieces. Fry them slowly in a small pot until the fat renders and the skin turns golden, then lift out the cracklings. Measure out 4 tbsp chicken fat and make up any shortfall with the vegetable oil from the ingredients. Keep the stock for cooking the rice, mixing the sauce and serving.
3. Rinse the rice and drain it. Over medium-low heat, fry the 50 g chopped garlic in the chicken fat until pale golden, then add the rice and toss to coat. Pour in 840 ml of the poaching stock, bring to a boil, cover tightly, turn the heat to its lowest and cook for 10–15 minutes, until the stock is absorbed and the grains have no hard centre. Turn off the heat and let the rice steam, covered, for 10 more minutes, then gently fluff it from the edge of the pot.
4. Blend the ginger, garlic, chili and cilantro stems for the dipping sauce with all the sauce ingredients, then loosen with 60 ml of the stock. Heat in a small pot until it bubbles, then take it off the heat. Dip a slice of chicken in it to taste; if it is too salty, add 1 tbsp stock at a time until it goes down easily with rice.
5. Heat the remaining stock and season it with salt to taste. Take the chicken off the bone and slice it, and lay it on the rice. Serve with the cucumber, the dipping sauce and the clear broth, scatter on the cilantro leaves, and eat while the rice is still warm.

## Key points

- **Dry, tough breast**: the water boiled too hard, or the chicken kept cooking after it was done, so the juices were squeezed out. Keep the water at a bare simmer, check the temperature piece by piece, and lift out the cooked pieces first (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Wet, sticky rice with broken grains**: the rice was not drained, or it was stirred repeatedly while cooking and released too much starch. Measure the stock, do not stir once the lid is on, and fluff after the steaming rest (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Bitter garlic**: garlic keeps darkening in hot oil, so stopping when it is deep brown is too late. Add the rice when the garlic is pale golden to cool the pan, and start over if the garlic has burned black (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
