*Parmigiana di melanzane* stacks fried eggplant, tomato, cheese and basil and bakes them together. The eggplant is soft and moist, the tomato is rich and the cheese is baked until melted. The eggplant is fried until tender and tinged with gold, then goes into the oven to merge with the sauce. The thin flour coating helps the surface hold its shape and the hot oil drives the water out (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Once the cheese melts, it joins the layers together.

The key to this dish is water. The eggplant is salted and then dried, the tomato is cooked down first, and the mozzarella is drained. The oven can steam off a little water, but it cannot rescue a whole dish of thin liquid. Choose a snug baking dish so a thin layer of sauce spreads evenly between the eggplant (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Trim the stems off the eggplants, peel them and slice lengthwise about 1 cm thick. Sprinkle a little salt between the layers and leave them in a colander for about 30 minutes, until the surface sweats and the slices soften slightly, then blot each slice dry. If you used too much salt, rinse quickly and dry thoroughly.
2. Put the drained canned tomatoes and the olive oil in a wide pan and cook over medium heat until reduced by about half. Drag a spatula across the bottom of the pan: it should leave a brief empty track, with no ring of thin liquid around it. Then taste and add salt.
3. Fill a deep pot with the frying oil, no more than half the height of the pot, and heat until a small piece of eggplant dropped in bubbles steadily without smoking. Only just before frying, coat the slices on both sides with a thin layer of flour and shake off the excess. Fry in batches without overlapping.
4. Fry the slices on both sides until golden and soft in the centre. Lift out, drain and lay flat on a rack or paper towels. Between batches, skim the loose flour bits out of the oil and let the oil come back up to heat. Do not stack the fried slices in a thick pile.
5. Preheat the oven to 205°C (400°F). Thinly coat a baking dish about 28 × 18 cm with butter and lay down a layer of eggplant. Spread a thin layer of tomato sauce over it, then mozzarella, Parmigiano and torn basil. Repeat the layers, finishing the top with eggplant and the remaining Parmigiano.
6. Bake on the upper rack and check after about 20 minutes. If a pool of liquid has collected at the edges, press the eggplant gently with the back of a spoon so the juices run to the edges, spoon off the excess liquid, then bake about 15 minutes more, until the edges bubble and the surface has golden spots.
7. Let it rest for a few minutes after it comes out, until the large bubbles stop and the layers have set slightly, then cut. The cut eggplant should be soft, the cheese melted and only a small amount of thick juice on the plate.

## Key points

- **The whole dish is like eggplant soup**: the tomato was not reduced enough, or the fresh cheese brought in too much water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain the tomato and the cheese separately first, check midway through baking and spoon off any liquid.
- **Eggplant is greasy and the coating is wet and thick**: the slices were not dried, too much flour was used, or too many slices went into the pot at once (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Use a thin coat of flour, fry in batches and drain promptly.
- **Too salty**: the salt from salting the eggplant and the salt in the cheese add up (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Salt the tomato lightly at first, and taste a piece of cooked, salted eggplant before deciding on seasoning.
