Shanxi oil-passed pork has the tenderness of thin pork slices, the crunch of garlic scapes and the aroma of aged vinegar. The meat is coated in a thin starch marinade, loosened in oil (passed through warm oil), then stir-fried briefly together with the vegetables. The egg and [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) set the surface of the slices first, so they do not have to stay in the wok long when everything is combined.

The point of "passing through oil" is for the slices to separate and change colour while the finished dish stays smooth and tender. The garlic scapes are stir-fried first, and the wood ear and onion go in later, so each vegetable keeps its own texture. The aged vinegar goes in just before the dish comes out, so the vinegar aroma stays clear (see [when to add acid](/principles/acid/)).

## Steps

1. Wash the soaked wood ear, cut off the hard root and tear it into small pieces. Cut the garlic scapes into sections of about 3 cm, the onion into diamond-shaped pieces, the green onion into sections, and the ginger and garlic into slices. Drain the vegetables first, so water does not collect in the wok when stir-frying.
2. Mix 5 ml of the light soy sauce, the Shaoxing wine and half of the ground Sichuan peppercorns into the pork slices. Add the beaten egg in portions, mixing until every slice is moist and there is no pool of egg at the bottom of the bowl, then add the starch and mix into a thin coating. Mix in 5 ml of the oil, and refrigerate to marinate for 15 minutes.
3. Add the remaining oil to a small, deep pot and heat over medium heat until the surface of the oil flows but has not started to smoke. Test with one slice first: fine bubbles around it and a coating that slowly sets are right. Add the meat in batches and gently separate it with chopsticks; once the slices change colour and the coating has set, lift them out and drain off the oil. For managing oil temperature, see [passing through warm oil](/principles/oil-temperature/).
4. Leave the hot oil in the original pot to cool a little, take a separate wok and add 10 ml of the oil used for passing through. Stir-fry the green onion, ginger and garlic over medium heat until fragrant, add the garlic scapes first and stir-fry until the colour is bright and they no longer feel raw and stiff when snapped, then add the wood ear and onion and stir-fry until the edges of the onion turn slightly translucent.
5. Add the remaining light soy sauce and ground Sichuan peppercorns, return the meat, and stir-fry until all the slices are cooked through and a thin sauce coats the vegetables. Taste a cooked vegetable before deciding whether to add salt; if using chicken bouillon powder, mix it in here.
6. Drizzle the aged vinegar in along the edge of the wok, toss evenly and plate at once. There should be only a little glossy sauce on the plate; if the wok has a lot of liquid, take out the meat first, reduce the vegetable liquid slightly, and then combine them.

## Key points

- **Slices clump together in thick lumps of egg**: too much egg made the coating stick the slices to each other. Follow the idea of [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and coat thinly; after the meat goes into the oil, separate it gently and wait until it has set before moving it.
- **Slices are hard with browned edges**: the oil was too hot or the time too long, and the second stir-fry kept drawing out water. See [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/); lift the meat out once it has changed colour, and in the final combining only cook it through and season.
- **Saltiness covers the vinegar aroma**: the light soy sauce already supplies salt. Taste before adding more salt, and add the vinegar at the end (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
