The hu la tang common in Henan is a thick soup of beef and gluten, with pepper as its main spice. Washing the dough rinses the starch of a wheat dough into the water and leaves behind elastic [gluten](/principles/gluten/). Both go into the soup: the gluten gives chew, and the starchy water [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when heated and thickens the soup.

The pepper goes in close to the end of cooking, so its fragrance stays clear, though it also scatters easily. The wood ear, daylily buds, tofu sheets and noodles are all cut short so each spoonful lifts both broth and fillings. The vinegar is added bowl by bowl to keep its sour fragrance, and to shorten the time vinegar and starch cook together.

## Steps

1. Knead the flour, the salt for the dough and 250 ml of water for about 3 minutes, until it forms a firm dough. Cover and rest for 10 minutes, then knead for 1 minute, until the surface is fairly smooth. Cover and rest for another 30 minutes, until the dough can be stretched out in one continuous piece.
2. Add 720 ml of washing water to the dough, soak for 5 minutes, then knead and wash for 3 minutes, until the water is milky white. Strain off the starchy water, then wash with the remaining 1680 ml in five rounds, using 480, 480, 240, 240 and 240 ml of water for 3, 3, 4, 4 and 4 minutes respectively. Combine all the starchy water. Finally, wash with a separate 500 ml of water for about 5 minutes, until the gluten holds together in one mass and the rinse water is nearly clear; discard this rinse water.
3. Cover the gluten and rest it in the refrigerator for 2 hours, until it can be stretched into a thin sheet without snapping back. Cover the starchy water and refrigerate it too, and stir it well again before it goes in the pot. Use this waiting time to stew the beef and soak the soup ingredients.
4. Put the beef shank, beef stock, ginger, star anise and cassia bark in a pot and heat for about 5 minutes, until the broth boils and foam appears; skim it off. Cover and simmer over low heat for about 90 minutes, until a chopstick slides into the beef. Strain out the spices and cut the beef into small cubes; top the broth back up to 1000 ml.
5. Soak the wood ear, daylily buds and noodles in separate bowls of warm water for about 30 minutes, until the wood ear has opened out, the centre of the daylily buds is soft and the noodles bend. Discard the soaking water. Trim the hard base off the wood ear and shred it, remove the hard stems from the daylily buds, and cut the noodles into pieces about 5 cm long.
6. Bring the beef broth to a boil. Stretch the gluten thin, tear it into small pieces and add them to the pot. Cook over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the pieces float and are set inside. Add the diced beef, wood ear, daylily buds, tofu sheets and noodles, and cook for another 8–10 minutes, until the noodles have no hard core and everything is cooked through.
7. Stir the washing starch water well, and pour it in a little at a time while stirring the soup. Keep it at a gentle boil for about 5 minutes, until the soup coats a spoon and has no raw starch taste. If the soup has already thickened before the starchy water is used up, stop and keep the rest in reserve.
8. Mix the white pepper, black pepper, five spice powder and light soy sauce with two tablespoons of hot soup, pour it back into the pot, add the salt and turn off the heat right away, stirring until the spices are evenly dispersed. Stir in the toasted sesame oil, ladle into bowls, add the vinegar to each bowl and eat hot.

## Key points

- **Gluten tears as soon as you pull it**: the dough was not fully hydrated before washing, or it was not rested after washing (see [Gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Complete both rests, rest the washed gluten in the refrigerator, then stretch it into thin sheets.
- **Soup has starchy lumps or sticks to the bottom**: the starchy water settled and was not stirred up, or it was not pushed apart as it went in (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir to the bottom before every pour, add it in portions, and push slowly along the bottom of the pot with the spoon.
- **Pepper clumps and the sourness tastes dull**: the powder was sprinkled straight onto a thick soup, and the vinegar was cooked too long (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Loosen the pepper in hot soup first, and add the vinegar in the bowl.
