Spring pancakes are thin wheat pancakes from northern China, eaten wrapped around stir-fried fillings. They are soft, thin and springy, and do not crack easily when rolled. Half the flour is scalded with boiling water, half mixed with cold water, and then the two are kneaded together. In the scalded flour the starch takes up water and gelatinizes and the proteins denature with heat, so the dough resists shrinking back (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The cold-water dough keeps its [gluten](/principles/gluten/), which gives the pancake a little chew.

Oil is brushed between two pieces of dough, which are rolled and cooked together, then pulled apart while still warm. The oil keeps the two layers separate and lets them support each other, so they can be rolled out wide and thin. The pancakes have only a few light brown spots and stay soft. This dough makes about 36 small spring pancakes, to eat with a mixed stir-fry such as bean sprouts, chives and egg.

## Steps

1. Put 150 g of flour in a bowl and stir with chopsticks while pouring in 180 ml of boiling water, until the flour takes up the water and forms a sticky, soft dough. Let it sit for 5–10 minutes, until warm and not too hot to touch, then take it out with a scraper.
2. Add 90 ml of room-temperature water to the other 150 g of flour and stir into a mass. Stack the two pieces of dough, lightly dust your hands and the work surface with flour, and knead for about 5 minutes, until the colour and softness are even and the dough is slightly sticky but does not leave large smears of batter on your hands.
3. Cover and rest for 30 minutes, until the dough is softer and does not spring back when stretched. If it still clearly shrinks back when you test-roll it, cover and rest 10–20 minutes more, until it presses thin easily.
4. Divide the dough into two logs and cut each into 18 pieces. Work with one log first and keep the rest covered. Press two pieces at a time into small rounds, brush a thin layer of oil over the whole surface of one, then cover with the other, lining up the edges.
5. Roll each stacked pair from the centre outwards into a thin round about 13–15 cm across, turning as you roll so both layers are equally thin. Dust only lightly to prevent sticking. Cook each pair as soon as it is rolled, because wet dough rounds left sitting will stick together.
6. Preheat a heavy-bottomed skillet over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until a small crumb of dough dries quickly. Turn the heat to medium-low, lay in one stacked pancake and cook the first side for about 30 seconds, until small bubbles puff on the surface and light brown spots appear on the underside.
7. Flip and cook for about 30 seconds more, until the other side also has light spots and no wet raw dough remains anywhere. Take it out and leave it for about 1 minute, until it is cool enough to handle, then pull it apart from the edge into two pancakes, put them in a covered dish, and cook the rest of the dough.
8. Serve the thin pancakes with the stir-fried filling. Put just a small pinch of filling in the centre of each, leaving the edge clear all round. Fold up the bottom edge first, then roll it up, so the shredded filling does not spill out from below.

## Key points

- **It shrinks back as soon as you roll it**: the dough has not rested, or too much flour was dusted in while kneading (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Cover and wait 10–20 minutes before rolling, and dust only a thin layer.
- **The two layers will not pull apart**: oil was missed in some places between the layers, and wet dough touching wet dough sticks (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Brush oil right to the edge, and pull the pancakes apart from the thin edge while still warm after cooking.
- **Pancakes are dry and hard, and crack when rolled**: the heat was too low so they cooked too long, or the cooked pancakes were left uncovered and dried out (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cook each pair briefly until done, then cover straight away to keep them soft.
