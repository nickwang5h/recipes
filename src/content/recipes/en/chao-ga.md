*Cháo gà* is Vietnamese chicken congee, long-grain rice cooked in chicken stock. The rice absorbs water, swells and gradually bursts open, and the starch it releases turns the stock milky white (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The result is thick yet pourable: when stirred the rice and stock merge, and a spoonful still pours smoothly back into the pot.

The chicken is cooked in the congee first, then taken out and cut into pieces to be dipped in ginger-lime fish sauce. The congee is mild, and the dip's [acid](/principles/acid/) and ginger fragrance stand out against it. The meat and the rice each have their own finish line: the rice is cooked until soft and broken down, while the chicken is stopped as soon as it is done (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

## Steps

1. Make the dip. Stir the ginger with the lime juice and sugar for 1–2 minutes, until no obvious sugar grains remain, then mix in the fish sauce. Let it stand for 30 minutes, until the ginger fragrance has moved into the liquid and the sour, sweet and salty flavours have blended; keep cooking the congee in the meantime.
2. Rinse the rice. Stir the rice in cold water and drain off the cloudy water, then rinse gently once more; leave some of the starch, and do not wash repeatedly until the water runs clear. Cut the white parts of the green onions into sections and keep the green parts and cilantro for the bowls.
3. Start the congee. Put the rice, stock, chicken legs, ginger slices and green onion whites in a heavy-bottomed pot and heat over high heat for 8–12 minutes, until the surface boils, then partly cover. Keep a fairly lively simmer for 5 minutes, until the rice grains move up and down in the stock, scraping the bottom of the pot once; then turn to low heat so only fine bubbles break the surface.
4. Take out the cooked chicken. Cook for another 15 minutes, until the thickest part of the legs reaches 74°C (165°F) and the joints move freely, then lift them out and cover. Take the temperature away from the bone; if it has not reached 74°C (165°F), cook for another 5 minutes. After the legs are out, the rice stays in the pot and keeps cooking.
5. Cook until the rice blooms. The congee cooks for about 60 minutes in total from when you turn to low heat; gently scrape the bottom of the pot every 10 minutes. When most of the grains have split with no hard core, and the stock is milky white with some body, turn off the heat. If there are still hard cores, cook 10 minutes more at a time, keeping the pot partly covered so the liquid does not evaporate too fast.
6. Prepare the chicken. Let the legs cool for 10 minutes, until you can hold them steadily, then take the meat off the bone and cut into bite-size pieces, keeping the skin. Put the meat on a shallow plate; serve the dip in a small bowl and do not pour it into the pot.
7. Serve the congee. Pick out the ginger slices and green onion whites and stir in the salt. Lift a spoonful to check: the congee should pour in a wide ribbon and flatten out when it lands in the pot. Divide among four bowls, sprinkle with green onion greens and cilantro, and serve with the chicken and ginger-lime fish sauce; dip a piece of chicken, then take a mouthful of hot congee.

## Key points

- **Burnt smell from the bottom of the pot**: after the rice sinks, starch in one spot gelatinizes and sticks to the pot (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Use a heavy-bottomed pot and scrape the bottom on a schedule; if it has already scorched, move the congee on top to a clean pot and do not scrape up the burnt layer.
- **Congee is like a rice paste and will not flow**: too much evaporated with the lid off, or the rice kept absorbing water after the heat was off (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir in 100 ml hot water at a time until it is pourable again, then taste for salt.
- **Chicken is dry and the skin falls apart**: the legs cooked too long in the congee (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take the chicken out by its centre temperature and keep cooking the rice on its own until soft.
