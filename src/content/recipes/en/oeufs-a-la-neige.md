*Œufs à la neige* is a French home dessert: ice-cold vanilla custard sauce with soft clouds of poached meringue floating on top and a few streaks of caramel with a slightly burnt edge. The meringue is as light as snow and melts on the tongue. The common way is to poach the meringues in milk flavoured with sugar and vanilla, then make the custard from that milk. You can also make the custard first and chill it thoroughly, and poach the meringues in water, so the sauce is colder when it reaches the table.

The meringue is a [foam](/principles/foams/): the whites are whipped until frothy and the sugar goes in a spoonful at a time, while the proteins form a film around countless small bubbles. In the pan, the proteins in the bubble walls [set](/principles/protein-coagulation/) with heat and the foam holds its shape. The custard is the same sauce as [crème anglaise](/recipes/creme-anglaise/), and the caramel is [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) at work. Makes 16 meringues, 2 per person.

## Steps

1. For the custard: put the milk and 100 g sugar in a medium saucepan. Split the vanilla bean lengthwise, scrape out the seeds, and put the seeds and the pod in the milk.
2. Whisk the egg yolks in another bowl. Fill a large bowl with ice and a little cold water, nest a medium metal bowl in it, and set a fine sieve over the top.
3. Heat the milk until steaming. Whisk some of the hot milk into the yolks, then pour the yolks back into the pan.
4. Cook over medium-low heat, scraping the bottom, sides and corners of the pan constantly with a heatproof spatula, until the sauce coats the spatula and a thermometer reads at least 74°C (165°F). Do not let it boil.
5. Immediately strain the sauce into the bowl set in the ice water, wipe the vanilla pod and put it back in the sauce, and stir to help it cool. Once cool, refrigerate it until thoroughly cold.
6. For the meringues: line a baking sheet with a clean tea towel or paper towels. Fill a wide pan halfway with water and bring it to a lively simmer.
7. Put the egg whites and salt in a stand mixer and whip at medium speed until foamy. Turn to high and whip until the whites start to hold their shape, then add the 65 g sugar a tablespoon at a time, and stop as soon as the whites stand up when you lift the whisk.
8. With two large soup spoons, scoop a heap of meringue onto one, so high it almost falls off, and scrape it off with the other so the oval drops into the simmering water. Do about 6 per batch, so they float freely.
9. Poach 3–4 minutes, turn them over with a slotted spoon and poach another 3–4 minutes, until the whole meringue feels set. Push a thermometer into the centre of the thickest one; at 74°C (165°F), lift them onto the towel and poach the next batch; if it isn't there yet, turn them and keep poaching.
10. Pour the custard into a chilled wide serving bowl and set the meringues side by side on top.
11. For the caramel: put the 150 g sugar and 3 tbsp water in a skillet over medium heat, swirling as little as possible and only if it colours unevenly, until it turns a medium amber.
12. Turn off the heat, drizzle the caramel thinly over the meringues with a spoon, and serve right away.

## Key points

- **The meringues are dry and tough**: the whites were overwhipped (see [foams](/principles/foams/)). Stop as soon as they hold their shape when lifted, and add the sugar a spoonful at a time.
- **The custard has bits of cooked egg**: the heat was too high or it boiled, so the yolks overcooked (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Keep the heat at medium-low and scrape the bottom constantly; strain it into the iced bowl as soon as it coats the spatula; for the thickness to look for, see [crème anglaise](/recipes/creme-anglaise/).
- **The meringues stick together in the pan**: too many went in at once and cooked unevenly. Do about 6 per batch and leave them room to drift.
- **The caramel is bitter**: it went past amber and started to burn (see [sugar syrup](/principles/sugar-syrup/)). Turn off the heat as soon as it reaches medium amber.
