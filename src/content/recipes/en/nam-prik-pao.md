*Nam prik pao* is a sweet-salty chili paste used in Thai home cooking. Dried chilies, garlic and shallots are toasted until fragrant, then ground fine with dried shrimp, tamarind, fish sauce and palm sugar, and cooked slowly in oil until thick. The finished paste is a dark red-brown that you can spread with a spoon, sweet and sour, with the savouriness of dried shrimp and a mild heat.

Toast first, then fry. Toasting brings out the aroma of the chilies, garlic and shallots, and frying lets the oil carry the aroma while cooking off the extra moisture (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Dried shrimp and fish sauce lift the savouriness (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)). This is a base for cooking, and one batch makes about 500 ml. For [Hoy Lai Pad Nam Prik Pao](/recipes/hoy-lai-pad-nam-prik-pao/), scoop out paste together with its oil; the paste is both sweet and salty, so count it when seasoning.

## Steps

1. Snip the dried chilies open and remove the stems, seeds and white ribs. Rinse off any dust, then wipe until no water drops remain on the surface. Prepare the garlic and shallots as listed in the ingredients.
2. Toast the chilies in a dry pan over medium heat for 3–5 minutes, stirring constantly, until a few dark spots appear on the skin and you smell roasted chili. Pour them out at once and spread to cool for 3 minutes, until they are crisp and no longer hot to the touch. Do not toast them black all over.
3. Add the garlic and shallots to the dry pan and toast over medium-high heat for 8–10 minutes, turning, until several sides have brown spots and the outer layers are soft. Pour them out and spread to cool for 3 minutes, until the steam clearly fades.
4. Grind the chilies to a coarse powder in a spice grinder, then add the dried shrimp and grind fine. Transfer to a food processor with the garlic, shallots, tamarind, fish sauce, palm sugar and the optional shrimp paste, and blend; add the reserved 60 ml of oil in portions, and blend to a thick paste with only fine bits.
5. Put the paste and the remaining 120 ml of oil in a heavy-bottomed pot and cook over low heat for about 20 minutes, scraping the bottom and corners constantly. At first it throws large bubbles; slowly the paste darkens and thickens, oil sizzles out at the edges, and scraping the bottom leaves a short-lived trail.
6. Turn off the heat and divide the paste among clean, heatproof small containers. Let it cool for about 20–30 minutes, until it no longer steams hard, then cover and refrigerate. Take it out with a clean, dry spoon, and stir the oil and paste together as you scoop.

## Key points

- **Very hot, with the sweet aroma of garlic and shallot covered**: too many small, very hot dried chilies. Use mainly large, mild chilies and remove the seeds and white ribs first; what you want is chili fragrance and colour (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Bitter, with burnt black bits**: one part got too hot during toasting or frying. Toast the chilies only until a few dark spots appear, cook the paste over low heat and scrape the bottom clean; redo a batch that has burnt black (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Thin paste with a raw onion taste**: there is still a lot of water and the garlic and shallots are not cooked through. Keep cooking over low heat, scraping and stirring, and check every 3 minutes; stop when the thick paste spreads and oil bubbles out at the edges, and it will be thicker as it cools (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
