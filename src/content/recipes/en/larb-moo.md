*Lap mu* is a minced pork salad from northeastern Thailand. It is mainly sour and spicy, moist to eat, with a little meat juice. The pork is cooked first, then tossed with lime juice, fish sauce, toasted rice powder and herbs, and eaten warm with sticky rice and crisp vegetables. The shallot adds pungency, the mint adds coolness, and the toasted rice powder gives a roasted-rice aroma and fine grains.

Cook the minced pork briefly so it does not turn tough; see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Season off the heat so the lime and mint keep their freshness. The rice powder is made by dry-toasting raw rice. Fresh herbs lose their aroma once cut, so mix them in only after the pork has stopped steaming hard; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/) and [Acid](/principles/acid/).

## Steps

1. Put the raw glutinous rice in a dry pan and stir constantly over low heat for about 15 minutes, until the grains are golden and smell of toasted rice. Tip out and cool, then grind to a coarse powder like fine sand and set aside.
2. Toast the chilies in a dry pan over low heat for about 3–5 minutes, until a little darker, brittle and fragrant. Take off the heat, cool, grind to a coarse powder and measure out the amount you need. Toasting chilies gives off hot fumes, so turn on the range hood and don't lean over to smell them.
3. Slice the shallot thinly, cut the green onion into thin rings, pick the mint leaves and cut the cilantro into coarse sections. Cut the cabbage into small pieces, wash, drain and put them on the serving plate. Don't use the board that cut the raw meat for these.
4. Put the water and minced pork in a small pot and cook over medium heat for about 5 minutes, breaking the meat up as it cooks. Once the pork changes colour, keep stirring so the larger clumps break up as well. Put a thermometer into a clump of the pork, and turn off the heat at **71°C (160°F)**, leaving a little meat juice.
5. Stir the shallot, fish sauce, lime juice and chili powder into the warm pork. Taste it with the meat: first the sourness, then the salty savouriness and the heat. Fish sauce is salty, so add more a little at a time.
6. Once the pork has stopped steaming hard, mix in the toasted rice powder, green onion, mint and cilantro. Toss until the powder is even and the herbs have just touched the meat juice, then plate. Eat at once, picking up the minced pork in cabbage leaves.

## Key points

- **Sour and spicy but missing the toasted rice aroma**: the rice was not toasted enough, or the powder sat so long that its aroma faded. Toast raw rice over low heat until golden and then grind it, and use it freshly made; if there are black specks or a burnt, bitter taste in the pan, start over. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **The minced pork is dry and hard, like stir-fried mince**: the water cooked off while the heat stayed on, and the proteins kept tightening. Stop once it is broken up and cooked, leave a little meat juice and then season; it doesn't need to be cooked until browned. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **The mint blackens and the lime tastes dull**: the herbs and lime juice were cooked too long in a hot pan, and the fresh aroma was lost. Season off the heat, add the herbs once the hard steam has cleared, and eat right after tossing. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
