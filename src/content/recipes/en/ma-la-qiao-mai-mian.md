Spicy soba is a home-style noodle soup with milk and peanut butter. Hot pot base gives the numbing-hot flavour, peanut butter thickens the broth a little and milk adds a milky note. The fat in the broth has to be dispersed to taste rich and smooth, and the milk proteins and the fine particles in peanut butter help with that (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). So stirring the peanut butter loose first is more reliable than dropping it in whole and waiting for it to melt.

Semi-dried noodles, baby napa cabbage and lettuce cook at different speeds. The noodles soften through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), the cabbage stems need longer, and the lettuce needs only a short cook. Once the milk is in, keep the pot at a gentle bubble and add the vinegar only after taking it off the heat. Milk proteins in hot soup may curdle into small flecks on meeting [acid](/principles/acid/), so use only a little vinegar to keep the broth smooth.

## Steps

1. Wash the baby napa cabbage and lettuce. Cut the thick stems of the cabbage narrow lengthwise and tear the lettuce into large pieces. Look at the cooking time on the soba package. If you use dried noodles, do not apply the minutes for semi-dried noodles to them directly.
2. Bring the water listed in the ingredients to a boil and add the thicker part of the cabbage first, cooking until it begins to soften. Then add the soba and the tender leaves. Separate the noodles gently, and once the soup returns to a boil, turn to low heat.
3. Ladle a small bowl of the hot soup and stir in the peanut butter in portions into a thin paste with no hard lumps. Add the hot pot base to the pot and stir until fully dispersed, then pour the peanut paste and chili oil back in.
4. Add the milk and light soy sauce and keep small bubbles coming up gently, cooking for about 5 minutes and tasting the noodles along the way. If the package time is shorter, move to the next step sooner. The noodles are ready when there is no hard core in the centre but they still have some chew, and the cabbage stems bite through easily.
5. Add the lettuce and keep cooking gently for about 1–2 minutes, turning off the heat when the leaves have wilted but still have colour. Push through from the bottom with a spoon so the peanut butter does not settle into thick lumps.
6. Stir in the Sichuan pepper oil and put the noodles and soup in a heatproof bowl. If you want vinegar, first try a little with a small amount of soup. Once you are happy with the sourness and the colour of the broth, add the rest of the vinegar in portions. You can leave some out, and do not keep cooking just to use it all.

## Key points

- **Lumps in the soup and sticking at the bottom**: the peanut butter was not first loosened with water, and the thick paste settled and overheated in one spot. Mix it into an even thin paste before it goes in, give the [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/) enough water, and push through the bottom often.
- **The noodles are mushy and the greens still hard**: whole thick cabbage stems were cooked a long time with the noodles, so the noodles were past done by the time the greens were cooked. Judge the end point by tasting the noodles (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)), cook the thick stems first and add the lettuce last.
- **Small flecks in the soup after adding vinegar**: [acid](/principles/acid/) makes milk proteins clump, more so at high heat. Taking it off the heat and adding the vinegar in portions reduces sudden curdling but does not guarantee none. For a smooth broth, leave the vinegar out.
