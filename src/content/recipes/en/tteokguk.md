Tteokguk is made with oval slices of rice cake in a clear beef broth. It is the traditional food of the Korean Lunar New Year and is eaten often through the year as well. The beef and fish sauce together carry the [umami](/principles/umami/), and the egg strands, green onion and nori add fragrance when the bowls are filled.

The rice cakes are already steamed, and after refrigeration they turn hard as the [starch retrogrades](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Soaking in water lets the slices come apart, and hot broth then cooks the centres soft. Starch from the surface goes into the broth, so the broth thickens slightly. Cooked rice cakes are springy and the broth still flows; divide it into bowls as soon as it comes off the heat.

## Steps

1. Soak firm refrigerated or frozen rice cakes in 1000 ml of cold water for 20 minutes, until the slices can be separated one by one. Drain. Fresh, soft rice cakes only need a rinse.
2. Pour 2160 ml of water into a pot and heat over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water boils steadily. Add the beef and minced garlic and cook for 5 minutes, until the beef changes colour and the foam gathers on top. Skim off the foam with a spoon.
3. Cover, turn the heat to medium-low and cook for 10 minutes, until the beef is not tough when bitten and the broth smells meaty. Keep the surface at a gentle simmer; a hard boil stirs the foam and fat back into the broth.
4. Stir the egg yolks with half the salt. Add the vegetable oil to a skillet and heat over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until the oil flows easily, then wipe off the excess. Pour in the yolk and spread it into a thin sheet. Cook over low heat for 1 minute, until the surface is mostly set, then flip and cook for 1 more minute, until both sides are cooked through, the centre reaches 74°C (165°F) and it is not browned. Remove and cut into thin strands.
5. Add the rice cakes, fish sauce and the remaining salt to the broth, and nudge the bottom of the pot gently so the slices separate. Cover and cook over medium heat for 7–8 minutes, until the rice cakes float and are soft in the centre when bitten, with no hard core.
6. Lightly beat the egg whites and pour them in a thin stream into the boiling broth. Leave them alone for 30 seconds, until all the white has set into white egg ribbons, then nudge them apart gently.
7. Add the green onion, toasted sesame oil and black pepper, stir, and turn off the heat. Divide among three bowls and top with the egg yolk strands and the crumbled nori. Eat while the rice cakes are chewy and the broth still flows.

## Key points

- **Rice cakes float but still have a hard core**: frozen slices stick together and the centres heat slowly. Soak them first, separate them as they cook, and bite into one slice to judge; floating is only a hint. See [starch gelatinization and retrogradation](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The broth keeps thickening and the edges of the rice cakes dissolve**: the rice cakes cooked too long and released too much starch. Make the beef broth first, add the rice cakes last, and divide into bowls as soon as they are cooked through. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Egg strands are thick, tough and have brown spots**: the pan was too hot or the egg sheet too thick, so the egg overcooked. Spread it thin, use low heat, and take it out as soon as both sides are just cooked. See [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
