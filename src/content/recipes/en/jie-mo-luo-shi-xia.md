This wasabi prawn dish has a thick, pungent sauce. The prawns are first fried in the shell for fragrance, then briefly braised in a bowl sauce. When the prawn meat is heated, its [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and it goes from translucent to opaque. A red shell only shows that the surface is done, so check the thick part of the meat too.

The starch in the bowl sauce absorbs water, thickens with heat and makes the soy sauce, oyster sauce and butter cling to the prawns; see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The pungency of wasabi fades with long cooking, and garlic and butter also need only brief heat, so have everything ready first and the finish will not drag on.

## Steps

1. Snip off the long feelers, legs and sharp points of the prawns, cut open the shell along the back with kitchen scissors, score the meat shallowly, and remove the vein and the visible sand sac in the head. Keep the head shell. Rinse, and dry both the opening and the shell. Mince the garlic and slice the chilies into rings.
2. Stir the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar, white pepper, wasabi and water together, then add the starch and stir to disperse it, leaving no dry lumps at the bottom of the bowl. Starch sinks as soon as it stands, so stir again before it goes in the pan.
3. Add the vegetable oil to a frying pan, heat over medium heat, and spread the prawns in a single layer. Fry until one side turns red and slightly yellow where it touches the pan, then turn over.
4. When the shell on both sides has coloured, add the garlic and chilies and toss until you smell the garlic. Turn to low heat and add the butter, then stir at once when it melts, so the garlic and butter do not scorch.
5. Stir the bowl sauce again and pour it slowly in along the edge of the pan, tossing. Cover and braise for about 2 minutes, keeping the sauce at a gentle boil. Lift the lid and check the thick meat of the largest prawn: it is done when it is pearly white and completely opaque; if it is still translucent, braise a little longer.
6. Uncover, push gently, and reduce the sauce until it is thick, glossy and evenly coats the prawns. If the sauce has become too thick while the prawns are not yet cooked, add a little water and cook again. Taste the sauce for saltiness, add salt if needed, and plate at once.

## Key points

- **The sauce forms starch lumps**: the starch was not dispersed in the cold liquid, or it settled at the bottom of the bowl and was poured straight into the hot pan (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir the sauce once when mixing it and once more before it goes in the pan.
- **Prawns are hard and the sauce is dry and sticky**: it was still being reduced after the prawns were cooked, and the proteins kept contracting (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take the pan off the heat when the sauce clings to the prawns, and do not aim for a dry finish.
- **Garlic is bitter and the wasabi aroma is faint**: the garlic scorched and the wasabi evaporated in long cooking (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the garlic late, pour in the bowl sauce as soon as the butter melts, and plate promptly once the prawns are cooked.
