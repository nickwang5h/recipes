*Chǎo mó* stir-fries steamed buns into small pieces that are fragrant outside and soft inside, golden on the surface and scented with cumin and chili. The buns are already cooked, so the first job in the pan is to fry off the surface water; only then can the sugars and proteins turn golden in the hot pan and give a toasted aroma (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Slightly firm day-old buns cut neatly and are less likely to be crushed when tossed.

Egg adds a layer of egg flavour, but the coating must be thin. [Protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) sets this egg coat; if there is still egg left in the bowl and the whole bowl goes into the pan, the bun cubes stick into big clumps. Cumin, chili and five-spice powder all go in at the end for a quick stir-fry, so the aroma stays on the buns.

## Steps

1. Cut the buns into small cubes or pieces of similar size and shake off the loose crumbs. Slice the green onions, and set out the seasoning powders separately; do not fry thin slices and thick cubes in the same pan.
2. If using egg, beat it and pour it over the bun cubes in stages, tossing lightly. Stop when each piece is moist and has a thin layer of egg on it; keep the egg left at the bottom of the bowl to cook separately. If not using egg, go straight to frying the cubes.
3. Pour the oil into the pan and heat over medium heat until the next bun cube sizzles lightly, then add the rest of the bun cubes and spread them out. Let the underside set for about 1 minute before turning, so the egg that has not yet set is not smeared around.
4. Turn the heat to medium-low and stir-fry for about 4–6 minutes, until several sides are golden and the surface is dry, and the pieces separate when tossed. If the pan is small, fry in batches, and often move cubes from the centre of the pan to the edge so the same side is not always against the hot pan.
5. If there is leftover egg, take out the bun cubes first, pour in the egg and stir-fry until fully set, then return the cubes to the pan. With the egg used up, there is no standing liquid at the bottom of the pan to block browning.
6. Sprinkle the salt, cumin, chili flakes, five-spice powder and a little white pepper (optional) evenly into the pan, turn to low heat and toss. It is enough that the powder just coats the cubes and you smell the aroma; do not leave the spices frying on the bare bottom of the pan.
7. Add the green onions, toss a few times, turn off the heat and take out. For a crisper surface, fry a little longer before adding the spices; when plating, spread the cubes out, because hot cubes piled in a deep bowl are softened by the steam.

## Key points

- **Burnt outside, still cold inside**: the pieces are cut too thick and the heat too high, so the surface goes too far first (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Cut evenly and stir-fry over medium-low heat; if you smell burning, take the pan off the heat to cool.
- **Cubes stick into one lump**: too much egg, mixed in before it set (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Coat in stages, cook the rest separately, and combine once it has set.
- **Spices taste bitter and only the heat is left**: fine powder fried too long against the hot bottom of the pan loses its aroma and scorches (see [spices and aromatic extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Sprinkle it on just before the end, turn to low heat and toss quickly.
