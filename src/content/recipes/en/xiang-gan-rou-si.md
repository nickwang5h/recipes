Shredded pork with pressed tofu has three different textures: tender tenderloin, chewy tofu and green chili that keeps a little crunch. The pork gets a thin coat of light soy sauce and starch. Where the starch meets the water on the surface of the meat, heat [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) it into a thin film that holds the sauce and protects the pork; whether the pork stays tender still depends on the heat.

Tenderloin has little connective tissue, so if it is stir-fried for too long its [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) too tightly and the shreds turn dry and woody. So cook the pork first and set it aside, then combine everything once the tofu and green chili are ready, which keeps the pork in the hot pan for a short time. Cut all three into strips of similar thickness so each bite is even; see [knife work and heat](/principles/knife-work/).

## Steps

1. Cut the tenderloin into shreds of even thickness and toss with 3 ml of the light soy sauce and 3 g of the starch. Mix until the surface is thinly tacky with no dry starch showing, and let it rest a moment. Cut the pressed tofu into strips, stem and seed the green chilies and cut them into shreds, and slice the garlic.
2. Mix the remaining 2 g of starch with 10 ml of cold water and keep it for the thin glaze at the end. The coating and the glaze use 5 g of starch in all, so portion it out now and you will not need to measure more halfway through the stir-fry.
3. Put 15 ml of oil in the pan and heat it over medium-low heat until it flows freely, then add the pork and gently break it apart with a spatula. Let the shreds set before moving them. When they have all turned white and are cooked through at the centre, move them at once to a clean plate.
4. Leave the oil in the pan, add the garlic and pressed tofu, and add 2 ml of light soy sauce. Stir-fry over medium heat for about 2–3 minutes, until the tofu is hot through and lightly browned with soy sauce on the surface. Lift and turn the strips with the spatula instead of poking them repeatedly, or they will break.
5. Add the green chili shreds and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until they are hot through and slightly soft, then return the pork. Toss to combine, taste a piece of tofu, then add the chicken bouillon powder and a little salt. The bouillon powder is already salty, so do not add all the salt.
6. Stir the starch slurry again and pour half of it along the saucy edge of the pan, tossing quickly until the glaze turns from cloudy to glossy. Stop when the three ingredients are coated in a thin sauce; add the rest of the slurry only if needed. Once the pork is back in the pan, do not keep stir-frying it; plate it right away.

## Key points

- **Pork shreds clump together or wear a thick, floury coat**: the coating is too thick, or the pork was stirred hard before it set (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Divide the 5 g of starch between the coating and the glaze, spread the pork out when it goes into the pan, and only then gently break it apart.
- **Pork is dry and the green chili is limp**: heating continued after everything was combined (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook the pork first and set it aside, and serve as soon as the thin glaze turns glossy after the pork returns.
- **Tofu breaks up and the pork is coarse and hard**: the strips are uneven, or the tofu is cut too thin (see [knife work and heat](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut the tofu into thin strips sturdy enough to lift; if you use soft tofu instead, cut it thicker, and keep the pork even.
