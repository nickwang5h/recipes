Tiger-skin pork shank has soft skin and loose meat, and it holds together when you lift the whole shank. The method is to fry the skin first, then stew it soft with moist heat: the skin loses water and takes on colour, then softens and wrinkles in the stewing liquid, while the connective tissue inside keeps heating and turns to gelatin (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/) and [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).

The red-brown colour comes from [caramel colour](/principles/caramelization/) and a little dark soy sauce; cook the caramel only to red-brown and stop, never to black. At the end, reduce the broth to concentrate it first, then thicken with a thin starch glaze; the gelatin in the broth already gives some body, so use little starch.

## Steps

1. Soak the shank in water in the refrigerator for about 1 hour, check for leftover hairs and pull them all out with tweezers.
2. Put the shank in a pot of cold water with the white part of one green onion, 3 cloves of garlic, 3 g of ginger and the Shaoxing wine. After it comes to a boil, cook for about 5 minutes, lift out the shank and remove any foam stuck to it. Skim and strain the liquid and keep it as the stewing liquid.
3. Dry the surface, folds and bone crevices of the shank thoroughly, then leave it on a rack to air-dry further. Put frying oil in a deep pot, and when it reaches about 160°C (325°F), lower the shank slowly in along the side of the pot, adjusting the heat to hold the temperature, and use a splatter guard that leaves a vent for steam.
4. Using long-handled tools, turn the shank about every 3 minutes so the skin touches the oil evenly. Fry for about 20 minutes, and lift it out once the whole skin is light brown; do not chase a darker colour with higher heat. Drain the oil and set aside.
5. In a small pot, put 8 g of rock sugar and 50 ml of water and cook over low heat until the sugar melts, the original water has mostly evaporated, the sugar is red-brown and the fine bubbles increase; take it off the heat. Add the remaining 200 ml of hot water in portions along the side of the pot to dissolve it, standing back from the steam, to get about 200 ml of caramel colour.
6. Put the shank, caramel colour, light soy sauce and dark soy sauce, white vinegar, spices and the remaining green onion, ginger and garlic in the pressure cooker, and add the reserved broth as your cooker's instructions require. The liquid must sit between the cooker's minimum and maximum levels. After the cooker comes up to pressure, time about 40 minutes, turn off the heat and wait for all the pressure to release before opening.
7. Check the thickest part. Take the shank out once the skin is easily pierced and the meat loosens at the bone; if it is still tough, top up the liquid and stew longer. Strain out the spices, skim the fat, move the stewing liquid to a wok and reduce it to about half its original amount, then taste and add salt.
8. Stir the starch with 100 ml of cold water, and drizzle it into the boiling stewing liquid in portions. Stop when the sauce thinly coats the back of a spoon; you do not need to add all of it. Cook until the glaze is clear, then pour it over the shank.

## Key points

- **Violent spattering when it goes into the oil**: there is still water in the skin folds, which vaporizes quickly on contact with heat (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Dry it thoroughly and air-dry it before frying, and leave room in the pot.
- **Sauce turns black and tastes bitter**: the caramel was cooked too far (see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Add the hot water at red-brown to stop the cooking, and make a fresh batch if it has turned burnt black.
- **Meat is cooked but will not pull apart**: shank size or cooker pressure differs, and the collagen has not softened yet (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Treat 40 minutes only as a starting point, and check the thickest part after the pressure releases.
