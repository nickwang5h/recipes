Green beans with pork and olive vegetable is mainly about the bean pieces, with the minced pork and olive vegetable giving them richness and savouriness. The chopped pork belly is fried first, and the garlic and olive vegetable go in once the water has cooked off and the edges start to turn yellow. The meaty aroma comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/), and in the short stir-fry the garlic's heat moves into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

Green beans have to be cooked until fully done. Cut small, the heat has a short way to reach the centre; add a little hot water while stir-frying and cover the pan so the beans keep heating, then uncover and cook dry. The cooked bean pieces keep their shape, but they are not crisp.

## Steps

1. Wash the green beans, trim both ends, pull off the tough strings and cut into small pieces of even size. Split especially thick pods lengthwise too, so some pieces aren't soft while others are still hard in the centre.
2. Smash and mince the garlic, cut the chili into small pieces, and chop the pork belly to a mince after removing the skin. Keep both fat and lean, since the rendered lard helps carry the flavour of the olive vegetable and garlic.
3. Put 10 ml of oil and the minced pork in the wok and fry over low heat for about 2 minutes, breaking it up as you stir. Keep stir-frying until the pork is fully cooked and no juices collect in the pan, the fat has begun to render, and the edges are pale gold.
4. Add the garlic, olive vegetable and the optional chili, and stir-fry briefly until fragrant. Fine pieces need turning promptly; add the green beans once the olive vegetable has spread out and the garlic hasn't turned yellow-brown.
5. Add the green beans and stir-fry over medium heat so every piece is coated with pork fat. Add 30 ml of hot water, cover with the lid and keep it boiling, and cook until the bean pieces are completely cooked through; if the water is gone and the beans are still hard, add hot water a little at a time and keep cooking.
6. Uncover and stir-fry to cook it dry. The total stir-frying and cooking time after the green beans go in is at least about 5 minutes, judged by the centre of the largest bean piece being cooked and soft, not by taking it out when the time is up.
7. Drizzle the light soy sauce around the edge of the wok, add the chicken bouillon powder, white pepper and sugar, stir well and taste, then decide how much salt to add. Plate when there is no obvious liquid at the bottom of the wok and the beans and pork are mixed.

## Key points

- **The bean pieces have changed colour outside but are still hard in the centre**: dry stir-frying heats unevenly, and time alone is not enough. Cover-cook with water and steam until fully done, using [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/); undercooked green beans can make you sick.
- **The pork looks boiled and isn't fragrant**: other ingredients were added before the meat juices had cooked off. Spread the pork out and break it up first, add the next thing only when a little golden edge appears, and you get the aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The garlic is burnt and bitter and the olive vegetable is faint**: the fine pieces stayed too long in hot oil. By [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/), add the beans once you smell the fragrance, and finish by cooking dry, without stir-frying to black.
