Chinese cucumber salad is crisp, cool and strong in garlic and vinegar, and it depends on the cracked edges of the smashed cucumber to hold the dressing. Cucumber smashed until it splits and then cut into pieces keeps its crunch better than cucumber smashed to a pulp; for the principle see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/). Irregular pieces are fine as long as they are close in size, so small bits aren't too salty and large pieces aren't bland.

Curing in sugar, like curing in [salt](/principles/salt/), draws the water out of the cucumber, and the longer it goes, the less crisp it is. Pour off the liquid before dressing so the flavour is not diluted. The vinegar gives sourness and fragrance and the toasted sesame oil adds a sesame aroma; see [Acid](/principles/acid/). Leave the final toss until just before serving.

## Steps

1. Wash the cucumber under running water, dry it and cut off both ends. If the skin is thick, peel off a few alternating strips and leave the tender skin on. Don't use a bitter cucumber, because the seasoning cannot cover bitterness.
2. Set the cucumber on a steady board and smash it lightly with the flat of a wide knife, splitting it along its length, then cut it into pieces about 3 cm long. Hold back your strength so the pieces can still be picked up. The large, watery seed cavities can be cut out.
3. Put the cucumber in a bowl, add 5 g sugar and toss until all the surfaces are coated. Let it stand for about 15 minutes. It is ready when a little water has collected at the bottom of the bowl and the cucumber is still firm.
4. Pour off the water at the bottom of the bowl, let the cucumber drain briefly in a strainer, then tip it back into a dry mixing bowl. Don't squeeze it. The broken edges should be moist but not dripping, and the pieces still whole.
5. Peel, smash and mince the garlic, and stir it with the vinegar, light soy sauce and oyster sauce. Add part of the salt first and taste with a piece of cucumber, adding more if needed so it doesn't suddenly turn too salty.
6. Pour the dressing over the cucumber and fold gently from the bottom up so the cracks also pick up dressing. Add the toasted sesame oil last and fold again. There should be only a little dressing at the bottom of the bowl, with the cucumber surface lightly glossy.
7. Plate and eat at once. To prepare ahead, refrigerate the washed, dried whole cucumber and the dressing separately, then smash, cut, cure and toss just before serving. Don't leave the cucumber in the dressing for long.

## Key points

- **The cucumber breaks up and gets softer the more you toss it**: it was smashed too hard and too much tissue was damaged (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Stop smashing when it splits on its own, and fold lightly after cutting; don't press and mix repeatedly with a spoon.
- **Watery soon after tossing**: the water from curing was not fully poured off, or the salad stood too long after tossing; water keeps moving out (see [Salt](/principles/salt/)). Drain after the short cure and add the dressing just before serving; a sugar cure cannot keep it crisp.
- **Adding more vinegar only makes it sharper**: the pooled water has already diluted the other flavours, and vinegar alone just stands out (see [Acid](/principles/acid/)). Deal with the water first, then judge the balance of sour and salty from a piece of cucumber with dressing on it.
