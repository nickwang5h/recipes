Egg drop soup here is a small bowl with a lot of egg and little water. The sweet version uses sugar and the savoury version uses salt. The egg ribbons are soft, and the soup is thicker than an ordinary egg drop soup. The beaten egg is poured in a thin stream into hot water and quickly [sets](/principles/protein-coagulation/) into soft sheets or wisps.

Do not pour boiling water straight onto cold beaten egg. The water, egg and bowl exchange heat quickly, and however hot the water was, the mixture may not be hot through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The steadier method is to keep a small pot heating, pour the egg into the water, then check doneness. When the egg ribbons turn opaque they have taken shape, but whether the egg is cooked is judged by temperature.

## Steps

1. Crack the egg into a bowl and beat with chopsticks for about 1–2 minutes, until the colour is even and there are no clear strands of white. Do not just break up the yolk and leave ropes of white.
2. Choose either the sweet or the savoury version. Put the matching sugar or salt in a small pot and stir in the water. Add only one seasoning, and do not pour both amounts into the pot.
3. Bring the water to a clear boil and stir gently, checking that the sugar or salt at the bottom has dissolved. The water in the pot is shallow, so watch the heat and do not let it boil dry quickly.
4. Lower the heat to keep the water at a gentle boil, pour in the egg in a thin stream, and draw the chopsticks slowly through it so the egg spreads out. Pouring it all in at once sets into a thick lump, and the centre of a thick lump is harder to cook through.
5. Keep stirring gently and heating until all the egg ribbons are opaque and there is no slippery raw egg. Stir the soup evenly and measure with a food thermometer; turn off the heat once it reaches at least 74°C (165°F).
6. Lift a little to check: the ribbons should be soft and the soup should still flow. As soon as it is cooked, pour it into a bowl and do not leave it in the hot pot to keep cooking. If you like finely broken ribbons, nudge them apart gently before serving.

## Key points

- **Transparent, slippery egg remains**: the water temperature dropped after mixing and part of the egg did not get hot through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Leave it on the heat and stir gently. Do not judge by pouring a kettle of boiling water over the egg.
- **Coarse, hard ribbons and water seeping into the soup**: it was heated too long, and the [protein network](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tightened and squeezed out water. Pour the egg in a finer stream, and stop the heat and serve as soon as it is cooked.
- **Egg clumps into one mass, cooked outside and raw inside**: the egg was poured too fast, and the inside of a thick lump heats slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Beat it evenly first, then pour a thin stream along the surface of the pot. If thick lumps form, gently break them apart and keep cooking until done.
