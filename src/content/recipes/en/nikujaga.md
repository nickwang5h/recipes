*Nikujaga* is Japanese home-style stewed potatoes and meat: thin slices of meat, potatoes and onion simmered in a sweet and salty liquid until soft. The beef used here is the kind common in the Kansai region. The meat slices flavour the liquid, and the potatoes are the star of the plate. Only a little liquid is left at the end. When you lift a potato, its edges are slightly floury and there is no hard core.

Potatoes soften through heat and water: once the starch inside [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), the potato turns floury and soft. Cook them too long and keep stirring, and the pieces fall apart. So use a drop lid that sits right on the food and lets the liquid circulate underneath it, which is much gentler than stirring all the time with a spatula (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the potatoes into pieces about 4 cm across, shave off the sharp edges, rinse off the loose starch on the cut faces and drain. Cut the carrot into rolling-cut pieces about 3 cm long, cut the onion into thick wedges, and cut the beef into slices about 3 cm wide.
2. Heat the oil over medium heat in a pot about 20 cm wide, spread out the beef, and stir-fry until the surface changes colour. Add the potatoes, carrot and onion and turn gently, until the surfaces are coated in oil and the edges of the onion start to turn translucent.
3. Pour in the dashi, bring to a boil and skim off the foam. Set a drop lid slightly smaller than the pot, or a round of parchment paper with a hole cut in the middle, directly on the food, so that steam can escape. Keep a small, steady boil and cook for about 8 minutes first.
4. Test the thickest potato piece with a bamboo skewer. When it slides to the centre smoothly, add the sugar and mirin, swirl the pot gently so the flavours dissolve, put the drop lid back and cook for about 8 more minutes. If the centre of the potato is still firm, cook it soft before seasoning.
5. Add both soy sauces, swirl gently to mix, put the drop lid back and cook for about 8 more minutes. Partway through, spoon a little of the liquid over any food sticking out. The liquid slowly turns shallower, and the edges of the potatoes turn slightly floury while the pieces stay whole when lifted. There should always be liquid in the bottom of the pot.
6. Add the snow peas with their strings removed and cook until bright green and just tender. Turn off the heat, let it sit for a while, then ladle into bowls with a little of the liquid. If the saltiness is right, do not reduce the liquid further, and the pot does not need to be cooked dry.

## Key points

- **The potatoes break down into mash**: the potatoes are too floury and were cooked too long or turned too often. To keep the shape, choose Yukon Gold, shave off the edges, and after seasoning only swirl the pot and spoon the liquid over (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Soft outside, hard centre**: the pieces are cut too big, or the pot heats unevenly, so heat has not reached the centre. Cut them to about the same size and confirm with a skewer that they are soft before going on (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The liquid gets saltier as it cooks**: the dashi itself has salt, or the liquid was reduced too far after the soy sauce went in. Use unsalted dashi and taste the liquid in the last step; if it is too salty, add hot water to dilute it and do not reduce it further (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
