Stir-fried onions and eggs has the sweetness of onion and the savoury richness of egg curds. Thin onion slices go straight into the beaten egg and are fried together. The moment the egg meets the hot pan it undergoes [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and becomes soft curds that hold up the onion; fry longer and the protein network tightens and the egg loses water and turns hard.

Slice the onion thin so it can soften in the short time the egg takes to set. Thick onion slices take longer, and by the time the onion is cooked the egg is often overdone (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). With a generous amount of oil, the finished dish has the fragrance of fried egg. When plating, lift out the egg curds and leave the leftover oil in the pan.

## Steps

1. Peel off the dry outer layers of the onion and cut it into thin slices of even thickness, then separate any layers stuck together. Wash the green onion, drain, chop and keep it for after cooking.
2. Crack the eggs into a bowl, add the onion slices and salt and mix for about 60 seconds. The white and yolk should be fully blended with no clear clumps of white, and the onion slices dispersed through the egg.
3. Pour the oil into a wok and heat over medium heat until it flows freely and is not smoking. Swirl the wok so the bottom is coated with oil, then pour in the egg mixture and spread out the onion slices so they don't pile up in the centre.
4. Fry for about 30–45 seconds first, and when the edges set and the bottom can be lifted by a spatula, flip. Break into large pieces and keep stir-frying for about 2–3 minutes in all, until the onion slices are soft and the egg curds have no runny egg inside. If the edges colour fast, lower the heat.
5. Drizzle in the Shaoxing wine, toss, let the liquid in the pan cook off, then turn off the heat right away. Lift the egg curds with the spatula, draining off the excess oil, plate and sprinkle with the green onion. The curds should still be soft; don't leave them in the hot pan.

## Key points

- **Egg is hard and the onion is still raw**: the onion slices are too thick, so their centres don't heat as fast as the egg sets (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Slice thin, separate the layers and spread them out in the pan. Don't fix thick slices by frying the egg longer.
- **Burnt bottom, wet top**: the heat concentrates where the egg touches the pan (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Flip in time and lower the heat so the wet egg reaches the bottom. If the oil starts to smoke, move the pan off the heat to cool first.
- **Curds weep water and the texture is dry**: after setting, it was fried over and over, and [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) went too tight, squeezing out water. When you see no runny egg inside, finish the seasoning and take it out right away.
