*Hachis parmentier* is the French home dish of chopped cooked beef covered with mashed potato and baked. The meat usually comes from a pot-au-feu that has already been stewed tender, keeping its fibres and a little gelatin (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). This is the layered baking dish version made at home; Escoffier's early recipe of the same name instead mixes the meat and potato and stuffs it back into whole baked potato shells.

When the potatoes are cooked soft, their [starch has gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); crushed, they still keep loose granules, and with warm milk they become a smooth top layer. Bread crumb soaked full of milk and mixed into the filling keeps the chopped meat from going dry and crumbly. The breadcrumbs on top dry out and colour in the oven, giving the toasty aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). One wide, shallow large baking dish holds it, and it divides into four hearty main-course portions.

## Steps

1. Peel the potatoes and cut them into pieces of similar size, put them in cold water that covers them, and add a little salt. Cook until a thin skewer passes through the centre with no hard core, about 20–25 minutes.
2. Meanwhile, soak the bread crumb in 100 ml of the milk until it is soft all the way through. Remove the tough sinew from the beef, then chop it or gently pull it into short fibres, keeping some small bits of meat and not turning it into a paste.
3. Set aside a little butter to lightly grease the baking dish, and use the rest to cook the onion, shallots and garlic over medium-low heat until soft and translucent without taking on deep colour. Spread out to cool slightly, then mix with the cooked beef, the bread crumb after a light squeeze, and the egg, and add a little salt and pepper.
4. Drain the potatoes well, return them to the hot pot, and turn them over low heat until the surface is dry. While hot, crush them with a potato ricer or food mill, mix in the 20 g of butter, then add the warm milk in stages; the finished mash should spread flat yet still hold shallow ridges drawn by the back of a spoon.
5. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Spread the meat filling evenly on the bottom of the baking dish, cover with the mashed potato, and smooth it out to the sides so it sticks to the dish walls. Sprinkle the breadcrumbs thinly and evenly, without piling them into a thick layer.
6. Bake about 25–30 minutes, until the breadcrumbs are golden and fine bubbles show at the edges. Insert a thermometer into the filling at the centre; at 74°C (165°F) it is done. Take it out and let it rest a few minutes, then serve with meat and mashed potato together.

## Key points

- **Mash is gluey and stringy**: it was worked too hard, so too many potato cells broke and released too much starch. Use a ricer, add the milk in stages, and stop once it is mixed. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Filling is dry and crumbly**: the cooked meat is too lean, the bread crumb was not soaked through, or the mixed filling was cooked dry again. Choose beef shoulder or brisket that has already been stewed tender, squeeze the soaked bread only lightly, and put the filling straight into the dish after mixing. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **Crumbs are burnt black and the centre is still cold**: the dish is too deep, so the surface and centre heat too unevenly. Use a wide shallow dish, and once the top has coloured, cover loosely with foil and keep heating until the centre of the filling is hot. See [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
