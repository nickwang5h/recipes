Jangjorim is a salty-sweet side dish for rice. Once the beef is braised, it is shredded along the grain and topped with a spoonful of the braising liquid, to eat with rice and other side dishes. Boiled eggs, green peppers and whole garlic cloves are common additions to the same braise.

Beef brisket has a clear grain and connective tissue that needs time to soften (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Cooking the beef first and adding the soy sauce afterwards lets you control tenderness and saltiness separately. When the braising liquid reduces, the salt does not evaporate with the water (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)); leaving some liquid that still flows is easier to eat than reducing it to a sticky paste.

## Steps

1. Trim the thick fat off the brisket and cut it into long pieces of similar size, with the fibres running along the long side so they are easy to shred at the end. In a medium pot, put 960 ml of water, the onion, green onion whites, daikon, garlic, ginger and black peppercorns, cover, and heat over medium-high heat for about 10 minutes, until the water boils, then cook for another 5 minutes, until the aroma is clear.
2. Add the beef and bring back to a boil for about 3 minutes, until foam appears on the surface; skim it all off. Turn the heat to medium-low, cover and cook for 50 minutes, until the meat is cooked through and a chopstick goes in but still meets some resistance; keep the broth at a gentle simmer and avoid a violent boil.
3. If using eggs, heat 600 ml of water in a separate pot over medium-high heat for about 5 minutes, until it boils, lower the eggs in gently, and cook over medium-low heat for 11 minutes, until the whites and yolks are fully set. Put them in 500 ml of cold water to cool for 5 minutes, until you can hold them, then peel and set aside.
4. Lift out the beef, strain 600 ml of the cooking broth back into the pot, and keep another 100 ml of the original broth in reserve. Add the soy sauce, sugar, mirin and beef, and heat over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until the sugar has dissolved and the liquid bubbles again.
5. Turn the heat to medium-low, cover and braise for 20 minutes, until the surface of the beef has turned brown and a fork can pick it apart along the grain. If it is still tough, add the reserved 100 ml of broth and braise 10 more minutes at a time, testing again. Once the meat is tender, leave the lid slightly open and reduce the liquid for 5–10 minutes, until about 250 ml is left; stop reducing once it tastes salty enough.
6. Add the optional cooked eggs, green peppers and kombu, and simmer gently for 10 minutes, until the outer white of the eggs has taken on colour and the peppers have softened, then remove the kombu. Turn off the heat and rest for 10 minutes, then use a clean fork to shred the beef into thin strips along the grain, and lift it out together with the braising liquid.
7. For the meal, put a small amount of beef and liquid in a small dish. Pack the rest into shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours of finishing, then eat within 4 days or freeze; do not leave the whole pot at room temperature waiting for it to cool completely.

## Key points

- **Beef is brown but hard to shred**: the colour only shows the beef has touched the soy sauce, and the connective tissue may still be tough. Check tenderness first, add the reserved broth as needed and keep cooking gently, then reduce the liquid. See [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **Braising liquid too salty**: too much water was reduced off, or too much soy sauce was added. Taste while reducing, stop when it is salty enough, and when serving take less liquid and eat it with rice. See [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/).
- **Shreds are long and hard to chew**: whole pieces were pulled into very long fibres. After shredding fine, cut across the grain into short lengths of about 3 cm, so they are easier to bite through. See [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
