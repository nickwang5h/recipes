*Orecchiette con cime di rapa* is a leafy-green pasta from Puglia. The cupped side of each orecchietta holds the garlic oil and the softened greens. Here the greens are the **rapini / broccoli rabe** sold in Canadian stores, with tender leaves, small flower buds and a slightly bitter taste, which is different from ordinary broccoli and from broccolini. Trimming shrinks the volume a lot, so weigh the whole bunch.

The greens cook in the same pot as the pasta, which softens the leaves first (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Garlic and chili release their fragrance slowly in the oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The anchovies dissolve and give savoury saltiness. At the end a little pasta water is tossed with the oil to make a thin sauce that clings to the pasta (see [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

## Steps

1. Trim the rapini. Remove yellow leaves and woody stalks, and keep the flower buds, tender leaves and any stems that snap easily. Cut longer tender stems short, and rinse several times, especially where the leaves join the stem, to wash out grit.
2. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it lightly. Add the rapini and cook for 2 minutes, until the tender stems begin to soften and the leaves wilt. Then add the fresh orecchiette and cook for about 5 minutes more. If you use dried pasta, cook it for the time on the package first and add the greens only near the end, so they do not wait too long.
3. Put the olive oil, garlic and chili in a skillet and heat over low heat for about 5 minutes, until the garlic is clearly fragrant and its edges have just turned pale gold. Add the desalted anchovy fillets and press them apart with a spatula. When they have melted into small bits, take the pan off the heat. Do not let the garlic turn brown.
4. Taste a piece of pasta and a piece of stem. When the thick part of the pasta is cooked through but still springy and the stem bites through easily, set aside 1 cup of the pasta water. Lift the pasta and greens together into the pan of garlic oil. Let the greens bring a little water with them, and do not squeeze them dry.
5. Take out the garlic. Heat briefly and toss so the greens settle into the hollows of the pasta. If the bottom of the pan looks dry, add a little pasta water. Toss until glossy with no separate layer of oil, then taste and plate.

## Key points

- **The greens are mushy but the pasta has a hard core**: fresh and dried pasta cook for different times. With dried pasta, add the pasta first and add the greens according to how soft the stems are. Taste both before lifting them out. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The garlic oil is bitter and tastes burnt**: the garlic coloured too far in hot oil, and it keeps darkening off the heat. Move the pan once the garlic is pale gold. Redo the oil and garlic if they have turned dark brown or black. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **The greens and pasta sit apart and all you taste is oil**: they were lifted out too dry, so the oil has no water to join with. Add a little pasta water and toss quickly. The leaves should be soft but not pressed into a purée. See [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
