Oden is a home-style pot of daikon, eggs, konnyaku and various fish cakes gently simmered in dashi. The broth uses [dashi](/recipes/dashi/), where the [umami](/principles/umami/) of kombu and bonito supports the flavour, and the soy sauce gives only a light saltiness and colour. The fish cakes can vary; a common combination is chikuwa, satsuma-age and fish balls.

Thick daikon is first cooked soft in plain water. Fish cakes are already cooked, so they go in later and only need to be heated. Fried fish cakes are blanched briefly first to remove surface oil (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Keep small bubbles on the surface of the broth, and the pieces will stay whole and the broth will have fewer crumbs.

## Steps

1. Trim the sharp edges off the daikon rounds and cut a cross about 5 mm deep into one face. Put them in 1500 ml of cold water, heat over medium heat until boiling, then cook over low heat for about 20 minutes in all from the time it was put on cold water, until a bamboo skewer goes into the centre with slight resistance. Lift out and rinse off the crumbs on the surface.
2. In another pot, bring 1000 ml of water to a boil, which takes about 5 minutes, carefully add the eggs and cook at a gentle boil for 8 minutes. Lift out and rinse under cold running water for about 2 minutes, until they do not feel hot, then peel.
3. Score the konnyaku in a shallow grid on both sides and cut into triangles. Bring 500 ml of water to a boil, about 3 minutes, add the konnyaku and cook for 3 minutes, until the odour has faded, then lift out. In the same pot, blanch the fish cakes and thick fried tofu for 30 seconds, until oil floats on the surface of the water, then lift out and drain, and discard the blanching water.
4. In a large pot, add the dashi, usukuchi soy sauce, sake and mirin, then add the daikon, konnyaku and eggs. Heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until bubbles form at the edge of the pot, then turn to low heat and cook uncovered for 20 minutes, until a bamboo skewer pierces the daikon easily and the outer ring looks slightly translucent.
5. Add the fish cakes, the thick fried tofu cut into large pieces, and the knotted kombu, and simmer at small bubbles for 10 minutes, until the fish cakes puff slightly and are hot in the centre, and the cut faces of the fried tofu are soaked with broth. Press fish cakes that float up gently into the broth, and do not stir back and forth.
6. If you are serving with mustard, mix the mustard powder with 1 tbsp of hot water, cover and leave for 5 minutes, until the powder is moistened through and there is no dry powder. Taste the broth in the pot and adjust it with 0.5 tsp of salt to a light saltiness. Serve with the pot ingredients, with the mustard on a separate dish for dipping.

## Key points

- **The daikon is soft outside and hard inside**: thick slices heat slowly in the centre, and they were not pre-cooked enough before going in the broth. Cook them in plain water until a skewer goes to the centre, then move them to the seasoned broth (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The broth is greasy and the dashi aroma is unclear**: surface oil from the fried fish cakes and fried tofu got into the broth. Blanch them briefly first and discard the blanching water (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)).
- **The fish cakes swell, then split, and the broth is full of crumbs**: the heat was too strong or the pot was stirred too much. Keep only small bubbles at the edge of the pot and cook the fish cakes for the last 10 minutes (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The broth gets saltier as it cooks**: the fish cakes have their own salt and the broth is evaporating. Add the salt after the fish cakes are in the broth; if it is too salty, add 60 ml of unsalted dashi at a time (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
