Tomato beef egg drop soup is a home-style soup made with plain water. The tomatoes, once cooked soft, bring their sourness and savoury flavour into the water, and the beef goes in afterward, so the [umami](/principles/umami/) of the two work together. The beaten egg is drizzled in at the end and sets into thin sheets in the hot soup (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), giving the soup soft egg ribbons.

Use tenderloin, which suits a short cook. Cooking the tomatoes soft first and adding the beef afterward means the slices spend less time over heat. Cut the beef thin across the grain, as even in thickness as you can; thin slices heat quickly, and the cut muscle fibres are easier to bite through (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the beef across the grain into slices about 2 mm thick and trim off any obviously thick connective tissue. Cut the tomatoes into small pieces, slice the ginger, mince the garlic and chop the white and green parts of the green onion separately. Beat the eggs until the white and yolk are mixed and keep them in a bowl that is easy to pour from.
2. Add 2 g of salt and half of the pepper to the beef, mix well and marinate in the refrigerator for 15–20 minutes. Make sure the seasoning touches every slice, and if the slices have stuck together in a thick clump, loosen them before they go in the pot.
3. Put the water, ginger slices, white parts of the green onion, minced garlic and tomatoes in a pot, bring to a boil and turn to medium-low heat. Cook until the edges of the tomatoes are soft and their juices have blended into the soup, pressing a few pieces gently with the back of a spoon so the soup tastes sour and savoury; keep some small pieces whole.
4. Adjust the heat so the soup is gently boiling and add the beef, spreading the slices out, gently pushing apart any that stick together. Let the soup come back up to heat and cook until all the slices are cooked through; if there are thick slices, pick them out to check on their own, and do not cook the whole pot longer for the sake of a few thick pieces.
5. Turn to low heat and drizzle in the beaten egg in a fine stream at different places in the pot. After each drizzle wait for the surface to set and then push gently once; the egg ribbons should be pale yellow soft thin sheets, and you should still see the pieces of tomato in the soup. Keep heating until all the egg has set, with no clear raw egg left.
6. Add the rest of the pepper, then add the reserved salt in stages, stir and taste the soup. Sprinkle in the green parts of the green onion, turn off the heat and ladle out; serve as soon as it is seasoned, so the beef and egg ribbons stay soft.

## Key points

- **The beef is dry and tough**: the beef went in first and was then cooked while waiting for the tomatoes to soften, so it was heated too long. Once the proteins in beef contract they squeeze out water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), so make the soup base first and cook the slices briefly at the end.
- **The egg turned into fine bits**: the soup was stirred too fast when the egg went in, and it was broken up before it set (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Drizzle it in a fine stream in different places, wait a moment and then push gently.
- **Some slices in the pot are tender and some tough**: the slices were cut with the grain, were uneven in thickness or had tough connective tissue left on. Cut thin across the grain with even thickness (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)), then cook through over gentle heat.
