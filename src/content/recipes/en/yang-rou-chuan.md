Lamb skewers are charred on the outside and juicy inside, with a sharp hit of cumin and chili. Small pieces of lamb with a mix of fat and lean are grilled over charcoal. The fat melts and runs, which keeps the meat moist; as the surface gradually dries and browns, the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) brings the grilled-meat aroma.

Onion and [salt](/principles/salt/) lay the groundwork for the meat, while cumin and chili are held back until the skewers are halfway done. Fine powders have a large contact area, so their aroma comes out fast, but they also burn and turn bitter easily (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Let the surface of the meat set first, then sprinkle the powder onto the meat surface where the fat has come out, so the cumin aroma is clear.

## Steps

1. Bone the lamb leg and trim off thick, tough connective tissue, keeping the fat. Cut the lean and the fat separately into pieces about 1.5–2 cm, to a total of 700 g, of which about 175 g is fat. Do not cut meat with long sinew straight into big cubes; a short grilling time will not soften the sinew.
2. Toss the meat with the sliced onion and 1 tsp salt, cover and refrigerate for 40 minutes, until the surface of the meat looks slightly moist and the onion clings to the pieces. Keep the cumin and chili powder in a small bowl on the side and do not mix them into the raw meat yet.
3. During the last 20 minutes of marinating, light the charcoal outdoors and wait about 20 minutes, until the open flame has died down and the coals are covered in white ash but still glowing red inside. Keep the grate about 10 cm above the coals, and leave one side with fewer coals so that skewers that colour quickly can be moved over.
4. Discard the onion and thread the meat onto flat metal skewers to make about 10 skewers, alternating fat and lean, with about 6 pieces of lean and 2 pieces of fat on each. The pieces should just touch, not be packed into a thick mass; share the thicker pieces evenly among the skewers.
5. Set the skewers over the hot coals and grill for about 2–3 minutes first, turning every 30 seconds or so, until the surface goes from red to pale, every side has set, and the fat begins to bead with oil. If dripping fat causes flames, move the skewers at once to the side with fewer coals and put them back when the flames die down.
6. Sprinkle the cumin and chili powder over all sides in two rounds, turning the skewers as you sprinkle so the powder sticks to the meat surface where the fat has already come out. Keep grilling for about 5–7 minutes, still turning every 30 seconds or so, until the surface is evenly browned, the fat has shrunk and the edges are slightly crisp. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece; at 71°C (160°F) it is done.
7. Take the skewers off the heat, put them on a clean plate and serve hot. The skewers may not all reach doneness at the same time: take off the first ones that are done, grill the thicker ones 1–2 minutes more, then check the centre temperature and the colour again.

At home you can simplify this with the oven's top element, a rack and a drip pan: preheat the top element for about 5 minutes until the rack is hot, then grill for about 8–10 minutes, turning every 1 minute, and judge by the same browning and centre temperature.

## Key points

- **Lean meat tough and dry**: the fat was trimmed too clean, or skewers that were already cooked stayed over the fire. Grilled flavour needs the surface to lose water, but continued heating also dries the inside of the meat (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Keep a mix of fat and lean, and take the skewers off once they are done.
- **Cumin bitter and chili blackened**: the fine powder went on too early, or flames from dripping fat burned the seasoning directly (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Sprinkle after the surface has set, and move away from the flame.
- **Charred outside and raw inside**: the pieces were too big, or fat and lean were packed too tightly. Surface browning moves faster than the centre heats (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Cut the pieces evenly, move the skewers to the side with fewer coals to keep grilling, and check the thickest piece.
