This pot of crawfish is red, glossy, numbing and spicy: the meat is coated in the fragrance of Sichuan peppercorns, dried chilies, green onion, ginger and garlic fried in oil, with the savouriness of doubanjiang and soybean paste underneath. The whole spices are first released slowly in warm oil; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The two pastes are then stir-fried apart until the red oil comes out, so the flavour of the [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/) melts into the fat.

After the beer goes in, hot liquid and steam cook the meat. Crawfish vary in size, so before turning off the heat check the biggest one, and serve as soon as they are cooked.

## Steps

1. Pick live crawfish and throw away any that have died; thaw frozen ones in the fridge as the label directs. Scrub the belly, leg joints and shells under clean water with a brush, rinse off the grit and drain.
2. To remove the vein, gently pull the middle tail fin at the tip of the tail and draw out the intestine; if it breaks, do not squeeze the meat over and over. Cut the leek into sections about 2 cm, mince the ginger and garlic, chop the doubanjiang fine, and measure the spices ahead of time.
3. Put 70 ml of oil in the pot and warm it over low heat to lukewarm, then add the bay leaves, star anise, cassia bark, green Sichuan peppercorns, Sichuan peppercorns and dried chilies. Stir gently; when the spices start to smell and the chilies are still red, move to the next step, and do not wait until they blacken.
4. Add the leek, ginger and garlic, and stir-fry until the raw taste of the garlic has gone but the colour is still pale. Add the doubanjiang and soybean paste, and stir-fry over medium-low heat to loosen them. When red oil seeps out and the pastes have not dried into hard lumps, add the crawfish.
5. Add the drained crawfish and toss over medium-high heat so every surface is coated in red oil. When the shells slowly turn red, pour in the beer, adding it slowly along the edge of the pot so it does not rush into the hot oil and splatter.
6. When the beer comes back to a boil, add the light soy sauce, cover and turn to medium heat, keeping the broth bubbling, and cook for about 15 minutes, turning a few times along the way so the crawfish on top also get into the hot broth. If the broth is evaporating too fast, lower the heat and cover; do not cook it down to a dry stir-fry.
7. Take the largest one: the tail meat should be opaque and come away from the shell. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part and turn off the heat only at 74°C (165°F). Red shells do not count; if it has not reached that, keep cooking.
8. When all are cooked, taste the sauce and add salt if it is not salty enough. Leave a sauce that still flows, turn off the heat and plate. Eat the tail meat; the head and innards are not eaten.

## Key points

- **The spicy oil is bitter**: the Sichuan peppercorns, chilies or doubanjiang burnt (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Infuse the whole spices over low heat first, and keep the pastes moist and easy to push around as you fry them.
- **Meat is dry**: after the centre was cooked, the sauce was reduced for a long time, and the [proteins denatured and coagulated](/principles/protein-coagulation/) too tightly and lost water. Stop once they are cooked and leave some sauce; do not aim for a dry finish.
- **Sauce is too salty**: the two pastes and the light soy sauce are salty to begin with, and more so once concentrated (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Add salt at the end, after tasting.
