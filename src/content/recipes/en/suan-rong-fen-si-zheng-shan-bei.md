For steamed scallops with garlic and glass noodles, the scallop meat sits on glass noodles and is seasoned with garlic oil and a soy dressing. The noodles catch the scallop juices, so each bite has both garlic fragrance and the savoury taste of shellfish. The minced garlic is fried gently in oil first. The pieces are small, so the flavour passes into the oil quickly; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

Scallops stay tender through a short steam; if [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) goes too far, they shrink and turn hard. The glass noodles are soaked soft and drained first, so they finish heating together with the scallops during steaming. Half-shell scallops can be set out one by one. At home, shucked scallop meat on a heatproof shallow plate steams just as well.

## Steps

1. Remove the tough side muscle from the scallop meat and blot it dry with paper towels. For scallops in the shell, take out the meat first, remove the black innards and the frill, and scrub the shells clean. If using frozen scallop meat, thaw it in the refrigerator for about 8–12 hours, until there is no ice core in the centre, then pat dry.
2. Bring 500 ml of water to a boil in a small pot over high heat, about 3–5 minutes, then take it off the heat. Add the glass noodles, loosen them and scald for about 30 seconds, until the strands bend and separate. Lift them out, rinse with cold water and drain for about 3 minutes, until they no longer drip in streams.
3. Mince the garlic finely. Put it in a small pot with the vegetable oil and heat over medium-low for about 1–2 minutes, until the garlic bubbles finely and smells fragrant but is still pale. Take it off the heat right away. Do not fry the garlic to a deep brown.
4. Spread the noodles thinly on a heatproof shallow plate or in cleaned half shells, and set the scallop meat on top in a single layer. Divide the garlic and about half of the garlic oil over the scallops, leaving gaps so steam can pass. The shallow plate needs a rim to catch the scallop juices.
5. Add the light soy sauce, sugar and 80 ml of water to the remaining garlic oil. Cook over low heat for about 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves and the dressing starts to bubble, then take it off the heat and set it aside. Hold the soy dressing until after steaming, so the noodles do not soak up too much salty liquid early.
6. Add water to the steamer and boil it over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water is rolling, then put the plate in. Cover, keep plenty of steam and steam for about 2 minutes first, until the centre of the scallops is opaque and still springy when pressed lightly, and the thickest part reaches 74°C (165°F). If not, steam for 1 more minute at a time and check.
7. Take out the plate and keep the scallop juices. Reheat the dressing over low heat for about 30 seconds, until it bubbles again, then spoon it evenly over the scallops and noodles. Scatter on the optional cilantro leaves and serve right away. The noodles should be soft with whole strands.

## Key points

- **The scallops are tough and clearly shrunken**: they were steamed too long, and the proteins kept contracting and losing water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Check doneness at the thickest part, and take them out once cooked. Do not leave them in the steamer after the heat is off.
- **The garlic is bitter**: finely cut garlic scorches easily in hot oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Start the garlic in cold oil, heat over medium-low, and take it off the heat when it begins to bubble and smell fragrant.
- **The glass noodles clump and are partly hard**: they were not loosened while scalding, or were piled too thick on the plate (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Soften all the strands first, drain, spread thinly and lay the scallops in a single layer.
