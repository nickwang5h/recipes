Lao You pork rice noodles is a bowl of sour, spicy, salty rice noodles with sliced pork. The sour fragrance of the bamboo shoots and the salty savouriness of the black beans in the broth come from [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/). Stir-fry first, then add water, so the aromas open up. Fermented black beans are strong and the broth already has plenty of saltiness, so leave the salt until the end and taste first.

Drain the sour bamboo shoots and stir-fry off the extra water, then briefly fry the garlic and black beans in the oil; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The rice noodles are soaked soft first so the centres cook through easily in the broth; see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The noodle weight in the ingredient list is the soaked weight, which is much heavier than dry noodles.

## Steps

1. Soak the rice noodles as the package directs. For dry noodles that suit a warm soak, use water at about 50°C (120°F) for 30 minutes, until they bend without snapping at once. For products that must be boiled first, follow the package. Drain and weigh out the amount needed.
2. Cut the pork into even thin slices and mix with the Shaoxing wine, the soy sauce for marinating, the starch and a little white pepper. Mix until each slice has a thin coating and no dry starch is left at the bottom of the bowl, then set aside. Keep the broth's soy sauce separate and don't pour it into the pork bowl.
3. Drain and shred the sour bamboo shoots, lightly chop the black beans and mince the garlic. Heat a pan over medium heat with no oil and add the bamboo shoots, stirring, until no water collects at the bottom and the shoots stop dripping but are still moist and pliable.
4. Push the bamboo shoots aside to make a gap in the middle. Add the oil, garlic, chopped chili and black beans, turn the heat to medium-low and toss together. Move on as soon as you smell the garlic and black beans; don't wait for the minced garlic or beans to burn black.
5. Add the marinated pork, spread it out and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the slices change colour on the surface and separate. Pour in the broth's soy sauce, the rice vinegar, the optional sugar and the broth water, and cook until the whole pot returns to a boil.
6. Add the soaked, drained rice noodles and keep it boiling for about 3 minutes. Snap one strand to check: the centre should have no hard core, and it should be soft but chewy when bitten. Check a thicker slice of pork as well and stop only when it is cooked through; if any is raw, keep cooking.
7. Taste the broth and add a little salt only if it lacks saltiness, then serve at once. The broth should hold up the noodles, with the strands separate. Don't leave the whole pot of noodles sitting in the broth after the heat is off; they keep absorbing liquid, which uses up the broth and makes the noodles soggy.

## Key points

- **Only sourness, with no garlic or black bean aroma**: the sour bamboo shoots carry water, so the aromatics are boiled as soon as they go in (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Stir-fry the shoots until no water pools, then add oil and fry the aromatics briefly.
- **The broth tastes burnt and bitter**: the minced garlic and black beans stayed too long in hot oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the pork and water as soon as you smell the aroma to bring the temperature down, and don't stir in any blackened bits.
- **The broth is too salty**: the salt from the black beans, chopped chilies and soy sauce adds up (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Keep the two portions of soy sauce, for the pork and for the broth, clearly separate and don't keep topping up. If it is already salty, dilute it with water.
- **Noodles soft outside and hard inside, or broken and mushy**: the soaking and cooking times don't suit the noodles you have (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Read the package first, and at the stated time snap one strand; they are done when there is no hard core and the strands are whole.
