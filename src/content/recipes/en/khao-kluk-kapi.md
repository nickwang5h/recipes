*Khao kluk kapi* is shrimp paste rice set on a plate and surrounded by sweet braised pork, shredded egg crêpe, Chinese sausage, dried shrimp and crisp raw sour green mango, cucumber and long beans. At the table you squeeze lime over it and mix everything together. The rice carries the salty savoury depth of fermented shrimp paste, the pork supplies the sweetness, and the raw sides give sourness and crunch.

The shrimp paste is fried in oil first and then mixed into the rice, so it coats every grain evenly (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). The sweet pork is browned for fragrance, simmered until the liquid is gone, and finished with palm sugar, which [caramelizes](/principles/caramelization/) into a sticky brown glaze. The sour green mango and lime go in at serving time, so the [acidity](/principles/acid/) balances the sweet pork and the shrimp paste.

## Steps

1. Prepare the sides first: peel the green mango and cut it into thin shreds; peel the cucumber and slice it on the diagonal; cut the long beans into short pieces; thinly slice the shallots; chop the chilies. Cut the lime into wedges.
2. Make the sweet pork: cut the pork into small thin pieces. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a pan over medium heat and stir-fry the shallot slices, garlic and cilantro roots until fragrant. Add the pork and stir-fry for a few minutes, then add the dark soy sauce, light soy sauce, fish sauce and water and bring to a boil. Cook until the pork is soft and the liquid has reduced by about half. Add the palm sugar and stir until it dissolves and the sauce turns a sticky brown and is nearly dry. Cook for 10 more minutes, until the centre of the pork reaches 71°C (160°F).
3. Make the egg ribbons: beat the eggs. Heat a frying pan over medium heat, wipe on a thin layer of oil and pour in the egg, tilting the pan so it spreads into a thin sheet. When the edge lifts easily, flip it and cook until fully set. Take it out, roll it up and cut it into thin ribbons.
4. Fry the dried shrimp: heat oil in a pan over medium heat, add the dried shrimp and fry until crisp. Lift them out and drain.
5. Fry the sausage: put the sausage pieces in the pan and fry over medium heat until just coloured. Lift them out and drain.
6. Fry the shrimp paste rice: put 2 tbsp oil in the pan and add the shrimp paste over medium heat, pressing it apart and stir-frying until fragrant. Add the rice and toss until the grains are evenly coloured and any clumps are broken up. When the centre of the rice reaches 74°C (165°F), it is heated through.
7. Plate: press the rice into a bowl and turn it out onto a plate. Arrange the sweet pork, egg ribbons, dried shrimp, sausage and raw sides around it. At the table, squeeze on the lime and mix everything together.

## Key points

- **One bite very salty, the next bland**: the shrimp paste is still in small lumps. Press it apart and fry it in the oil first, then add the rice. When shopping, look for fermented shrimp paste labelled kapi (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)).
- **The sweet pork tastes bitter**: after the palm sugar went in, the pan was not stirred constantly and the bottom scorched. Once the sugar has dissolved, stir often, and turn off the heat when the pork is dry, brown and sticky (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)).
- **The whole plate is cloyingly sweet, then soggy once mixed**: there is not enough acidity, or the raw sides carry water. Choose sour, crisp, unripe green mango, drain the washed vegetables well, and squeeze the lime in only when you eat (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
