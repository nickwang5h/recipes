*Gaeng jued woonsen* is a clear Thai home-style soup. Pork bones, meatballs, napa cabbage and tofu give the base flavour, and mung bean glass noodles carry the broth into the mouth. The cilantro stems and white pepper go in while the soup simmers, and the green onion and crispy garlic are kept for serving (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The surface shows only small bubbles, so the meat and tofu stay whole.

The noodles are first soaked until they bend, then cooked in the soup at the end. Soaking until soft and cooking through are two different things, and the noodles still have to finish [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) in the hot soup. If they go in too early, they keep absorbing water, and the soup shrinks. Scoop the meatballs into small lumps and don't cook them long once done. When cooked too long, the proteins tighten and squeeze out water, and the meatballs turn hard (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

## Steps

1. Put the ribs in a pot, add 1200 ml of water, and the fish sauce, light soy sauce, sugar and white pepper for the soup base. Heat over medium for about 5–8 minutes, until the soup begins to bubble. Turn to low and cook another 5–10 minutes, until grey-white foam collects on the surface, and skim it off thoroughly.
2. Add the cilantro stems and the optional daikon, leave the lid slightly open and simmer over low heat for another 25–30 minutes, until a fork pierces the rib meat easily and the daikon is translucent. Keep the surface at gentle small bubbles. When the soup level drops, add 60 ml at a time from the reserved hot water, so the soup doesn't reduce and turn too salty.
3. While the soup simmers, soak the noodles in 500 ml of room-temperature water for 7–10 minutes, until they bend and no longer snap. Drain and cut into lengths of about 10 cm. Mix the ground pork with the meatball seasonings for about 1 minute, until the meat is even and slightly sticky, and refrigerate.
4. To make the crispy garlic, put the garlic and oil in a small pot and heat over medium-low for about 3–5 minutes, stirring gently, until the garlic is light golden and the bubbling has clearly died down. Pour it at once into a small heatproof bowl, so the leftover heat in the pot doesn't brown the garlic further.
5. When the ribs are tender, take out the cilantro stems. Use two teaspoons to scoop the meat mixture into small balls of about 2 cm into the soup, and cook over medium-low heat for about 3 minutes, until the surface sets and the soup returns to small bubbles.
6. Add the cabbage and tofu, push them gently apart, and cook for about 2 minutes, until the cabbage leaves are soft and the soup bubbles again. Add the noodles and cook another 3 minutes, until they are transparent with no hard core when bitten through, and the centre of a meatball reaches 71°C (160°F).
7. Scatter on the green onion and the reserved cilantro leaves. Divide among four bowls, and if you made the crispy garlic, share the garlic and garlic oil among the bowls. Serve while the noodles are still slippery.

## Key points

- **The soup keeps shrinking and the noodles swell into a mass**: the noodles went in too early and kept absorbing the soup. Cook the ribs and meatballs first, and cook the noodles last, only until transparent with no hard core. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The meatballs are hard and the tofu is broken**: the soup was at a hard boil for a long time, the meat was overcooked, and the tofu was knocked apart by stirring. Keep the simmer at small bubbles, push the tofu gently after it goes in, and don't cook the meatballs long once done. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **The crispy garlic is bitter**: the garlic had already turned deep brown in the pot, and leftover heat keeps heating it after it comes off the burner. Take it out of the pot at light golden. The bubbling dying down is also a sign that little water is left. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
