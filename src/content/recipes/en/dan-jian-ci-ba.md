Egg-fried ciba has a lightly crisp outside and a soft, chewy inside. Cooked glutinous rice cakes are coated in beaten egg and pan-fried in a little oil. The shell takes shape as the egg protein coagulates (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), but the centre has to wait while heat slowly travels in. A yellow egg coating doesn't mean the inside is soft. The ciba is fried soft when it bends slightly when you pick it up and the cut face has no cold, hard core.

Ciba turns hard as it cools, which is starch retrogradation, and softens again when heated (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cut the strips evenly and fry both sides in turn over low heat, so the outside and the centre don't differ too much. The brown sugar goes on last, which keeps its sweet fragrance and keeps it from burning on the bottom of the pan first. Eat it while warm, because the texture tightens again as it cools.

## Steps

1. Cut the two pieces of cooked ciba into small rectangular strips, keeping the thickness as even as possible. Thaw frozen product as the package says, until it is soft enough to cut, and wipe off surface water so it doesn't splatter oil in the pan.
2. Crack one egg into a wide bowl, add 2 g of salt and beat until the white and yolk are mixed. Put the ciba into the egg and turn it several times, so the front, back and sides each pick up a thin coat of egg.
3. Pour 10 ml of vegetable oil into a skillet and heat over low heat, tilting the pan to spread the oil. Add the ciba, leaving gaps between the pieces. A light sizzle as it goes in is enough, and the oil shouldn't be heated until it smokes.
4. When the egg on the bottom has set and a spatula can lift each piece whole, flip it and keep frying over low heat. Pour the remaining egg over the ciba in several additions so it sets against each piece, rather than pouring it all at once into a thick omelette.
5. Fry both sides in turn until golden, and the ciba bends slightly when picked up. Cut open the thickest strip: the centre should be hot and soft, and the egg outside completely set with no runny raw egg. Then take the rest out of the pan.
6. Break up the brown sugar and sprinkle it evenly over the ciba while it is warm. Wait a moment for the sugar to stick to the surface, then eat. When biting in, let the centre cool a little first, since the glutinous rice centre just out of the pan is hotter than the surface.

## Key points

- **Burnt egg coating, hard centre**: the heat is too high or the ciba is too thick, so the surface heats faster than the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut small and even and fry slowly over low heat. If the colour is already dark enough, don't raise the heat to rush it.
- **Egg coating comes off when flipped**: the egg hasn't set into a continuous layer (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Wait for the bottom to set before flipping, and add the extra egg in several additions.
- **Pieces stick together in one clump**: once softened, the ciba is sticky on the surface, and the egg joins neighbouring strips. Leave gaps when they go in the pan, pour in extra egg around each piece, and separate them with a spatula once the egg has set.
