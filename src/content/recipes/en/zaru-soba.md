Zaru soba is buckwheat noodles that are boiled, rinsed cold and dipped in sauce, topped with shredded nori. A traditional bamboo *zaru* lets the water run away from under the noodles; at home, a rack set over a plate does the same. The noodles go through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) in hot water, but the loose starch left after cooking makes the surface slimy, so rinsing matters as much as boiling.

The dipping sauce combines the [umami](/principles/umami/) of kombu, bonito and soy sauce. Make a concentrate first, then dilute it with cold water, which makes it easy to control the saltiness of each cup. Soba should be eaten as soon as it is cooked, so cool the sauce and cut the accompaniments first, and cook the noodles last.

## Steps

1. Put the sake and mirin in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 2 minutes until it boils, then cook another 30 seconds, until the sharp alcohol smell fades. Add the soy sauce, kombu and bonito flakes, turn to low heat and cook for 5 minutes, keeping the surface gently bubbling and the bonito flakes wet and sinking.
2. Take off the heat and steep for about 20 minutes, until the pot is only warm. Strain through a fine sieve without pressing the bonito flakes. Set the container with the strained concentrate in 1500 ml of ice water and cool for about 10 minutes, until the container feels cool.
3. Measure 90 ml of the concentrate, mix with 270 ml of ice-cold drinking water and divide among four cups. Bottle the rest of the concentrate separately and refrigerate. Cut the nori into shreds, and put the green onion and wasabi in separate dishes.
4. Bring the water for the noodles to a boil in a big pot over about 8–10 minutes, until the surface keeps rolling. Add the soba, fanned out, and stir gently to separate; cook for about 4–5 minutes as the package directs, until there is no hard, starchy core in the centre and the noodles are still chewy.
5. Lift the noodles into a colander and rub them gently under cold running water for about 1 minute, until the water runs mostly clear and the noodles no longer feel slimy. Move them into the other 1500 ml of ice water and soak for 30 seconds, until they are cold inside and out.
6. Lift the colander and drain for about 1 minute, until the bottom no longer drips steadily. Divide onto racks or bamboo *zaru* and scatter the nori shreds on top. Pick up a small bundle to dip in the sauce, and add green onion and wasabi with each bite.

## Key points

- **Noodles stick in a mass**: the loose starch on the surface was not rinsed off. Rinse as soon as they are cooked and rub gently until the water runs clear; the more gelatinized starch stays on, the more they stick together as they sit (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Noodles snap when picked up and feel mushy**: they were cooked too long, or pulled hard while rinsing. Bite one to test ahead of time, and support the noodles while rinsing gently, without rolling them into a ball.
- **The sauce is right at first and gets weaker and weaker**: water left on the noodles keeps carrying into the cup. Drain thoroughly first, then plate; taste the saltiness together with the noodles (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **The sauce is only sweet and salty, without savouriness**: the kombu and bonito were left out, or the concentrate was diluted too much. Keep the two steps of extraction and dilution (see [umami](/principles/umami/)).
