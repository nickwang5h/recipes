*Tori no teriyaki* is Japanese pan-fried chicken glazed with a sweet and salty sauce. Whole skin-on chicken legs go in the pan and are cut after cooking, which helps keep the juices. The sake, mirin and soy sauce are used in similar amounts with a little sugar, and in the end only a thin layer of sauce is left to cling to the skin.

Browning and glazing are done in two stages. First fry off the water on the surface of the skin so the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) builds browning and a roasted aroma. Then add the teriyaki sauce and let the water evaporate while the sugar and soy sauce concentrate. If the sauce goes in too early, a wet surface is hard to colour, and the sugar easily burns at the edge of the pan.

## Steps

1. Bone the chicken legs and open them out, trimming away the cartilage and any obvious sinew. Cut across the thick parts of the meat and spread it to a thickness similar to the rest, keeping the skin intact. Blot both sides dry with paper towels and prick a few shallow holes in the skin.
2. Mix the sake, mirin, soy sauce and sugar and stir until there are no obvious grains of sugar. Set out a clean plate for the cooked chicken to rest on.
3. Heat a skillet over medium heat, wipe in the oil, and spread the chicken skin-side down. Fry until the skin is golden brown, the edges start to release fat and a spatula slides under easily; this usually takes 5–7 minutes. If a spot curls up, press it gently so the skin touches the pan.
4. Turn the chicken meat-side down, cover, turn to medium-low and keep cooking for about 10 minutes. Test the thickest part from the side, and move the chicken to the clean plate once the centre reaches 74°C (165°F); if not, keep cooking. Heat takes time to [reach the centre](/principles/heat-transfer/), and the colour of the skin does not tell you whether it is cooked.
5. Pour off most of the loose fat in the pan, keeping the brown juices. Pour in the teriyaki sauce and bring to a boil over medium heat, scraping up the brown film on the bottom, and reduce until the bubbles get denser and the sauce begins to coat a spoon slightly.
6. Return the chicken to the pan, turning it and spooning sauce over the skin. When only a thin layer is left in the pan and the chicken surface is shiny, take it off the heat at once. Let it rest briefly, cut into strips that are easy to pick up, and spoon the remaining sauce over.

## Key points

- **Pale skin that sticks to the pan**: the surface is too wet or the pan is too full, and the steam blocks browning. Blot the skin dry first, leave space in the pan, and turn only after the underside has set. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The sauce turns bitter and sticks like hard candy**: it was reduced too far, and once the water is nearly gone the temperature rises quickly in spots. Take it off the heat as soon as it starts to cling to the meat; throw out any sauce that already tastes burnt. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The finished dish is too salty**: reducing only removes water, and the salt in the soy sauce stays. Do not salt the chicken separately, and do not substitute dark soy sauce for Japanese soy sauce. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
