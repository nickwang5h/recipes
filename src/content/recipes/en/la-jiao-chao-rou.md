Hunan-style pork with chili peppers is the smoky char of green chilies together with the tenderness of thin slices of pork, done with two short bursts of heat: first the chilies are dry-fried until the surface wrinkles, then the thin pork is stir-fried, and the two are combined at the end. Once the surface moisture of the chilies has gone, the parts touching the pan brown easily; for the browning and aroma on the pork, compare the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).

Lean pork has little fat and loses water more easily once cooked. Slicing it thin and even heats it more evenly than simply turning up the heat (see [knife work](/principles/knife-work/)). Long green chilies suit dry-frying; bell peppers also work, though their heat and the water they release are different, so adjust by how the pan looks.

## Steps

1. Wash and dry the chilies, remove the stems, take out the seeds and white ribs to taste, and cut on the diagonal into pieces. Pieces of similar size wrinkle at the same time; mince the ginger, and smash the garlic and slice it.
2. Slice the lean pork thin against the grain and blot off surface moisture. Mix in 3 ml of light soy sauce, 3 ml of oyster sauce and about 1 g of salt, toss well and marinate for 10 minutes; the slices should be loose from each other, with no large amount of liquid in the bowl.
3. Put the chilies in a dry wok over medium-high heat, spread them out and turn from time to time so the skin touches the bottom of the pan. Once the skin wrinkles and has scattered brown blisters, sprinkle in about 1 g of salt, stir-fry 1 minute more and take out. Lower the heat if the bottom of the pan starts to blacken.
4. In the same wok add 8 ml of oil and heat until the oil spreads easily, then add the ginger and garlic. Stir-fry about 15 seconds, and add the pork as soon as you smell the fragrance and the edges of the garlic haven't darkened; don't wait until the oil smokes.
5. Add the pork, quickly spread it out and break the slices apart, and stir-fry about 2 minutes. When the slices change from translucent to opaque and separate from each other, return the chilies and stir together about 1 minute, until the pork is cooked through and the chili pieces are evenly hot.
6. Stir in the fermented black beans to taste and the 2 ml of finishing soy sauce, and stir-fry about 30 seconds more. Taste a piece of pork and a piece of chili first, and add the rest of the salt if it isn't salty enough; plate when the liquid at the bottom of the wok has reduced enough to coat the ingredients.

## Key points

- **The chilies release a pan of water**: the surface wasn't dried, or too many went in at once and the steam couldn't escape (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Dry-fry in batches, add the salt after they wrinkle, and keep the finished dish with little liquid.
- **The pork is stringy and tough to chew**: slicing with the grain left long fibres, or uneven thickness left the thin slices cooking too long (see [knife work](/principles/knife-work/)). Slice thin against the grain, have the chilies ready before stir-frying the pork, and combine as soon as the pork is cooked through.
- **The pork and chilies are both too salty**: light soy sauce, oyster sauce and fermented black beans already contain salt (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The limit for the whole dish is 3 g of salt, including what went in when the chilies were dry-fried; decide whether to add more only after the finishing soy sauce is in.
