Sigeumchi namul is a common Korean side dish: spinach cooked and seasoned with garlic, green onion, soy sauce and toasted sesame oil. The leaves are soft, the stems keep a little crispness, the sesame aroma clings to the greens, and there is no thin puddle of dressing at the bottom of the plate. It works as a small dish on the table, and it also goes well with grilled meat.

Blanching the spinach briefly in boiling water and then cooling it keeps the softness and firmness at just the right point to eat (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). The spinach is still hot inside when lifted out, and cooling it quickly slows the softening from residual heat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Squeezing out the water caught between the leaves keeps the small amount of seasoning from being diluted.

## Steps

1. Remove the roots and any yellowed leaves from the spinach, and separate the stems to wash out the grit. Finely mince the garlic and finely chop the green onion. Have 1000 ml of cold water ready first, so you can cool the spinach right after blanching.
2. Add 2000 ml of water to a large pot and heat over high heat for about 5–7 minutes, until the water is at a steady rolling boil. The spinach must be able to sit in the water. After it goes in, press down floating leaves with the back of a spoon so stems and leaves all touch the hot water.
3. Leave the pot uncovered and blanch for 30–60 seconds. Lift the spinach out when the leaves have just wilted, the colour is bright green, and the tender stems bend but still have a little spring. For small, thin-leaved spinach, start checking at 30 seconds.
4. Put the spinach in the cold water you prepared and gently pull it apart for about 30 seconds, until the stems and leaves no longer feel warm. Lift it into a colander and drain for about 1 minute, until the steady stream of water turns into occasional drips.
5. Divide it into small handfuls and squeeze out the remaining water with both hands until it no longer comes out in streams. Shake the squeezed clumps apart and cut them into pieces about 5 cm long, so you don't pull up one long stem at the table.
6. Put the spinach in a mixing bowl and add the garlic, green onion, soy sauce, toasted sesame oil and toasted sesame seeds. Toss by hand or with two spoons until even, loosening the leaves that cling together so every small pinch is coated with seasoning. Transfer to a small dish and serve.

## Key points

- **Leaves are mushy and the stems lost their crispness**: it was blanched too long, then piled up and kept warm after lifting. Have the cold water ready first, lift the spinach as soon as it reaches the right state, and don't wait for the whole pot to cook into a clump. See [blanching](/principles/blanching/).
- **Toasted sesame oil is in, but it still tastes watery**: too much cooling water is trapped between the leaves and washes the dressing away. After cooling, squeeze out the water first and then shake the spinach loose, and don't add more soy sauce to make up the flavour. See [blanching](/principles/blanching/).
- **Spinach squeezed into a tight rope**: it was wrung too hard and the clump was not pulled apart, so the seasoning only reaches the outer layer. After blanching, squeeze in small handfuls to keep the leaves soft, and toss it loose pinch by pinch. See [blanching](/principles/blanching/).
- **Garlic and sesame aroma turn dull**: the seasonings were cooked with the spinach, so the aroma was lost with the heat. Finish the blanching and cooling first, then toss in raw garlic and toasted sesame oil. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
