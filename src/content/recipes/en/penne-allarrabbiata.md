*Penne all'arrabbiata* is a common spicy tomato pasta from Rome. The base is garlic, chili, olive oil and tomato, with parsley added at the end. The ridges and hollow centre of the penne catch the sauce, so pasta and sauce come in together with each bite. Pecorino can be sprinkled on the plate to taste.

Garlic and chili are gently heated in the oil first, so their aroma and heat spread into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The pasta water carries [starch](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and a little added at the end and tossed in helps the oil and tomato juice come together into an even, thin sauce (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

## Steps

1. Crack the garlic, slice the chili into rings and chop the parsley. Crush the tomatoes with a fork or by hand. Boil a separate pot of water for the pasta, and add salt once it boils.
2. Put the olive oil, garlic and chili in a wide skillet and heat over low heat for about 3–4 minutes, until the garlic smells strongly and the surface is just starting to turn light golden. Do not let the garlic go dark brown or the chili turn black.
3. Add the tomatoes and a little salt and cook over medium heat for about 20 minutes, dragging a spoon across the bottom now and then. When the tomato pieces are soft and the juice has clearly thickened, and the spoon no longer leaves a rush of clear liquid behind it, it is nearly done.
4. When the sauce has about 10 minutes left, start cooking the penne, and taste a piece earlier than the package time. Lift it out when the outside is soft and the centre still gives a little resistance, setting aside a cup of the pasta water before draining.
5. Take the garlic out of the tomato sauce, put the pasta straight into the pan and toss over medium heat for about 1 minute. If the sauce is too dry to move, add a tablespoon of pasta water, toss, then look again rather than pouring in the whole cup at once.
6. When the surface of the pasta is evenly glossy, every piece is coated in sauce and there is no separate puddle of water at the bottom of the pan, turn off the heat and stir in the parsley. Plate immediately, and sprinkle on Pecorino for those who like cheese.

## Key points

- **The sauce tastes scorched and bitter**: the garlic or chili burned before the tomato went in, and the bitterness has gone into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Start the base over in a fresh pan. Next time heat gently over low heat and add the tomato as soon as the garlic is light golden.
- **A puddle of red water runs out beside the pasta**: the tomato was not cooked down enough, or too much water was added at the end (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Reduce the sauce well first, then add pasta water a little at a time. If the pasta is already cooked, take it out first, reduce the sauce, then toss it back in.
- **The pasta is mushy but the sauce is not coating it**: the pasta was already soft in the water, and tossing in the sauce pan keeps softening it (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Lift it out with some bite left, going by tasting and not only by the timer.
- **The parsley aroma is very weak**: it cooked too long in the hot sauce. Keep the chopped parsley until the heat is off, then toss it in and plate right away so the aroma is retained (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
