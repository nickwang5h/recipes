*Tương đậu phộng* is the peanut dipping sauce served with Vietnamese fresh spring rolls. Hoisin sauce brings a fermented-bean savouriness and a salty-sweet taste, peanut butter adds a nutty fragrance, and a light meat broth thins the two thick sauces to a dippable consistency. The base flavour of hoisin sauce comes from fermented soybeans. Once made, the sauce is served in small bowls with [Vietnamese spring rolls](/recipes/goi-cuon/).

Peanut butter contains fat and tiny peanut particles. Stirring the broth in gradually spreads the fat and particles evenly (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The finished sauce should flow slowly off a spoon and leave a thin coat on a rice paper roll. The garlic is first fried in the oil to bring out its fragrance before the water-containing sauces are added (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)); if the minced garlic burns black, the bitterness stays in the whole bowl of sauce.

## Steps

1. Loosen the two sauces. Put the peanut butter, hoisin sauce and sugar into a small bowl and add a total of 75 ml of light meat broth in two additions. Stir to a brown sauce with no lumps of peanut butter. If natural peanut butter has oil floating on top after opening, stir it back in inside the jar before measuring.
2. Fry the garlic. Put the vegetable oil and minced garlic in a small pot and stir-fry over medium-low heat for 30–60 seconds, until the garlic is clearly fragrant and still pale. Keep spreading it out as it cooks, and leave no blackened garlic at the bottom of the pot.
3. Cook the sauce evenly. Pour in the mixed sauce and cook over low heat for 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until the sugar melts and small bubbles appear evenly across the surface. When you scrape the bottom of the pot, the sauce should slowly close back together, and it should fall from the spoon in a continuous stream, not in a lump.
4. Let it cool to warm. Turn off the heat and pour it into a shallow bowl. Leave it for 5 minutes, until it no longer gives off obvious steam, stirring twice. As the temperature drops the sauce will thicken; divide it into 4 small bowls so everyone can dip their spring rolls.
5. Add the toppings. Just before serving, sprinkle on the chopped peanuts, and add minced red chili if you like heat. Keep the peanuts for last so the pieces stay crunchy and it is easy to see whether the sauce itself is smooth.

## Key points

- **Oil floats on top and lumps form inside**: the peanut butter was not stirred in evenly, or the broth went in too fast (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Take the pot off the heat, break up the lumps slowly with a small whisk, starting from the lumps, then return to low heat and cook evenly.
- **The sauce sticks into a lump**: too much water has been reduced (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). This pot only needs to be cooked until it bubbles evenly; it thickens more as it cools, so do not reduce it in the pot until it stands on a spoon.
- **The garlic tastes bitter**: the minced garlic was cooked too long (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Mix the sauce before turning on the heat and pour it in as soon as the garlic smells fragrant; throw out garlic that has already burnt black and start again.
