Stir-fried baby bok choy has soft leaves and crisp stems with a little bite. The method is to soften the leaves in the pan first, then add a little water to braise the stems through. The water boils at the bottom of the pan and carries heat between the piled-up leaves (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Judge doneness by the stems; collapsed leaves do not count.

The stems are thick and the leaves thin, so they do not cook at the same speed. Split thick stems lengthwise to shorten the distance heat has to travel to the centre (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Sugar only adds sweetness. To keep a bright colour, stop as soon as the greens are cooked and do not keep heating to fill time.

## Steps

1. Break the baby bok choy into small pieces and wash off the grit between the base and the leaf stalks. Drain off the surface water, split thick stems lengthwise, leave the leaves in large pieces, and peel and chop the garlic.
2. Heat the pan over medium or medium-high heat, add the oil and swirl to spread. After about 30 seconds, when the oil flows lightly without smoking, add the greens; with a thin pan you do not need to wait the full time.
3. Tip the greens into the pan and scoop up from the bottom to stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the leaves begin to soften and the stems still stand firm. Do not cram the pan; if there are many greens, fry in batches, taking the first batch out before cooking the next.
4. Add the garlic and toss until it is spread through the greens. Add 70 ml water and part of the salt, and keep stir-frying for about 1 minute; it is enough for the water to boil at the bottom of the pan, and it does not need to cover the leaves.
5. Add the sugar, turn to low heat, cover, and braise briefly for about 2 minutes. Tender baby bok choy can be checked early: uncover once the stems have no hard core and the leaves are still whole; for thick stems, uncover only when they are cooked.
6. Uncover and toss, taste a stem with some sauce, then decide whether to add the rest of the salt. If there is a lot of liquid in the pan, leave it uncovered to reduce for a bit, then plate; do not keep the cooked greens in the pan to stay warm.

## Key points

- **Leaves turned mushy while the stems are still hard**: the stems are too thick to cook as fast as the thin leaves (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Split thick stems lengthwise and taste the thickest part at the end; do not assume it is done because the leaves have collapsed.
- **Plate looks like vegetable soup**: the wash water was not drained and water was then added to "cover the leaves" (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add only the water the short braise needs, and uncover at the end to cook off the extra.
- **Salty and sweet but the colour is still dull**: too much sugar and salt, and braised too long. Decide the seasoning after the final taste (see [salt](/principles/salt/)); reduce the sugar to taste, and keep the colour by taking the greens out in time.
