Stuffed green peppers fill whole long peppers with seasoned pork filling, then pan-fry and braise them. Salting and stirring the filling dissolves some of the muscle protein, so the filling turns sticky and holds together in one mass (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Stirring until sticky is the key.

The pepper skin touching the pan wrinkles quickly, but the filling has to wait for heat to pass through the skin and the meat layer (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Pan-frying to blister the skin gives flavour, and the covered braise afterwards cooks the centre through. Choosing peppers of similar thickness and controlling how thickly you stuff them is more reliable than judging by the colour of the skin.

## Steps

1. Add the ginger, green onion, 15 ml light soy sauce and about 1 g salt to the filling, and keep stirring until it is sticky and holds together. If you like, add Shaoxing wine, pepper, sugar and oyster sauce; the egg makes the filling softer and can be left out at first. Cover and marinate for 10 minutes.
2. Wash and dry the peppers, cut off the stem end, and gently remove the seeds and white ribs through the opening, keeping the peppers as intact as you can. If using starch, dust 5 g of it thinly inside, turning each pepper so the starch clings, and shake out the loose excess.
3. Stuff the filling in through the opening in portions, so it touches the pepper wall, and fill to level with the opening. Do not press hard until the pepper bulges, and do not leave large gaps; keep peppers of different thickness apart, because thicker ones need longer to braise through.
4. Mix the remaining 15 ml light soy sauce, 5 ml dark soy sauce and 100 ml water into a sauce. If you plan to thicken, mix the remaining 5 g of starch with a little cold water and keep it for the end; do not pour it into the sauce early to braise for a long time.
5. Put 30 ml oil in the pan. Over medium-low heat, first set the exposed meat side on the bottom of the pan and fry until it sets, then lay the peppers down and fry them side by side. Take them out when the skin wrinkles and brown spots appear; hold them with tongs when turning so you do not break the skin.
6. Leave the oil in the pan, optionally stir-fry the garlic until fragrant, then pour in the sauce and bring to a boil. Return the peppers, cover and braise over medium-low heat for 5–8 minutes. Insert a thermometer into the centre of the filling in the thickest pepper; it is cooked at 71°C (160°F). If it is not there yet, add a little hot water and keep braising.
7. Uncover and reduce the sauce, tasting the liquid before deciding whether to add salt. If you want a thin glaze, stir the starch slurry well and pour it in a little at a time; cook until the sauce turns glossy and clings to the peppers, then plate, leaving a little flowing sauce in the pan.

## Key points

- **Filling falls out when picked up**: the filling was not stirred until sticky, or it was stuffed too loosely (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Stir until it holds together, then pack it in portions; the dusting of starch helps it cling but cannot replace the stickiness of the filling itself.
- **Pepper skin is soft but the filling is not cooked**: the meat layer is too thick or the braising too short (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Choose peppers of similar thickness, check the centre temperature at the thickest point, and add water and keep braising if it falls short.
- **Sauce sticks to the pan like thick glue**: the starch went in too early or in too large an amount (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Thicken thinly after the peppers are cooked, add the slurry in portions, and always leave some sauce in the pan.
