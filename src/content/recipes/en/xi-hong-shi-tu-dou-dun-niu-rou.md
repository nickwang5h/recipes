In beef stew with tomatoes and potatoes, the beef is tender, the potatoes are soft without falling apart, and the sauce is sweet-sour and thick. It is made in two stages of stewing. The connective tissue of beef chuck or brisket is softened first by moist heat (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)); the potatoes are added afterwards and cooked until soft but intact. Tomatoes and onion are fried into a base sauce, and the beef cooking liquid ties the whole pot together.

The spices release their aroma in oil first and are then lifted out, so you do not bite into whole Sichuan peppercorns (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Whether the potatoes break up depends on how long they stew, how hard the pot boils and how much you stir.

## Steps

1. Cut the beef into pieces about 5 cm, and the potatoes to the same size. Dice the onion. Cut a shallow cross in each tomato, scald it in boiling water until the skin curls up, then cool slightly, peel and cut into pieces. Keep the chopped green onion aside and cut the rest into sections; slice the ginger.
2. Put the beef in cold water and cook until the surface changes colour and scum rises, then lift out and rinse. Put it in a pressure cooker with the green onion sections, some of the ginger slices, 20 g Shaoxing wine and water within your cooker's limits. Once it comes to pressure, time about 20 minutes.
3. Turn off the heat and wait for the pressure to release completely before opening the lid. Lift out the beef, discard the green onion and ginger from the liquid and keep the warm cooking liquid. The beef may still be a little tough at this point, as it will keep stewing later.
4. Add 15 ml of oil to the wok and stir-fry the Sichuan peppercorns, star anise and bay leaves over medium-low heat. When they smell fragrant and have not turned black, lift them out. Add the beef, the remaining ginger slices and the chopped green onion, and stir-fry until the surface no longer sheds water. If the bottom of the pan gets dry, add a small spoonful of the cooking liquid.
5. Add the light soy sauce, 15 ml Shaoxing wine, the pepper and the tomato paste, and mix well. Add the onion and stir-fry until the edges turn translucent, then add the tomatoes and stir-fry until the pieces collapse and red juice comes out.
6. Pour in cooking liquid until it reaches the level of the beef, bring to a boil, then turn to low heat, cover and simmer gently. When a chopstick can be pushed into the beef but with a little resistance, add the potatoes and stew for another 30–40 minutes, keeping it at a gentle bubble and pushing gently from the bottom now and then to keep it from sticking.
7. Check that the beef can be pierced easily and the potatoes have no hard core. If there is a lot of liquid, uncover and reduce it briefly, then taste and add salt; if the tomatoes are sour, add a little sugar. Turn off the heat when the sauce coats the beef and potatoes, and stop stirring.

## Key points

- **Beef is still tough but the potatoes have broken up**: the two ingredients soften at different speeds (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Stew the beef first and add the potatoes when it is close to tender.
- **Burnt bottom and bitter sauce**: the tomatoes and potatoes sank to the bottom and there is too little liquid (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep it at a gentle simmer, add a little hot stock, and push gently from the bottom.
- **Spices cover the tomato flavour**: whole spices were fried in the oil for too long (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lift them out as soon as they smell fragrant and add no more spices afterwards.
