Katsudon (*katsudon*) briefly simmers [tonkatsu](/recipes/tonkatsu/), onion and egg in a sweet-savoury dashi, then lays it over rice. The bottom of the cutlet soaks up the liquid and softens, the top still shows the outline of the panko, and the egg layer ties the strips of meat together. The coating first sets by losing water in [frying](/principles/oil-temperature/), and at the end it has to take in the liquid.

Dashi and soy sauce carry the [umami](/principles/umami/), and the egg goes in last, setting by [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) into a soft topping. Leave enough simmering liquid to moisten the top layer of rice; if you reduce it to a syrup, the rice turns salty and dry. Use a shallow pan that lies flat under both cutlets, so the egg spreads evenly.

## Steps

1. Prepare the pork: make a few shallow cuts at the line between fat and lean, and pound the thick parts lightly so the two pieces are of the same thickness. Dry the surface and sprinkle with the salt and black pepper. Put the flour, the beaten egg for coating and the panko on three separate plates.
2. Coat the cutlets: coat them thinly in flour first and pat off the loose flour, then dip in the beaten egg, and finally cover completely with panko, pressing lightly until the crumbs stick to the meat and also cover the sides.
3. Put the frying oil in a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 5–8 minutes, until a thermometer reads 175°C (350°F). Fry one cutlet at a time for about 5–7 minutes each, turning partway, until the coating is evenly golden and the centre of the meat reaches 71°C (160°F); lift onto a rack and drain for 2 minutes, until no more oil drips from the surface.
4. Cut the cutlets into strips about 2 cm wide, keeping the original shape of each cutlet. Break up the 3 eggs for the topping with chopsticks, keeping some streaks of white. Put the onion and dashi in a shallow pan, cover, and cook over medium heat for 5 minutes, until the onion is almost translucent and bends easily.
5. Add the soy sauce, mirin and sugar and stir until the sugar dissolves. Arrange the cutlets on the onion, cover, and cook over low heat for 2 minutes, until the liquid is gently simmering again and the underside of the cutlets is soaked; there should still be liquid that flows at the bottom of the pan.
6. Pour the egg evenly over the cutlets and onion, cover, and cook over low heat for about 1 minute, until the egg layer has set into soft large curds with no runny raw egg, and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Turn off the heat right away and don't stir any more.
7. Divide the hot white rice between two bowls, lift the cutlets and egg layer with a wide spatula onto the rice, share the simmering liquid from the pan between them, scatter on the green onion, and eat right away.

## Key points

- **Cutlet browned outside but raw inside**: the meat is too thick or the oil too hot, so the surface colours first. Keep the thickness even, and even when the coating is golden, still check the centre temperature. See [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Egg layer broken and weeping**: the egg was stirred before it set, or kept cooking after it set. After pouring in the egg, cover and disturb as little as possible, and take it off once it reaches doneness. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Topping too salty and the rice dry**: the stock is salted, or it simmered uncovered too long. Use unsalted dashi, cook the onion covered, and leave some flowing liquid at the end. See [Umami](/principles/umami/).
