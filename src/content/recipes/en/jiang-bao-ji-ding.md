Diced chicken in sweet bean sauce uses sweet bean sauce for a salty-sweet fermented flavour, with the chicken thinly coated in sauce and little liquid left on the plate. The sauce itself already has sweetness and savouriness from fermentation (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Frying the sauce thick first means the chicken only needs a short toss when it goes back in, which makes it easier to keep both the sauce flavour and the tenderness.

Cut the chicken breast into even small dice and coat with wine and starch. The thin coating [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat and makes the surface smooth, but how tender the meat is still depends on cooking time. The cucumber, seeded, goes into the pan last. It cuts down on thin liquid and adds a fresh crunch to the thick sauce.

## Steps

1. Trim the membrane from the chicken breast, cut it into thick slices, then into dice of about 1.5 cm. Keep the size as even as you can. Drain off surface moisture from the cut meat so the marinade does not turn into a bowl of thin batter.
2. Toss the chicken with the salt, the Shaoxing wine for marinating and the ginger, then with the cornstarch, and mix until the surface is slightly tacky and there is no free liquid in the bottom of the bowl. Refrigerate for 15 minutes, until the coating clings evenly to the chicken. Use this time to prepare the cucumber and green onion.
3. Halve the cucumber lengthwise, scrape out the soft seeded core, and cut into dice close in size to the chicken. Pat the cut surfaces dry. Stir the sweet bean sauce, sugar, the Shaoxing wine for the sauce and the water together, breaking up lumps of sauce, and set it beside the stove.
4. Preheat a flat-bottomed wok over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until 1 tbsp of oil added to it flows quickly. Spread the chicken in the pan and fry for 0.5 minute to set the bottoms, then spread it apart and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until all sides turn white and the pieces separate. Take it out.
5. Turn the heat to medium-low in the same pan. Add the other 1 tbsp of oil and the green onion and stir-fry for about 0.5 minute, until the green onion smells fragrant but has not turned brown. Pour in the mixed sauce and stir for 1–2 minutes, until the colour is deep and glossy and the spatula briefly shows the bottom of the pan as it passes.
6. Return the chicken, with the juices on its plate, to the pan and stir-fry over medium-high heat for 1–2 minutes, until each piece is evenly coated. Cut open the largest piece: it is done when the centre reaches 74°C (165°F); if not, stir-fry a little longer.
7. Add the cucumber and stir-fry for about 0.5–1 minute, until the cut faces are hot through and still hold their shape. Drizzle on the toasted sesame oil and toss. When no thin liquid remains in the pan, plate it, so the chicken does not stay in the hot pan and keep drying out.

## Key points

- **Chicken clumps together and the coating feels thick and floury**: the surface of the meat was too wet, or the starch was not mixed in. The [thin coating gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and only needs to cling to the surface. Drain the meat first, then toss it evenly, and separate the pieces promptly in the pan.
- **Bitter sauce and meat without fragrance**: the thick sauce burnt on an overheated pan bottom. Following the handling of [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/), lower the heat and keep stirring, and combine in the pan as soon as the sauce is thick and glossy. If it has turned bitter, make the sauce again.
- **Dry, tough chicken**: the [proteins keep contracting](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and the meat loses water. Take the meat out first and fry the sauce separately, and plate it promptly once it is cooked through and coated, rather than cooking the meat while slowly reducing the sauce.
- **Water at the bottom of a lunch box**: the cucumber seeds and core and an unthickened sauce carry water in. Reduce off thin liquid before packing, spread the food in a shallow box, and refrigerate promptly once it stops steaming, within 2 hours of leaving the pan. Cool it fully before sealing the lid. Reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) at the centre.
