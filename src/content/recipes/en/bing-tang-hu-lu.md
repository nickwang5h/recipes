Tanghulu has a thin, clear layer of hard candy on the outside; the sugar shell cracks first when you bite, followed by the sourness of the hawthorn. The moment the syrup is ready you have to dip, with no time to prepare slowly, so thread and dry the skewers and set out the tray for the dripped sugar before you turn on the heat.

The sugar shell hardens as water evaporates, the syrup concentrates and then cools. It is different from the caramel colour discussed under [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/): here you stop the heat before it clearly browns, to keep the shell clear. Concentrated syrup also recrystallizes easily into white grains, so stir as little as possible once it has come to a boil, and do not carry sugar grains from the sides of the pot back into the syrup.

## Steps

1. Wash the hawthorn and remove the stems and the leftover calyx at the bottom. Cut around the middle, dig out the hard core and put the two halves back together. Pat dry with paper towels and spread out to dry for about 15 minutes, until there are no water drops on the skin or the cut surface; squeeze as little as possible when coring so the fruit keeps its shape.
2. Divide the hawthorn into 6 skewers, running a bamboo skewer through both halves so they stay closed. Each skewer is about 50 g, with a little space between the fruits. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper, and set 200 ml cold water and a bamboo skewer for testing the sugar beside it.
3. Put the sugar and 145 ml water in a small-diameter heavy-bottomed pot and swirl the pot gently so all the sugar is wet. Heat over medium heat for about 2–3 minutes, until the grains are completely dissolved and the syrup boils; from this point do not stir with a spoon.
4. Keep boiling over medium heat for about 5–7 minutes, until the syrup is slightly pale yellow with dense bubbles. Dip a bamboo skewer in a little syrup and immediately plunge it into the cold water for about 0.1 minutes; when the sugar piece has cooled, take it out and it should snap cleanly, not bend soft or stick to your teeth. If it is still soft, boil another 0.5 minutes each time and test again, and once it snaps brittle, move the pot off the heat.
5. Tilt the pot gently so the syrup gathers on one side, quickly dip the skewers one by one, turn once, and lift out as soon as they are coated in a thin layer. Set them on the parchment with space between the skewers, and hold the skewer by its end, well away from the syrup and the rim of the pot.
6. Leave for about 5 minutes, until the sugar shell feels cool and sounds hard and brittle when tapped, then peel the skewers gently from the paper and eat. Make and eat them fresh, while the shell is crisp.

## Key points

- **Shell sticks to the teeth and has soft sugar threads**: the water has not evaporated enough to reach hard candy (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Go by whether it snaps brittle in cold water; bubble size and how many minutes it has boiled are only a guide.
- **Sugar turns to white grains and will not coat the fruit**: the sugar has recrystallized (see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Let all the sugar dissolve at the start, do not stir after it boils, do not scrape sugar grains from the sides of the pot back in, and do not dip the skewers repeatedly.
- **Shell turns brown with a bitter taste**: the concentrated syrup kept heating and slid into deeper [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/). Turn the heat down as it nears the brittle stage and take the pot off the heat as soon as it snaps; the residual heat in the bottom of the pot keeps heating the syrup after you take it off.
