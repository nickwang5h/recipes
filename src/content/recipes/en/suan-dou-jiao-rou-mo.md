Stir-fried pickled long beans with ground pork puts the sour, salty taste of the pickled beans, the fat flavour of the pork, and the aroma of chili and garlic into one pan. The beans are cut into small pieces so every spoonful picks up both vegetable and meat. The sourness comes entirely from ready-made pickled beans (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). They are already sour and salty, so taste one before cooking.

The ground pork is stir-fried apart first and its surface moisture driven off, so the edges can develop [browning aroma](/principles/maillard-reaction/). That oil is then used to stir-fry the ginger, garlic, doubanjiang and chilies (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). The pickled beans join last and are only heated through, so the result is loose bits of meat and still-crisp beans, with no pool of liquid at the bottom of the pan.

## Steps

1. Mix the ground pork with 1 tbsp oil, the cornstarch, light soy sauce and Shaoxing wine until no dry starch is visible. Refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the seasoning is absorbed by the meat and there is no obvious liquid at the bottom of the bowl.
2. Drain the pickled long beans for 3 minutes, until they stop dripping brine, and cut them into small pieces about 5 mm. Taste one piece, and if it is especially salty, rinse quickly and pat dry. Cut the green and red bell peppers into dice of similar size, and prepare the ginger, garlic and chilies.
3. Heat the remaining 2 tbsp oil in a wok over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until the oil flows easily. Add the ground pork, spread it out and stir-fry for 3–4 minutes, pressing it apart as you go, until all the meat has changed colour and the edges are slightly golden, and a small pile pushed together reads 71°C (160°F) in the centre.
4. Push the meat to one side, add the ginger and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the ginger smell is clear; add the garlic and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the edges of the garlic are pale yellow, then mix with the meat.
5. Turn to medium heat, add the doubanjiang and the green and red bell peppers, and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the oil turns red and the edges of the pepper dice are slightly soft, with the doubanjiang not burnt black.
6. Add the chili rings, pickled long beans, sugar and white pepper, turn to medium-high heat and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the beans are hot through but the pieces are still distinct, and there is no pooled liquid when you push the bottom of the pan clear.
7. Before taking it out, taste a bite with the pork; the saltiness of the pickled beans and doubanjiang should be enough. Plate right away and serve with white rice. Do not leave it in the hot pan, where the beans would keep braising soft.

## Key points

- **The pork clumps and tastes only of cooked meat**: it was not broken up soon enough after going into the pan, and water built up on the surface (see [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Spread the meat out and break it up, and add the vegetables once the steam eases.
- **So salty it covers the sourness**: the saltiness of the pickled beans and doubanjiang stack up (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Taste the beans first, rinse them if necessary, and add no salt while stir-frying.
- **Bitter garlic and doubanjiang**: the aromatics stayed too long on the dry hot bottom of the pan (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Mix the garlic back into the meat when it is just pale yellow, and stir-fry the doubanjiang briefly over medium heat.
