Jiuniang (also called laozao) is sweet glutinous rice and rice liquid with a faint wine aroma. The rice is cooked first, which is [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); the enzymes in the yeast then break the starch down into sugar, and fermentation goes on to bring out the wine aroma (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). The sweetness and wine aroma change over time, so when you smell a clean, sweet wine aroma it is time to cool it down.

Cooking the rice and mixing in the yeast take only a short while, but fermentation takes 24–48 hours. Weigh the yeast by the dry rice weight and the package instructions, and don't add extra. The 3.2 g here matches a product with 8 g of yeast per 2000 g of rice. Use only a little of the boiled and cooled water at first to help mix, and add the rest as liquid once it is ready, so the rice isn't soaking in water from the start.

## Steps

1. Wash the container and tools, scald them with boiling water or boil them according to their material, and drain. Rinse the glutinous rice and drain it thoroughly, put it in a rice cooker with 720 ml of water and cook on the rice setting.
2. Cook the rice until the centre of each grain is soft, with no hard core, and the grains stay separate. Spread it in a clean container to cool, measure several spots with a thermometer, and mix in the yeast only when all of them have dropped to about 30°C (85°F).
3. Dissolve the yeast in the 20 ml of boiled and cooled water from the recipe, at about 30°C (85°F), and drizzle it evenly over the rice. Then add the 100 ml of boiled and cooled water in stages and toss, breaking up the lumps of rice so every part is coated with the yeast liquid. The rice should be moist, not sitting in water.
4. Pack the mixed rice into the fermenting container, press it level lightly, and make a hole in the centre so you can watch it. Put the lid on to hold in moisture. Fermentation gives off gas, so if you use a glass jar, don't screw the lid tight.
5. Ferment in a place at about 28–32°C (80–90°F). Start checking after 24 hours; usually within 24–48 hours there is a clean, sweet wine aroma, clear liquid seeps into the centre hole, and the grains turn soft. Use clean tools when you open it, and open it as few times as you can.
6. When you smell the wine aroma and see clear liquid, refrigerate it and heat it to eat within 2–3 days. For more liquid, add the reserved boiled and cooled water, up to 480 ml. Refrigeration only slows fermentation; the aroma and sweetness will still change.

## Key points

- **Rice is hard and has no sweetness**: the rice was not cooked through, or hot rice killed the yeast when it was mixed in (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Check for a hard core first, then measure the temperature with a thermometer.
- **Wine flavour too strong and the grains hollow and loose**: too much yeast, or it fermented too long in the warmth. Weigh the yeast by the package ratio, refrigerate once it is ready, and don't add water and keep fermenting at room temperature.
- **Fuzz, black or red spots, or a spoiled smell**: most likely contamination by unwanted microbes. Throw out the whole batch; scraping off the surface or heating it again won't save it.
- **Liquid keeps appearing after refrigeration**: the cold slows fermentation (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)) but doesn't stop it completely. Refrigerate in small portions, finish them soon, and freeze any extra promptly.
