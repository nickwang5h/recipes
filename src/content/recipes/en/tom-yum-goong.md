*Tom yam kung* is a hot and sour clear soup in which the fragrance of lemongrass, galangal and makrut lime leaf wraps around the sweetness of the shrimp. This is the clear style, *nam sai*, with a distinct sour and hot edge. In Thailand it is eaten with rice and other dishes, each person taking a small bowl; with rice the flavour can be stronger, but you should still taste the shrimp itself.

Smash the aromatics, then cook them briefly, and the fragrance goes into the base (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lime juice and cilantro wait until the heat is off, so their fresh smell is kept (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Shrimp [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) set very quickly with heat, so soften the mushrooms and onion first and add the shrimp last: the vegetables are cooked and the shrimp are still springy.

## Steps

1. Snip the sharp points and long whiskers off the shrimp, butterfly down the back and devein, keeping the washed heads and shells. For easier eating, you can peel out the shrimp meat and keep the heads and shells to one side. Thaw frozen shrimp in the fridge first.
2. Smash the tender lemongrass core and cut into lengths, slice the galangal thinly and tear the lime leaves. Lightly smash the garlic and chilies. Wash your hands after handling chilies, then squeeze the lime juice and set it aside.
3. Put the water, lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves, garlic and chilies in a pot. If you peeled the shrimp, add the heads and shells now, cook until the soup smells clearly aromatic, about 8–10 minutes, then lift out the heads and shells.
4. Add the mushrooms, onion and tomato, keep at a gentle boil and cook until the mushrooms are slightly soft and the edges of the onion are translucent. Add the fish sauce and sugar, stir and taste the base.
5. Add the shrimp, separating any that overlap, and cook until the flesh turns from translucent to opaque. Insert a thermometer into the thick part of a larger shrimp, and turn off the heat at **74°C (165°F)**; do not keep it boiling.
6. Off the heat, pour in the lime juice and scatter on the cilantro. Taste a spoonful with a mushroom in it: first check whether the sourness and savouriness are enough, and if not, add fish sauce or lime juice a little at a time; if it is not hot enough, add another smashed chili.
7. Lift out the tough aromatics and ladle into bowls with the shrimp and vegetables. If you leave the lemongrass, galangal and leaves in the soup, remind everyone to pick them out.

## Key points

- **Hot but the fragrance is hollow**: the lemongrass was not smashed, or the galangal and lime leaf are missing. Use fresh or frozen and put together the full base fragrance; ordinary ginger and lime juice cannot stand in for these two. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Soup is very sour but without the fresh smell of lime**: the lime juice went in too early and cooked along with the soup. Add it off the heat, and when adjusting the sourness try it on one spoonful of soup first so the whole pot does not turn too sour at once. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
- **Shrimp are tight and hard to bite**: they cooked too long with the vegetables, and the shrimp proteins tightened too far. Cook the vegetables just right first, save the shrimp for last and take the pot off the heat once they are done. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
