*Panzanella* is a cold Tuscan salad made from old bread. The bread is soaked soft, squeezed dry, then rubbed into loose coarse crumbs and mixed with tomato, red onion and basil. Traditional unsalted bread is dense and still has grain after soaking. The country loaf you can buy in Canada should also be a plain, firm white bread.

Bread goes stale for two reasons: it loses water, and its starch re-forms and hardens, which is the retrogradation described under [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). To soften it again, the key is to squeeze out the soaking water so there is room for tomato juice, oil and vinegar. The result is soft, grainy bread with crisp vegetables. After mixing, it needs to rest in the refrigerator for about 1 hour.

## Steps

1. Cut the old bread into thick slices and soak them in cold water for a few minutes. Break one slice open by hand: when the centre is also wet and it crumbles when pinched, take it out. Bread differs in how fast it absorbs water, so do not soak it until the outer layer dissolves into the water.
2. Divide the bread into small portions and squeeze each hard with both hands. After you let go, it should break into coarse crumbs, with no water dripping continuously when you grip it. Rub it apart into a large bowl, keeping soft grains of different sizes.
3. Slice the red onion thin and dice the tomatoes small, and collect the juice from cutting the tomatoes into the bowl too. If using cucumber, peel and slice it thin. Wash the basil leaves, dry them and tear them.
4. Add the vegetables and basil to the bread, drizzle with the olive oil, and fold gently from the bottom with two spoons until the bread is evenly oiled and the tomato dice are spread out. Do not squeeze repeatedly, or it will press into a lump.
5. Cover and refrigerate for about 1 hour, so the bread takes up the juices from the vegetables. Just before serving add the white wine vinegar, salt and black pepper, toss lightly, and taste a bite with bread to judge whether the whole salad is sour and salty enough.
6. Look at the bottom of the bowl. If there is only a little tomato juice, and the bread grains are soft with no dry hard centre, it is ready to serve. If any part is still dry, spoon some of the juice from the bottom over it and fold once more, gently.

## Key points

- **The bread turns to a paste with no grain**: soft sandwich bread was used, or it was soaked too long and not squeezed enough (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Use dense day-old bread, take it out as soon as the centre is just wet through, and squeeze in small portions.
- **It gets saltier as it rests**: the bread has its own salt, and the amount of seasoning meant for traditional unsalted bread was used as it is (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Taste and add salt only after the vegetables and bread are mixed.
- **One bite sour and the next bland**: the vinegar was not mixed through, or only the juice at the bottom of the bowl was tasted (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Drizzle the vinegar around and fold lightly, then taste a bite with bread and tomato, and add a little more if needed.
