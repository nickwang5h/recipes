Steamed cured meats puts Hunan smoked cured pork, cured chicken and cured fish into one bowl and steams them until the dry, hard meat softens and the fat seeps out, and the different cured aromas gather in the juices at the bottom of the bowl. Fermented black beans add salty savouriness and dried chili adds dry fragrance; the fatty, moist cured pork and the firm chicken and fish are eaten together, and you can still taste what makes each different.

Steam condenses to water on the bowl and the food, carrying heat in (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cured meats are already salty, so washing them first and pre-boiling when needed takes the saltiness down (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Leave a little of the original juices in the bowl, so the meat softens in a moist bowl; some of the dried chili's aroma moves into the rendered fat (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Rinse the three cured meats with 500 ml of water and wipe off the smoke residue and surface salt. Bone the cured chicken; scale the cured fish, remove the main bones and run a hand over each piece for fine bones, then weigh by net weight.
2. Put the remaining 1000 ml of water and the cured meats in a pot, bring to a boil in about 8 minutes, then simmer on low for 5 minutes, until the surface has softened and the water tastes salty. Lift out and drain for 2 minutes, until nothing drips from the surface, and discard the pre-boiling water.
3. Cut the cured pork into slices about 4 cm long and 5–7 mm thick, and cut the cured chicken and fish into strips of similar size. If the pork rind is still hard and tough, cut it off; keep the fish in shape and don't chop it up.
4. Arrange the three cured meats separately in a heatproof bowl, the pork in the middle and the chicken and fish on the sides, skin side toward the bottom of the bowl. Don't pack them tightly, and leave room at the rim; pour over the Shaoxing wine and sprinkle with the fermented black beans and chili flakes.
5. Add 2000 ml of water to the steamer and bring to a boil for about 10 minutes, until it steams steadily. Set the bowl on the rack, cover tightly and steam over medium-high heat for 40–50 minutes, until the fat of the cured pork is translucent, the chicken and fish bite apart easily, and the centre of the thickest chicken reaches 74°C (165°F).
6. If the cured meats are still dry and hard, steam 10 minutes more. While steaming, check the water level every 20 minutes; when the water is nearly gone, add 500 ml of boiling water around the edge of the pot so the steam keeps going.
7. Take out and drizzle with toasted sesame oil, and serve in the bowl. To turn it out onto a plate, cover the rim of the bowl with a deep plate, flip it wearing oven mitts, then lift off the bowl, leaving the juices on the plate. At home you can also steam in a shallow bowl and serve in the bowl, which keeps the cured meats whole along with the juices.

## Key points

- **Every bite is too salty**: the salt of the cured meats and the fermented black beans adds up (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Discard the pre-boiling water, rinse the surface salt off the black beans, and add no salt at all when steaming together.
- **The outside is hot but the inside is still dry and hard**: the pieces are cut too thick or packed too tightly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut to similar thickness and arrange loosely, and the centre steams soft and moist too.
- **The chili bits are fragrant but bitter**: the water in the steamer boiled dry, and the bottom of the bowl lost its moist environment (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Top up with boiling water when it runs low, and don't let the steamed dish turn into dry roasting.
