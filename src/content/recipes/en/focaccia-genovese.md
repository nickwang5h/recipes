*Focaccia genovese* is a flatbread from Genoa, its surface covered in dimples pressed by fingertips. Salt water and olive oil run into the dimples. The raised parts bake golden, and the bottoms of the dimples stay soft and moist. This version is plain: you taste wheat, oil and salt, and the dough must be spread thin and even.

Yeast gives off gas and [gluten](/principles/gluten/) holds the gas in the dough. In the oven, the starch takes up water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and the bread sets its shape. Kneading builds structure, and two rises and a rest make the dough airy and easy to spread. The water in the dimples makes those spots dry out more slowly, which is what gives crisp tops and soft dimples.

## Steps

1. Take 60 ml from the dough water and mix it with the dry yeast. Let it sit for about 10 minutes, until fine bubbles appear on the surface. Add all the flour, the rest of the dough water, 9 g salt and 1 tbsp olive oil, and mix until no dry flour is left.
2. Knead the dough for about 10 minutes, until the surface is fairly smooth and the dough can stretch into a sheet without tearing at once. It should be soft but hold together. If it sticks to your hands, gather it with a bench scraper rather than keep adding flour, which makes the dough stiffer the longer you knead.
3. Grease a metal baking pan of about 23 × 35 cm with 1 tbsp oil, put in the dough and cover with a damp cloth. Let it rest for about 90 minutes, until the dough has clearly grown and springs back slowly when pressed lightly.
4. Use the heel of your hand to push the dough into the corners of the pan and spread it into an even thin layer. If it shrinks back as you pull, cover it and pause, then push again. Cover with a damp cloth and let it rise for about 45 minutes more, until the dough is loose and thicker. Meanwhile preheat the oven to 230°C (450°F).
5. With your fingertips held together, press dense dimples into the dough, pressing deep but not through the bottom. Stir together the water for the topping, 3 g salt and 2 tbsp oil, pour it over at once, and brush lightly to the edges so every dimple catches some liquid.
6. Bake on the middle rack for about 22–25 minutes, until the raised parts are golden, the edges are crisp and the underside has some colour. If the two sides colour unevenly, turn the pan. Turn the focaccia out onto a rack right away to let the steam under it escape, and cut it while it is still warm.

## Key points

- **It won't spread and shrinks back when you let go**: the tension from kneading hasn't relaxed. Cover and rest it, then push it out again, keeping the dough moist. Forcing it tears the gas network. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Heavy, with no soft air pockets**: it went into the oven before it had risen, or a lot of flour was worked in halfway through kneading. Watch whether the dough has grown and whether it springs back slowly, and wait longer if your kitchen is cool. The gluten must form first to hold the gas. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **The top is dark brown but the bottom is still soft and wet**: the top gets heat faster, and the bottom gets too little. Use a metal pan, preheat well, and if needed move it to a lower rack to finish colouring the bottom. After unmoulding, let steam escape on a rack. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) and the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
