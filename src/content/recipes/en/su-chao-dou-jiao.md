These beans are braised until soft and well seasoned, with a thin savoury sauce clinging to the outside. The method is to stir-fry, then braise: a little oil carries the aroma of the green onion and chili onto the pods, then water and steam carry heat into the pods (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Only after the beans are cooked through does the sauce get reduced.

The minced garlic goes in last to keep a brighter garlic aroma (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and oyster sauce already contain salt, and the saltiness gets stronger as the sauce reduces, so any extra salt is judged last.

## Steps

1. Wash the beans, snap off both ends, pull off any tough strings that come away, and cut diagonally into pieces 4–10 cm long. Keep pieces of similar thickness together, and split especially thick pieces so that some are not cooked while others are still raw in the same pan.
2. Mix the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and oyster sauce into a sauce, without adding salt yet. Chop the green onion, slice the chilies into rings, and mince the garlic and set it aside separately.
3. Add 10–15 ml oil to the pan and heat over medium heat until the oil spreads smoothly and is not yet smoking. Add the green onion and chilies and stir-fry until fragrant, without letting the green onion burn black at the edges.
4. Add the beans and stir-fry for about 30 seconds to coat all sides in oil. Pour in the sauce, raise the heat and stir-fry for about 2 minutes. If the sauce has already stuck to the bottom of the pan, go to the water step sooner.
5. Pour in 150 ml water, bring to a clear boil, then cover. Adjust the heat to one that keeps it boiling and braise for 8–10 minutes, stirring once in a while so the beans on top also soak in the hot liquid.
6. Check the thickest pod and keep cooking until whole pieces are soft and cooked, with no hard raw tissue inside; if still hard, add hot water and keep braising, going by doneness and not just the time. Once cooked through, uncover and reduce the sauce, leaving a sauce that thinly coats the beans.
7. Add the garlic and toss, heating just until the aroma comes out. Now taste a fully cooked piece of bean, add a little salt if needed, and plate right away.

## Key points

- **The outside has changed colour but the inside is still hard**: the heat has not fully reached the thick pods (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The beans must be cooked through; add hot water to keep it boiling and keep cooking, and do not taste raw beans for flavour.
- **The sauce sticks to the bottom and tastes bitter**: the liquid reduced too early, and the green onion, chili and sauce burned on the hot pan (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the beans as soon as you smell the aroma, and add water when the bottom of the pan goes dry.
- **Too salty after reducing**: the salt in the sauces stays in the pan and concentrates as the liquid reduces (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add extra salt only at the end; if it is too salty, add more cooked beans and toss with a little water.
