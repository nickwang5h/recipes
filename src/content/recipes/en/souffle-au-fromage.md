*Soufflé au fromage* is a French hot dish in which a cheese egg base is baked until it puffs up. The top is golden, and underneath it is soft and light. The classic base is a thick white sauce of butter, flour and milk, with egg yolks and cheese stirred in and whipped egg whites folded through. Once served it slowly settles, so wait until everyone is seated before it goes in the oven.

When egg whites are whipped, air is trapped in thin films of protein. In the oven the air in the bubbles expands and steam pushes the volume up further, and [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) then sets the bubbles in place. The white sauce has already undergone [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), so it has body, and the bubbles do not escape easily before baking. The whites must be whipped stiff and fine, folded gently, and the white sauce must not be as thin as milk.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 205°C (400°F). Use 5 g of the butter to grease a straight-sided soufflé dish of about 1.4 litres evenly, sprinkle in the cheese for coating the dish, and turn the dish so the inside has a thin layer all round. Separate the yolks and whites, and keep the bowl for the whites free of oil and yolk.
2. Melt 40 g butter in a small pot and stir in the flour. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring, for about 2 minutes, until fine bubbles appear and there is no raw flour smell. Keep it pale. Off the heat, add the hot milk and whisk it in quickly.
3. Add the salt, white pepper, nutmeg and cayenne, return to the heat and cook, stirring, for about 1 minute, until the white sauce is thick and a line drawn across the bottom of the pot stays visible. Off the heat, stir in the yolks one at a time, mixing each in fully, and let it cool until warm.
4. Whip the 5 egg whites until fine and glossy, with peaks that stand when the whisk is lifted. Stir about a quarter into the white sauce to loosen the base. Add the cheese, saving a spoonful for the top, then fold in the remaining whites in batches, lifting from the bottom, and stop as soon as no white streaks are visible.
5. Pour the batter into the dish, filling it about three-quarters full, smooth the top gently and sprinkle on the remaining cheese. Place on the middle rack of the oven and immediately lower the setting to 190°C (375°F). Do not open the oven door for the first 20 minutes.
6. Bake for about 30–35 minutes, until the top has risen and coloured and there is no runny batter in the centre. Insert a thin probe from the side into the centre; at 74°C (165°F) it is done. Serve at once, spooning it out at the table.

## Key points

- **It barely rises**: the whites picked up oil, or many bubbles were crushed while folding. Separate the eggs over a small bowl, stir a little white into the thick base to thin it first, then fold in the rest. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **White lumps inside, coarse texture**: the whites were whipped dry and clumpy and are hard to mix in evenly. Stop whipping when the peaks stand and are still glossy. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Dark brown on top, still runny in the centre**: the dish is too deep or the top heat is too strong, so the centre warms slowly. Leave enough room for it to expand, and once the top has coloured, loosely cover it with foil and keep baking until the centre is cooked through. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
