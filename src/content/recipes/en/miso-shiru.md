Miso soup (*misoshiru*) is savoury and gently warming, a Japanese home soup built on dashi and miso, with tofu, wakame and green onion varying by season. Miso comes from the [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/) of soybeans and other ingredients, and it supplies both salt and free amino acids. Together with kombu and bonito dashi, it gives the soup fuller [umami](/principles/umami/). Each bowl is a small serving, eaten with white rice and other dishes.

The saltiness of miso varies by product, so hold a little back and taste before deciding. Be especially careful with instant dashi that already contains salt (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Once the miso is dissolved, heat the soup only gently, because the aroma is lost in a long boil.

## Steps

1. Put the dried wakame in cold water and soak it as the package directs, until the fronds open out (usually about 3–5 minutes). Drain, and cut any very long pieces shorter. Drain and cube the tofu, and slice the green onion finely. All three toppings can be left out, keeping just the broth.
2. Pour 800 ml of unsalted [Dashi](/recipes/dashi/) into a small pot and heat over medium heat until fine bubbles form at the edge. Add the tofu and nudge it gently so every cube is submerged, without stirring back and forth with a spoon.
3. Add the soaked wakame and keep warming for about 1 minute, until the centre of the tofu is hot too. The surface should be moving only slightly. Turn off the heat right away and move the pot off the burner, which is still hot.
4. Ladle some hot broth into a small bowl, add 3 tbsp of red miso and stir to a loose paste with no lumps, then pour it back into the pot and stir through. Taste the soup, then dissolve the remaining 1 tbsp in a little at a time until the saltiness is enough to go with rice.
5. If the soup has cooled, return it to low heat and warm it gently. When you see steam rising, take it off the heat and do not let the surface boil. Divide the soup and toppings among four small bowls, sprinkle with green onion and serve at once.

## Key points

- **A mouthful of salty miso**: the paste was not dispersed. Dissolve it in a little broth first, then return it to the pot. For coarse miso, dissolve it in a small strainer to catch the larger bits of bean skin.
- **The aroma is faint, yet the soup keeps getting saltier**: boiling drives off the aroma, and evaporating water concentrates the salt. Add the miso last and serve right after it dissolves. If it is too salty, add unsalted dashi (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **Flat flavour, and more miso does not fix it**: the broth is too weak. Use enough kombu and bonito dashi rather than mixing miso into plain water. Before adding more umami, work out whether the soup is weak or just short of salt (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)).
