*Japchae* is a Korean dish of sweet potato starch noodles tossed with beef, spinach, mushrooms and other vegetables. The soy sauce gives a salty savoury base, and the sugar and toasted sesame oil round it off. It is often served as a shared side dish, and it can also fill two bowls as a light main. Cooked noodles are clear and chewy; they turn hard as they cool, and heating them again with a little water restores part of the softness (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

The vegetables are cooked separately and only combined at the end. The onion needs to soften, the carrot only needs to lose its raw crunch, and the beef must cook through. Cooking each in its own batch lets everything finish at just the right point (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Soak the dried shiitake in the fridge ahead of time, and squeeze out the water before you start.

## Steps

1. Toss the beef and the softened shiitake with the marinade, cover and refrigerate. Cut the carrot and bell pepper into thin matchsticks, slice the mushrooms and cut the onion into thin strips, keeping the thickness as even as you can.
2. Beat the egg yolk with a pinch of salt. Spread 1 tsp of the vegetable oil over a small nonstick pan and wipe off the excess. Over low heat, pour in the yolk and spread it into a thin sheet. Flip it and cook until both sides are set with no liquid egg left and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Take it out, let it cool and cut it into thin strips.
3. Blanch the spinach in boiling water for 30–60 seconds, until the leaves collapse. Lift it out, rinse with cold water, squeeze dry and cut into shorter lengths. Toss with 1 tsp of the soy sauce and 1 tsp of the toasted sesame oil, and put it in a large mixing bowl.
4. Bring the same pot of water back to a boil and add the noodles. Boil for 1 minute and stir to separate them, then cook about 7 minutes more. Check against the package: they should be clear all the way through, with no hard core when bitten, and still chewy. Drain and snip a few times with scissors. Toss with 2 tsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tsp soy sauce and 1 tsp sugar, and add to the mixing bowl.
5. Heat 2 tsp of the vegetable oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Stir-fry the onion and green onion for about 2 minutes, until the edges of the onion turn translucent, and transfer to the bowl. Fry the white mushrooms in 2 tsp oil for about 2 minutes, until soft and shiny, and transfer them too.
6. Stir-fry the carrot in 1 tsp oil for about 20 seconds, add the bell pepper and stir-fry about 20 seconds more, until the strips are slightly soft but still stand up. Transfer to the bowl. If you are leaving out the bell pepper, cook the carrot just until it loses its raw crunch.
7. Add 2 tsp oil to the pan and stir-fry the beef and shiitake until the strips of beef are cooked through and the shiitake are soft and shiny. Add them to the bowl with their juices, then stir in the remaining soy sauce, sugar, toasted sesame oil, minced garlic and black pepper until the colour is even.
8. Add the egg sheet and sesame seeds. Using two spoons, lift from the bottom of the bowl and toss until the noodles are loose and the vegetables evenly spread. Serve warm.

## Key points

- **Noodles are hard and tough to chew**: they were not cooked through, or the starch set again as they cooled. Bite one to check while boiling. If they have cooled and hardened, toss them in the pan with a little water and stop the heat once they soften. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Water pools at the bottom of the bowl and the seasoning is weak**: the spinach was not squeezed dry, or the mushrooms and onion were crowded in the pan. Cook the vegetables separately, drain off the extra liquid, then toss them with the noodles. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Noodles break up while the carrot is still raw**: the strips were too thick and were stir-fried together for too long at the end. Cut them thin, cook each one properly on its own, and toss gently. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
