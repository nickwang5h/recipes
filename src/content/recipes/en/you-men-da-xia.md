The flavour of oil-braised prawns comes from first getting the fragrance of green onion, ginger and Sichuan peppercorns into the oil, then coating the whole prawns in a salty-sweet sauce that carries the prawn flavour. The infused oil should be fragrant but not bitter, which depends on gentle heating and lifting out the solids in time (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lightly pressing the prawn heads lets the juices and fat inside run into the pan, and that makes the sauce red and glossy.

Frying, braising and reducing each do one job: frying brings out the fragrance of the shell, braising lets heat reach the flesh, and reducing concentrates the flavour. The cooked prawns come out first and the sauce is reduced on its own, so the prawns are not made into hard meat as the [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tighten during reducing.

## Steps

1. Snip the spike, long feelers and legs off the prawns, cut along the back and remove the vein, and clear out the visible sand sac in the head. Wash and blot dry with paper towels, especially along the cut on the back; wipe the water off the green onion sections and ginger slices as well.
2. Put the 40 ml of oil and the Sichuan peppercorns in the pan and heat over low heat until the peppercorns smell fragrant and bubble lightly. Take the pan off the heat, then add the green onion sections and 15 g of ginger slices in two rounds. If the aroma is not strong enough, put it back on low heat and cook until the edges of the green onion are light yellow, then strain out the green onion, ginger and peppercorns.
3. Set 5 ml aside from the infused oil and pour the rest back into the pan. Over medium heat, lay the prawns in a single layer. Fry until the underside changes colour, then flip. When the shells on both sides are red and glossy, lightly press the heads with a spatula and stop when a little red juice and oil seeps out.
4. Add the 5 g of ginger bits and, when fragrant, drizzle 30 g of rice wine along the edge of the pan. Add the water, rock sugar and part of the salt, with the water reaching about half the height of the prawns, up to 300 ml at most from the ingredients. Bring to a boil, then turn to low heat and cover.
5. After braising for about 10 minutes, uncover and look at the biggest prawn: when the flesh on its back is pearly white, opaque and springy, and the joint between head and body is cooked too, lift the prawns out and arrange them on a plate. If it is still translucent, simmer a little longer.
6. Strain the sauce back into the pan and reduce it on its own to about a quarter of the original, until it thinly coats the back of a spoon. Taste, then decide whether to add the rest of the salt, stir in the reserved infused oil, and pour it evenly over the prawns while hot.

## Key points

- **Infused oil bitter and prawns taste bitter too**: the green onion and peppercorns burned black and the burnt taste is already in the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Only cook them to light yellow; if the oil has burned, start over, because lifting out the solids will not remove the bitterness.
- **Prawn shells curled but the flesh is still translucent**: curling shells only mean the shell has shrunk from heat, and prawns of different sizes heat at different speeds (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). You can uncover while braising and check the biggest prawn.
- **Sauce is thick but the prawns turned hard**: the prawns were reduced along with the sauce for too long, and the [protein contracted](/principles/protein-coagulation/) too far. Take the cooked prawns out of the pan first, reduce the sauce on its own, then pour it back over.
- **Too salty after reducing**: the water evaporated and the salt stayed in the pan (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add the salt in two rounds, and taste the reduced thick sauce before adding more.
