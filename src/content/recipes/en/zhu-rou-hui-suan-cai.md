Skin-on pork belly stewed slowly with Northeastern pickled cabbage gives soft, tender skin and meat, and a broth with a sour fragrance. The meat is cooked as soon as it changes colour, but the skin and connective tissue keep softening in moist heat (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)), so judge by whether the thickest piece can be pierced through, not by the clock alone.

The sourness and saltiness of pickled cabbage come from curing and fermentation (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Brands differ in how salty and sour they are, so taste a little first and then decide how many times to rinse it. Reducing concentrates the salt, so it is easier to control if you save the salt until the end than if you add it all at the start.

## Steps

1. Cut the large green onion into sections. Divide the ginger in two: slice one part and mince the other. Mince the garlic. Shred the pickled cabbage and taste a little to judge how salty and sour it is. Rinse in clear water and squeeze dry. If it tastes very strong, rinse again; a milder one only needs a quick wash.
2. Put the pork belly whole in a pot, cover with cold water, and add the green onion sections, 50 g of sliced ginger and the Shaoxing wine. Bring to a boil over high heat and cook for 5 more minutes, then lift out, rinse off the foam, drain and pat the surface dry.
3. Let the pork belly cool slightly and cut it into pieces of similar size. Wash and dry the wok, add 30 ml of oil, and stir-fry the minced ginger and garlic over medium heat until fragrant. Add the pork and fry, turning, until the surface turns golden and the water at the bottom of the pan has cooked off.
4. Add the light soy sauce and a little five-spice powder and stir-fry for about 1–2 minutes, until the seasoning coats the pork evenly. Add the shredded pickled cabbage and stir-fry for about 3 minutes, so the squeezed and rinsed cabbage is thoroughly mixed with the pork fat.
5. Pour in about 1500 ml of water, enough to just cover the ingredients, and add the star anise. Bring to a boil, cover and turn to low heat. Keep it gently bubbling and stew for about 1.5–2 hours. Check the water level along the way, and add hot water if large areas of the ingredients are showing.
6. Poke the thickest piece of pork with a chopstick. When the skin and meat are pierced easily and a piece pulled up pulls apart along the grain, uncover and raise the heat slightly to reduce the sauce. Nudge the bottom of the pot gently as you turn things. When only a layer is left at the bottom and you can still spoon up broth, turn to low heat.
7. Remove the star anise. Taste the broth first, then add salt and any remaining five-spice powder in portions, stirring and tasting each time. There is no need to add the full 10 g of salt: turn off the heat once the taste is right, and serve the meat, pickled cabbage and broth together.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but tough, skin hard to bite**: the collagen has not turned into gelatin enough (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Keep stewing on low heat with liquid in the pot until even the thickest part is soft.
- **Suddenly too salty after reducing**: the pickled cabbage, light soy sauce and salt add up, and reducing concentrates them (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Judge the rinsing by taste, and add extra salt only after reducing.
- **Weak sourness, only spice flavour left**: the cabbage was rinsed too much, or too much five-spice powder went in at once (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Wash quickly and taste first to keep the sour fragrance, and start the powder with a small amount.
