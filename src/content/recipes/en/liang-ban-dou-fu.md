Cold tofu salad is a light, refreshing cold dish: tofu cut into pieces that are easy to pick up, tossed with green onion, garlic, light soy sauce and toasted sesame oil. Firm tofu holds together better than soft tofu, so it does not break up easily when tossed. Tofu is already a gel set from soy milk proteins (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), so choosing the right firmness at the start is easier than fixing a salad of crumbled tofu afterwards.

A short [blanch](/principles/blanching/) heats the tofu through and lightens its beany taste. Draining afterwards keeps the dressing from being diluted by water on the surface of the tofu. The green onion and raw garlic go in last to keep their fresh pungency; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The vinegar, sugar and chili oil are optional; choose them to suit the sourness, sweetness and heat you want.

## Steps

1. Open the tofu package, pour off the packing water and cut the tofu into cubes of about 2 cm. Gently separate the pieces along the cuts and set aside the crumbs from the edges, so they don't cloud the cooking water in the pot.
2. Bring 500 ml drinking water to a boil in a pot and gently slide in the tofu. If the pot is wide, add water until the tofu is submerged. Turn to a gentle roll and cook for about 1–2 minutes, until every piece is heated through.
3. Lift the tofu out with a slotted spoon into a clean strainer to drain. Spread it out until it is no longer hot to the touch and no water drips from the bottom, then move it to a wide bowl. Don't press it, and don't rinse it with cold tap water.
4. Rinse the small green onion, drain and cut into fine rings. Peel the garlic, smash and mince it.
5. Put the light soy sauce, toasted sesame oil and garlic in a small bowl. If you are using vinegar and sugar, add them too and stir until the sugar dissolves. The finished dressing should taste salty, savoury and garlicky; it's fine if the oil and water separate, so stir it again before pouring.
6. Pour the dressing over the tofu and scatter on the green onion; add chili oil if you like heat. With a spoon, lift a little tofu from the bottom of the bowl and fold it gently over so the upper and lower layers all pick up dressing.
7. Taste a piece with some dressing. The flavour should sit on the surface, with the beany taste of soy still inside. It can be eaten right away. If it has to wait, cover and refrigerate it, fold gently once before eating, and don't let the tofu sit in pooled dressing.

## Key points

- **The tofu is tossed into crumbs**: the tofu is too soft, or poking and pressing while tossing broke up the gel (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose firm tofu and fold with a spoon lifting from below. To keep whole cubes you can also pour the dressing over without tossing.
- **Bland, with lots of liquid in the bottom of the bowl**: the water on the surface of the tofu was not drained (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)); drain it after the short cook. Pour off the pooled water before adding the dressing, and don't add more light soy sauce to make up for the flavour.
- **Only soy sauce taste, no green onion or garlic fragrance**: the green onion and garlic sat too long, or were cooked with the tofu, so their fresh aroma faded (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Cut them fresh and toss them in once the tofu is no longer hot.
