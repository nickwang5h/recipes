*Đậu hũ nhồi thịt sốt cà chua* puts a meat filling inside tofu and serves it with a fresh tomato sauce. The tofu is opened from the side like a small book, with only a thin layer of filling inside. After frying, it is golden outside and still soft inside. The [umami](/principles/umami/) of tomato, fish sauce and pork comes together, so a little sauce is enough to go with rice.

The egg and meat in the filling [coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) with heat and hold the shiitake and green onion together. The tofu is blotted of surface water first, then fried over medium heat so it forms a firm, thin crust (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The tomato sauce is cooked separately in the same pan and spooned beside the tofu, so the difference between the fried crust and the tender centre stays.

## Steps

1. Soak the shiitake in 300 ml of warm water for 30 minutes, until the centre of the cap is soft, then squeeze dry, remove the stems and chop. At the same time, drain the tofu and let it rest between paper towels for 10 minutes, until there is no film of water on the surface. Don't weigh it down.
2. Cut a cross in the bottom of each tomato. Bring 1000 ml of water to a boil, which takes about 5–7 minutes, put in the tomatoes and blanch for 0.5–1 minutes, until the skin curls back at the cuts. Lift out, rinse in cold water, peel and seed, then weigh out 300 g and chop finely.
3. Mix the ground pork, green onion, shiitake, egg, the fish sauce for the filling, pepper and the cornstarch slurry, and stir for about 1 minute, until the filling holds together with no separate egg. Cut the tofu into slices about 5 cm square and 1.5 cm thick, about 10 slices. Slit each slice crosswise from the side, leaving about 1 cm joined at the far end.
4. Hold the tofu, lift the top half gently, divide the filling among the slices and spread it into a thin layer about 0.5 cm thick, then close and press lightly. Put the tofu back on paper towels to absorb water for 2 minutes, until there is no obvious wet mark underneath.
5. Put 4 tbsp of oil in a wide skillet and preheat over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the oil flows freely but isn't smoking. Fry the tofu in 2 batches, 4–6 minutes per side, until both sides are golden and the centre of the filling reaches 74°C (165°F). Use two spatulas to support the tofu when flipping, and move the fried pieces to a warm plate.
6. Leave 2 tbsp of oil in the pan and pour off the rest. Stir-fry the garlic over medium-low heat for 15 seconds, until fragrant. Add the tomatoes, 60 ml of water and the fish sauce for the sauce, and cook for about 5–7 minutes, until the tomatoes break down and the sauce thickens a little, and a line drawn across the bottom of the pan closes up quickly. Add the sugar, taste, add salt if needed, stir well and turn off the heat. Spoon the sauce onto the plate and set the tofu on it.

## Key points

- **Tofu falls apart as soon as it is slit**: the texture is too soft, or the cut went through to the back. Choose boxed tofu that can be cut into slices, keep one side joined and spread the filling thin. The egg and meat filling [sets and holds its shape](/principles/protein-coagulation/) only once heated.
- **Crust sticks to the pan and breaks when flipped**: the surface is still wet, or the thin crust hasn't formed (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Blot dry again before it goes in the pan, and flip only when the underside is golden and a spatula can lift the whole piece.
- **For lunch boxes, the sauce makes the tofu soggy**: before packing, reduce the tomato sauce for another 1–2 minutes, until it coats the back of a spoon. Pack the tofu and sauce in separate compartments and refrigerate soon after packing. The next day, reheat with a lid that lets steam out, until the centre of the tofu and filling reaches 74°C (165°F). Don't heat for long, which tightens the protein and makes the tofu firm (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
