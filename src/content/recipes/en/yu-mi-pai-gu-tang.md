Pork rib and corn soup has the savoury flavour of bone-in pork and the sweetness of corn and carrot. The ribs are blanched to remove scum, then browned until lightly golden, which adds a layer of pan-seared meat flavour to the soup. Then a slow simmer lets the [collagen in the connective tissue slowly turn into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), so the meat pulls off the bone and the soup gets a little body.

Corn holds up to long cooking, so it goes in first. Carrot goes in later, because if it goes in while the ribs are still hard, it will cook apart first. The vinegar leaves a little tang; how tender the meat gets depends mainly on how long you simmer. At the end the meat is soft, the vegetables are in chunks and the soup can be served as it is.

## Steps

1. Cut the corn into short sections and the carrot into rolling chunks. Cut the leek into sections and smack them lightly, cut the ginger into thick slices and chop the green onion. Clear any loose bone chips from the cut edges of the ribs. Keep the pieces about the same size so they all soften together.
2. Put the ribs in cold water, with enough water to cover them, and add the leek, half the ginger and the Shaoxing wine. Bring to a boil over high heat. When grey-brown scum gathers on top, skim it off, lift out the ribs, rinse off any dirt on the surface and drain. Throw away the blanching water, leek and ginger.
3. Add the vegetable oil to the pot, then the remaining ginger slices and the ribs, and brown over medium heat until lightly golden on the surface. Add the vinegar, if using, and stir-fry for about 1 minute. Then slowly pour in 1000 ml of boiling water; hot oil spatters easily, so pour carefully. The liquid should nearly cover the ribs; if the pot is too wide, add more boiling water.
4. Add the corn and black pepper, bring to a boil, then turn the heat to low, cover and simmer for about 20 minutes. Keep the surface bubbling gently with the ribs under the liquid. If the water has clearly dropped, top up with a little boiling water.
5. Add the carrot and simmer for about 40 minutes more. Test the thickest rib with a chopstick: the meat should pull apart easily and the connective tissue should be soft, and a chopstick should pass through the carrot while it still holds its shape. If the meat is still tough, keep simmering on low heat; do not go by time alone.
6. About 3 minutes before serving, add the salt in portions, stir and taste a spoonful of the soup. Sprinkle with the chopped green onion, turn off the heat and ladle out the soup, so it does not keep reducing and getting stronger.

## Key points

- **A lot of scum on the soup**: the coagulated residue on the ribs was not rinsed off after [blanching](/principles/blanching/). Rinse them clean after lifting them out, and skim any new scum when you start the real simmer.
- **Cooked but too tough to bite**: [collagen needs time](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) to soften. Keep simmering in moist heat; adding vinegar is no substitute.
- **The soup gets saltier as you drink it**: the water evaporated and the [salt](/principles/salt/) stayed in the pot. Add the salt last; if the soup is too salty, dilute with boiling water and taste again.
