Bang bang chicken has soft, fine shreds of chicken, dressed with a "strange-flavour sauce" that has numbing, hot, sweet, salty and sour notes and the fragrance of sesame; it is a Sichuan cold chicken dish. The chicken is cooked first, then pounded loose with a wooden stick and torn into fine shreds. It uses chicken breast, with sesame paste giving the sauce body and chili oil floating on top, glossy.

After pounding, the chicken tears easily into fine shreds, and the greater surface area helps the sauce cling; see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/). How tender the meat is, though, is settled during the poaching; see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/): meat that has been cooked dry cannot get its juices back, however much you pound it. The sesame paste has to be loosened smooth first, and then the chili oil mixed in, so the water and oil are distributed evenly and the sauce is not half lumps of paste and half oil; see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).

## Steps

1. Put the chicken breasts, ginger slices and the white parts of the green onion in a small pot and add 1000 ml of cold water. Heat over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until bubbles form at the edge and the water has just begun to boil, then turn to the lowest heat at once; do not let the water boil hard.
2. Cover and poach gently for 12 minutes, with bubbles only occasionally at the edge; turn off the heat and leave covered for another 5 minutes. Measure the thickest part of both breasts, and lift them out only when both centres reach 74°C (165°F) and the meat has set. If the temperature is not enough, poach 2 more minutes over low heat and measure again. Set aside 3 tbsp of the poaching liquid.
3. Put the chicken breasts in the prepared ice water and cool for 5–8 minutes, until they feel cold and the centre is no longer warm. Lift them out and blot the surface dry with paper towels; at the same time, cool the reserved poaching liquid in a shallow dish for 5–8 minutes, until it is no longer hot to the touch.
4. Stir together the oil floating on top of the sesame paste jar and the paste at the bottom, then measure out 1.5 tbsp. Add 3 tbsp of the cooled poaching liquid in portions, stirring until smooth and able to run off the spoon; then mix in the light soy sauce, vinegar and sugar, stirring until no sugar granules are visible.
5. Stir in the chili oil, ground Sichuan peppercorns, grated garlic and grated ginger until there are no lumps of sesame paste. Taste, and adjust with salt; the sauce should taste of sesame, with sweet, sour, salty, numbing and hot notes all coming through.
6. Gently pound the chicken breasts with a clean rolling pin until the muscle bundles loosen, then tear along the grain into long shreds about 3–5 mm wide. Pounding until loosened is enough; do not press the meat to a mush. Cut the cucumber into thin shreds and mince the green parts of the green onion.
7. Spread the cucumber shreds on the bottom of a plate, pile the chicken shreds loosely on top, pour over the strange-flavour sauce and sprinkle with the green onion. Toss gently before eating so the sauce seeps between the shreds and every strand is coated in sesame sauce.

## Key points

- **The shreds are dry, hard and like cotton wool**: it was cooked too long, and the protein in the meat contracted and lost water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Keep the water at a gentle poach and cool it as soon as it is cooked; pounding only helps to separate the shreds.
- **The sauce clumps into lumps of paste, with oil all around**: the sesame paste was not stirred smooth first, and the liquid was not mixed in a little at a time (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Stir the oil and paste in the jar together first, loosen with the poaching liquid, and mix in the chili oil last.
- **The chicken is bland and the sauce all runs to the bottom of the plate**: the shreds are too thick, or they carry a lot of water after cooling (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Blot the surface dry first, then pound and tear fine, and toss the sauce and shreds together before serving.
