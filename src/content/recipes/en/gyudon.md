*Gyūdon* is a sweet-salty rice bowl of thin beef and onion. The broth is water, soy sauce, mirin, sake and sugar; the beef gives up its flavour in the pot and is finally spooned over hot white rice together with the juices. The onion should be soft and sweet, the beef slices should still separate, and the rice at the bottom of the bowl should stay moist. Success depends first on the thickness of the meat: thin slices cook through quickly and are also easy to overcook (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

Thin slices with a little fat suit this better than a lean piece cut into thick strips. Hot pot beef slices from an Asian supermarket in Canada are easy to buy; after buying, thaw them in the refrigerator and separate the slices. If you slice it yourself, cut against the grain. Hot liquid sets the beef proteins, and long cooking makes them shrink and lose water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Simmer briefly in a shallow pot and keep the liquid moving, and you get soft onion and a moist topping together.

## Steps

1. Divide the cooked white rice between two deep bowls, fluff it gently and keep it hot. Cut the onion into slices about 5 mm thick, cut the thin beef slices to a bite-size length and separate them slice by slice.
2. Put the water, soy sauce, sake, mirin and sugar in a small pot, stir, and add the onion. Heat over medium heat until the liquid boils, the sugar is completely dissolved and the onion slices start to loosen.
3. Add the beef and use chopsticks to pull apart any slices rolled together, so the liquid reaches every slice. When the pot returns to a boil, skim off the foam, then turn to a gentle medium-low heat so the surface shows only small bubbles.
4. Keep cooking about 6–7 minutes, turning gently once or twice. When the onion is translucent and bends softly when lifted and all the beef has changed colour, check the topping: the slices should be loose and there should still be liquid at the bottom of the pot that you can spoon up. Don't keep cooking it dry to thicken it.
5. Divide the beef and onion over the hot rice, and spoon the broth over a little at a time, so the top layer of rice is moist. Taste the liquid left in the pot first; there is no need to pour all of it into the bowls, and if it is clearly too salty, add a little hot water before dividing. Serve right after assembling.

## Key points

- **Beef is tough to chew**: sliced too thick, cut with the grain, or boiled for a long time after it is cooked. Start with truly thin slices and cook them spread out, briefly. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Beef clumps together and is raw inside**: frozen thin slices went into the pot in one lump without being separated. Thaw in the refrigerator first, then pull the slices apart one by one so the hot liquid surrounds each slice. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Broth is too salty and the rice turns salty too**: high heat evaporated too much water, or the sake and mirin carry their own salt. Use unsalted sake and true mirin, keep enough liquid, and dilute with water if needed. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
