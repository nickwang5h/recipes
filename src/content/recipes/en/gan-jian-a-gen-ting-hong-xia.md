Pan-seared red shrimp should have a little toasty flavour on the shell while the meat inside stays plump. It is seasoned with white wine and olive oil. Pat the shrimp dry and put them in the pan in a single layer, so the contact surface loses water first and then undergoes the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). If water collects in the pan, it is hard to sear the shells fragrant.

Ginger, onion and cilantro stems first carry their aroma into the oil, and the minced garlic goes in later so the fine bits do not burn bitter during the whole sear (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Argentine red shrimp have a fairly red shell even when raw, so check the meat at the thickest part to judge doneness.

## Steps

1. Snip the sharp rostrum and long whiskers off the refrigerator-thawed shrimp, cut along the back and remove the vein. After washing, blot the water from the shell seams and the belly too; the finished shrimp should drip no water.
2. Slice the ginger, cut the onion into small pieces and mince the garlic, and keep the cilantro leaves and stems apart. Set aside a little olive oil for brushing the shrimp, and pour the rest into a preheated frying pan and heat over medium heat until the oil flows freely.
3. Add the ginger, onion and cilantro stems and fry for about 1 minute; lift them out when you smell the aroma and the edges of the onion are turning translucent. If the oil starts to smoke, move the pan off the heat to cool it first.
4. Turn to medium-high heat and lay the shrimp flat in one layer. Sear the first side for about 2 minutes, brushing the upward-facing shells with a layer of the reserved oil. Once the underside has light golden spots, turn them over, scatter in the minced garlic, and tilt the pan lightly so the garlic falls into the oil.
5. After searing the second side for about 1 minute, pour 20 ml of white wine in at the edge of the pan and keep cooking for about 1 minute. When the wine bubbles and the garlic aroma has spread, turn to medium-low heat and cook until the meat at the cut along the back has gone from translucent to pearly white.
6. Season with salt and black pepper and drip a total of 1 ml of light soy sauce over the shrimp. The meat of the largest shrimp should be opaque and springy; if there is still a translucent centre, heat a little longer, then scatter on the cilantro leaves and plate. Put the lemon at the edge of the plate and squeeze it over to taste when eating.

## Key points

- **Shell is not fragrant and water comes out in the pan**: the thawing liquid was not blotted off, or the shrimp were crowded, and the water slows the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Lay the shrimp flat, and sear large quantities in separate batches.
- **Minced garlic is black and bitter**: fine mince releases aroma fast and also burns easily (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add it only after turning the shrimp, and add the wine as soon as it turns light golden to bring the temperature down.
- **Shell is red but the meat is not cooked**: a red shell does not show doneness, so look at whether the meat has set (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)): pearly white and opaque at the thickest part means done; do not take them out just because four minutes have passed.
- **Shrimp meat is dry and hard, or too salty**: long searing makes the protein contract and lose water, and too much salt covers the sweetness. Take them out as soon as they are cooked, and add salt beyond the soy sauce only in small amounts.
