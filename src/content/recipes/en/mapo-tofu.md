The story goes that the dish came from a small eatery outside the north gate of Chengdu in the Qing dynasty, where the cook was a Mrs. Chen with a pockmarked face, known as "Chen Mapo" (Pockmarked Mother Chen). In Sichuan cooking it is often described with eight words: numbing, hot, scalding, fragrant, crisp, tender, fresh and lively.

## Steps

1. Cut the tofu into 2 cm cubes. Bring a pot of water with a small pinch of salt to a gentle simmer, add the tofu and soak-cook it for 2–3 minutes, keeping the water at a gentle simmer rather than a hard boil. Leave the tofu in the water until needed.
2. Mix the potato starch with the cold water into a starch slurry.
3. Heat the wok, then add the oil. Over medium heat add the ground beef, break it up, and keep stir-frying until the moisture has cooked off, the meat is browned and starts to release a little oil, about 3–4 minutes. Drizzle the Shaoxing wine down the side of the wok.
4. Turn the heat to medium-low and fry the Pixian chili bean paste for about 1 minute, until the oil is red and glossy and smells fragrant. Then add the fermented black beans, chili flakes, ginger and garlic, and stir-fry until fragrant.
5. Pour in the stock and bring it to a boil. Lift the tofu out, drain it and slide it gently into the wok. Push it forward with the **back** of the spatula rather than turning it, so every cube picks up sauce. Add the light soy sauce and simmer over low heat for 3–5 minutes to take on flavour.
6. Add the garlic shoots. Stir the starch slurry well and pour it in **in 2–3 additions**. After each one, give a few gentle pushes, and wait until the sauce returns to a boil and turns glossy before adding the next, until the sauce is thick, coats the tofu and red oil floats on top.
7. Transfer to a plate, sprinkle with the ground Sichuan peppercorns and eat while it is hot.

## Key points

- **Sauce is cloudy and tastes of raw starch**: the sauce was not boiling when the slurry went in, so the starch did not fully gelatinize. Wait for the sauce to return to a boil before each addition. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Sauce thins out and weeps after serving**: it cooked too long after thickening or was pushed too hard, which broke the swollen potato starch granules. Take the dish off the heat as soon as it is thickened.
- **Meat is not fragrant**: the chili bean paste went in before the moisture had cooked off, so the meat was boiling rather than frying and the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) could not get going.
- **Tofu breaks up**: tofu blanched in salted water has a firmer outer layer. Once it is in the wok, only push it, do not turn it.
