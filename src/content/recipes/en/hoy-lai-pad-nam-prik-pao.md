*Hoy lai pad nam prik pao* is shell-on clams stir-fried with roasted chili paste, finished with Thai sweet basil. The [Thai chili paste](/recipes/nam-prik-pao/) here is already sweet and savoury, and the clams release salty juices of their own, so cook with the paste first and taste the cooked juices before deciding whether to add fish sauce. The chili slices bring a fresh heat, and the basil leaves a clean, sweet fragrance.

The garlic goes briefly into the oil, then the paste loosens in the pan; the fresh basil goes in last (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Once the clams are in, a lid holds the steam so the whole pot heats evenly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). By the time they open, their own juices have thinned the paste into a light sauce. The finished dish should have a little sauce to spoon over rice and plump clam meat, so do not boil the sauce down after the shells open.

## Steps

1. Check the purged live clams one by one. Throw away any with broken shells, and any that are open and do not close when tapped. Scrub the shells under cold water to rinse off surface grit, then drain. Mince the garlic, slice the chilies and pick the basil leaves, and set them by the stove.
2. In a large wok or a deep stainless steel skillet, heat the oil over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until ripples appear on the surface. Add the garlic and stir-fry for 30–60 seconds, until fragrant and just turning pale gold at the edges.
3. Add the chili paste and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until it breaks up and blends evenly with the oil. Add the clams and chili slices and toss for about 30 seconds so the shells pick up the paste. Cover right away.
4. Cook over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes, holding the handle steady and shaking the pan once halfway through so the top and bottom layers swap places. Lift the lid and check: the shells should be opening one after another, a brown-red sauce should have collected at the bottom, and the clam meat should be opaque with the centre at 74°C (165°F). Throw away any clams that stay shut.
5. With the lid off, turn the clams up from the bottom so the released juices mix with the chili paste. Spoon up a little of the cooked juice and taste it. Add the 0.5 tsp fish sauce you set aside only if it tastes under-seasoned; leave it out if the saltiness is enough.
6. Add the basil leaves and toss for 15–30 seconds, until the leaves have just wilted and are still green, then take the pan off the heat and plate. Pour the thin sauce from the bottom of the pan over the dish and eat with steamed white rice while the clam meat is still plump.

## Key points

- **Clam meat shrinks to small pieces and is chewy**: it kept cooking long after the shells opened, squeezing the water out of the meat so it toughens (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Once the clams are done, toss in the basil and take the pan off the heat.
- **Sauce too salty, paste overpowers the clams**: the paste already has salt and sugar, and extra seasoning was added before the clams released their juices. Wait until the cooked juice has formed before tasting, and do not boil down this salty juice (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Basil turns black, garlic tastes burnt and bitter**: garlic fried too long turns bitter, and basil cooked too long turns black. Add the paste as soon as the garlic smells fragrant, and stir-fry the basil only briefly at the end (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
