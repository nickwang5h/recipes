Taiwanese braised pork rice cuts skin-on pork belly into thin strips and spoons them, with the braising sauce, over hot rice. The skin softens slowly in moist heat, and the gelatin it releases makes the sauce thick and silky; this stickiness comes from [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), so no starch slurry is needed. The shallots are fried first in the pork fat, and their aroma then spreads through the sauce (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The strips should keep all three layers, fat, lean and skin, and be cut thin and even so they soften at the same time. The fragrance of the shallots, the saltiness of the soy sauce and the sweetness of the rock sugar are the base flavours. The spice flavour is fairly strong, so leave out the five-spice powder if you don't like it. When the pork is done, the strips still hold their shape, the skin breaks with one bite, and there is enough sauce to toss with the rice.

## Steps

1. Cut the pork belly into thin strips about 0.7 cm thick and 2.5 cm long. If the meat is too soft, freeze it briefly until slightly firm; thaw frozen meat in the refrigerator until it can be cut. Mince the shallots and garlic separately.
2. Peel the optional hard-boiled eggs and make a few shallow cuts in the white, without cutting through to the yolk.
3. Put the oil in a pot and stir-fry the pork strips over medium heat. When the meat turns white, drizzle in 10 ml of the rice wine and keep stir-frying until the steam lessens, clear pork fat shows at the bottom of the pot and the edges of the meat are slightly golden.
4. Add the minced shallots and stir-fry for about 1 minute; when you smell the shallots, add the garlic and stir-fry for about 30 seconds more. Turn to low heat, add the crushed rock sugar and stir until it melts, then add the light soy sauce promptly so the shallots, garlic and sugar don't blacken.
5. Add the remaining rice wine, the water, white pepper, the optional five-spice powder, star anise, bay leaves and eggs. Bring to a boil, cover, turn to low heat and keep a gentle bubble, braising for about 1 hour. Turn and check every 15 minutes; if the liquid gets too low, add 100 ml of hot water.
6. Pick out one strip with skin to check: only start reducing the sauce when the skin bites through easily and the lean meat presses apart. If it is still tough, keep braising over low heat. Lift out the whole spices, uncover and raise the heat slightly, reducing until the sauce lightly coats the back of a spoon and still flows.
7. Divide the hot rice among bowls, spoon over the pork and sauce, add the braised eggs and mix well while hot.

## Key points

- **Pork is cooked but still tough**: the meat sets before the collagen softens (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Add hot water and keep braising gently; don't rush to reduce the sauce.
- **Bitter shallot flavour**: finely cut shallots and garlic scorch easily in hot oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lower the heat once they are fragrant, and add the soy sauce and water as soon as the sugar melts.
- **Sauce too salty or only oil left**: too little water was left, so the salt stayed in the pot (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add water to dilute it and stop reducing.
