Nagoya-style tebasaki is fried chicken wings coated in a sweet-salty sauce, with crisp skin, juicy meat and a distinct finish of pepper. The wings are thinly coated in starch and put into the oil in two rounds; they are dipped in the sauce only after coming out, so the sauce clings to the surface and no liquid is left on the plate.

The double fry separates cooking the inside from drying the shell (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The starch first absorbs water on the surface and gelatinizes with heat, then gradually dries into a shell (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The thicker the starch, the harder it is to fry through the damp starch trapped inside; the sweet-salty sauce is reduced on its own so that the sugar doesn't burn before the chicken is cooked through.

## Steps

1. Put the mirin, soy sauce, sake, sugar, ginger and garlic in a small saucepan and cook over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and the edges bubble. Turn to low heat and cook for 15–20 minutes, until the sauce lightly coats a spoon and about 50–60 ml is left; take out the ginger and garlic and turn off the heat.
2. Separate the wings at the joint and press dry with paper towels, especially drying the folds of the skin. Sprinkle evenly with the potato starch, toss, then shake off the loose starch from each piece so only a thin layer stays on the surface.
3. Pour the frying oil into the deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the thermometer reads 160°C (325°F) and the oil moves but doesn't smoke. Fry the wings in 2 batches, keeping the oil at about 160°C (325°F) after each batch goes in, for 8–10 minutes, until the outer layer has set and is pale gold, and the bubbles are fewer than when they first went in.
4. Lift each batch to a wire rack and drain for 3–5 minutes, until the surface stops dripping oil. After both batches have had their first fry, heat the oil for 2–3 minutes to 180°C (350°F), then fry again in batches for 3–5 minutes each, until the skin is golden and the edges are crisp. Test the thickest piece, away from the bone; it is done at 74°C (165°F). If not, fry for 1 more minute each time.
5. With clean tongs, dip the wings in the sweet-salty sauce one by one, lifting so the excess sauce runs back into the pan. Sprinkle with white pepper and toasted white sesame seeds, spread on a plate and eat while hot.

## Key points

- **Skin is soft and greasy to eat**: too many went in at once, so the oil recovered slowly and the shell stayed damp. Fry in batches, drain spread out on a wire rack, and coat in the sauce only after the second fry (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **White powder on the shell and sticky inside**: the starch coating was too thick, and the inner layer still holds water. Blot the wings dry before coating thinly, and shake until there are no piles of starch (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Too salty after saucing and bitter on the surface**: the sauce was reduced too dry, concentrating the salt, and the sugar burns easily. Turn off the heat once it coats a spoon, and dip the wings in only a thin layer of sauce (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
