Hobak jeon has a soft, pale golden egg coating, and the zucchini inside stays tender but keeps its shape. Each round is coated in flour, dipped in egg and fried into small pieces. Choose zucchini with thin skin and seeds that have not hardened, and slice it whole with the skin on to keep the bite.

Sprinkle [salt](/principles/salt/) first so the slices release some water, blot off the extra beads of moisture, and coat thinly in flour; the egg then clings evenly. In the pan the egg forms a soft coating through [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/); keep the heat gentle so the coating stays pale by the time the zucchini is cooked through.

## Steps

1. Slice the zucchini into rounds about 8 mm thick, spread them in a single layer and sprinkle salt evenly over both sides. Let sit for 15–20 minutes, until beads of water appear on the surface and the slices soften slightly. Blot the beads off gently with paper towels, leaving the surface slightly damp. Do not rinse them or squeeze the flesh.
2. Stir the soy sauce, water and rice vinegar together and set in a small dish. Beat the egg until the white and yolk are fully mixed. Spread the flour in a shallow plate, dredge the slices on both sides one at a time, shake off the loose flour and leave only a thin layer through which the texture of the slice is still visible.
3. Add 1 tbsp of oil to a large nonstick skillet and preheat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily but is not yet smoking. Dip half the slices one at a time to coat fully in the egg, lift out and let the extra egg drip off, then put them straight into the pan in a single layer with gaps between them.
4. Fry the first side for about 1 minute, until the egg at the edge has set and the underside is pale golden, then flip and fry for 1 minute. A slice should be easy to pierce, the centre should still have a slight spring, and the egg coating should be fully cooked; measured at the centre with a thermometer, 74°C (165°F) means done. If they brown first while the centre is still firm, turn the heat to medium-low and fry 1 minute more.
5. Move the first batch to a clean plate and spread in a single layer. Add the remaining 1 tbsp of oil to the same pan and fry the second batch the same way, about 1 minute per side, until the zucchini is cooked through and the egg coating is pale golden. Serve with the dipping sauce in a separate dish.

## Key points

- **Egg coating slides off, water pools around the slices**: the beads of water from salting were not blotted off, or the slices sat too long after being floured. Flour the slices while still slightly damp, and as soon as they are floured, dip in egg and put in the pan. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Egg coating turns hard and dark**: the pan is too hot and the protein tightens too fast. Keep the heat at medium and flip once the underside is pale golden; if the oil smokes before the next batch goes in, take the pan off the heat to cool down. See [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Water pools in the lunch box**: steam was trapped under the lid, or the dipping sauce was poured over the slices ahead of time. Let the slices give off visible steam in a single layer first, then put them in a shallow container and refrigerate; cover tightly once fully cooled and pack the sauce separately. Do not leave them out at room temperature for more than 2 hours, and eat within 2–3 days of refrigeration. When reheating in the microwave the next day, turn the slices and eat only once the centre reads 74°C (165°F). See [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
