The layers of a hand-pulled pancake come from dough rolled thin and a film of oil between the layers. Brushing with oil before rolling and coiling makes it harder for the sheets to stick back together into one lump. After frying, pulling the edges loose from the outside in is what exposes the thin layers. The dough is half-scalded: boiling water first [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) part of the starch, and cold water then lets you knead the rest of the flour in evenly, so the finished pancake is both soft and chewy.

The two rests each have a purpose. Resting after mixing lets the water spread evenly and relaxes the tension in the [gluten](/principles/gluten/). Resting again after coiling makes the dough easier to roll out. In frying, the inside cooks first and then the surface steam clears; for how the bottom of the pan passes heat to the dough, see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).

## Steps

1. Put the flour in a heatproof bowl, pour in the boiling water while mixing with chopsticks until the flour forms shreds, then add the cold water and mix evenly. When it is no longer too hot to touch, knead it into a soft dough, cover it and rest for 20 minutes, keeping the surface moist.
2. Divide the dough into 3 portions and roll each into a long thin sheet. If a sheet shrinks back as you roll, cover it and wait until it stretches out easily. Keep the rest of the dough covered at all times so the surface doesn't form a skin.
3. Divide the oil for brushing the sheets evenly, brush it on in a thin even layer, then sprinkle the salt evenly. Roll each sheet from the long edge into a thin strip, then coil it from one end into a snail shape with the tail tucked underneath. Cover and rest for 10 minutes.
4. Roll gently from the middle of each coil outward into a pancake of even thickness. Apply pressure in several passes to keep the layers you rolled in. If an edge splits open and leaks oil, stop for a moment, gently press it closed and continue.
5. Brush a nonstick pan with the oil for frying, and place a pancake in over medium-low heat. Fry until the underside is golden and the pancake starts to puff, then flip. Fry the other side until golden, with no wet, sticky raw dough inside. If it colours too fast, lower the heat and cover the pan briefly so the inside cooks through.
6. Uncover to let the surface dry out again. Push gently from two opposite sides with two spatulas, then pinch the edges loose to expose the thin layers. Take it out and spread it flat. Fry the next pancake to the same state, and don't stack hot pancakes and let them steam.
7. If using fillings, beat the egg and cook it in the oiled pan as a thin, fully set omelette, then cut it into pieces. Warm the cooked ham slightly. Divide the omelette, drained lettuce, ham and cheese evenly, place them on a hot pancake and roll loosely.

## Key points

- **Won't roll out and shrinks back as you roll**: the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) has not relaxed, and forcing it tears the dough. Cover the dough during both rests, let a soft dough rest first, and don't add a lot of flour and make it stiff.
- **Fried pancake looks like a solid flatbread**: the sheet was too thick, the oil film was uneven, or the coil was pressed too hard, so the layers stuck back together. Let the oil cover the whole sheet, then roll gently; [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) only cooks the dough, and the layers still depend on shaping.
- **Burnt outside, sticky inside, and soft when out of the pan**: the heat was too high so the surface coloured first, or hot pancakes were stacked and held in steam. Following [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), lower the heat first so it cooks through, uncover at the end to let the steam out, and spread the pancake out when it leaves the pan.
