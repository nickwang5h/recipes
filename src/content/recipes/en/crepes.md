*Crêpes* are thin French pancakes, soft and eggy, usually served with sugar, jam or fruit. Milk thinned with water makes the batter easier to spread thin, and melted butter keeps the crêpes soft. The batter has no leavening, so spreading it thin is all it takes.

When you stir, the flour absorbs water and forms [gluten](/principles/gluten/). Chilling lets the water spread more evenly and makes the batter easier to spread. In the pan, [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the heat on the egg yolks set the crêpe, and some water evaporates, so brown spots appear on the surface. Chill the batter for 2 hours. It makes about 10–12 small crêpes 15 cm across.

## Steps

1. Whisk the egg yolks, sugar, milk, water and rum together. If you leave out the rum, add the extra 45 ml of water as well, so the batter doesn't turn thick.
2. Stir the liquid into the flour in several additions. First make a smooth thick paste, then add the rest of the liquid. Stir in the warm butter and strain out any lumps. The batter should flow smoothly and run off a spoon in a continuous stream.
3. Cover the batter and chill for at least 2 hours. Before cooking, stir gently, checking the bottom of the bowl in particular, and don't whisk in a lot of air bubbles again.
4. Preheat a small skillet over medium heat and brush it thinly with butter. Pour in about 40 ml of batter, then right away lift the pan, tilt it and swirl once to cover the bottom. If the edge has already set while thick batter still pools in the centre, pour in a little less next time.
5. When no runny batter is left on the surface and the edge starts to loosen, flip with a thin spatula. The first side usually takes about 1 minute and the second only a dozen or so seconds. Take it out once light brown spots appear.
6. Stack the finished crêpes and keep them covered. Cook the rest of the batter the same way, stirring gently before each one. When the pan dries out, brush on a little more butter, but don't let the butter collect in a pool.
7. While still warm, fold each crêpe into quarters and serve with a little sugar, to taste. If the first one is clearly heavy, add water to the remaining batter a tablespoon at a time, stir well and try another.

## Key points

- **Crêpes are thick, tough and hard to fold**: the batter is too thick, it wasn't rested, or it cooked too long. Finish the chilling first, then thin the batter a little at a time. Take each crêpe out as soon as it sets (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Holes when flipping**: the underside hasn't set, or the spatula supports only a small part. Wait until the edge loosens and the surface is no longer wet and shiny, then lift the whole crêpe (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Many dark spots and a bitter taste**: the pan is too hot and the butter browned first. Wipe off the burnt bits, lower the heat a little and brush on a thin layer of butter. Keep the pan temperature steady between crêpes (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
