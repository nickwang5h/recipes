Hot and sour bracken root noodles are slippery, chewy and refreshing, coated in a hot and sour dressing. The noodles are made of starch. As they cook, hot water seeps into the dry noodles and they slowly soften and cook through (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cool them after cooking and drain off the water, so the noodles do not dilute the dressing into a bowl of weak soup.

The dressing uses light soy sauce, black vinegar and sizzled chili oil in the volume ratio 3 to 2 to 2. The sourness brightens the savoury saltiness, the chili oil carries aroma onto the surface of the noodles, and a little sugar rounds off the sourness (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Soy sauce already has salt in it, so when tasting, first decide whether it is salty, sour or hot and adjust each on its own, which works better than adding salt across the board.

## Steps

1. Fill a deep pot with enough water for the noodles, leaving room for it to boil. Once it boils, add the bracken root noodles, gently separate them and keep them submerged in the hot water.
2. Cook according to the package. At about 8 minutes take out a strand and taste it: if the centre has no hard core and no raw starch taste, lift them out; if still hard, keep cooking.
3. While the noodles cook, put the light soy sauce, vinegar and chili oil in a mixing bowl and stir. Stir the optional sugar in until dissolved; if the chili oil has sediment, stir it up from the bottom of the container before measuring.
4. Taste a little of the dressing. If it is salty, add a small amount of cool boiled water to dilute it; if it is sour, add a little sugar; add salt only if it is really not salty enough.
5. Cool the cooked noodles under cold drinking-quality water, gently separate them, and pour them into a sieve right away to drain fully. Do not leave them soaking in cold water while you wait for the toppings, or the texture turns soft.
6. Put the noodles in the bowl with the dressing and lift and toss from the bottom with chopsticks until every strand is coated. Let them sit for a moment and taste a strand, to taste the salty, sour and hot flavours as they are eaten, and not only the strong sauce at the bottom of the bowl.
7. Chop the optional bird's eye chili and garlic, and sprinkle them in with the green onion; toss lightly and eat. If you cannot take the heat, skip the bird's eye chili and start tossing with less chili oil.

## Key points

- **The noodles are hard in the middle and taste of raw starch**: they were undercooked, or the noodles are thick. Give them enough hot water and time as described under [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and taste the centre, not just whether the outside is soft.
- **The dressing is too salty and heavy on soy sauce**: the salt and soy sauce add up, or the dressing is too concentrated. Dilute with a little cool boiled water (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Sharp sourness, and the heat covers every other flavour**: the strength of vinegar and chili oil varies by brand. Mix by the ratio first, then adjust a little with sugar or use less chili oil (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
- **The noodles are slippery and soft, but the dressing is weak**: after [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) they were cooked too long, or they were not drained after cooling. Lift them out as soon as they are cooked, drain right after cooling, then toss them into the dressing.
