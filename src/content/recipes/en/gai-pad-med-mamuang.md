*Gai pad med mamuang himmaphan* is Thai stir-fried chicken with cashews. Chicken cubes, onion and bell pepper are coated in a salty-sweet sauce, and dried chilies leave a hint of roasted fragrance. It is common in Chinese-Thai restaurants, and at home you simply pan-sear the chicken cubes. The roasted chili paste brings the aroma of chili, garlic and shallot into the sauce, and a little fish sauce and oyster sauce give it a savoury saltiness.

The chicken cubes are spread out in the pan first. Only once the contact side is dry and brown does the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) give the seared flavour. The cashews are toasted separately and mixed in last, so their crunch contrasts with the soft chicken. The sauce is fairly thick and the vegetables are stir-fried only briefly, so the cashews and chicken both pick up just a thin coat of sauce.

## Steps

1. Marinate the chicken: toss the chicken with 2 tsp of the light soy sauce and refrigerate for 15 minutes, until every piece carries a thin coat of marinade. While you wait, cut the onion, bell peppers, garlic and green onions and keep them in separate piles so they can go into the pan one after another.
2. Toast the cashews: set the oven to 175°C (350°F) and preheat for about 10 minutes, until the temperature is stable. Spread the cashews in a single layer on a baking sheet and bake for 10–12 minutes, stirring once at 5 minutes, until the colour is a little darker than light gold and they smell clearly nutty. Take them out and spread them to cool for 5 minutes, until the surface is dry and they snap crisply when bitten.
3. Mix the stir-fry sauce: stir together the oyster sauce, the remaining 3 tsp of light soy sauce, the fish sauce, the sugar, the roasted chili paste and the optional toasted sesame oil, until the paste has loosened and there are no obvious grains of sugar at the bottom of the bowl. With all the seasonings mixed beforehand, the sauce goes into the pan in one go later.
4. Fry the optional dried chilies: put 2 tbsp of oil and the dried chilies in the wok and stir over low heat for about 1 minute, until the chilies puff slightly and turn deep red, then lift them out at once and drain. Leave the oil in the pan. If you are not using dried chilies, just add the oil and go on to the next step.
5. Sear the chicken: turn the heat to medium-high and heat the pan for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily but is not smoking. Spread the chicken cubes in a single layer and sear for 2 minutes, until the underside is brown, then stir-fry for 3–4 minutes, until the centre of the thickest piece reaches 74°C (165°F). Take the chicken out and leave the oil in the pan.
6. Stir-fry the vegetables: take the pan off the heat for about 30 seconds, then add the garlic, set the heat to medium and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the fine bits of garlic are just turning golden. Add the onion and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the edges are slightly translucent and the centres still firm. Turn to high heat, add the bell peppers, the chicken and the stir-fry sauce, and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the sauce coats everything and the peppers are hot but still crisp.
7. Off the heat, fold in the green onions and all the cashews, toss, and plate right away. Put the fried dried chilies on top and serve with hot jasmine rice. The cashews touch only a thin film of sauce, so they still crunch clearly.

## Key points

- **Pale chicken and a puddle of water in the pan**: the meat was piled up and the surface moisture had no time to evaporate. Spread it in a single layer in a wide pan, cook in two batches if there is a lot, and brown it before turning (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Soft cashews and a dish that tastes only of sweet sauce**: the cashews went in too early and absorbed the sauce. Toast them until they are crisp inside, and add them only after the pan is off the heat (compare the aroma from toasting with [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Dry, hard chicken while the bell peppers are done**: the chicken stayed in the pan and was stir-fried again and again after it was cooked. Take the chicken out first, and return it for a short toss once the vegetables and sauce are ready (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
