*Vitello tonnato* is a cold meat dish from Piedmont: veal sliced thin and covered in a tangy tuna sauce. It follows the egg-free version recorded by Artusi: tuna in oil, anchovies, olive oil, lemon and capers, giving a loose sauce that can flow between the slices. Leave time for cooling and for one night in the refrigerator.

Veal leg is lean. Cook it gently and cool it promptly so the slices come out flat; boiling it for long makes the meat lose moisture (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The fish provides [umami](/principles/umami/), and the lemon makes the oily sauce brighter (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).

## Steps

1. Remove any hard bones left in the anchovies and cut the four anchovy fillets for the poaching into eight small pieces. Cut eight shallow slits in the surface of the veal and push the anchovy pieces into them, then tie the veal loosely with kitchen string into an even cylinder.
2. Stick the cloves into the onion. Put it in a pot with the bay leaf, celery, carrot, parsley, water and salt and bring to a boil. Add the veal, make sure the liquid covers the meat, turn to low heat and keep the surface at a slow bubble.
3. Cook for about 1–1.5 hours and measure the thickest part from the side with a probe. For a fully cooked cold slice, take it out when the centre reaches 77°C (170°F). Remove the string and put it in a clean shallow container. Once the steam has gone, refrigerate promptly, and do not leave it at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
4. Mash the drained tuna and the four anchovy fillets for the sauce into a fine purée and push through a sieve to remove coarse bits. Stir in the olive oil and lemon juice in portions until the sauce runs slowly off a spoon, then stir in the chopped capers.
5. Once the meat is completely cold, slice it against the grain about 2 mm thick. Spread a little sauce on the bottom of a narrow shallow dish, then layer slices of meat and sauce alternately so both sides of every slice are coated, and cover the top completely with sauce.
6. Cover and refrigerate overnight so the slices and sauce meld. The next day, take out the amount you want to eat and serve cold; the sauce should cling to the meat softly. From the day of cooking, eat it within 2–3 days of refrigeration.

## Key points

- **The slices are dry and the edges crumble**: the heat was too high or the meat kept cooking after it was done, so the lean meat lost moisture; hot meat is also harder to cut neatly. Cook at a gentle bubble, take the meat out when done, and cool it completely before slicing. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Lots of coarse bits in the sauce**: the tuna and anchovies were not mashed fine first, and the particles are harder to remove after the oil goes in. Sieve first, then add the oil in portions, so the sauce keeps the texture of a loose fish purée. See [umami](/principles/umami/).
- **The sauce is sharply sour and the meat still bland**: the capers brought in too much vinegar and the slices were too thick. Squeeze out the vinegar, stir in the lemon in portions, slice the meat thin and let it take on flavour slowly in the refrigerator. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
