*Peek gai tod nam pla* is a common Thai fish sauce fried chicken wing, with a thin, crisp skin and savoury meat inside. The fish sauce has a little sugar and vinegar in it, so the saltiness has a touch of sweetness. Eat the wings straight from the fryer, or with rice.

The rice flour and cornstarch are not applied as a separate coating. They are mixed directly into the marinade and form a thin batter on the surface of the wings. In the oil the starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat and the water is fried off, so the thin batter becomes a crisp shell. The oil is set at 175°C (350°F) so the pieces fry through without burning outside and staying raw inside. Fry in two batches so the oil temperature does not drop too far (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).

## Steps

1. Pat the wings dry and put them in a large bowl. Add the fish sauce, sugar, white vinegar, rice flour and cornstarch and mix until every wing is coated in a thin batter with no dry flour visible. Cover and refrigerate to marinate for at least 30 minutes.
2. Pour the frying oil into a deep pot and heat over medium-high heat to 175°C (350°F), until the oil moves freely but does not smoke. Hold the thermometer probe in the oil, away from the bottom of the pot.
3. Take half the wings and put them into the oil one at a time, without overlapping. Fry for 8–10 minutes, turning midway, until the skin is golden and crisp. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece, away from the bone: at 74°C (165°F) it is done.
4. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and drain on a rack or a plate lined with paper towels. Let the oil return to 175°C (350°F) before frying the second batch.
5. Serve hot on an open plate, leaving gaps between the wings so the steam can escape.

## Key points

- **The skin is soft and greasy**: too many went in at once and the oil temperature stayed down for a long time. Fry in two batches and add the second only when the oil is back at 175°C (350°F) (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Raw flour when you bite in**: the flour was not mixed in and formed lumps in places. Mix until no dry flour is visible before marinating, and smear any lumps out with your fingers (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **The skin is dark but the meat near the bone is not cooked**: the wing pieces differ too much in size, or the oil is too hot. Pick pieces of similar size, lower the heat once they are golden, and confirm doneness with a thermometer rather than lifting them out by colour alone.
