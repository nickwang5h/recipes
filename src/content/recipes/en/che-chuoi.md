*Chè chuối* is a common Vietnamese sweet soup of banana and coconut milk. Ripe bananas are cut into thick slices and cooked with small tapioca pearls in sweet coconut milk, then served with chopped peanuts and toasted sesame seeds. Short bananas stay springy after cooking. Regular bananas work too: choose ones with yellow skin and firm flesh, and stop cooking as soon as they are soft.

The "sago" here is made from tapioca starch, so look for Small Tapioca Pearls on the label. As the pearls take up water and heat, they slowly go from white to translucent inside, which is [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Coconut milk is itself an [emulsion](/principles/emulsification/): fat dispersed in water. Cooking gently and stirring slowly along the bottom heats the pearls evenly and keeps the coconut oil from separating. A spoonful of this soup should have soft banana and springy pearls, and the liquid should still flow.

## Steps

1. Slice the bananas. Peel fresh bananas and cut them on the diagonal into slices about 1.5 cm thick. If you use frozen short bananas, thaw them in the refrigerator for about 8 hours, until the flesh slices easily, then weigh out 300 g.
2. Heat the coconut milk. In a small heavy-bottomed pot, combine the coconut milk, sugar, tapioca pearls and a small pinch of salt. Heat over medium-low for 5–7 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and small bubbles rise steadily at the edge. Stir slowly from the bottom throughout, so the dry pearls spread out and do not pile up in one spot.
3. Soften the pearls. Cover the pot and take it off the heat for 20 minutes, until the pearls have clearly swollen and the outer ring is translucent. Leave the pot on a heatproof mat and let the heat of the coconut milk keep hydrating the pearls.
4. Cook the banana. Return the pot to low heat, add the banana slices and stir gently. Cook for about 5 minutes, until the banana is soft through but still in slices and the pearls have no chalky white core. Scrape the bottom every 1 minute so the pearls do not stick. If a hard core remains, cook 2 minutes more and taste another pearl.
5. Serve. Turn off the heat and let the pot sit for 3–5 minutes, until the big wisps of steam die down and the soup thickens slightly. Divide among 4 bowls and top each with 0.5 tsp chopped peanuts and 0.5 tsp toasted sesame seeds. Eat it warm, to get soft, springy and crunchy textures together.

## Key points

- **Pearls are clear but hard in the centre**: the outside took up water first and the centre has not fully gelatinized (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Taste a pearl to check, and keep cooking over low heat in 2-minute steps. Do not judge by the outer ring alone.
- **A layer sticks to the bottom and smells scorched**: gelatinized pearls and starch settle on the bottom (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Use a heavy-bottomed pot, keep the spoon touching the bottom as you stir, and do not scrape the scorched layer into the soup.
- **Large pools of coconut oil on top**: the coconut milk boiled for too long and the emulsion separated (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Keep it at a bare simmer with gentle stirring, and do not use high heat to reduce the soup to a thick sauce.
