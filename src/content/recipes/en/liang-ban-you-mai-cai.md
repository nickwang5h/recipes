Cold Chinese lettuce salad is crisp, tender leaves coated in rich sesame paste. First stir the sesame paste and the watery seasonings into an even dressing so the leaves take only a thin coat of paste (compare [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The dressing should be smooth and pourable. A thick lump of paste dropped onto the leaves is hard to mix in evenly.

Smashed garlic has a bright, sharp fragrance (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The leaves are thin, so wash and drain them and toss just before serving for the best crispness. Light soy sauce and oyster sauce are already salty, so the dressing needs no added salt. If the salad sits, the leaves release water and the paste thins out (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).

## Steps

1. Separate the lettuce leaf by leaf and remove the tough base and any damaged leaves. Wash the blades and stalks under running water, opening up the folded parts to wash them, not just rinsing the outside.
2. Drain the washed leaves well, shake off the droplets gently, then blot the remaining water with clean paper towels. The leaves must carry no water droplets, and the bottom of the bowl should be dry too.
3. Cut the lettuce into pieces no longer than 4 cm and split wide stalks lengthwise. Put them in a wide bowl. Short pieces are easy to toss, and the thick stalks and thin leaves taste less different.
4. Peel the garlic, smash it and chop it finely. Stir the oil floating on the sesame paste into the settled paste in the jar, then measure 10 ml into a mixing bowl.
5. Stir the vinegar and light soy sauce into the sesame paste a little at a time, mixing until even before adding the next. If the paste turns thick for a moment, keep stirring it out. Press lumps apart with the back of a spoon until the paste runs off the edge of the spoon.
6. Add the oyster sauce, sugar and garlic and stir until no sugar grains remain, then mix in the toasted sesame oil. Taste a piece of lettuce with it: you should get sesame, garlic and sour-salty flavour. Do not taste only a spoonful of thick paste and then keep adding salt.
7. Pour the dressing over the pieces and toss gently from the bottom of the bowl so each piece of leaf takes a thin coat. When the leaves are evenly glossy, plate and eat. If you prepare ahead, refrigerate the dry leaves and the dressing separately.

## Key points

- **Some leaves are all paste and others have no flavour**: the sesame paste was not loosened, and thick lumps do not spread evenly (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Press out the lumps and stir in the liquids from the recipe a little at a time before tossing.
- **Leaves go limp and there is water on the plate**: leftover water on the leaves plus water released after tossing thins the dressing (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Blot dry after washing, eat as soon as it is tossed, and do not leave the lettuce sitting in the dressing.
- **Garlic heat covers the sesame flavour**: smashed raw garlic releases fragrance quickly, and too much fills the mouth with sharpness (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Start with 2 cloves. If you are wary of the heat, toss half first and taste a leaf with dressing before deciding.
