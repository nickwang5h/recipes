*Chukadon* (*chūkadon*) is a Japanese home-style Chinese-flavoured rice bowl. A thick, glossy sauce is poured over hot rice, and a single spoonful brings pork, shrimp and several vegetables, with the napa cabbage stems and wood ear still having some bite. Pork, shrimp and vegetables are stir-fried, seasoned with chicken stock, soy sauce and oyster sauce, and finally thickened.

This bowl relies on [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) to make the sauce cling. Potato starch is dispersed in cold water first, and only thickens by taking up water once it goes into hot liquid. The vegetables should be cooked to just the right doneness before thickening. Take it off the heat as soon as the sauce turns glossy: cooked longer, it thins out and the vegetables go limp.

## Steps

1. Soak the wood ear in 300 ml of warm water for 15 minutes, until the pieces are fully open with no hard core. Discard the soaking water, rinse, cut off the hard base and tear into small pieces. While it soaks, cut the other vegetables, keeping the slices a similar thickness.
2. Toss the pork and shrimp with the sake, salt and the potato starch from the seasoning group, and refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the surface is evenly coated with a thin batter. Separately, stir the potato starch for the slurry with 20 ml of cold water.
3. Preheat a wide wok over medium-high heat for 1 minute, add 2 tsp oil, spread in the pork and shrimp and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until the pork turns white and the shrimp turn pink. Remove to a plate; they go back in later to cook through.
4. Add the remaining oil to the same wok, then the carrot, bamboo shoots, wood ear and the stems of both greens. Stir-fry for 2 minutes, until the edges of the stems are translucent. Add the leaves and shiitake and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the leaves gather and wilt.
5. Pour in the chicken stock, 80 ml of water, and the sake and sugar for the sauce. Cook for 1–2 minutes to a boil, then over medium heat for 3–4 minutes, until the carrot can be bitten through and the napa cabbage stems are tender but not mushy.
6. Return the pork and shrimp, add the soy sauce, oyster sauce, toasted sesame oil and white pepper, and cook for 1–2 minutes, until the centre of the pork reaches 71°C (160°F) and the shrimp are fully opaque and reach 74°C (165°F).
7. Stir up the starch that has settled at the bottom of the bowl. Keeping the sauce at a bare simmer, pour it in in two additions, pushing gently for 0.25 minutes after each, until the sauce is clear and thinly coats the vegetable pieces. Stop adding once it is thick enough to flow slowly.
8. Divide the hot rice between two bowls and immediately pour the meat, vegetables and sauce over it, then eat while hot. The sauce should run down between the grains of rice, and whole pieces of vegetable should still be visible at the edge of the bowl.

## Key points

- **White lumps in the sauce**: the starch settled and was not stirred up again, or was poured into one spot all at once. Stir it up again before it goes in, pour it across the surface of the liquid and push gently as you pour. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Leaves are mushy while the stems are still hard**: stems and leaves went in together, and the thick stems had no time to heat. Keep them separate when cutting, stir-fry the hard ingredients first and add the leaves after. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **The meat and shrimp are tight, but the sauce has no flavour**: the main ingredients stayed in the pan waiting for the vegetables. Take them out first and return them to the sauce to cook through; get the sauce seasoned before thickening, so nothing is heated repeatedly. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
