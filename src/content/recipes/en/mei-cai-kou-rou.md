For this dish the pork belly is arranged skin side down in a bowl, topped with the preserved greens and steamed, then turned out onto a plate. The skin and connective tissue gradually turn to gelatin in moist heat, which gives the soft, yielding texture (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). The fried skin adds aroma, but steaming is still what makes it tender.

The savoury taste of the preserved greens comes from curing (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Saltiness and dryness vary widely from one package to another, so soak and rinse out the grit first and leave the salt until the end, once you have tasted. Stir-frying the greens drives off surface moisture, so the seasoning is not diluted and takes better when steamed with the pork.

## Steps

1. Soak the greens for at least 1 hour, until the stems are soft. Rinse them in several changes of water until no grit is left at the bottom of the bowl, then squeeze dry and chop. Dry greens that are still very hard should keep soaking, treated as the package directs.
2. Scrape any leftover hair and dirt off the pork skin. Put the pork in boiling water and cook for about 20 minutes, until a chopstick goes in. Lift out and drain, and wipe the skin and sides carefully dry with paper towels.
3. Rub a thin, even coat of dark soy sauce over the pork and leave for 15 minutes, then blot the surface dry again before it goes into the oil. Add the sugar and five-spice powder later, when mixing the sauce, so they do not scorch first in the frying pot.
4. Heat the frying oil over medium heat. Using long tongs, lower the pork close to the surface of the oil, fry the skin side first, then turn to fry the sides. After about 3–5 minutes, when the skin has darkened and formed small blisters, lift out and drain. If the oil keeps smoking, take it off the heat to cool.
5. In another pan, stir-fry the garlic and bird's eye chili in 10 ml oil until fragrant, then add the greens and stir-fry until large wisps of steam stop rising. Mix in the light soy sauce, sugar, five-spice powder and the optional bouillon powder, stir evenly and turn off the heat.
6. Slice the slightly cooled pork 0.5–1 cm thick and pack it tightly into a heatproof bowl, skin side down, then spread the greens and seasoning on top. Put in a steamer that is already steaming, cover and steam for 45 minutes, then check. If the skin still resists when poked gently with a chopstick, keep steaming.
7. Take it out when the skin is soft and the slices pull apart easily. Place a rimmed plate over the bowl and, holding it steady with a dry cloth, flip it over, taking care that hot juices do not escape. Taste the juices, then decide whether to add salt.

## Key points

- **Oil spatters when frying the pork**: surface water turns to steam rapidly in hot oil (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Pork that has been boiled and rubbed with soy sauce must be wiped dry and lowered in close to the oil surface.
- **Cooked but hard to bite**: the collagen has not softened enough (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). 45 minutes is where you start checking; top up the steamer with hot water and steam until the skin is easy to pierce too.
- **Whole plate too salty**: the salt of the greens and soy sauce add up (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Soak and rinse the greens first, add salt last, and do not add extra bouillon powder without tasting.
