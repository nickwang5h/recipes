Red-braised eggplant is coated in egg batter, fried first and then braised. The shell is golden, softens after going back into the pan and meeting the liquid, and the eggplant inside is soft and juicy. The batter sets with heat and keeps losing water until it forms a shell (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).

The starch in the batter also helps thicken the braising sauce (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Tomato and onion bring sweetness and acidity, and the result is a thick sauce that clings to the eggplant. Coat thinly and fry in batches, or you get a thick layer of dough outside and eggplant that is still hard inside.

## Steps

1. Wash the vegetables. Chop the Chinese leek and smash the garlic; cut the tomato, onion and green pepper into small pieces. Cut the eggplant into slices about 2 cm thick, then into strips, then diagonally into diamond-shaped pieces, and pat the surface dry.
2. Add the starting water to the flour in portions and stir into a sticky batter; add the starch and the separate 30 g of water and stir until there is no dry flour. Beat in the egg, add a small pinch of salt, and mix until a chopstick lifted from the batter lets a thick ribbon fall and thinly coats the eggplant. Use any leftover starting water to adjust the thickness.
3. Toss the eggplant in the batter, pausing when you lift a piece so the extra batter drips back into the bowl. A thin coat on each side is enough; do not coat the thick batter that has settled at the bottom of the bowl.
4. Add the frying oil to a deep pot with plenty of room, and heat until a drop of batter in the oil rises quickly with continuous bubbles around it, and the oil is not smoking. Add the eggplant in batches without crowding, and fry until the shell is golden and a chopstick pierces the eggplant flesh, then lift out and drain.
5. Leave the frying oil somewhere safe to cool. In a separate wok, heat 5 g of oil and stir-fry the garlic and Chinese leek for about 15 seconds. Add the onion and green pepper and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, then add the tomato and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until the tomato starts to release its juice.
6. Add the fried eggplant, pour in water to about 80% of the height of the ingredients, and add 5 g of soy sauce. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and braise, pushing gently from the bottom with a spatula so the coating does not stick to the pan and the eggplant does not break up.
7. Braise until the eggplant is soft and cooked through, then uncover and reduce the sauce. When the liquid has dropped to about one fifth to one third of the height of the ingredients and the sauce clings to the eggplant, taste, add a little salt if needed, toss gently and plate.

## Key points

- **Shell is dark brown while the eggplant centre is still hard**: the oil was too hot or the pieces were uneven in thickness (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Lower the heat, fry in batches, and do not wait for the oil to smoke before adding the pieces.
- **Coating thick and gummy, sauce sticks to the pan**: the batter was too thick, and once in the liquid the starch absorbs a lot of water and thickens (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Let the extra batter drip off, and push gently along the bottom of the pan while braising.
- **Every bite is only salty**: both the batter and the sauce have salt, and reducing concentrates it (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add only a small pinch to the batter, and add more to the sauce only once it has finished reducing.
