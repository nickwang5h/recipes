In stir-fried chives and eggs the eggs are tender and the chives are just soft, with their aroma intact. The two are cooked separately: the egg first sets into soft large curds and comes out of the pan, then the chives are stir-fried until just softened, and finally the two are folded together. The [protein network](/principles/protein-coagulation/) of an egg holds water when it sets, but in a hot pan it keeps tightening, so the eggs come out first and their tenderness doesn't have to wait for the chives.

Cut chives release their aroma easily, and long cooking drives it off (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Drain them well after washing: a handful of wet chives in the pan won't stir-fry, and the egg curds end up sitting in the liquid when they return. Have the beaten eggs, chives and salt at hand before you start so the cooking runs in one go.

## Steps

1. Prepare the chives. Wash away the grit from the leaves and the roots, remove any tough or yellowed parts, drain and shake off the water. Cut into pieces about 3 cm long. Keep the thicker root ends separate from the tender leaves so the leaves don't go too soft waiting for the roots.
2. Beat the eggs. Crack the two eggs into a bowl and stir with chopsticks until the yolk and white are blended, picking out any obvious strands of white. Beat until even; you don't need a thick layer of foam.
3. Cook the eggs. Add 10 ml of oil to the pan and heat over medium heat, then pour in the eggs. When the edges begin to set, push the set part toward the middle with a spatula so the liquid egg reaches the bottom of the pan. As soon as the curds are all set but the surface is still moist and shiny, take them out.
4. Stir-fry the chives. Add 5 ml of oil to the same pan and turn the heat to high. Add the thicker root ends first, then the tender leaves right after, and toss quickly for about 30 seconds. The leaves should be bright, slightly shrunken, and the roots should bend; there should be no pool of liquid in the pan.
5. Combine and season. Return the eggs to the pan, sprinkle in half the salt and fold gently from the bottom so the chives are spread evenly between the curds. Once the curds are hot again and there is no liquid egg, taste a small piece and add the rest of the salt if needed.
6. Serve. Turn off the heat and move the dish to a plate at once, spreading it out a little to let the steam escape; don't leave it in the hot pan. The curds should lift whole yet stay soft, and the chives should still keep the shape of green leaves.

## Key points

- **Eggs dry and brown at the edges**: they were cooked too long the first time and heated again on their return. Following [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/), take them out as soon as they set, and only fold briefly at the end.
- **A pool of water on the plate**: the chives were not drained, or too many went into the pan at once, so the heat went into boiling water first (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain thoroughly, and for a large amount stir-fry in batches and combine afterwards.
- **Chives limp and the aroma faded**: continued heat takes the crispness out of the leaves and drives off the volatile aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Stir-fry the chives for only about 30 seconds, and once they wilt, add the eggs back and serve.
