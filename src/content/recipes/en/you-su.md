*Youso* is a paste of flour and oil. It is spread on the dough sheet, rolled up and rolled out again, so the layers do not stick into one block. The oil coats part of the flour, so neighbouring layers of dough cannot join into a continuous [gluten network](/principles/gluten/). After griddling, the dough peels apart layer by layer along these weak points.

This is the thin roux used for home griddled flatbread; four flatbreads share 40 ml of oil, about 30 g of flour and 2 g of salt. Hot oil loosens the flour, but the pot and bowl also keep passing on heat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), so do not heat the oil until it smokes before pouring. You want a paste that is even, can flow, and also stays on the dough sheet; let it cool before spreading it on the dough.

## Steps

1. Weigh 30 g of all-purpose flour and 2 g of salt into a dry, deep heatproof bowl and mix them first. Keep chopsticks or a heatproof spatula at hand; stir as soon as the oil goes in, so no dry flour is left at the bottom of the bowl.
2. Pour 40 ml of vegetable oil into a small pot and heat over low heat. Take it off the heat when the oil surface begins to move slightly and the oil is still clear. Do not wait for smoke, and do not leave the pot heating unattended.
3. First spoon a little hot oil over the flour as a test: the flour should wet easily without browning at once or giving off a sharp burnt smell. Then pour the rest of the oil in rounds along the inside of the bowl, lifting the flour from the bottom as you stir.
4. Stir until every bit of flour is wet with oil, press apart small clumps with the chopsticks and scrape the bottom and corners of the bowl. The paste should be an even colour with no dry clumps, flow slowly downward and leave a thin film on the side of the bowl.
5. Let it come to room temperature and stir again. Divide it evenly among the four flatbreads, spread it thinly with a brush or the back of a spoon, leaving a ring at the edge for sealing, then fold, roll out and griddle as in [Chinese Griddle Flatbread](/recipes/lao-bing/). The roux is not eaten raw on its own; it cooks when the flatbread is griddled.
6. To make more or less, work from 10 ml of oil, about 8 g of flour and 0.5 g of salt per flatbread. Measure by the number of flatbreads first; the oil in the griddle pan is not counted in the layers. If the flatbreads are of different sizes, the thickness of the spread should change accordingly.

## Key points

- **Dry flour lumps and it will not spread**: after pouring the oil, the powder at the bottom of the bowl was not lifted, so the outside is wet but the inside of the lump is still dry. Add the oil in rounds and stir as you add, and press apart small lumps first; do not keep pouring in more oil to flush it, because the ratio will go off.
- **Dark colour with a burnt bitter taste**: the oil was too hot, so the flour overheated in spots as soon as it touched it (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Take the hot oil off the heat early, test with a small amount first, and keep the flour pale.
- **Layers of the griddled flatbread stick together**: the roux was not spread evenly, so the dough layers stuck directly together once folded (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Spread a thin film over the whole inner sheet, leave a bare edge for sealing, and do not press through the layers when rolling.
