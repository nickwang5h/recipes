Steamed noodles with pork and celery are soft and chewy, each strand separate and coated in the meat and vegetable sauce, with no pool of soup in the pot. The method is to steam thin fresh noodles hot, toss them with the sauce so they take it in, and steam them a second time. The two steamings have different jobs: the first heats the noodles and sets them, tossing with the sauce gives them water and flavour, and the second keeps pushing [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) along.

The noodles must be thin and loose. Steam can get around the strands and condense on their surface, so heat is spread evenly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). For the sauce, the pork belly is stir-fried first to release its fat, the green onion, ginger, garlic and the optional dried spices are fried fragrant in that fat, and then water is added to simmer (see [spice aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Remove the skin from the pork belly and cut into slices about 2 cm wide, 6 cm long and 0.5 cm thick. Rinse the celery stalks, split the thicker ones lengthwise, then cut into 2 cm pieces. Slice the leek thinly, shred the ginger, chop the garlic and cut the bell peppers into pieces.
2. Preheat the wok over medium heat and add the oil for the sauce. Add the pork slices and stir-fry, then add the leek, ginger, garlic and the optional Sichuan peppercorns and dried chilies. Keep stir-frying for about 1 minute, until the surface of the meat changes colour and the aroma comes up. Do not let the pan smoke continuously.
3. Add the Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce and dark soy sauce and stir-fry for about 1 minute, then pour in 500 ml of hot water. Bring to a boil, cover and simmer for about 3 minutes.
4. Add the celery, the optional bell peppers and the five-spice powder, cover and simmer for about 3 minutes more, until the vegetables are slightly soft and there is still sauce in the pan. Taste the liquid, add salt if needed, turn off the heat and set aside.
5. Add the starting water to the steamer and line it with a damp steaming cloth. Shake the fresh noodles loose and spread them loosely. Once the water boils and steams, steam for 15 minutes, then take them out and loosen them with chopsticks. Let them cool a little until they can be tossed; do not touch the hot noodles directly with your hands.
6. Add the noodles to the pan of sauce in portions and toss gently with chopsticks and a spatula, so the sauce coats each strand evenly. Toss until all the noodles have taken on colour and the surface is moist without sitting in liquid; leave any extra sauce in the pan.
7. Top up the steamer with water and bring it back to a boil. Spread the tossed noodles and vegetables loosely back on the steamer rack and, once it steams, steam a second time for 10 minutes, until there is no raw-dough taste and the noodles are soft and chewy. Turn off the heat, loosen the noodles and plate.

## Key points

- **Noodles clump and are raw in the middle**: the layer of noodles is packed too tight for steam to get through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Shake them loose before both steamings, and steam in batches if the pot is small.
- **Noodles are hard and unevenly coloured**: the sauce did not reach every strand, so there was not enough water for [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Toss in the sauce in portions and turn until every strand is moist.
- **Noodles are soggy or too salty**: all the extra sauce went into the noodles, or too much [salt and soy sauce](/principles/salt/) stacked up. Stop once the noodles have taken enough sauce, add salt in small amounts at first, and keep the remaining sauce aside.
