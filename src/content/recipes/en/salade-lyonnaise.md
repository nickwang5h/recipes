*Salade lyonnaise* is a classic salad from the bistros of Lyon: curly endive, fried bacon pieces, crisp bread and a poached egg. The endive is slightly bitter and its leaves are sturdy enough to hold up under warm fat. Once you cut into the yolk, it mixes with the vinaigrette into a smooth mouthful.

A poached egg is cooked gently so the white [sets](/principles/protein-coagulation/) first and leaves a soft yolk. In the vinaigrette, mustard helps disperse the oil droplets (see [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The hot toppings go on last, so the leaves do not sit and go limp on the plate. Buy curly frisée, not the tight, pale Belgian endive.

## Steps

1. Wash the endive, spin it dry and tear it into bite-sized pieces, removing the coarse outer leaves. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Toss the baguette cubes with 2 tbsp of olive oil, spread them in one layer and bake for about 10–12 minutes, until the edges are golden and the centres are crisp too.
2. Put the bacon in a cold pan and cook slowly over medium heat to render the fat, turning until golden on all sides and crisp on the outside. Remove the bacon, measure out 15 ml of the fat and keep the rest for another use.
3. In a bowl, whisk together the garlic, Dijon mustard, red wine vinegar, salt and black pepper. Add the warm bacon fat, then gradually whisk in 45 ml of olive oil until the dressing is an even colour. If the bacon gave off less fat, make up the difference with olive oil, so the fat totals 60 ml.
4. Heat the 1000 ml of water with the white vinegar until it bubbles gently, then turn the heat down. Crack the eggs into small bowls one at a time and slide each in gently, close to the surface of the water, cooking 1–2 at a time and leaving enough space between them.
5. Poach gently for about 3 minutes, then check with a slotted spoon: the white should be fully set and white, holding up the yolk, with the centre still soft. Lift out onto clean paper towels to drain, trim off any stray strands of white, and plate promptly once drained.
6. Toss the endive lightly with the vinaigrette and divide it among four plates, then add the hot bacon and the croutons. Put one poached egg on each plate, sprinkle with black pepper and serve right away; cut into the yolk and toss it through the leaves as you eat.

## Key points

- **Egg white spreads into strands**: the egg is not fresh enough, or the water boiled too hard. Use fresh eggs, slide them in from a small bowl close to the water, and keep the water at a bare simmer (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Salad is greasy and there is water in the bottom of the plate**: the leaves were not spun dry, the poached eggs were not drained, or the vinaigrette had separated. Drain first, and whisk the dressing again before tossing the leaves (see [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).
- **Bacon is soft and bread is not crisp**: the pan or tray is crowded and the steam cannot escape. Spread them out to cook, and add the hot toppings just before serving (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Safety of runny eggs**: the yolk of a classic poached egg is not fully cooked. For pregnant women, older adults and young children, cook the eggs hard, to 74°C (165°F) at the centre; to keep a runny yolk, use pasteurized eggs in the shell (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
