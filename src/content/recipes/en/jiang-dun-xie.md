Stewed crab has tender, salty and savoury meat, and the sauce on the plate is thick, for dipping the crab in. The crab is laid on a fried bean sauce and steamed, so all of its juices stay on the plate. Homemade doubanjiang from Pinghu is strongly salty and savoury, and that taste comes from the flavour compounds in [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/). Here a mild yellow soybean paste stands in for it, and adding rock sugar and ketchup makes the dish sweeter; salty or sweet is a matter of taste.

The crab pieces go cut side down, so their juices meet the bean paste on the bottom of the plate. Steam condenses on the shell and the plate and carries heat inward to the thick meat; see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/). There is no need to pour a lot of water onto the plate, because the sauce loosens in the juices the crab gives off as it steams.

## Steps

1. Scrub the prepared crab clean, remove the inedible parts such as the gills, stomach and intestines, and clear out bits of shell. Cut river crabs in half; cut larger swimming crabs further into pieces that are easy to arrange. Cut the ginger into mince and slices, cut the green onion into pieces, and keep a little for chopped garnish.
2. Heat the canola oil in a pot over medium-low heat and fry the minced ginger and yellow soybean paste, pushing the paste apart as you stir. For the sweet version, add the rock sugar and fry until it dissolves, then stir in the ketchup; leave out both for the salty version. When the paste begins to give off fine bubbles and smells of soybean paste, turn to low heat.
3. Stir in the dark soy sauce and Shaoxing wine, bring briefly to a boil and take off the heat. The paste is already salty, so use little dark soy sauce at first and stop when the colour is deep enough. Taste a bit of the fried sauce for saltiness, then spread the sauce on the bottom of a shallow heatproof plate, keeping some back to spoon over the crab.
4. If using ground pork, spread it thinly over the sauce, not in a thick lump; arrange the crab pieces neatly cut side down, spoon the reserved sauce over them, and add the green onion pieces and ginger slices. If using the egg, crack it into a gap at the edge of the plate and spread it out so the crab shell does not cover it entirely.
5. Bring water to a boil in the steamer and put in the crab plate, leaving space around the edge of the plate for steam to pass. Cover, keep the water boiling and steam for about 10–12 minutes, until the thick meat of the largest crab piece is completely opaque. If the larger pieces are not done, keep steaming; do not go by the shell turning red alone.
6. If you added egg or pork, check the centre of that part separately: the egg must be fully set and the pork must have no raw, soft spots. Measure the centre with a thermometer; it is cooked at 71°C (160°F). When done, take out the plate, sprinkle with the chopped green onion, and serve the sauce on the plate together with the crab.

## Key points

- **The sauce burns black and tastes bitter**: the bean paste and sugar were heated too long on the bottom of the pot (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Fry over medium-low heat while breaking the paste up, and move on to the next ingredient once it forms fine bubbles and smells fragrant.
- **Thick crab pieces or the ground pork on the plate are undercooked**: the plate was too full or the pork too thick, so heat reaches the centre more slowly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a shallow plate with the pieces spaced out, spread the pork thinly, and check each for doneness separately.
- **Crab meat is dry and the egg turns hard**: it kept steaming after it was done, and the [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) contracted and lost moisture. Take out the plate as soon as the thick parts are cooked through.
