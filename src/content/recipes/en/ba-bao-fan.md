Eight-treasure sweet rice pudding, steamed and turned out, is a dome: the glutinous rice is moist and sticky, with a layer of red bean paste hidden in the middle, and jujubes, lotus seeds and nuts arranged on the outside. This is the lard and bean paste version common in Jiangnan. The fruits and nuts differ from family to family; anything that needs soaking or cooking is handled separately first and then packed into the bowl.

The glutinous rice first absorbs water, then steam heat completes [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The first steaming cooks the rice, and the second joins the rice, bean paste and fruit into one piece. Sugar and lard are mixed in while hot, so they blend easily and the rice also clings to the bowl more readily. When pressing, keep the grains whole, so the outline is clear after turning out.

## Steps

1. Soak the rice and lotus seeds. Wash the glutinous rice and soak it in 1200 ml of cold water; soak the lotus seeds separately in 300 ml of cold water. Cover both and refrigerate for 12 hours, until a grain of rice can be pinched in two and the lotus seeds have clearly swollen, then drain each.
2. Prepare the fruits and nuts. Soak the jujubes in 100 ml of warm water overnight and the longan in 100 ml of warm water for 20 minutes, until the flesh is soft; drain, and slice the jujubes. Put the lotus seeds in 500 ml of water and simmer over low heat for 20–30 minutes, until they can be crushed with the back of a spoon, then drain. Halve the chestnuts and dice the candied winter melon.
3. Steam the rice the first time. Add 2000 ml of water to the steamer and bring to a boil over high heat for about 10 minutes, until steam is rising steadily. Spread the drained glutinous rice on a damp steaming cloth about 2 cm thick and steam over medium heat for 30 minutes; at the 15-minute mark, fluff it and sprinkle evenly with 60 ml of hot water. Take it out when the grains have swollen and there is no hard core when you bite one open.
4. Mix the sweet rice. While it is hot, add 25 g of lard and 15 g of sugar and fold gently for 1–2 minutes, until the sugar has dissolved and the grains are evenly glossy. Do not press hard; keep the grains whole, and stop when there is no free fat visible in the bowl.
5. Fill the bowl. Grease a heatproof bowl about 16 cm across with the remaining 5 g of lard and arrange the eight fruits and nuts on the bottom. Add about two thirds of the sticky rice, pressing lightly along the bottom and the sides and leaving a hollow in the centre; put in the bean paste, leaving a ring of rice around it, then seal it flat with the remaining rice.
6. Steam the second time. Put the bowl in the steamer once steam is rising and steam over medium heat for 30 minutes, until the rice and bean paste are hot through and the surface has softened. At the 15-minute mark, check the water; if the level is close to the bottom of the pot, add 500 ml of hot water, and keep the water from touching the bottom of the bowl.
7. Turn it out. Take it out and rest for 10 minutes, until the steam from the surface has eased and the rice is still warm. Run a thin spatula around the edge of the bowl, cover with a plate, hold the bowl and plate together and flip them, then lift the bowl straight up to reveal the whole dome of fruit, and serve warm.

## Key points

- **The centre of the rice is hard while the outside is already very sticky**: it was not soaked long enough, or the first steaming ran short of water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). After the first steaming, break open a grain; if the centre is still hard, sprinkle 30 ml of hot water and steam 10 minutes more, and pack the bowl only once it is cooked through.
- **It sticks to the bowl and the dome falls apart when turned out**: the bowl was not evenly greased, or there were big gaps between the rice. Press gently against the sides with the back of a spoon, and rest 10 minutes after steaming before unmoulding. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The rice is hot but the lotus seeds are still hard**: different ingredients differ in how fast [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) softens them. Boil the lotus seeds until they can be crushed first, use cooked chestnuts, and do not expect the second steaming to cook all the fruit.
