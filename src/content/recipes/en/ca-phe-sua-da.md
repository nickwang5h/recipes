*Cà phê sữa đá* is made from strong coffee, sweetened condensed milk and ice. The coffee is dripped through a Vietnamese metal filter, a phin, and stirred into the condensed milk while still warm, then poured over ice. The dark roast needs plenty of bitter aroma; thin coffee is swamped by the sweetness of the condensed milk and the water from the melting ice.

A phin has a perforated bottom, a press plate that sits on the grounds, and a lid, and it brews while resting on a heatproof glass. Hot water passes slowly through the grounds and carries out the coffee's soluble flavour. The filter holds little, so keep it covered while brewing to reduce heat loss; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Vietnamese dark-roast coffee can be found at Asian supermarkets, though ordinary French Roast also works; sweetened condensed milk is in the baking aisle of mainstream Canadian supermarkets.

## Steps

1. Set out two glasses. One heatproof glass catches the coffee; the other holds 100 g of ice cubes. Choose a phin of about 180 ml capacity, take off the lid and press plate, and check that the holes in the bottom are clear. Put 2 tbsp of ground coffee in the filter and shake it gently level.
2. Put the press plate back. It only needs to rest on the surface of the grounds; a screw-type plate should be turned until lightly fixed. Set the filter steadily on the heatproof glass that catches the coffee. The rim of the glass must support the base, so the filter does not tilt as you pour.
3. Heat the water. Heat 240 ml of drinking water in a small kettle for about 2–3 minutes, until it is at a steady rolling boil, then brew right away once it is off the heat. Pour the water as soon as it boils; do not leave it standing in a measuring cup first, because a metal filter cools it quickly.
4. Wet the grounds. First pour in 30 ml of just-boiled water evenly, cover and let it sit for 30 seconds, until the grounds are soaked and coffee begins to drip from the bottom. Before you pour in the rest of the hot water, a screw-type plate can be loosened slightly to leave room for the water to flow.
5. Finish the drip. Add 130 ml of hot water, cover and wait about 4 minutes, until the water level has dropped to the grounds and the drip from the bottom slows and nearly stops. If nothing drips at all partway through, loosen the plate a little so the coffee drips drop by drop again.
6. Stir in the milk and add ice. Take the filter away, add 1.75 tbsp of sweetened condensed milk and stir until there are no white clumps at the bottom and the coffee is an even dark brown. Pour it into the glass of ice and stir for 15 seconds, until the outside of the glass is clearly cold. Drink right away.

## Key points

- **Weak taste, only milky sweetness**: the water cooled before brewing, so the coffee's flavour did not fully reach the water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Brew as soon as the water boils, cover the filter after wetting the grounds, and stir in the condensed milk only after the whole cup has dripped.
- **It quickly turns into thin iced milk coffee**: too little ice, so the hot coffee melts it fast (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Put the solid ice in first as the list says, then pour in the coffee after it has dripped. Stir the condensed milk in well beforehand, and once it is over the ice, stir only until the outside of the glass is cold.
