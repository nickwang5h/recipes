*Panna cotta* is a cream dessert from Piedmont. Vanilla, sugar and cream give it flavour and gelatin gives it shape. Once chilled, it stands up, trembles when the plate is shaken, and is soft to the bite as the cream flavour slowly spreads. Heating and handling take about twenty minutes, and then it needs at least six hours in the refrigerator, so it suits making half a day ahead.

It depends on [gelatin setting](/principles/collagen-gelatin/): the gelatin first absorbs water and dissolves in the hot cream, then on cooling it weaves into a network that traps the liquid. When the temperature rises the network loosens again. Heating is only to dissolve the sugar and gelatin. If the cream is boiled too long, water evaporates and the concentration rises, so the same gelatin sets harder. In the refrigerator, heat has to leave slowly from the walls of the cup and from the centre (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Submerge the sheet gelatin completely in cold water and soak for 5–10 minutes, until each sheet is soft with no hard core. Brands differ in thickness, so go by the softened state and the package directions.
2. Put the cream, sugar, vanilla seeds and the split vanilla pod in a small pot and stir over low heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the sugar is gone and fine bubbles appear at the edge of the pot, then take it off the heat right away. Do not reduce the cream.
3. Remove the vanilla pod, lift out the gelatin and squeeze out the soaking water, and stir it gently into the hot cream. When no strands or lumps of gelatin remain, pour it through a fine strainer into a pitcher with a spout. Stir only to dissolve the gelatin and work in as little air as possible.
4. Divide the liquid evenly among four small moulds or dessert cups and skim off the bubbles on top. Let some of the heat escape, then put them in the refrigerator. Do not wait on the counter until fully cold. Cover once the surface no longer steams.
5. Chill for at least 6 hours, until the centre has also set and the whole thing trembles lightly when you tilt the cup. Cups can be eaten as they are. To unmould, dip the outside of the mould briefly in warm water, dry it, and turn it upside down, stopping as soon as the edge loosens.

## Key points

- **Hard and bouncy like rubber**: too much gelatin or the cream was reduced too far (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Weigh the gelatin and do not substitute a number of sheets. When changing brands, check the bloom strength and the equivalent amount.
- **Lumps of gelatin in one place and no set elsewhere**: the gelatin was not fully bloomed, or it did not dissolve after it was added (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Let each sheet absorb water fully, then stir it through the hot liquid and strain.
- **Liquid runs out when unmoulding**: it was not chilled long enough, or the warm water melted too much of the outer layer (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Make sure the centre has set, and warm only the outside of the mould. Do not soak it in hot water for long.
