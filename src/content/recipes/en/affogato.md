*Affogato* is an Italian dessert of a small serving of hot espresso poured over ice cream; "affogato" means "drowned". Vanilla, milk and the bitter aroma of coffee meet in the cup, and it reaches the table with the centre still in the shape of a scoop and a ring of warm-cool milky coffee around it. There are only two ingredients, ice cream and coffee. Choose plain vanilla with no nuts, chocolate pieces or other mix-ins, so you get a smooth melted layer.

This dessert depends on how fast [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) happens. The hot coffee touches the outer layer of the ice cream first and melts the ice crystals into liquid; the centre takes longer to warm. The cup matters too: a warm cup melts the ice cream faster, a cold cup slows it down. How much coffee you pour, how soft the ice cream is at the start and how long you wait after pouring decide whether you eat a dessert with cold and warm layers or a cup of evenly mixed milky coffee.

## Steps

1. Prepare two small heatproof dessert cups or coffee cups. Wash and dry them, and chill them in the refrigerator ahead of time. The rim must be wide enough for a small spoon, and deep enough that the spoon can reach both the scoop and the coffee at the bottom.
2. Divide the ice cream between the two cold cups, 40 g each, scooped into a compact ball as far as you can. If it is too hard straight from the freezer, wait until a spoon can scoop it; do not wait until the surface is wet and the ball has spread out before filling the cups.
3. Put the filled cups back in the freezer for a short wait, then pull a total of 60 ml of espresso. Measure the two portions separately, 30 ml per person. Do not add sugar to the coffee; the ice cream supplies the sweetness.
4. Take the ice cream out and slowly pour one portion of freshly made hot coffee over the scoop in each cup. When you see the edges start to melt and milky coffee pool at the bottom while the centre still stands, serve at once; you can also let each person pour it at the table.
5. With a small spoon, dig from the centre of the ice cream out to the coffee at the edge, so each bite has both textures. Let it keep melting as you eat; there is no need to stir the whole cup evenly beforehand.

## Key points

- **The coffee turns it into soup as soon as it is poured**: the ice cream was too soft to begin with, or it waited too long in a warm cup. Keep the ice cream in the freezer until the coffee is ready, and eat right after pouring. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The milky coffee is thin, with the ice cream floating in a big cup of coffee**: too much coffee adds heat and dilutes the milk flavour. Use one small cup of espresso per serving, not the amount you would pour from a large cup of drip coffee. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The coffee has cooled and the outer layer won't melt**: the two components are too close in temperature, so the cold-and-warm layers are not clear. Have the ice cream ready first, pull the coffee last and pour it at once; do not hold both while you wait for other dishes. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
