*Sha zhu cai* is a pot of northeastern pickled napa cabbage, pork and blood sausage stewed together, with soft ribs and sour, fragrant cabbage. The ribs make the base of the broth. Pressure stewing slowly softens the [collagen](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) around the bones, and the pickled cabbage brings the sour aroma of lactic fermentation. The cabbage is stir-fried first and then stewed with the ribs, so the broth brings the flavour of the pork fat and the cabbage together.

The casing of blood sausage breaks at a squeeze, so at the end it is warmed gently in a separate pot and then joined to the stew.

## Steps

1. Shred the pickled cabbage and taste a little first to judge how sour and salty it is. If it is moderately sour and salty, rinse it quickly and squeeze it dry; if it is strong, wash it once more, but do not soak it until it loses its [fermented flavour](/principles/fermentation/). Smash the garlic cloves and snip the dried chilies into sections.
2. Put the ribs in a pot of cold water with the Shaoxing wine and bring to a boil, skimming off all the foam. Lift out and rinse off anything clinging to them, drain, and blot the surface dry.
3. Heat the canola oil in a wok over medium-low heat and stir-fry the garlic cloves and dried chilies until fragrant. Add the ground ginger for a brief stir-fry, then add the ribs at once. Stir-fry until the side of the ribs touching the pan is slightly golden, then add the pickled cabbage and 10 g of toasted sesame oil and stir-fry over high heat for about 2 minutes, until all the shreds are coated in oil.
4. Pour in 600 ml of hot water and move everything into an electric pressure cooker with the bay leaves, star anise and green onion knot. The pickled cabbage is already salty enough, so do not add salt yet. Do not fill the cooker past its maximum line, and follow the manual for the amount of water.
5. Choose the high-pressure meat stew programme and time about 40 minutes once it comes up to pressure. Release the pressure as the manual directs, and open the lid only once the pressure is fully gone. When the meat pulls easily away from the bone and the cabbage is soft, you can go on; if the meat is still tough, keep stewing.
6. As the ribs near done, heat water in another pot and put in the precooked blood sausage whole. Keep the heat low without a vigorous boil and warm it for about 10 minutes. Raw blood sausage must be cooked through by the method on its package and cannot be heated this way. Adjust to the product's instructions, and take the sausage out once the centre reads 74°C (165°F), then cut it into thick sections. Do not pierce it or put it in the pressure cooker under pressure.
7. Move the stew to a regular pot, or use the open-lid heating mode of the pressure cooker, add the hot blood sausage and simmer together over low heat for about 2 minutes, until the broth is hot through as well. Pick out the spices and the green onion knot, taste for saltiness and add salt if needed, then ladle into a deep bowl.
8. Mix the chili oil, light soy sauce, minced garlic and 2 g of toasted sesame oil into a dip and serve it in a small dish. Dip the ribs and blood sausage as you like, and eat the pickled cabbage and broth directly with rice.

## Key points

- **Ribs are cooked but still tough**: the connective tissue softens more slowly than the meat cooks. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/); after releasing the pressure, check the meat near the bone and decide whether to stew longer.
- **Blood sausage bursts and the broth is full of loose filling**: the casing was pierced, or it met a vigorous boil. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/); precooked blood sausage only needs gentle warming through, and goes in last.
- **Too little sourness, or so salty it turns bitter**: the pickled cabbage was washed too long, or its own salt stacked on top of added salt. See [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/); taste the cabbage first and then decide how many times to wash it.
