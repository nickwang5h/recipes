In braised lamb chops with noodles, the lamb is tender enough to come away from the bone, and the noodle sheets are soaked with lamb broth. The meat has to turn soft first, then the noodles are cooked: the sinew, skin and connective tissue of the ribs soften slowly in moist heat, and part of the collagen turns into gelatin, which is what lets the meat leave the bone (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).

The dough for the wide noodles is made soft. Resting it twice and oiling it makes it easy to roll into thin sheets (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). The sheets are finished by braising in the lamb broth: as they absorb water, the [starch gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the broth slowly thickens. Keep the broth before the noodles go in, and do not reduce the lamb dry beforehand.

## Steps

1. Put the lamb ribs in a pot of cold water, bring to a boil and skim off the foam. Lift them out, rinse off the clinging scum with warm water and drain well. Cut the green and red bell peppers into chunks, cut the leek into sections, slice the ginger, and have the Sichuan peppercorns and dried chili ready.
2. Put the oil for the lamb and the sugar in a wok. Over low heat, stir until the sugar melts and turns a light amber. Add the drained lamb and toss to coat. If the sugar has already turned black or tastes burnt and bitter, throw it out and start again.
3. Stir-fry the lamb until the surface stops dripping, then add the ginger, Sichuan peppercorns, dried chili and dark soy sauce and toss. Pour in the stewing water, just enough to cover the meat, bring to a boil and keep boiling for about 10 minutes. Then cover and stew over low heat for 30 minutes.
4. While the lamb stews, mix the flour, the dough salt and the dough water into a dough. Cover and rest for 10 minutes. Knead until the surface is even and smooth, cover and rest for 5 minutes. The dough should be soft without cracking or falling apart; do not knead in dry flour over and over to make it stiff.
5. Roll the dough into a long rope and cut it into 6–8 small pieces. Rub them with the oil for the dough, cover and leave for 5 minutes. Roll each into a thin, wide sheet, with edges and centre close in thickness. Keep the finished sheets apart so they do not stick in a stack.
6. Check the lamb. Only start braising the noodles when a chopstick slides easily into the thickest piece and the sinew is also soft. If it is still tough, keep stewing over low heat, and top up with boiling water to keep the amount of broth. Taste the broth and adjust the salt.
7. Add the bell peppers and leek and toss. Lay the noodle sheets one at a time between the lamb and the broth, and gently move them apart so each one touches the hot broth. If there is so little broth that it does not wet the sheets, add boiling water first; the sheets cook by braising in the broth.
8. Cover and braise for about 4 minutes, then uncover and gently turn the sheets over. Tear open the thickest sheet: when there is no white floury band in the centre and it is springy when bitten, turn up the heat to reduce the sauce briefly until it coats the noodles, then plate at once. If there is a white core, add water and keep braising.

## Key points

- **Lamb is cooked but still hard to bite**: the collagen has not had enough moist heat (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Stew the meat until soft before adding the vegetables and noodles, and top up with water in time while stewing.
- **Noodle sheets shrink back and the edges crack**: the dough was not rested enough, or too much dry flour was added to stop sticking (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Keep the planned amount of water, knead evenly, cover and rest, and rub with a thin film of oil before rolling.
- **Noodles are gummy on the surface but raw in the centre**: the sheets were stacked, or there was not enough broth, so the centre did not absorb enough water and heat (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Lay the sheets in one at a time and gently spread them apart. After four minutes, cut one open to check, and reduce the sauce only once it is cooked through.
