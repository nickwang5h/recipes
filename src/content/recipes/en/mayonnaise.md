*Mayonnaise* is a French cold sauce made of egg yolk, oil and vinegar, suited to cold fish, cooked vegetables and cold meats. Its thickness comes from [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/): the lecithin and proteins in the yolk help wrap tiny oil droplets so the oil stays suspended in the liquid from the yolk and vinegar. The more droplets, and the more tightly packed, the thicker the sauce. The vinegar brings [acid](/principles/acid/), and a little Dijon mustard adds spice.

When making it by hand, the most important thing is to add the oil slowly at the start. If the next big stream goes in before the water phase and emulsifier have dispersed the first oil, the sauce easily separates into a pool of oil and a pool of egg. With large yolks and a mild vegetable oil, the finished sauce is pale yellow and piles into a soft shape on a spoon. The quantity makes about one small jar, which can be eaten over several meals as a dip.

## Steps

1. Warm a mixing bowl with warm water, pour it out and dry the bowl. Add the egg yolks and whisk until even and sticky, so that lifting the whisk pulls up a short ribbon of yolk.
2. Add the wine vinegar, Dijon mustard and a little salt, and whisk until the salt dissolves and no small lumps of mustard remain. Set a damp cloth under the bowl so it stays steady while you whisk continuously.
3. Whisk constantly while adding the oil one drop at a time. After each small batch, pause the oil and keep whisking until the oil streaks disappear. Move on once the sauce turns paler and thicker, like heavy cream.
4. Add the rest of the oil in small streams, whisking each one in fully. When the sauce is so thick the whisk is hard to turn, whisk in a few drops of vinegar first, and add more oil once it softens again.
5. Whisk in the boiled water in stages until the sauce is smooth and coats a spoon. Taste, adjust with a little salt, white pepper or vinegar, then cover and put in the refrigerator right away.

## Key points

- **Stays thin and oil floats on top**: the oil went in too fast at the start and the droplets did not disperse (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Take a clean bowl that has been warmed, whisk 1 tsp Dijon mustard with a small spoonful of the failed sauce into a creamy emulsion, then whisk in the rest of the sauce a teaspoon at a time.
- **Gets stiffer as you whisk, then leaks oil**: there is not enough liquid and the oil droplets are too crowded. Before it leaks, whisk in a few drops of vinegar or water; if it has already separated, rebuild the emulsion as above.
- **Tastes heavy, only oily**: not enough acid or salt (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Add vinegar drop by drop and salt in small amounts, mixing fully and tasting each time, so you do not thin the sauce in one go.
- **Risk of raw egg yolk**: the sauce is not heated, and neither the vinegar nor that little boiled water sterilizes it. Choose pasteurized egg products and refrigerate promptly after making.
