*Chả cá Lã Vọng* is Hanoi's turmeric and dill fish. The fish is marinated and grilled, then pan-fried in hot oil until fragrant, and set on rice noodles together with green onion and dill, with peanuts and a lime-sharpened mắm tôm dipping sauce added. The dill goes into the pan by the bunch and comes out as soon as it wilts.

Turmeric gives the colour, galangal gives a pungent aroma, and the sour fermented rice mẻ gives the marinade its acidity. A short acid marinade mainly changes the surface of the fish; see [acid](/principles/acid/). The green onion and dill are held back for the end of the pan-frying, so the oil carries the aroma out while the fresh herb smell is kept; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

## Steps

1. Marinate the fish. Pat the fish dry and cut it into pieces about 2.5 × 5 cm and 2 cm thick, and mix with all of the marinade ingredients. Cover and refrigerate for 1 hour, until the surface is evenly coated in the yellow marinade paste.
2. Cook the rice noodles. Put 2000 ml of water in a pot and cook over high heat for 8–10 minutes, until the water boils. Add the dry noodles and cook for 4–6 minutes, until there is no hard core in the centre; follow the time on the package. Lift out and rinse in cold water, drain for 5 minutes until no longer dripping, and divide among three bowls.
3. Make the dipping sauce. Stir together 2 tbsp of shrimp paste, the sugar, lime juice, minced garlic and minced chili. Heat the 1 tbsp of oil for the dipping sauce over medium heat for about 1 minute, until it shows ripples, take it off the heat and pour it into the sauce, then stir for another 1 minute, until the sugar grains disappear and fine bubbles show on the surface. Divide among small dishes.
4. Grill the fish. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F), which takes about 10–15 minutes, until the temperature indicator shows it has been reached. Arrange the fish in a single layer on a foil-lined baking sheet and bake for 5–7 minutes, until the marinade on the surface has set, the edges of the fish have turned white, and the juices released have not dried up. Take it out and pan-fry right away.
5. Pan-fry and stir-fry the herbs. Put 3 tbsp of oil in a wide skillet and heat over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until the fish will sizzle when it goes in. Add the fish and fry each side for about 1 minute, until the surface is golden and the thickest part is opaque and flakes apart. Add the green onion and dill and fold gently for 1–2 minutes, until the green onion greens are soft and the dill has just collapsed but is still bright green, and take it off the heat at once.
6. Fill the bowls. Lift the fish, green onion and dill onto the noodles, sprinkle on the peanuts, and add the optional basil. Keep the dipping sauce in its small dishes, and dip the fish and noodles in it as you eat; do not leave the herbs in the hot pan to turn yellow.

## Key points

- **Galangal chews like sawdust**: the tough fibres were not broken down and the aroma is hard to spread evenly (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Use a fine grater or mortar, discard the hard fibres left over, and then measure the fine mince.
- **Dill goes yellow and the pan tastes only of oil**: fresh herbs went in early and were heated too long, so the volatile aroma was lost (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the green onion and dill only after the fish is cooked, and take them out as soon as they collapse.
- **Fish breaks up and the outside is dry**: it marinated too long or was overheated (see [acid](/principles/acid/) and [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Marinate for the stated time, grill until the surface has set, pan-fry until cooked, and turn gently with a wide spatula.
