Stewed eggplant and potatoes cooks soft eggplant, floury potato and pork in one pot. The eggplant first takes up oil and broth, then softens; the potato needs heat and water to reach its centre so its starch can complete [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). A smaller amount of liquid does not mean the potato is cooked through, so check the thickest piece before reducing the sauce.

A few potato crumbs thicken the sauce slightly, so no starch slurry is needed. The pork strips are stir-fried apart first, the potato and eggplant go into the pot one after the other, and moist heat then cooks the insides through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The garlic goes in last so its aroma stays distinct (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Wash the eggplant, potato and green chili, peel the potato, and peel and smash the garlic. Using a separate clean cutting board for the pork, cut it into thin strips about 3 cm long, as even in thickness as you can.
2. Cut the eggplant and potato into small pieces of similar size, about 2 cm square; cut the chili into bite-size pieces. Do not leave any especially large, thick chunks of potato, or the eggplant will have fallen apart while the potato centres are still hard.
3. Add 15 ml oil to the pot and heat over medium heat until the oil spreads easily but is not yet smoking. Add the chili and stir-fry until you smell it; add the pork strips right away and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, breaking them up well, until the surface starts to change colour.
4. Add the potato and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, then add the eggplant and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, so every piece is coated with oil. Scoop up from the bottom with the spatula; once the eggplant has soaked up the oil, the bottom of the pot dries quickly.
5. Add 15 ml soy sauce, turn to medium heat and keep stir-frying for about 5 minutes. When there is no flowing liquid at the bottom, add about 5 ml water down the side each time and lower the heat a little; as soon as food starts to stick, add the stewing water early.
6. Add water to about nine tenths of the height of the ingredients, bring to a boil, cover, and stew at a heat that keeps it at a gentle simmer. Turn the bottom layer gently once midway; if the water is nearly gone and the potato is still hard, add hot water and keep cooking.
7. When a chopstick pierces the potato easily, the eggplant has collapsed and the pork is cooked through, uncover and reduce the sauce. When the sauce has fallen to about one tenth of the height of the ingredients and clings to the pieces, add the garlic and toss, heat briefly to release its aroma, taste and add salt if needed, then turn off the heat and plate.

## Key points

- **Sauce is gone but the potato still has a hard centre**: the pieces are too big, or there was not enough water during stewing (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add hot water, cover and keep cooking until the centres are soft, then uncover and reduce.
- **Sauce looks like paste and the pieces have all fallen apart**: the potato was mashed repeatedly after gelatinizing, so a lot of starch and crumbs went into the sauce (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Turn gently while stewing, and only reduce to thicken once it is cooked; do not keep mashing with the spatula.
- **Faint garlic, bitter chili**: the garlic was cooked too long, and the chili was overheated when sizzled in the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the pork as soon as the chili smells fragrant, and keep the garlic for the end of the reducing.
