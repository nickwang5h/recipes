In stir-fried beef with hot peppers, the beef is thin and tender, the peppers are just cooked, and the seasoning is simple. Cut the beef against the grain to shorten the long muscle fibres. Slices of similar thickness in one batch cook evenly in a quick stir-fry (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Stir-frying the peppers first and adding the beef after means the slices do not have to wait in the pan while the vegetables cook.

Minced garlic gives off its aroma quickly in oil, and burns quickly too. Add the peppers once it turns light golden, so they take up the hot oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The soy sauce and sugar go into the marinade and coat the beef thinly after cooking. If a lot of thin liquid shows up at the bottom of the pan, the beef was too wet or the pan too crowded, and slow stir-frying from there will dry the meat out first.

## Steps

1. Trim the sinew from the beef, pat it dry and slice it thinly against the grain, keeping the thickness as even as possible. Slice the ginger and put it with the beef, add all the light soy sauce and sugar, toss to coat and refrigerate to marinate for 30–40 minutes. Leave the salt until just before serving.
2. Mince the garlic finely, cut the green onion into pieces, and stem and cut the hot peppers. If the peppers are very hot, remove the white pith inside. Drain washed pepper pieces. Set everything beside the pan so you are not cutting in a rush after the garlic goes in.
3. Take the ginger out of the marinated beef, loosen the slices, and lift them out leaving the liquid at the bottom of the bowl. Keep the marinade in the bowl and add a little of it back with the meat later to cook through. Do not tip the wet meat and a whole bowl of liquid into the pan together.
4. Heat the oil in the pan over medium heat until it flows freely and add the minced garlic. Keep stirring gently. When it turns light golden and smells of garlic, add the hot peppers. Do not wait until the garlic turns dark brown.
5. Stir-fry the peppers until the skin wrinkles slightly and the colour brightens, with their shape intact. When the pan temperature has recovered and there are no obvious water droplets on the peppers, add the beef, spread it out and loosen the slices so they touch the hot surface of the pan.
6. Stir-fry until most of the slices have changed colour. Add the green onion and the small amount of marinade kept in the bowl, toss, and let the marinade cook through. Break open a thicker slice and look: when there is no raw look at the centre and the pepper pieces are no longer hard, taste.
7. Add a little salt to taste, toss, turn off the heat and plate. Thin slices should not wait in the hot pan once cooked. Leaving a little glossy sauce on the plate is fine, and you do not need to fry all the liquid away.

## Key points

- **Stringy slices that are tough to chew**: cut with the grain, so the muscle fibres are too long (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Trim the sinew first, then slice thinly against the grain. Sinew will not soften in a marinade.
- **Tough beef and raw peppers**: the pepper pieces are too big, or were not stir-fried until slightly wrinkled first, so by the time the peppers are done the [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) in the beef have tightened hard. Stir-fry the peppers first, add the thin beef and plate it as soon as it is done.
- **Bitter garlic and a blackened pan**: the finely minced garlic was overheated in the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the peppers at light golden. The sugar goes in the marinade, so do not fry sugar and garlic in plain oil.
