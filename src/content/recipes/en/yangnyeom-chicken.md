Korean fried chicken (*yangnyeom chicken*) is fried first, then tossed in a garlicky sweet and spicy red sauce. The wings are cut into drumettes and flats of similar thickness, so they are easy to fry through in batches at home. Ketchup brings sweet-and-sour, while the gochujang and rice syrup make the sauce cling to the surface.

The crisp shell comes from the coating losing water under heat: the starch first absorbs water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), then dries out as frying continues. The first fry cooks the chicken, and the second fry at a higher oil temperature dries the outer layer out again (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Spread the wings out to let steam escape after lifting them out, and toss with the sauce last, so the shell is less likely to be softened by trapped steam.

## Steps

1. Dry the wings with paper towels and toss with the salt, black pepper and minced ginger. Cover and refrigerate for 2 hours, until the seasoning is evenly stuck on and the surface is slightly moist. Do not rinse raw wings; the splashing water would dirty the counter.
2. Put the 2 tsp of oil for the sauce in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily. Add the garlic and stir for 30 seconds, until light golden. Pour in the ketchup, rice syrup, gochujang and vinegar and cook over low heat, stirring, for 1–2 minutes, until the whole pot bubbles and thick sauce runs off a lifted spoon. Take it off the heat.
3. Toss the potato starch with the wings, working piece by piece, until each piece has a thin, continuous coating with no heaps of dry powder. Add 1200 ml of oil to a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 10–12 minutes, until the thermometer reads 160–165°C (320–330°F).
4. Fry the wings in two batches, putting them in piece by piece, about 6 minutes per batch, until the coating has set and is a light golden colour. Use tongs to separate wings that stick together and turn them. Lift them onto a rack; before the second batch goes in, heat the oil for about 2 minutes, until it recovers to 160–165°C (320–330°F).
5. Leave the wings on the rack for 5 minutes, until there is no obvious steam from the surface and the coating is slightly dry. Heat the oil for about 2 minutes, to 175–180°C (350°F). Fry again in two batches, about 5 minutes per batch, until the shell is golden and feels hard and crisp when touched lightly with tongs. The centre of the thickest part must reach 74°C (165°F); keep the probe away from the bone. If it has not reached it, fry 1 minute more each time and measure again.
6. Drain the twice-fried wings on the rack for 1 minute, until the surface no longer drips oil. Reheat the sweet and spicy sauce over medium heat for 1 minute, until it bubbles again, then turn off the heat. Add the wings and toss for about 30 seconds, until each piece is thinly coated with sauce. Sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds, spread out on a plate and eat right away.

## Key points

- **Coating greasy and never crisp**: too many went in at once and the oil temperature did not recover. Keep them in a single layer, fry in batches, and measure the oil temperature after the wings go in. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Dark brown outside and not cooked near the bone**: the oil was too hot or the pieces too big. Use separated wings, control the oil temperature for the first fry, and check the thickest part at the end. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Quickly goes soft after saucing**: the wings were piled up, or cooked too long in the sauce pan. Let steam escape on the rack, turn off the heat once the sauce is hot and toss quickly, and do not cover the plate. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
