Garlic-infused soy sauce is a freshly made, oily dipping sauce that suits poached chicken or dumplings. Smashing the garlic releases its smell, and brief heating turns the harsh raw garlic into a mild cooked garlic fragrance, which the oil then carries into the soy sauce; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The sesame seeds are toasted, so they add a roasted aroma when stirred in directly.

15 ml of oil is only a thin layer and overheats easily, so the garlic goes into cold oil and is heated slowly. After the oil and soy sauce are stirred together they still separate slowly. This is a temporary dispersion; see [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/). Give it a light stir before eating, so each dip picks up oil, soy sauce and garlic.

## Steps

1. Peel and smash the garlic and cut it into small pieces of similar size, then pat the surface dry. Do not cut it into a very fine paste, or it heats faster and sticks to the bottom of the pan and scorches first.
2. Put the light soy sauce and toasted white sesame seeds in a heatproof dipping bowl and stir. If you only have raw sesame seeds, toast them in a dry pan over low heat for about 1 minute, until slightly yellow and nutty-smelling, then pour them out and measure.
3. Put the peanut oil and garlic in a small pot and turn on low heat. Nudge the garlic so every piece touches the oil. When fine bubbles appear at the edges, let the garlic colour slowly, and don't let the oil smoke steadily.
4. About 30 seconds after bubbling starts, begin watching the colour. When the edges of the garlic have just turned pale yellow and you smell cooked garlic, turn off the heat and transfer the garlic and oil to another small heatproof bowl at once. Piece size and pan temperature change the timing, so go by colour.
5. When the vigorous bubbling stops, spoon the garlic and oil into the soy sauce bowl in several additions and stir gently. Even a small amount of hot oil bubbles when it meets the water in the soy sauce, so keep your hands and face away from the bowl.
6. Stir until the sesame seeds and garlic are evenly distributed and serve. It is most convenient made fresh and used the same day. If you need to keep it, refrigerate it promptly at 4°C (40°F) or below, and take it out with a clean spoon before dipping.

## Key points

- **The garlic turns dark brown and the dip is bitter**: the oil was too hot, or the garlic stayed in the hot pan too long (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Transfer it at pale yellow, because residual heat keeps colouring it.
- **A sharp raw garlic smell**: the garlic pieces were too big, or the heat was turned off right when bubbling started (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Cut evenly and heat on low until you smell cooked garlic.
- **Oil on top and soy sauce below**: the oil and soy sauce were not truly [emulsified](/principles/emulsification/), so they separate after a while. Stir before eating. There is no need to cook off the water.
- **Leftovers sat on the table for a long time**: a garlic-oil sauce needs prompt refrigeration. Soy sauce and briefly fried garlic cannot guarantee safe storage at room temperature, so do not keep it as a long-term pantry oil.
