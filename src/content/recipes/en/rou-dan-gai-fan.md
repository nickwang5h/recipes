A pork and egg rice bowl does the browning, the egg braising and the rice topping all in one pan. The ground pork is spread thin first so the side against the pan loses water and takes colour, giving the toasty flavour of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The sauce goes in later, so the meat has time to brown and the sugar doesn't burn too early.

The eggs sit whole on the meat and are heated by the bottom of the pan and the steam under the lid. Stop the heat when the whites are set and the yolks cooked through; the texture is still soft (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). This is a bowl with a lot of meat and egg and little rice; fill the two bowls of rice while hot and the sauce poured over will mix in easily.

## Steps

1. Stir the dark soy sauce, light soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and the optional shallot oil into a bowl sauce until no sugar grains are visible at the bottom. Divide the cooked rice between the bowls and keep warm.
2. Heat the vegetable oil in a flat-bottomed pan over medium heat, until the oil flows easily and is not smoking yet. Add the ground pork, spread it into a thin layer of even thickness, and don't rush to turn it.
3. When the underside of the meat turns light brown, cut it into several pieces with a spatula, flip and keep browning. Thick parts need to be cooked through; measure the centre of the meat with a thermometer and continue once it reaches 71°C (160°F).
4. Leave four gaps in the meat and crack in the eggs one at a time, keeping the yolks whole. Turn to medium-low heat, cover, and braise until the whites go from clear to milky white and set at the edges.
5. Pour the bowl sauce into the gaps between the pieces of meat, not straight over the yolks. Cover again and cook on low for about 3 minutes, so the sauce bubbles gently. The yolks must be cooked through; if not, braise a little longer.
6. Uncover and look at the bottom of the pan: the sauce should flow and cling to the meat. Turn off the heat when the eggs reach 74°C (165°F), spoon the meat and eggs with sauce over the rice in the two bowls, sprinkle with green onion and eat right away.

## Key points

- **The meat has no toasty flavour and water collects at the bottom**: the layer of meat is too thick and the water can't evaporate (see [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Switch to a wide pan or fry in batches, and let the underside take colour before flipping.
- **The surface is scorched but the inside is not cooked**: uneven thickness slows the centre's heating (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Spread the meat thin and measure the thickest part rather than going by the surface colour.
- **The eggs are hard and the sauce is bitter**: long cooking over high heat makes the [proteins tighten](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and the sugar and soy sauce can also scorch on a dry pan bottom. Keep the heat low once the bowl sauce is in and take it out as soon as it is done; add a little hot water when the bottom is nearly dry.
