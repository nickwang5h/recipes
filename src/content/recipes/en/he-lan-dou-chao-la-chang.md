Stir-fried snow peas with Chinese sausage is about two textures: crisp-tender pods and salty-sweet sausage. [Blanching](/principles/blanching/) first heats the pods evenly, then a short stir-fry picks up the sausage's rendered fat and fragrance, so the final time together in the pan can be short.

The sugar in the sausage easily scorches in a hot pan, while the pods need to heat up fast. So this dish uses the heat in two stages: fry the sausage over low heat first, then turn to medium-high for the snow peas (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Both the light soy sauce and the sausage carry salt, so taste for saltiness only after tossing.

## Steps

1. Check the sausage package. Sausage that needs cooking is steamed first as the label says, until the centre of the thickest part reaches 71°C (160°F); precooked products can be used directly. Let it cool slightly, then slice thinly on the diagonal, keeping the slices as even in thickness as you can.
2. Pinch off the stem ends and tail tips of the snow peas and pull off the tough strings along both sides. Rinse, then pick out any shrivelled pods or ones with hard strings; larger pods can be cut diagonally in two.
3. Bring a pot of water to a boil, add the snow peas and blanch briefly for about 45 seconds. They are ready when the pods turn bright green and are slightly pliable but still keep their shape. Add them in batches so the water returns to a boil quickly; any that still feel clearly raw and hard get a little longer.
4. Lift the pods into cold water to stop the residual heat, then drain thoroughly and discard the water.
5. Add 10 ml of oil to the pan and heat over low heat until the oil flows easily. Add the sausage slices and keep turning them until they curl slightly at the edges, the fat turns translucent and you smell the fragrance; don't let them scorch black.
6. Add the snow peas, turn to medium-high heat and stir-fry for about 30 seconds so the pods touch the hot pan evenly. If there is a lot, stir-fry in batches, and don't pile them into a thick layer.
7. Add part of the light soy sauce down the side of the pan and stir-fry another 20–30 seconds. Taste a cooked pod, and add the rest of the soy sauce according to the saltiness. Take it off the heat as soon as the pods are hot through, there is no raw hardness when bitten, and the bottom of the pan has no pooled water.

## Key points

- **Sausage turns black and tastes bitter**: the heat was too high when frying it for fragrance (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add the pods once you smell the fragrance and the edges start to curl, and remove any scorched pieces.
- **Snow peas go limp and dull in colour**: residual heat after blanching, or too long in the second stir-fry (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Drain well after cooling in water, and stir-fry the second time only until hot through.
- **Finished dish is too salty**: the salt already in the sausage was forgotten (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add the light soy sauce in stages, and decide whether to add the rest after tossing and tasting.
