*Guo ta dou fu* is a Shandong dish built on "fry first, then simmer". The tofu is cut into thin slices, dredged in starch, dipped in egg, fried golden on both sides, and then clear stock is added. The egg coating ends up soft, the tofu stays in whole slices, and a little flavourful sauce is left. The egg coating has to be fried until set first, or it will not survive the simmer.

Egg sets into a coating when heated (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)); the thin layer of starch also gradually sets when it meets the tofu's moisture and hot oil (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Adding stock for a short simmer then softens the coating again. Frying for colour and simmering for tenderness need different heat, so you can't stay on high heat the whole time.

## Steps

1. Cut the tofu into slices about 1 cm thick and 4 cm wide, lay them flat on paper towels and drain for 5 minutes, until no water beads remain on the surface. Sprinkle lightly with the salt for seasoning. Keep each slice whole, and don't press it with a weight.
2. Mix the chicken stock, light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and white pepper. Set out a shallow plate of starch and a shallow plate of beaten egg, and keep the ginger and green onion at hand, so the stock can go in right after frying.
3. Dust both sides of each tofu slice thinly with starch, shake off the loose powder, then dip in the egg. Coat one slice and fry it before the next; don't stack the coated tofu, or the egg runs off and the starch layer soaks thick.
4. Put 2 tbsp of the oil in a frying pan and preheat over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until the oil spreads easily. Add a single layer of tofu and fry about 2 minutes per side, until the egg coating is golden and the edges are set, then support each whole slice with a thin spatula to flip it. Fry in batches and set aside.
5. Add the remaining 1 tbsp oil, the ginger and the white part of the green onion to the pan and stir-fry over medium-low heat for 30 seconds, until the ginger and green onion smell fragrant. Put the tofu back, pour the simmering liquid down the side of the pan, and cook over medium heat for 1 minute, until the liquid is at a small bubble.
6. Cover and simmer over low heat for 8–10 minutes, until the coating has soaked up the liquid and softened and the tofu is hot through. Uncover and cook another 1–2 minutes, until the liquid only flows in a shallow layer on the bottom of the pan and the centre of the tofu reaches 74°C (165°F). Drizzle with toasted sesame oil, scatter the green part of the green onion, and serve with the remaining sauce.

## Key points

- **Coating falls off, tofu breaks**: the surface was too wet, or the slice was flipped before the egg had [set](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Blot off the water beads first, and lift with a wide spatula once the edges are set.
- **Thick, gluey outer layer that tastes floury**: the starch dusting was too thick, and [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) turned it into a thick paste. Shake off loose powder, and fry each slice as soon as it is coated.
- **Falls into pieces after simmering**: the liquid boiled hard or the tofu was handled too much. Following [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), simmer gently in a shallow liquid, swirl the pan instead of stirring repeatedly, and serve while a thin layer of sauce is left.
