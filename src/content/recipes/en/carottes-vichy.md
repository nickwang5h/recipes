*Carottes Vichy* is a classic French carrot side dish: carrots cut into thin slices are cooked soft in water, butter and sugar, then reduced to a glossy glaze, and finished with parsley. The carrots are still orange, coated in a shining glaze, with sweetness and butter flavour that are both light and clear.

At the start, the water carries heat around the carrots and cooks the insides soft first; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). As it nears dry, the sugar syrup thickens, and shaking the pan lets the melted butter disperse temporarily in the little remaining water, see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/), and coating the carrots with it makes them shine. Stop when the glaze clings to the vegetables and a thin glossy film is left on the bottom of the pan; if it goes brown, the clean sweetness this dish wants is gone.

## Steps

1. Peel the carrots thinly and cut them into rounds about 3 mm thick; where the thickness varies a lot, cut the wide end into half-moons. The slices must be an even thickness so they soften together, instead of small slices breaking up while large pieces are still hard.
2. Spread the carrots in a snug, wide, shallow pan and add the water, butter, sugar and a little salt. The water should come close to the top layer of carrots; if the pan is so wide that the water is only a shallow layer, switch to a smaller pan.
3. Bring to a boil, then keep at a gentle boil with the lid half on and cook for about 15–20 minutes, shaking the pan gently now and then. Test a thick slice with a thin skewer; when it goes in easily and the slices are still whole, take off the lid.
4. Cook off the remaining water over medium heat and let the liquid slowly thicken. When the water is nearly gone, switch to shaking the pan and turning gently so the carrots are evenly coated, until the foam goes from loose and scattered to fine and dense and the bottom of the pan changes from standing liquid to a glossy film.
5. Take it off the heat when every slice has a shine and only a little thick glaze is left in the pan. Taste a slice, then add salt, sprinkle on the parsley, toss gently, and serve right away; do not leave it in the hot pan.

## Key points

- **Water is gone but the carrots are still hard**: the pan was too wide and evaporation too fast, so the water ran out before the insides softened. Add 30 ml of hot water, cover for a while, cook until a skewer goes through, then uncover and reduce. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Carrots are mushy and the pan is still a pool of water**: too much water, or the slices were cut too thin. Lift the carrots out, reduce the liquid on its own until it coats a spoon, then pour it back and toss gently; do not let the vegetables keep cooking along with the liquid. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Only an oily shine, no even thin glaze**: the water phase has evaporated too completely, and the butter has gathered into free fat. Off the heat, add a small spoonful of hot water and shake gently until the thick liquid coats the vegetables again; next time stop before the pan is completely dry. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **Gets saltier as it reduces**: the salt stays in the pan and concentrates as the water decreases. Use little at the start and taste only when it is near its final thickness; once the sauce is nearly reduced, do not season to the taste of the cooking water. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
