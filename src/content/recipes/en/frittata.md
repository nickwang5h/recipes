*Frittata* is an Italian flat egg cake that you cut into wedges. It is soft and tender and tastes strongly of egg, and it often has cheese or cooked vegetables mixed in; this version is eggs with Parmigiano. It is cooked in two steps: first the bottom is set on the stove, then the oven broiler finishes the top. The key is to cook the whole cake slowly over low heat.

When egg is heated, its proteins unfold and join into a net that holds water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The pan passes heat to the bottom layer first, so the upper layer cooks more slowly. The last bit of raw egg is left to the broiler, so the bottom doesn't have to stay on the heat the whole time (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). If it is overcooked, the net tightens and squeezes the water out, and the frittata turns dry and hard.

## Steps

1. Choose a flat-bottomed pan of about 25 cm with an ovenproof handle that is clearly rated for broiling. Preheat the oven broiler, and have the finely grated cheese and a plate that will hold the whole frittata ready. Don't send a pan with a coating that can't take broiler heat under the element.
2. Beat the eggs with a fork, only until the whites and yolks are mixed. They don't need to be beaten to a foam. Add the cheese, a little salt and black pepper, and stir until the cheese is evenly spread, so it doesn't pile up in one spot after the eggs go into the pan.
3. Put the butter in the pan and melt it over medium heat. When it forms fine bubbles but has not yet browned, turn the pan so the fat coats the bottom, pour in all the eggs, shake gently to even out the thickness, and lower the heat to low at once.
4. Cook over low heat for about 8–12 minutes, until the edge has set, the bottom can be lifted gently with a spatula, and a layer of moist egg is still on top. At the start you can lift the set edge slightly and let a little egg run underneath. After that, let the frittata set steadily and don't keep stirring it apart.
5. Put the pan under the broiler and watch it the whole time, usually about 1–2 minutes. Broil until the egg on top has set and the centre no longer moves when you shake the pan lightly. Measure at the thickest spot from the side, and take it out when the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Don't keep broiling for a deep brown surface.
6. Take out the pan with an oven mitt, loosen the edge, and slide the frittata onto a plate with a wide spatula. Let it cool a little until warm and cut it into four wedges. Serve it with bread or lightly dressed vegetables. Don't leave it in the hot pan while you wait for other dishes, since the leftover heat will keep cooking it dry.

## Key points

- **The bottom is burnt while the top still flows**: the heat was too high, and the bottom layer sat in high heat for a long time. Lower the heat as soon as the eggs go in, and when the bottom has set, switch to the broiler so top and bottom share the heating. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **It is tough to bite and water seeps onto the plate**: the proteins coagulated too far. Take it out once the centre measures 74°C (165°F) and move it off the hot pan. Stop as soon as the surface has just set, and don't aim for a thick, browned crust. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **The outer ring is cooked but the centre is thick and hard to cook**: the pan is too small, so heat has a longer way to reach the centre. Keep a pan of about 25 cm. When scaling the recipe, change the pan size too, and don't pour double the eggs into the same pan. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
