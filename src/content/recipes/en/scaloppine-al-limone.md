*Scaloppine al limone* is thin veal slices with a lemon butter sauce: the meat is tender and the sauce is bright and sour with a creamy edge. Pounded evenly, the slices cook through very quickly, and they must come out of the pan as soon as they are done. Left in the hot pan, the muscle proteins keep contracting and squeeze out water, and the meat turns tough (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

There is only a thin coat of flour on the surface. When it meets the hot lemon juice and meat juices, it thickens the sauce through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and the butter stirred in at the end makes the sauce smoother. The result should be slices coated in a glossy sauce, with a little loose sauce left on the plate. Parsley goes in at the end to add a fresh aroma.

## Steps

1. Put the slices between two sheets of parchment paper and pound them gently with the flat side of a meat mallet to about 3 mm thick, as even as you can. Snip any tight connective tissue at the edges, and blot the surface dry. Have a warm serving plate ready.
2. Heat the vegetable oil and 30 g of the butter in a skillet over medium-high heat until the butter foam begins to subside. Just before they go into the pan, dredge one batch of slices in flour on both sides and shake until only a thin layer is left and the outline of the meat is still visible.
3. Put the slices in the pan in a single layer with gaps between them. Cook about 30 seconds on each side, flipping once pale golden spots show. When a thermometer inserted from the side into the thickest part reads 71°C (160°F), move the slices to the warm plate and sprinkle with salt and pepper. Cook the remaining slices the same way, in batches.
4. When all the slices are out, pour in the lemon juice and the juices collected on the plate, and scrape up the light brown bits stuck to the bottom of the pan over medium heat. Let the sauce boil briskly for about 20 seconds; there must still be liquid in the pan, and it must not cook down to a dry floury crust.
5. Turn the heat to low, add the remaining 15 g of butter and the parsley, and swirl the pan until the sauce is glossy. As soon as the butter has melted, return the slices and coat both sides with the sauce; the meat is already cooked, so it only needs to warm briefly.
6. Plate immediately, pour the sauce from the pan over the meat and add the lemon slices. The sauce spreads slowly over the hot plate, and the edges of the slices are still tender.

## Key points

- **Slices are tough and curl into bowls**: the thickness was uneven, the connective tissue was not snipped, or they cooked too long in the sauce. Pound thin, fry fast, take the meat out and then make the sauce (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Surface looks like wet batter and the sauce tastes floury**: the flour coat was too thick, or the slices sat too long after dredging. Dredge only just before frying and shake off loose flour; the sauce needs a brief boil so the thin flour coat can absorb water fully (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Oil floats on the plate and the sauce does not cling to the meat**: the liquid reduced too far, or the heat was too high at the end. Take the pan off the heat, add 1 tsp of water at a time and swirl to bring the sauce back together, then warm the slices through again (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).
