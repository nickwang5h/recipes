Iced lemon tea is a common cold drink in Hong Kong tea restaurants. The black tea is first brewed strong enough, then served in a glass with syrup, ice and lemon slices with the peel on. Before drinking, press the fruit lightly with a spoon so the juice goes into the tea, and the lemon peel adds a distinct fragrance (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The sourness, sweetness and astringency of this tea come from the lemon, the sugar and the tea leaves respectively. Sugar softens the feeling of sourness and astringency but does not remove the acid (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). The tea base and syrup are made ahead and the lemon goes in only when you are about to drink, so the tea does not steep for long with the peel.

## Steps

1. Boil the 300 ml of brewing water for about 5 minutes, until at a rolling boil. Put the black tea in a heatproof pot, pour in the boiling water, cover and steep for 10 minutes, until the leaves are soaked through and opened out and the tea is a strong reddish brown.
2. While the tea steeps, put the 20 g sugar and 20 ml syrup water in a small pot and cook over low heat, stirring, for 2 minutes, until the sugar grains are completely gone and the liquid is clear. Take it off the heat and pour it into a small glass.
3. Once the tea has steeped for 10 minutes, strain it, separating the leaves from the liquid. Set the heatproof pot holding the tea in a basin of cool water to cool for about 15 minutes, until the wall of the pot feels close to room temperature; change the cool water once partway through. Cool the syrup at the same time, until the wall of the glass is no longer hot to the touch.
4. Wash and dry the lemon, cut off both ends and cut it into rounds about 0.5 cm thick, picking out the seeds. Divide all the lemon slices between two glasses and press the fruit lightly with a long spoon 2–3 times, until juice comes out and the peel stays intact.
5. Put 100 g ice in each of the two glasses, and divide the syrup and the cooled tea between them. Stir from the bottom up with the long spoon for about 30 seconds, until the syrup is dispersed and the glass is cold on the outside, with unmelted ice still visible.
6. Drink as soon as it is poured. If you want a stronger lemon taste, press the fruit lightly as you drink to work the juice out bit by bit; stop when the sourness suits you, with the tea aroma, sweetness and sourness all still tasting distinct.

## Key points

- **Sharp and astringent, and adding sugar doesn't make it smooth**: the tea steeped too long, or the hot tea was cooled with the leaves in it. Steep first, strain out the leaves in time, then cool (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)); for the next pot, fix the amount of tea and the 10 minute steeping time.
- **Bitter peel covers the tea aroma**: the lemon was pressed too hard, the white pith was crushed, or it sat in the tea too long. Both cutting and pressing speed up the release of aroma and bitterness (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)); slice and seed the lemon, add it only when filling the glass, and put the spoon on the fruit and press lightly just until juice comes out.
- **Weak but not cold right after pouring**: the tea was still hot and melted too much ice. Cool the tea before filling the glass, and keep the full 200 g of ice; once the ice melts, the tea turns cool and weak, and extra lemon juice cannot bring back the tea flavour (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
