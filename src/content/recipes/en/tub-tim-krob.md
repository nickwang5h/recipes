The "rubies" in *tub tim krob* are diced water chestnuts dyed red. They are coated in tapioca starch, and in boiling water the starch layer absorbs water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) into a clear, soft, springy skin. When you bite in, the water chestnut inside is still crisp, and the starch skin has to be thin so the two textures stay distinct.

The syrup and coconut milk are cooked and cooled separately, and are only combined in the bowl. Pandan leaf slowly [releases its fragrance](/principles/aromatics/) into the water and the coconut milk, and the small amount of salt in the coconut milk brings out the sweetness. The rubies are best cooked and assembled the same day; the syrup and coconut milk can be made ahead, but leave the starch skins until just before serving.

## Steps

1. Put 360 ml of water and 1 knotted pandan leaf in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 4–6 minutes until it boils. Then simmer over low heat for 10 minutes, until the water clearly smells of the leaf. Add the white sugar and stir for about 1 minute until the sugar crystals disappear. Lift out the leaf.
2. In another pot, put the coconut milk, 60 ml of water, the salt and the other pandan leaf. Heat over medium-low heat for about 3–5 minutes, until bubbles form around the edge. Then simmer for 5 minutes at a gentle boil and lift out the leaf.
3. Pour the two liquids into separate shallow containers. Cool for about 20 minutes, until they no longer give off obvious steam, then refrigerate for about 1 hour, until both the containers and the liquids are cold.
4. Cut the water chestnuts into dice of about 1 cm. Stir in the water for colouring and the liquid food colouring and soak for about 10 minutes, until the cut surfaces are evenly red. Drain off the red water and keep the surface damp; do not dry them with paper towels.
5. Sprinkle in the tapioca starch in two portions, tossing gently each time so every side of the water chestnuts is covered in white starch and they stay separate. Tip them into a sieve and shake off the loose excess starch. Do not soak them in water again before they go in the pot.
6. Bring 2000 ml of water to a rolling boil over high heat, about 8–12 minutes. Meanwhile put the cooling water and 200 g of ice cubes into a large bowl. Cook the rubies in two batches, about 2–3 minutes each, and 1 more minute after they float.
7. Lift out 1 ruby and dip it in the ice water for a few seconds to check that the starch skin is clear with no large white starch core. If there is still a white core, let that batch in the pot cook 1 more minute. When cooked through, lift them into the ice water and soak for about 2 minutes, until the surface is cool and the skin has set, then drain. Treat the second batch the same way.
8. Divide the rubies among four bowls. Divide the syrup and the coconut milk into four portions each and pour them over. Add 50 g of crushed ice to each bowl, stir gently and eat right away.

## Key points

- **The starch skin is white and tastes of raw starch**: the starch layer has not gelatinized through. Floating does not mean done; lift one out, cool it and look at how clear it is. If the skin is thick, cook 1 more minute. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Several pieces stick into a clump and the skin is heavy**: the water chestnuts were not drained well or the excess starch was not sifted off, so wet starch joins the pieces and gelatinizes together. Drain off any pooled water before coating, sift after coating, and add to the boiling water in batches. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The skin turns hard after refrigeration**: gelatinized starch retrogrades. Refrigerate only the syrup and coconut milk, and cook and assemble the rubies on the day. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
