*Tiramisù* alternates coffee-moistened ladyfingers with mascarpone cream, topped with unsweetened cocoa. This version follows the registered recipe of Le Beccherie in Treviso: the cream is egg yolks, sugar and mascarpone, with a rich, soft texture. Whipping the yolks brings in a little air, and they are then mixed with the cheese so the fat and water stay bound together (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

The ladyfingers take in coffee, and keep drawing water from the cream in the fridge. They do not need to be soft all the way through right after dipping; a little dry core in the centre leaves room for the moisture that arrives later. Chilling makes the cut faces neater, but ladyfingers that were soaked too wet cannot be rescued by chilling. This cold dessert is not heated, so the yolks must be pasteurized.

## Steps

1. Brew strong coffee, pour it into a shallow bowl and let it cool completely. Choose a dish that holds two layers of ladyfingers and test-fit dry ladyfingers first: 15 per layer, filling gaps with broken pieces.
2. Put the pasteurized egg yolks and sugar in a bowl and whisk until the colour is clearly paler and the volume has increased, and the batter falling from the whisk leaves a short-lived ribbon on the surface. Mix the sugar into the yolks promptly so it does not clump in places.
3. Add the mascarpone to the yolk mixture in batches and mix with a spatula or at low speed until even, soft and free of lumps of cheese. Stop once everything is combined and do not keep whipping at high speed; the cream should spread, with no liquid separating at the bottom of the bowl.
4. Turn each ladyfinger over once quickly in the cold coffee and take it out at once, then lay it down as the first layer. Wet surfaces are enough; do not press them into the coffee to soften. They should still hold together when lifted.
5. Spread about half of the cream on top and gently push it level with a spatula. Lay a second layer of quickly dipped ladyfingers, spread the remaining cream over it so that it covers the edges of the ladyfingers, and smooth the top.
6. Sift a thin layer of cocoa over the top, cover and refrigerate for at least 3 hours. When you take it out, the cream should be fairly firm with no pooled coffee at the bottom of the dish. Slice with a clean knife and wipe the blade after each cut.

## Key points

- **Cream is grainy and weeping**: the cheese was overbeaten, or the temperatures were too different when combined. Keep the ingredients cool, mix in batches at low speed and stop once smooth; once the fat and water have clearly separated, chilling cannot bring the emulsion back. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **Coffee runs out when cut and the ladyfingers collapse**: they were dipped too long, or the coffee was still hot. Use cold coffee, turn them quickly and take them out, and leave the further softening to the fridge; ladyfingers that have already turned to mush are best replaced. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Yolks are not heated**: neither whipping nor chilling kills the pathogens in raw eggs. This cold dessert must use pasteurized yolks; keep even carton products chilled as the label says, and return the dessert to the fridge as soon as it is made. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
