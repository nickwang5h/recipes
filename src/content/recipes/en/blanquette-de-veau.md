*Blanquette de veau* is a pale veal stew: the meat is first simmered gently in broth, and mushrooms and small onions are served in a creamy white sauce. The connective tissue in veal shoulder needs time to soften (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). The meat is not browned and the butter and flour are cooked only until bubbling, so the finished dish keeps a mild meaty flavour.

The white sauce thickens in two stages. First a velouté is made from the cooking broth and a white roux, using [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) as the base; then egg yolks and cream finish it, making the sauce finer, smoother and richer. At the end, stir the hot sauce into the yolks a little at a time, then pour it back into the pot and heat over low heat.

## Steps

1. Cover the meat with cold water by about 5 cm, heat slowly to a bare simmer and cook for about 2 minutes. Lift out, quickly rinse off the clinging scum and wash the pot.
2. Return the meat to the pot and add chicken stock to cover by about 1 cm. When it simmers, skim off all the scum, add the clove-studded onion, carrot and herb bundle, and simmer gently with the lid partly on for about 75–90 minutes, until a fork slides easily into a piece of meat and the connective tissue is soft.
3. Take 120 ml broth from the meat pot and put it in a small pot with the pearl onions and 15 g butter. Cover and simmer gently for about 30–40 minutes, until the centres of the onions are soft and the surface still white, and leave them in their liquid.
4. Lift out the cooked meat and strain the broth, discarding the vegetables and herb bundle used for simmering the meat. In another pot melt the butter for the roux, add the flour and stir over low heat for about 2 minutes, until evenly bubbling and still pale.
5. Off the heat, stir in 770 ml of the meat broth in several additions; once smooth, return to the heat, bring to a boil and simmer gently for about 10 minutes. Add the mushrooms tossed with lemon juice and cook another 10 minutes or so, until the mushrooms are cooked and the sauce coats the back of a spoon; return the meat and pearl onions and season with salt, white pepper and the remaining lemon juice.
6. Mix the egg yolks and cream, scoop in about 240 ml of the hot sauce a little at a time while stirring constantly. Pour back into the meat pot and push gently over the lowest heat until the sauce is smooth and the whole dish reaches 74°C (165°F), then take it off the heat; do not let bubbles of a boil appear at the edge. Serve at once, with rice or mashed potatoes if you like.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but still hard to chew**: the collagen in the shoulder has not softened enough. Keep it at a gentle simmer and test the thickest piece with a skewer; do not use fierce heat to save time. See [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **White sauce has lumps or a raw-flour taste**: the flour was not dispersed, or the sauce was not cooked through after the broth went in. Stir the roux well, add the broth in stages, and cook until both flavour and texture are smooth. See [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Specks of cooked egg at the end**: the yolks met the hot sauce suddenly, or the sauce boiled after going back in the pot, so the protein set in places. Stir the hot sauce in slowly, and take the temperature while stirring after it goes back, stopping when it reaches temperature. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
