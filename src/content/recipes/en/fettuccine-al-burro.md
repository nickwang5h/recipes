*Fettuccine al burro* is a common, simple Italian white-sauce pasta: soft, eggy noodles, each one thinly coated in a creamy sauce. You toss hot egg fettuccine with butter and Parmigiano. Don't use so much butter that it pools on the plate, and grate the cheese fine so it spreads quickly through the hot noodles.

The smoothness of the sauce comes from [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/). Tossing breaks the melted butter into fine droplets that spread through the water the noodles carry into the bowl. The cheese supplies protein, and the starch in the pasta water helps hold the fat in place. While the pasta cooks, its starch swells with water and part of it dissolves into the water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The noodles must be hot and the bowl warm, but once the cheese is in the bowl, it does not go back on the heat.

## Steps

1. Cut the butter into small pieces and let it soften. Grate the cheese to a fine powder and fluff it up. Warm a large bowl, big enough to toss all the noodles easily, with hot water, then pour the water out and wipe any water from the bottom.
2. Bring the pasta water to a boil, add salt, then add the egg fettuccine and gently separate the strands. Cook for the time on the package (fresh fettuccine usually takes about 2–4 minutes). Taste a strand early, and cook until it is soft and springy with no raw flour taste in the centre. Fresh pasta cooks quickly, so have everything for tossing ready first.
3. Scoop out 1 cup of the pasta water and set it aside. Lift the noodles into the warm bowl; they don't need to be shaken completely dry, and don't rinse them in cold water. Right away, sprinkle in about a quarter of the cheese and toss quickly from the bottom up, so the cheese clings evenly to the hot noodles.
4. Add half the butter and the second portion of cheese, and keep tossing until the butter melts. Then toss in the remaining cheese, and last the remaining butter. Lift and drop the noodles again and again until every strand is glossy.
5. If the sauce looks dry, add 1 tbsp of warm pasta water at a time, toss, and look again. Stop when the sauce coats the noodles in a thin layer and there is no loose water or floating fat at the bottom of the bowl. Divide into warm plates at once, and put the cheese on the table.

## Key points

- **The cheese clumps into stringy balls**: some spots are too hot, or too much cheese went in at once, so the cheese proteins bunch together. Use a warmed large bowl for the hot noodles, add the fine cheese in portions while tossing, and don't cook the sauce over the burner. Once hard clumps form, they are hard to toss back out. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **Fat pools on the plate and the noodles are dry**: there is too little water phase, so the butter hasn't spread evenly. Add 1 tbsp of pasta water, toss quickly, and stop once the shine returns. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **The sauce turns into a thick paste and the noodles stick in a clump**: the noodles have absorbed the water, and cooling tightens the texture. While the noodles are still hot, loosen with a little warm pasta water and eat right away; warmed plates slow the cooling. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
