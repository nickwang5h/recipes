Ma lai go is a brown sugar steamed cake common in Cantonese teahouses, soft and springy when cut, with an egg and evaporated milk fragrance. Traditional restaurants use a fermented starter, and the cut face shows longer air channels. This home version uses whole eggs beaten up and baking powder, and the crumb is finer.

The batter first holds in air. When it is heated, the gas expands, and the [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) of the eggs sets the shape. Cake flour keeps the cake tender. Once the flour is in, fold it through and stop, because over-mixing builds up the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) and makes the texture tough.

## Steps

1. Leave the eggs and the measured evaporated milk on the counter for 15 minutes, until the containers no longer feel cold. For a bamboo steamer about 23 cm across, line the bottom and sides with baking paper, with the paper edge a little higher than the batter, leaving a path for steam on the outside.
2. Put the eggs, brown sugar and vegetable oil in a bowl and beat with an electric mixer on high speed for 5 minutes, until the sugar lumps have dispersed, the batter is paler and larger in volume and the surface is covered with fine bubbles.
3. Add the evaporated milk, vanilla extract and custard powder and beat for 1 minute more, until the custard powder is dispersed and there is no powder settled at the bottom. Mix the cake flour, baking powder and salt, sift them in and fold from the bottom of the bowl upward with a spatula, stopping when no large patches of dry flour remain.
4. Cover the batter and rest it for 30 minutes, until it thickens slightly, there are fewer flour lumps and fine bubbles come up on the surface. Fold a few times gently with a spatula to mix the bottom and top, without pressing out the bubbles that have formed.
5. Pour the batter into the lined steamer and leave for 10 minutes so the surface levels itself. Meanwhile bring the water in the steamer pot to a boil, which takes about 8–10 minutes, until steam is rising steadily. The water level must be below the base of the steamer, so boiling water does not touch the batter.
6. Once the water is boiling, put the steamer on, cover and steam over medium-high heat for 30 minutes, with steam going steadily. When the time is up, insert a bamboo skewer into the centre. It is done when it comes out without wet batter and the cake springs back when lightly pressed. If there is still wet batter, steam for 5 minutes more.
7. Move the cake, still in the steamer, to a rack and cool for 10 minutes, until the surface moisture has gone and the cake can be cut cleanly. Lift out the lining paper, cut into 8 pieces and serve while warm.

## Key points

- **The cake is low and tight at the bottom**: not enough air was beaten in, or it was over-mixed after sifting in the flour. Beat up fine bubbles first, then fold the dry ingredients lightly so the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) does not become too strong.
- **The centre is sticky and wet and sinks after steaming**: the inside did not set, or the steam ran short partway. Following the path of [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), check the water in the pot and the steam channels, and take it out only when the skewer is clean. Do not just pile the batter thicker when you scale up.
- **Wet dents on the surface**: condensation from the lid dripped onto the cake, steam condensing on a cold surface (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Tilt the lid outward when you lift it so the water runs outside the pot, and leave room between the steamer and the lid for the cake to rise.
