*Minestrone* is a thick Italian vegetable soup, and its vegetables change with the region and the season. This version is from Romagna: onion, carrot, celery, potato and beans are cooked slowly together, with tomato added only as a light background note. The vegetables keep their shape in the soup, but the texture is soft, thick and silky.

The vegetables go into the pot in batches and are sweated in oil first, so their aroma has time to build (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Over the long cooking, starch from the potatoes cooks into the broth and the beans thicken it too (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cook until the vegetables and the broth have melded and nothing is left crisp.

## Steps

1. Cut all the vegetables. Cook the white beans separately until they crush easily; beans must be fully cooked before they meet the tomato. Have a heavy-bottomed pot ready that is large enough for all the vegetables.
2. Put the olive oil, butter and onion in the pot and cook over medium-low heat until the onion is soft and pale golden at the edges. Stir often so the onion touches the bottom evenly, since thin edges burn first.
3. Add the carrot and cook for about 2–3 minutes. Then add the celery, potato and green beans one at a time, stirring each for about 2–3 minutes until it is coated in oil and starting to soften before adding the next.
4. Add the zucchini and cook for another 2–3 minutes. Then add the cabbage and cook until the shreds collapse and the volume in the pot clearly shrinks.
5. Pour in the tomatoes, stock and water, and add the cheese rind if you have one. Bring to a boil, turn the heat to low, leave the lid slightly open and simmer slowly for about 2.5 hours. The surface should only bubble here and there. Stir from the bottom now and then, and add hot water if the soup reduces too fast.
6. Add the cooked white kidney beans and cook for about 30 minutes more, until the vegetables are very soft and the soup is thick but still flows. Lift out the cheese rind, stir in the grated cheese, then taste and add salt. Served up, the bowl should be mostly vegetables with the soup filling the gaps.

## Key points

- **Beans stay hard after long cooking**: dried beans that meet tomato before they are soft slow down, because acid delays softening (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). Cook them through separately first. If you use canned cooked beans, drain them and weigh them as cooked beans.
- **Thin soup, with each vegetable tasting separate**: the slow cooking and concentrating did not go on long enough (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep the heat low and the lid slightly open until the potato corners go loose and the soup has body.
- **Bitter base**: the onion or another vegetable scorched (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Stir after each addition, and lower the heat if the bottom of the pot looks dry.
