Stir-fried cabbage, eggs and glass noodles gives you crisp-sweet cabbage shreds, tender egg curds and soft, springy noodles in one dish. The noodles are soaked, then blanched until they separate, so afterwards they only need a short toss to take up the sauce (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). For noodles soaked in cold water, bite one open and check the centre; do not judge only by whether it bends.

Cook the eggs just until set and take them out, then fold them back into the sauce at the end. They get one less round of heat, so they stay tender (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Carrot is firmer than cabbage, so cut it finer and put it in first; that way both vegetables are just cooked at about the same time (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Mix the sauce before it goes in the pan, so you do not have to stop and measure seasonings mid-stir-fry.

## Steps

1. Soak the glass noodles in cold water for about 1 hour, until they bend and the strands in a bundle loosen. Wash and drain the cabbage, remove the hard core and cut into fine shreds; cut the carrot finer, and cut the green onion, garlic and dried chilies into small pieces or thin slices.
2. Blanch the soaked noodles in boiling water until soft, separating them as they cook. Taste one: when there is no hard centre and it still has some chew, lift them out and drain. Cut any that are too long so they are easy to toss.
3. Crack the eggs into a bowl, add a small pinch from the 2 g total salt and beat for about 15 seconds, until yolks and whites are blended. Separately mix the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and oyster sauce, and keep the remaining salt to taste at the end.
4. Add half the canola oil to the pan, pour in the eggs and push them apart as soon as the edges set. Cook until there is no runny egg and the curds are still soft, then take them out; do not leave them in the pan to dry out.
5. Add the remaining oil, then the green onion, garlic and dried chilies and stir-fry for about 8 seconds, until fragrant and the garlic and chilies have not blackened. Finely cut aromatics call for a lower heat.
6. Add the carrot shreds first and toss, then add the cabbage. Stir-fry together for about 30 seconds, until the shreds are slightly softened and evenly glossy but still crisp; if the thicker shreds are still hard, keep cooking until just cooked before adding the noodles.
7. Add the soft, drained noodles and pour in the mixed sauce at once, tossing with chopsticks and the spatula. The noodles should take on an even colour and separate strand by strand, with neither a pool of liquid in the bottom of the pan nor dry, burnt, sticky patches.
8. Return the eggs to the pan and toss for about 15 seconds to mix the curds with the noodles and vegetables. Taste, add the remaining salt if needed and plate immediately, because residual heat keeps tightening the egg.

## Key points

- **Noodles clump or have a hard centre**: blanched too soft they clump, not blanched through they stay hard after stir-frying (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Blanch until there is no hard centre but still some chew, drain and loosen them before they go in the pan.
- **Dry, hard eggs**: they were cooked too long both the first time and when returned, and the protein network kept tightening (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take them out as soon as they are cooked, and toss for just a few seconds at the end before serving.
- **Cabbage is cooked but carrot is still hard**: the cut size and the order of adding are wrong (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut the carrot finer and stir-fry it first; after about 30 seconds check whether the shreds are just cooked instead of only counting time.
