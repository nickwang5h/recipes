*Tod mun pla* are small red-curry fish cakes with a springy bite, with fine rings of long bean and shreds of makrut lime leaf inside. In Thailand they are usually made with featherback fish (*pla krai*); in Canada, tender basa works too. The fish and the salty curry paste are blended into a sticky paste, and when heated the proteins link into a network, which is what lets the cakes hold their shape (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

Blend the paste fine and sticky, so it holds a mound on a spoon, then fold in the beans and herbs. Remove the tough vein from the makrut lime leaf and cut it very fine, so the fragrance spreads evenly (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Shape the cakes thin before they go into hot oil: by the time the surface sets, the centre is cooked through (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). This makes about 18 cakes, with the sweet-sour cucumber sauce in a small bowl on the side.

## Steps

1. Blend the garlic, red chili, 100 g of sugar, the salt, the vinegar and 45 ml of water for the dipping sauce into a liquid with tiny flecks of chili. Pour into a small pot, bring to a boil over medium heat for about 2 minutes, then cook over low heat for 3–5 minutes, until it is like a thin syrup that lightly coats the back of a spoon. Take off the heat and let cool for about 15 minutes, until it stops steaming, and use this time to make the fish paste.
2. Press the fish dry and cut into small pieces. Put them in a food processor with the red curry paste, egg yolk and 1 tsp of sugar. Blend for about 1 minute, until no fish pieces are visible, and scrape down the sides; then blend in short bursts for 2–3 minutes more, until the paste is fine and sticky, holds a small mound when scooped, and the bowl is still cool.
3. Scrape the fish paste into a bowl and mix in the long bean rings, the shredded basil and the shredded makrut lime leaf. Mix just until the additions are evenly spread.
4. Pour the frying oil into a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 4–6 minutes. First fry a small test piece of paste for 1 minute, until bubbles keep rising around it as it goes in and the surface sets, and a cut piece shows no translucent paste and reads 74°C (165°F) in the centre. Taste it, then decide whether to mix the fish sauce into the whole bowl of paste.
5. Wet the spoon and your hands with cold water, scoop up 1 heaping tablespoon of paste and pat it in your palm into a small cake about 5 cm wide and 1 cm thick. Lower each into the oil as you shape it, 4–5 per batch, with no overlapping.
6. Fry each batch for about 3–4 minutes, turning partway, until both sides are golden brown and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), and a cut piece shows no wet raw paste. Lift out and drain for 30–60 seconds, until they stop dripping oil. Before the next batch, let the oil heat back up for about 1 minute, until a small test dab of paste bubbles quickly again.
7. Toss the cucumber slices with the cooled sweet-sour sauce, plate the fish cakes and serve the dipping sauce in a separate bowl. Lay the cakes out in a single layer and eat while the surface is still dry and crisp.

## Key points

- **Paste is loose and will not shape into cakes**: the thawing water was not pressed out, or the fish was only chopped and not blended until sticky. Press the fish dry first, then blend until it holds a mound (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Burnt outside, raw and wet inside**: the cakes are too thick, or the oil is too hot. Pat into thin cakes and fry in batches; do not judge by the colour of the crust alone (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Tough shreds of leaf in every bite**: the central vein of the makrut lime leaf was not removed, or the shreds were cut too coarse. Stack the leaves, roll them tightly and slice very fine so the fragrance spreads through the paste (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
