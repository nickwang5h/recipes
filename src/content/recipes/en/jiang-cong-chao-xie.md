Ginger and green onion crab has sweet crab meat in a thin sauce scented with ginger and green onion. Chopped raw sea crab is stir-fried briefly with ginger slices and large pieces of green onion, then covered and braised with a little Shaoxing wine. The ginger and the white part of the green onion are fried in the oil first, and the crab pieces pick up that fragrance. The green part goes in last to keep its fresh smell; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

The cut faces of the crab are dusted thinly with flour and seared until the flour sticks to the meat, then the crab goes back to the pan for braising. The flour meets the wine and the crab juices and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) into a thin sauce that clings to the pieces at the end. Covering the pan lets steam reach the base of the legs and the claws, but doneness still has to be judged at the thickest parts; see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).

## Steps

1. Use crab killed that day and ask the store to open the shell and clean it. Before cooking, remove the gills, stomach sac and mouthparts, and check for loose bits of shell. Rinse quickly and pat dry. Keep the top shells. Cut each crab body in half, and crack each big claw once so steam can get in.
2. Dust the large cut faces of the crab pieces with a thin layer of flour and shake off the loose flour. Set the ginger, green onion and all the seasonings next to the stove, with the white and green parts of the green onion separate. The meat exposed at the cut of the body should carry a thin coat of flour, with no wet, thick batter on the surface.
3. Preheat the wok over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is dry. Add 4 tbsp of the oil and swirl for about 15 seconds, until the oil runs freely with no heavy smoke. Put the crab in cut side down, with the claws and top shells around it, and sear for about 30 seconds, until the flour coating is stuck on and the cut faces have set slightly. Turn the pieces over and remove them from the wok.
4. Pour out the oil left in the wok and wipe away loose flour, then add 2 tbsp of oil. Over medium-high heat, fry the ginger slices for about 15 seconds, until the edges turn slightly yellow, then add the white part of the green onion and stir-fry for about 15 seconds, until the pieces bubble and smell fragrant.
5. Return the crab to the wok and stir-fry over high heat for about 15 seconds, until the pieces sizzle again. Pour the Shaoxing wine in along the edge of the wok, cover at once, turn to medium heat and braise for about 3 minutes, until the shells turn red, the meat at the base of the legs turns white and a little sauce is left in the bottom of the wok.
6. Lift the lid and check the thickest leg joints and the cracked claw meat. The meat should be opaque and come away from the shell, and it is only cooked when the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). If it is not done, cover again and braise 1 minute at a time, checking after each. Measure the thick claws on their own; do not go by the shell colour alone.
7. Add the green part of the green onion, the light soy sauce, sugar and white pepper, and stir-fry over high heat for about 10 seconds, until the green onion has just softened and the sauce looks glossy and coats the crab thinly. If the bottom of the wok still has thin liquid, stir-fry for another 30 seconds, until no water pools, then plate at once.

## Key points

- **Shell is red but the claw meat is still translucent**: the inside of a thick claw heats slowly, so the shell colour says nothing about the centre (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut the body open, crack the big claws, and check each thick part.
- **Sauce is too thick and the cut faces are coated in lumps**: the crab was not dried well, or the flour was too thick (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Dust only the large cut faces, thinly, shake off the loose flour, and do not add a thick starch slurry.
- **Ginger and green onion turn black and bitter, and the burnt smell covers the aroma**: the empty wok sat too long, or the crab did not go in right after the aromatics were fried (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Measure everything out beforehand and add the crab as soon as you smell the aroma.
