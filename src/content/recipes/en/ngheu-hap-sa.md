*Nghêu hấp sả* uses the clean fragrance of lemongrass to carry the salty savouriness of clams. The clams go straight into a covered pot with a little water and cooks them with steam and the juices the clams release. Thai sweet basil is often added at the end, and the pot comes off the heat as soon as the leaves have just softened, which keeps their fragrance.

The lemongrass is cut open and stir-fried briefly first, so its aroma reaches the oil and steaming liquid in the pot more easily (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The lid keeps the steam in, so heat reaches the shells on top (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The water only needs to be enough to start the steam. The clams release more liquid as they open, so the finished dish should have a layer of rich, savoury broth.

## Steps

1. Check the clams. Discard any with broken shells and any live clams that do not close after a light tap. Scrub the shells clean, rinse, then drain for 2 minutes, until the shells stop dripping. Purged clams can be used as they are. While waiting to cook, keep them in the refrigerator under a damp cloth, in a container that can breathe.
2. Prepare the aromatics. Peel off the loose dry layers from the lemongrass, cut off the hard root end and the woody top, and slice the remaining tender stalks thinly on the diagonal. Lightly smash the garlic, slice the chilies into rings, and pick, wash and drain the basil leaves for 2 minutes, until there are no drops of water on the leaves.
3. Stir-fry the lemongrass. Heat the oil in a wide, deep pot over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily and is not yet smoking. Add the lemongrass and garlic and stir-fry for 30–45 seconds, until the fresh aroma is strong and the garlic is still pale.
4. Steam the clams. Add the clams, the 30 ml water and the optional chilies, toss and cover. Heat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until steam keeps rising from the pot, then keep steaming for 4–9 minutes, until the shells open and the meat is opaque. Toss once at about 3 minutes so the top and bottom layers trade places. Discard any that stay shut after the full steaming time.
5. Finish and plate. Add the optional basil and toss for about 15 seconds, until the leaves have just softened, then turn off the heat. Taste a spoonful of the broth and add the listed salt if needed, then serve with the broth. If there is grit at the bottom of the pot, lift the clams out first, then pour the clear broth from the top into the dish and leave the sediment behind.

## Key points

- **The meat has shrunk and is rubbery**: it kept boiling hard after it was cooked, so the proteins contracted and lost water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose clams of similar size and plate them right after steaming; do not leave them in the hot pot to reduce.
- **The top layer won't open while the bottom layer has dried out**: the pot is too narrow or the steam too weak, so heating is uneven (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a wide pot that holds the clams comfortably, cover it tightly and toss once during steaming.
- **The lemongrass tastes burnt and bitter**: the thin slices were fried too long in the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the clams and water as soon as you smell the fragrance, and don't wait for the garlic to turn dark brown.
