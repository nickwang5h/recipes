*Rau muống xào tỏi* is the stir-fried garlic greens of the everyday Vietnamese table. Tender water spinach keeps its leaves and thin stems, seasoned with garlic, oyster sauce and a little sugar, and it goes with plain rice and fish dishes. The good state is stems that bite through easily and keep a little crunch, with leaves cooked soft and thinly coated in garlic oil.

On a home stove, blanch the greens briefly before they go into the wok. Stems and leaves cook at different speeds, so treating them separately takes care of both (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Garlic slices release their fragrance in the oil and the greens added afterwards take it up (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Drain off the water left after blanching, or the wok spends its time boiling that water off and dilutes both the garlic oil and the oyster sauce.

## Steps

1. Prepare the water spinach. Pinch off tough stems that still pull long fibres when bent, keep the tender leaves and stems, and cut them into pieces about 8 cm long. Break apart the forks and rinse off the grit, slice the garlic thin, and set the oyster sauce and sugar by the stove. Tender stems snap easily by hand; throw away thick, hard ones.
2. Bring the blanching water to a boil. Put 2000 ml of water in a pot and heat over high heat for about 6–8 minutes, until it boils constantly. Leave room in the pot to stir; if the water is too high, use a bigger pot so it doesn't overflow when the greens go in.
3. Blanch the water spinach briefly. Put it in the boiling water and stir so the leaves and stems are all submerged. Blanch for 1–2 minutes, until the leaves are soft and the tender stems bend but still have some crunch. Lift into a strainer and drain for about 3 minutes, until a handful held up no longer drips. If the thickness varies a lot, add the thick stems 30 seconds first, then the leaves and tender tips.
4. Bring out the garlic aroma. Heat the oil in a wide wok over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until it spreads easily and is not yet smoking. Add the garlic and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until fragrant and just pale yellow at the edges. With the greens drained ahead of time, they can go in right away once the garlic reaches this state.
5. Stir-fry the water spinach. Add the drained greens and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the leaves and stems are evenly coated in garlic oil and the whole pan is hot again. Use tongs or a spatula to shake the greens loose so they touch the pan, and don't press them into a mass that steams.
6. Season and serve. Add the oyster sauce and sugar and keep tossing for 1–2 minutes, until the sugar dissolves, the leaves are glossy and there is no large pool of liquid on the bottom. Taste a stem with leaf, add the listed salt and pepper if needed, and plate right away, to eat with [Đậu Sốt Cà Chua](/recipes/dau-sot-ca-chua/) and plain rice.

## Key points

- **The stems are tough and the leaves are mushy**: tough old stems were used, or stems and leaves were not given separate heating times (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Pinch off the stringy hard parts, blanch the thick stems first, and add the leaves later.
- **A pool of water on the plate and a bland taste**: the greens were not drained after blanching, or the wok was overfilled (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Shake the greens loose before they go in; if the wok is small, cook the oil, greens and seasonings in two batches.
- **The garlic is bitter and the greens taste scorched**: the garlic waited in hot oil too long for the greens (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Finish washing, blanching and draining first, then turn on the wok.
