Pork with celery and pressed tofu has three textures: tender pork slices, chewy pressed tofu that soaks up the sauce, and crisp celery. The three cook at different speeds, so they go into the pan in order: the pork first, then the tofu to take in the sauce, with the green pepper and celery saved for last. The thick celery stalks are first split lengthwise and then cut into short sections; with a shorter distance for heat to reach the centre, they cook as quickly as the other ingredients (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

Thin Chinese celery can go straight into a short stir-fry, while thick celery is blanched before it goes into the pan (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Pressed tofu is already cooked and only needs to be heated through; the pork must come out as soon as it is cooked, because if it is stir-fried too long its [proteins denature and coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) too tightly, and the meat turns dry and tough. So cut all the vegetables first, then heat the pan.

## Steps

1. Remove the leaves from the celery and cut it into sections of no more than 4 cm, first splitting the thick stalks lengthwise into thinner pieces. Cut the pressed tofu into strips as wide as a pinky finger, slice the garlic and cut the green peppers into strips. Cut the pork across the grain into thin slices of similar thickness and blot the surface dry.
2. If you are using thick celery, blanch it in boiling water for about 1 minute, until the centre is less raw and hard, then lift out, rinse in cold water and drain. Particularly hard pressed tofu can also be blanched separately until slightly soft; thin Chinese celery and softer pressed tofu do not need blanching.
3. Put 12 ml of oil in the pan and heat over medium heat until it flows easily. You can add the Sichuan peppercorns, then the garlic slices and stir-fry until fragrant. When the edges of the garlic have just turned light yellow, add the pork, and if the peppercorns have darkened, remove them so they do not keep scorching.
4. Spread out the pork slices and stir-fry until they have all turned white and separate from each other, then add 8 ml of dark soy sauce and toss. If the pork slices are cut fairly thick, take out one slice first to see whether it is cooked through.
5. Add the pressed tofu and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, so every side is coated with the sauce colour. Then add the green pepper and stir-fry for about 1–2 minutes, until the strips are heated through and slightly soft, with a little flowing sauce still in the bottom of the pan.
6. Add the celery and about 2 g of salt and stir-fry for about 1 minute. The thin stalks should be bright and bend yet remain crisp; blanched celery only needs to be heated evenly, so do not stir-fry it until it collapses. Lift and turn with the spatula throughout and press as little as possible, so you do not break the pressed tofu.
7. Add the oyster sauce, and the chicken bouillon powder if you like, and toss quickly. Taste a piece of celery and a strip of pressed tofu first, and if it is not salty enough add a little more salt. Plate right away once the pork is cooked, the pressed tofu is heated through and the celery is still crisp.

## Key points

- **Celery is soft outside and hard inside**: the thick stalks were not split, and a short stir-fry is not long enough to cook the centre (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Split thick celery lengthwise and blanch it ahead of time, and do not overcook the pork waiting for the centres to cook.
- **Celery is watery and the colour in the pan is diluted**: it was not drained after blanching (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)). Drain it well after lifting it out, wipe the surface of the vegetables dry as well, and add them to the pan last for a short stir-fry.
- **Pork is tough while the pressed tofu is soft**: the pork was cut thick or stayed in the hot pan too long (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). If the pressed tofu is too hard, blanch it soft first and slice the pork thin; when making a larger amount, stir-fry the pork in batches.
