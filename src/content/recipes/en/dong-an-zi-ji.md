Dong'an chicken is named for Dong'an in Hunan. The chicken is tender, the shredded ginger is hot and the rice vinegar is cleanly sour, with only a thin film of sauce on the plate. The chicken is first poached in water until mostly cooked, then pulled into skin-on strips and returned to the pan. The rice vinegar seasons the dish and also keeps the rich chicken from tasting greasy (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).

Poaching first and then stir-frying splits the cooking into two steps: the whole bird's heating, and the final seasoning. Once the chicken goes back in the pan it cooks quickly, and the proteins don't have time to keep tightening, so the meat doesn't turn dry (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). At the end, a little starch holds the sour sauce on the chicken (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)); the skin, ginger shreds and chili shreds all stay distinct.

## Steps

1. In a deep pot that holds the whole chicken, bring 4000 ml water to a boil, which takes about 12–15 minutes, until the surface is rolling. Add the chicken, the smashed ginger and 1 green onion, and boil over high heat for 2–3 minutes, until the water returns to a boil. Skim off the foam, then turn to low heat and cook for 10 minutes, until the surface of the chicken turns white and the skin is taut.
2. Lift the chicken onto a clean tray, drain and let it cool for 3–5 minutes, until you can handle it with gloves. Keep 60 ml of the poaching broth. The chicken is not fully cooked yet, so pull the meat and go straight to the pan in one continuous run.
3. Pull the skin-on meat from the bones and cut it along the grain into strips about 4 cm long and 1 cm wide. Cut the ginger, fresh chili and green onion greens into fine shreds of similar length. Stir the potato starch with 2 tsp cold water.
4. Add the lard to a wok and heat over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until the lard has melted and runs freely. Add the ginger shreds, fresh chili shreds and the optional dried chili, and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until fragrant and the chili shreds are still bright.
5. Add the chicken and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the surface is evenly coated in oil. Drizzle the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the wok, then add the rice vinegar, Sichuan pepper oil, salt and the reserved 60 ml of broth.
6. Boil over high heat for 1 minute, until the liquid bubbles, then turn to medium heat and keep turning and spooning the sauce over the chicken for 3–5 minutes, until the centre of the thickest piece reaches 74°C (165°F) and the cut surface is fully opaque. Only a shallow layer of sauce should remain; if it isn't cooked through, cook for 1 more minute and test again.
7. Stir the starch slurry again and pour it into the sauce at the bottom of the wok, stirring for 30 seconds, until the sauce thickens slightly and can cling to the meat. Add the green onion greens and stir-fry for 30 seconds more, until the shreds have just softened; take it off the heat, stir in the toasted sesame oil and plate immediately.

## Key points

- **Chicken is dry and the skin is torn**: the first poach was too long and the return to the pan cooked it for a long time too (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose a small young chicken, pull the meat as soon as it is poached, and finish as soon as it is cooked in the pan.
- **Sourness is heavy and the colour is dull**: the wrong vinegar was used (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Use clear, unseasoned rice vinegar so the ginger, chili and skin keep their original colours.
- **Thick paste on the plate**: too much starch was added, or the sauce wasn't reduced enough (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cook it down until only a shallow layer of sauce remains, then use half a teaspoon of starch to hold it lightly.
