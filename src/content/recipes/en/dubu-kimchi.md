Dubu kimchi puts warm plain tofu next to kimchi stir-fried with pork. The tofu keeps its light bean flavour, while the kimchi and pork belly are cooked to a rich, sour, spicy flavour, and you eat them together. In Korea it is both a home-style dish to go with rice and a common snack with drinks; the tofu only needs to be heated through and plated separately.

This dish suits ripe kimchi that is already clearly sour. The sourness from [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/) goes well with the fat of the pork belly, and the stir-fried stalks turn softer. In the pan, the meat and vegetables first release liquid; only after that evaporates does the sauce cling thickly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain the surface water from the tofu so it does not thin the sauce you have just reduced.

## Steps

1. Separate the pork slices one by one and cut them into pieces about 4 cm wide. Mix them with the kimchi, onion, green onion, ginger, garlic, soy sauce, sugar, toasted sesame oil, gochujang and black pepper. Cover and refrigerate for 15 minutes, until every side of the pork is coated with the red seasoning.
2. Put 1000 ml of water in a small pot and bring to a boil over high heat for about 5–7 minutes, until the surface is rolling. Cut the tofu into two large pieces and lower them into the water with a wide spatula, turn to medium heat and keep a gentle simmer.
3. Simmer the tofu gently for 5 minutes, until the whole block is heated through and still holds its shape when you shake the pot gently. Lift it out with a slotted spatula and drain for 2 minutes, until it no longer drips steadily, then cut into slices about 1 cm thick and arrange them at the edge of the plate. Do this step in between the stir-frying.
4. Preheat a large skillet over medium-high heat for 1 minute, until food sizzles when it goes in. Add the marinated meat and vegetables and spread them in a fairly thin layer; the sesame oil in the seasoning and the fat from the pork belly are enough.
5. Stir-fry for about 5–6 minutes, until the pork slices are fully separated, the kimchi stalks are soft and bent and the liquid in the pan has mostly cooked off. Insert a thermometer into a thicker slice of pork: at 71°C (160°F) it is done; if not, turn to medium heat and stir-fry 1–2 minutes more.
6. Spoon the stir-fried kimchi and pork next to the tofu and sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds. Once plated, there should be no obvious ring of water around the tofu, and the meat and vegetables should carry a thick sauce. Eat a slice of tofu with a bite of the meat and kimchi.

## Key points

- **Only heat, with no sour fragrance**: you used fresh kimchi that has not fermented enough. Choose ripe kimchi that is already clearly sour rather than adding more gochujang to make up for it. See [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/).
- **It turns into a pot of thin soup**: the kimchi brought in too much loose juice, or the pan was too small. Use kimchi with its clinging juice and do not add extra kimchi brine; stir-fry spread out in a wide pan, and reduce until a spatula dragged across the bottom no longer lets a pool of water flow back. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The meat is tough and the tofu is crumbled**: the pork was cut thick and stir-fried too long, and the tofu was moved around with it. Cut the pork slices to a uniform thinness and stop once it reaches 71°C (160°F) and the sauce is thick; heat the tofu gently on its own and move it by supporting it from underneath. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
