These sesame flatbreads are cooked in a flat pan. They are crisp outside and light inside, and they peel apart in layers, with fragrant white sesame seeds stuck to the surface. The dough is wrapped around the oil paste, rolled up and rolled thin, and the fat keeps neighbouring layers of dough apart, so the cooked bread peels into layers. Kneading and resting let the dough stretch (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Spread the oil paste evenly and keep the dough sheet an even thickness, so the layers form and the paste does not leak.

The rest is short. It mainly lets the water spread through and the dough relax, so it does not need to double in size. The yeast keeps working during shaping and the first part of cooking, but what you want here is thin, crisp layers. When cooking, use gentle heat first so the centre cooks, then brown the surface (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Put the yeast and sugar in the warm water and activate it as the yeast package directs. Knead it with the flour and oil into a dough. Knead until there is no dry flour and the surface is fairly smooth, then cover and rest for 10 minutes. Do not let the dough dry out and form a skin.
2. Stir the flour, salt, thirteen spice powder and oil for the oil paste together until there are no dry flour lumps and it is a paste you can spread. Set aside.
3. Roll the dough into a rectangle, as even in thickness as you can. Spread the oil paste over it evenly, leaving an edge you can pinch shut. Roll it up from one end, firmly, but without tearing the dough sheet.
4. Cut the roll into 7 equal pieces. Fold each piece in half, tuck the cut side that shows the oil paste inward, pinch the seam shut and shape it into a small round.
5. Rub a little water on one side of each round and press it into the white sesame seeds so they stick well. Roll gently into small rounds of even thickness. If the dough springs back as you roll, cover it and let it relax for a while.
6. Preheat a flat pan and brush it with a layer of oil. Lay in the flatbreads and brush a thin layer of oil on their tops. Cover and cook over medium-low heat until the underside is golden, then flip and keep cooking. Cook in batches and leave space between the flatbreads.
7. When both sides are golden, split one open and look at the centre. The layers should be cooked through with no wet, sticky raw dough, and the sesame seeds should smell toasty. If the centre is still sticky, lower the heat and keep cooking. If you use an electric griddle, choose the large-flatbread setting, and judge by the same signs after the beep.

## Key points

- **Shrinks back when rolled, or the dough tears easily**: the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) has not relaxed, or the surface has dried out. Cover and rest a little longer. Do not force it by dusting on a lot of flour.
- **Layers stick into one mass**: the oil paste was spread unevenly, so the dough layers fused (see [gluten and fat](/principles/gluten/)). Spread the paste as a thin, continuous layer, tuck the cut ends in, and roll gently at the end.
- **Burnt outside, wet and sticky inside**: the heat is too high or the flatbread too thick, so the centre heats slowly. Lower the heat and cook longer, following [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). The [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) only governs colour, so cut one open to see whether it is cooked.
