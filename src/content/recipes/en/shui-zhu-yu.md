For Sichuan boiled fish, the fish slices are poached in a seasoned broth, so both the fish and the vegetables pick up the numbing, spicy flavour. This is a lower-oil home version: the fish is marinated in doubanjiang, green Sichuan peppercorn oil provides the numbing aroma, and there is no egg white and starch coating and no hot oil poured at the end. Whether the fish is tender mainly depends on whether it keeps cooking after its [proteins coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/).

Cauliflower and lettuce cook at different speeds, so treat them separately first (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)). Doubanjiang and minced garlic are fried briefly in oil, and the water goes in as soon as you smell them, so the sauce does not fry to burnt bits (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Thaw the basa in the refrigerator and drain off the thawing liquid. Cut it on the diagonal into thin slices about 5 cm long and 3 cm wide, keeping the thickness similar, and don't mix thick chunks in with the thin slices.
2. Toss the fish gently with 30 g of doubanjiang, 10 ml of green Sichuan peppercorn oil and 3 g of white pepper, then mix in 5 ml of canola oil. Cover and marinate in the refrigerator for 30 minutes. The doubanjiang is already salty, so don't add other salt for now; mix just until the surface is evenly coated, and avoid breaking the fish up.
3. Blanch the cauliflower in boiling water until a chopstick can pierce the stem, then lift out and drain. In a separate skillet, stir-fry the drained lettuce until all the leaves have wilted and there are no raw stems, then spread both vegetables in the bottom of a large bowl.
4. Heat 20 ml of canola oil in a small pot, turn to medium heat, and fry the remaining 10 g of doubanjiang, the minced garlic and the optional fermented black beans. When the sauce has loosened, the oil has turned red and the garlic smells fragrant, pour in 500 ml of hot water and 2 g of sugar, and cook until the broth is boiling evenly.
5. Turn to low heat, add the fish slices one by one, and nudge them apart gently. If the pot is small, cook in two batches, making sure the broth can flow between the slices each time. Once the slices have set, only push them apart; don't stir repeatedly, and keep the broth at a light simmer.
6. Check the thickest slice. The fish should be completely opaque and separate into flakes with a gentle nudge. Lift each batch onto the vegetables as soon as it is cooked. Finally bring the broth to a boil, taste for salt, add a little if needed, then pour it over the fish and serve at once.

## Key points

- **Fish falls apart and the bottom of the bowl looks like fish porridge**: it was handled too hard in mixing, or stirred often in the pot, and set meat breaks easily (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Add the slices one by one, and nudge gently once they have set.
- **Fish is cooked outside and raw inside**: the thickness is uneven and the thick slices were not cooked through. [Blanch](/principles/blanching/) the vegetables until cooked first, poach the fish in batches, and take the thickest slice out only when it is done.
- **Broth too salty or bitter and burnt**: doubanjiang and fermented black beans add up in saltiness, and fine minced garlic burns easily (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Taste last before adding salt, and add water as soon as the red oil comes out.
