*Steak au poivre* has a spicy crust of pepper on the outside and the flavour of the meat inside, topped with a sauce made in the same pan from stock, cognac and butter. Crush the black pepper just before use and leave pieces of different sizes, so the aroma is released in the hot oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)); ground to a fine powder, it burns more easily.

Dry the surface of the steak first, so it can form a brown crust through the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The browned bits stuck to the pan also taste of meat, and dissolving them in liquid gives the sauce its meaty flavour. Butter goes in at the end off the heat, dispersing the fat into the reduced liquid (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The sauce should only coat the meat thinly, and the steak rests on a warm plate while you make it.

## Steps

1. Crush the black pepper. Pat the top and sides of the steaks dry, and press the pepper onto both sides. Cover and refrigerate for about 30 minutes; just before cooking, look at the surface and gently blot any drops of water.
2. Add the oil and butter for searing to a frying pan and heat over medium-high heat until the butter foam begins to subside. Put in the steaks with space between them. Sear for about 2–3 minutes to form a brown crust on the bottom, then turn and cook the other side for about 2–3 minutes; if the pepper gives off black smoke, lower the heat.
3. Lower the heat and keep cooking until the centre reaches at least 63°C (145°F), then move the steaks to a warm plate and sprinkle with salt. Insert the thermometer from the side into the centre of the meat; for mechanically tenderized steaks, turn them at least twice while cooking. While you make the sauce, the steaks rest on the plate for about 5 minutes.
4. Pour off the extra frying oil from the pan and keep the browned bits. Add half a tablespoon of butter and the shallot and cook over low heat for about 1 minute until soft, then pour in the stock, scrape loose the browned bits and cook until the liquid has clearly reduced.
5. Move the pan off the heat and pour in the pre-measured brandy, then return it to the burner for 1–2 minutes, until the sharp alcohol smell fades and the sauce begins to coat a spoon. Do not flame it, and leave a pourable sauce in the pan.
6. Turn off the heat and move the pan aside. Swirl in the finishing butter a little at a time, letting each batch melt into an even, glossy sauce. Taste, add salt, spoon the pan sauce over the steaks and serve right away.

## Key points

- **The steak is grey and there is a puddle in the pan**: the surface was too wet, or the pan was too crowded, so the heat went into evaporating water first (see [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Blot the meat dry, and if two steaks do not fit, use two pans or cook in batches.
- **The pepper is black and the sauce bitter**: fine pepper or too high heat burned the spice (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Use coarse pieces and lower the heat when it gives off black smoke; if there are black burnt bits in the pan, pour them out and make the sauce in a clean pan.
- **The sauce floats oil**: the butter went in while it was still boiling, or the sauce was reduced too dry (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Move off the heat, add a teaspoon of water and swirl again; keep some liquid when reducing, and add the finishing butter in small pieces.
