*LA galbi* is made from cross-cut short ribs, where each slice of meat has several flat sections of bone in it, and a marinade of pear, soy sauce, garlic and ginger gives a sweet-salty flavour. It is also a common grilled meat in Korea; when buying, look for the thin-slice cut, and leave thick-cut short ribs for [braised short ribs](/recipes/galbi-jjim/). Cutting thin shortens the distance heat has to travel to the centre of the meat (see [knife work](/principles/knife-work/)).

Marinating in the refrigerator lets salt move gradually from the surface into the slices (see [salt and curing](/principles/salt/)). When grilling, drain off the extra marinade so the surface moisture clears and gives the grilled aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Pear purée and sugar pile up at the bottom of the pan and scorch easily, and the meat needs only a thin coat of marinade.

## Steps

1. Put the short ribs in a deep bowl and rinse off the bone dust left from cutting gently under cold water, without letting the water splash on the counter. Check each slice at the bone edges, remove any loose bone chips, then blot the surface of the meat dry with paper towels.
2. Purée the pear, onion, garlic and ginger finely, and mix with the soy sauce, water, sugar, honey, mirin, toasted sesame oil, green onion and pepper until the sugar crystals dissolve and the purée is evenly spread in the liquid.
3. Coat the meat slice by slice with the marinade, lay flat in a lidded container and refrigerate for 8 hours. Turn once at the 4-hour mark, moving the top layer to the bottom; when done, each slice should be evenly moist, with no dry folded spots when you open it out. These 8 hours need to be planned ahead.
4. Preheat a heavy-bottomed grill pan over medium-high heat for 3–5 minutes, until a drop of water evaporates quickly. Lift out a batch of short ribs, let the extra marinade drip back into the container, scrape off the purée piled on the surface, and lay them in a single layer with gaps between.
5. Grill the first side for 2–3 minutes, until the edges are brown and the meat releases easily from the pan; flip and grill another 2–3 minutes, until both sides have brown marks and the meat next to the bone is cooked through. Cut into the thick part to check, and if it is still raw in texture, lower the heat and grill 1–2 minutes more.
6. Take out this batch, wipe the black sugar residue out of the pan, and reheat for 1–2 minutes, until the meat sizzles clearly when it goes in again, then grill the next batch. On a charcoal or gas grill, preheat for 10–15 minutes first, until the grate is fully hot, then work the same way in a single layer with the same turning times.
7. Use scissors to cut the cooked meat apart along the bones, sprinkle with the optional sesame seeds, and serve with lettuce and rice. Cut the meat next to the bone off the bone before wrapping it, and throw away the raw-meat marinade that is left.

## Key points

- **The meat is grey and looks boiled**: too much marinade came with the meat or the pan is crowded, and moisture holds down the surface temperature. Drain the marinade, cook in batches and spread the meat out so it touches the hot pan directly. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The sugar residue is black and the meat next to the bone is still raw**: purée has piled up or the slices are too thick. Buy cross-cut slices about 8 mm thick, wipe off the burnt residue, and lower the heat to finish cooking. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The outside is too salty and the inside bland**: more soy sauce was added to make up for too little marinating. Keep the full 8 hours in the refrigerator so the thin slices are evenly in contact with the marinade. See [salt and curing](/principles/salt/).
