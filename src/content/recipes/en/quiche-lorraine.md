*Quiche lorraine* is the savoury cream and bacon tart of Lorraine. The classic filling is eggs, cream and bacon, and once baked the cut surface is fine-textured and soft, with a bottom that is crisp rather than wet. Use a tart pan about 20 cm across and about 3 cm deep, so the custard is the right thickness and has room to puff up in the oven.

The pastry needs limited [gluten](/principles/gluten/) development: the butter coats the flour first, and after the water goes in you only work the dough until it comes together. The filling relies on [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) to hold the cream. The shell is blind-baked first so the bottom dries and sets; afterwards a slightly lower oven temperature lets the filling set slowly, so the outside does not dry out while the centre is still liquid.

## Steps

1. Rub the flour, salt and cold butter together with your fingertips into coarse sand, leaving small flakes of butter. Add 45 ml ice water first and gather with a scraper into a dough; if dry flour still will not bind, add the rest of the water bit by bit. Flatten, wrap and refrigerate at about 4°C (40°F) for at least 1 hour.
2. Roll the dough into a round about 3 mm thick and lay it in the 20 cm tart pan, pressing it lightly into the bottom and sides without stretching the dough. Trim the excess so the edge sits slightly above the rim; refrigerate the extra dough for another use, and chill the shell for another 15 minutes.
3. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Prick the bottom of the shell lightly with a fork, line it with parchment paper and fill with baking weights to hold up the edge. Bake for about 10 minutes until the sides are set, remove the paper and weights, and bake about 3 minutes more, until the bottom is dry and just starting to colour.
4. Simmer the bacon strips in plain water at a gentle boil for about 5 minutes, drain, rinse and pat dry. Lightly pan-fry in a skillet until the surface takes on a little colour, then remove and drain off the fat; this step removes some of the smokiness and salt, and the bacon should still be soft, not fried to hard, brittle bits.
5. Whisk the eggs, cream, nutmeg, pepper and a little salt together, without whipping. Spread the bacon over the bottom of the shell and pour in the custard until it comes about three quarters of the way up the shell, scatter 15 g of butter dice on top, and set the tart pan on a metal baking sheet.
6. Lower the oven to 190°C (375°F) and bake the tart on the upper-middle rack for about 25–30 minutes, until the edges are set and the centre trembles as one piece when gently shaken, with no ripples of liquid. Take it out when the centre reads 74°C (165°F); if the crust darkens first, cover the edge and keep baking the filling.
7. After it comes out, let it rest on a rack for about 10 minutes so the filling steadies before cutting. Once unmoulded, do not leave it on the hot baking sheet, or steam collects underneath; eat it warm with a fresh salad.

## Key points

- **Pastry shrinks at the edge and is tough**: the dough was worked too long, or the dough was stretched when lining the pan. Work cold, only gather it into a mass, and chill after rolling (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Soggy bottom with a raw dough layer**: the shell was not baked dry first, so the custard kept the bottom wet. Blind-bake until the bottom is dry and the sides are set before adding the filling (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Honeycomb texture, watery liquid when cut**: too much air was whisked into the custard, or it was baked too long and the protein network tightened. Mix only until combined, and take it out promptly once the centre is done (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
