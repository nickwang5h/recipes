Chive and egg pockets are half-moon pies, fried against the pan in a thin wrapper until golden, with chives, egg and shrimp inside. The wrapper is a hot-water dough: hot water lets part of the starch swell first and also limits [gluten](/principles/gluten/) formation, so the dough is soft and, rolled thin, cooks through easily. The eggs are scrambled into small curds before they go into the filling, and [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) turns the runny egg into a solid that can be wrapped.

With this many chives, the filling's enemy is water. Drain the chives well after washing, let the cooked egg and shrimp cool, and add the salt only at the moment of wrapping. A hot filling softens the chives, and salt added too early draws water out of them (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Wrapped pockets should be flat and full, with a clean ring of bare dough at the seam.

## Steps

1. Pour the hot water into the flour in a thin stream while stirring with chopsticks into shaggy flakes. Once it is cool enough to touch, knead into a soft, even dough. Cover and rest for 30 minutes, and don't let the surface dry into a skin.
2. Drain the chives thoroughly and chop them. Pat the shrimp dry and cut into small dice, and beat the eggs. Keep each one separate, and don't let the raw shrimp touch the chopped chives.
3. Using the oil set aside for the eggs, scramble the eggs into small curds that are just set, and take them out once there is no liquid egg. Then, using the oil set aside for the shrimp, stir-fry the diced shrimp until they are completely opaque in the centre, and take them out to cool.
4. Mix the chives, cooled egg curds and cooked shrimp with the sesame oil. Just before you start wrapping, add the salt and mix until even. The filling should be loose and moist, with no obvious pooling at the bottom of the bowl.
5. Divide the dough into 12 pieces and roll each into a thin round. Divide the filling evenly, put it on one half of each wrapper, fold over into a half-moon, press out large pockets of air and pinch the edge shut. If filling touches the seam, push it aside before pinching.
6. Fry the pockets in batches with the remaining oil. Cover the pan and fry the first side over medium-low heat; after about 3–4 minutes check the colour underneath, and when it is golden, flip. Keep frying until the other side is golden, with no wet, raw dough layer on the wrapper and the inside hot through.
7. Uncover and fry briefly to let the steam off the surface, then lift the pockets onto a plate. Cut one open first to see whether the wrapper is cooked and the filling is hot, and take out the later batches at that same state. Let them cool a little before eating.

## Key points

- **Filling leaks and the seam bursts**: the chives were wet or the salt went in too early, and water moving outward thinned the filling (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Season only at the moment of wrapping, and drain off any liquid that collects.
- **Wrapper shrinks back and won't roll out**: the tension in the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) hasn't relaxed yet. Cover it and wait a little longer, then roll thinner a bit at a time; don't keep dusting with flour until the soft dough turns stiff.
- **Burnt outside, gummy on the inner side of the wrapper**: the thick parts aren't cooked yet while the bottom has already taken colour. Spread the filling flat and press the seam thin, lower the heat and cover the pan so the heat reaches the inside; cooking the egg and shrimp beforehand makes frying easier to control (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
