In poulet basquaise the chicken is tender, and the sauce has the sweetness of bell pepper and tomato, the savoury saltiness of ham, and a little heat.

The chicken skin is browned golden first, and the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) brings the roasted flavour. Then it simmers with the vegetables over low heat so the meat cooks through and the sauce takes on flavour. The proteins in chicken set as it heats, so once it is stewed tender you can take it off the heat (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

## Steps

1. Cut a cross in the bottom of each tomato, pour boiling water over them and leave for 1 minute; lift them out when the skin curls, then peel and chop. Slice the onions and bell peppers, chop the garlic, and cut the ham into strips.
2. Pat the chicken pieces dry and season with salt and black pepper. Heat the olive oil in a large pan over medium heat, and fry the chicken skin side down for 6–8 minutes, until the skin is golden brown. Turn and fry about 3 minutes more, then take it out.
3. Cook the onions in the oil in the pan for about 5 minutes, until soft. Add the red and green bell peppers and cook for 10 minutes, stirring as they cook; add the garlic and ham strips and mix.
4. Add the tomatoes and tomato paste, then pour in the white wine, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom. Add the chili pepper, bay leaf and thyme, and season with salt and black pepper.
5. Return the chicken to the pan and press it into the sauce. Cover and simmer over low heat for about 35 minutes, until the meat is cooked through and the sauce is thick. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part of a leg, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) it is done.
6. Pick out the bay leaf, taste and adjust the salt, and serve hot. If there is a lot of sauce, uncover and cook a little longer.

## Key points

- **The skin is pale and has no roasted flavour**: the surface of the chicken was too wet, or too many pieces went in at once, so the water in the pan could not escape easily. Dry the chicken and brown in batches if needed (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **The sauce is thin, like a soup**: the tomatoes released a lot of water and the pot stayed covered. After stewing, cook uncovered so the extra water evaporates.
- **For lunch boxes**: pack into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly; reheat the next day until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F) before eating.
