*Kor moo yang* is a popular Thai grilled pork jowl, often eaten with sticky rice and green papaya salad. The streaks of fat running through the meat keep it juicy, and the browning and aroma on the surface come from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Trim off any overly thick hard fat on the outside and keep the fine streaks in the meat, so the meat and fat grill well together.

Pork jowl has a clear grain, so after grilling [slice it thin against the grain](/principles/knife-work/) and each bite is easy to bite through. It is served with a *jaew* toasted-rice dip: the sour, salty heat holds up the smoky grilled flavour, and the [acidity](/principles/acid/) keeps the fatty meat tasting fresh.

## Steps

1. Pound the white pepper fine, then add the garlic, cilantro roots and lemongrass and pound to a paste. Stir in the palm sugar, coconut milk, oyster sauce and light soy sauce for the marinade, until no grains of sugar are visible.
2. Trim thick fat from the pork and rub the marinade all over. Cover and refrigerate for 2 hours. Turn it over at 1 hour so both sides are coated with marinade; before grilling, scrape off the paste that has piled up and let the dripping liquid drain away.
3. Toast the glutinous rice in a dry pan over medium heat, stirring, for 5–8 minutes, until the grains are brown and smell of toasted grain. Spread out to cool for 5 minutes, until no longer hot to the touch, then grind to a fine, sandy powder. Dry-roast the chili flakes separately over low heat for a few minutes, until slightly darker and fragrant.
4. Stir together the fish sauce, lime juice and the sugar for the dip, then add the shallot and chili flakes. Stir until the sugar dissolves, and save the cilantro and rice powder for just before serving.
5. Preheat the grill for about 10 minutes, until it reaches about 200°C (400°F) inside, leaving one area without direct flame. Grill the pork 4–5 minutes per side, until the surface is golden brown and the fat at the edges is translucent; insert a thermometer in the thickest part, and if it hasn't reached 71°C (160°F), move the pork to the gentler zone and grill 2–4 minutes more.
6. Rest the cooked pork on a clean plate for 5 minutes, until the juices running out slow down. Look at the grain and slice across the fibres into slices about 3 mm thick; stir the rice powder and cilantro into the dip and serve right away.

## Key points

- **The surface is wet and pale and the grilled flavour is weak**: too much marinade is clinging, and the evaporating moisture slows browning. Drain off the marinade before grilling and leave gaps between the pieces. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Bitter and burnt outside, raw in the centre**: the sugar and fine spices met strong heat first. Once browned, move the pork to the gentle zone to cook through, and don't judge doneness by black char. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The cooked meat is hard to chew**: it was sliced with the grain, leaving long fibres, or the slices are too thick. Look at the grain first, then slice thin against it; if the piece is narrow, cut at an angle. See [knife work](/principles/knife-work/).
