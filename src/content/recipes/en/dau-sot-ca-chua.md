*Đậu sốt cà chua* is Vietnamese tofu in tomato sauce. Firm tofu is fried until golden, then added to a sweet and sour sauce made from fresh tomatoes, and finished with the green part of the green onion. The meatless home version uses a little fish sauce for savouriness, or light soy sauce for a fully vegan dish, and goes with white rice and [Rau Muống Xào Tỏi](/recipes/rau-muong-xao-toi/).

Frying first dries the surface of the tofu and builds a firmer thin skin, so it keeps its shape during the gentle simmer later (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The ripe tomatoes and fish sauce in the sauce supply [umami](/principles/umami/). The tomatoes are cooked soft first and the tofu goes in afterwards, so the sauce clings without the tofu being cooked dry and hard.

## Steps

1. Drain the tofu. Pour off the packing water, sandwich the tofu between clean kitchen towels or paper towels and let it sit for 20–30 minutes, until the surface feels free of a film of water. Replace the damp paper, then cut the tofu into pieces about 1.5 cm thick and 4 cm wide. Don't press hard; the inside should stay soft.
2. Prepare the tomatoes. Remove the hard cores and cut into pieces about 1 cm, keeping the juices. Mince the garlic and shallot, and separate the green onions into white and green parts. Measure out the water, fish sauce and sugar.
3. Fry a thin crust. Put 3 tbsp of the oil in a skillet and heat over medium-high heat for about 1–2 minutes, until the oil shimmers but is not yet smoking. Arrange the tofu in a single layer and fry each side for about 3–4 minutes, until golden and a spatula can nudge it loose, then flip. Fry in two batches, about 6–8 minutes per batch for both sides, and drain on paper towels for 1 minute.
4. Cook the tomato sauce. In a separate pan, heat the remaining 1 tbsp oil over medium heat for about 1 minute. Stir-fry the garlic, shallot and green onion whites for about 30 seconds, until fragrant but not browned. Add the tomatoes, 240 ml water, fish sauce, sugar, salt and the optional chili, and cook for about 2 minutes, until the sauce starts to bubble. Then turn the heat to low and simmer for 6–8 minutes, until the tomatoes can be crushed with a spatula and the sauce has thickened slightly.
5. Simmer in the tofu. Add the fried tofu to the sauce and simmer over low heat for 3–5 minutes, until every piece is coated in red sauce and hot through the centre. Turn gently once halfway, or spoon the sauce over; keep small bubbles at the edge of the pan and leave a little space between the pieces.
6. Finish and plate. Stir in the green onion greens and the optional pepper, and cook for about 30 seconds more, until the greens just soften, then turn off the heat. The sauce should flow slowly, and the inside of the tofu should still be white and tender.

## Key points

- **Oil spatters and the tofu is slow to colour**: the surface has too much water, and the heat goes first into evaporating it (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Blot it dry before frying, and fry only one layer per batch.
- **Skin is thick and tough, inside is dry**: the tofu was fried too long and kept losing water (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Take it out once golden, and simmer only briefly in the sauce.
- **Tofu breaks into small pieces**: the sauce boiled too hard, or the tofu was moved too often (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lower the heat, spoon the sauce in from the edge of the pan, and support the whole piece when turning.
