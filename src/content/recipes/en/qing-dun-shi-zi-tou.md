Clear-stewed lion's head meatballs are the large meatballs of Huaiyang cooking: pork belly cut fine into small pieces, mixed with water and seasoning, then stewed in clear broth under a cover of napa cabbage. The surface of the meatballs looks slightly rough, but they are soft and loose in the mouth, and a spoon pressed gently will part them, while the broth keeps the clean freshness of the pork and cabbage.

Cutting by hand keeps the fat and lean in distinct pieces (for the cutting, see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). When mixing the filling, [salt](/principles/salt/) helps some of the muscle protein dissolve, so the pieces bind together and can hold the water added in portions. The thin slurry and egg white help the shape set, and gentle heat then completes the [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). The mixture has to be sticky enough to hold, while keeping some softness between the pieces.

## Steps

1. Put the pork belly in the freezer for 1 hour, until the outside is firm and the knife still goes in with some give. Cut the lean meat into pieces of about 3–4 mm and the fat into pieces of about 4–5 mm; gather them together and chop a few times, keeping the pieces distinct.
2. Mix the starch for the meat mixture with 1 tbsp water. Add the ginger, minced green onion white part, 1.5 tbsp wine and the salt for the meatballs to the meat, and stir for about 3–5 minutes, until the pieces turn sticky. Beat in the 75 ml cold water in 3 portions, waiting each time until the water is taken up before adding more; stir in the egg white and starch slurry until the mixture is soft and can gather into a mass.
3. Blanch the cabbage in 2000 ml boiling water for 1–2 minutes, until the leaves are soft and the stems bend. Lift out, rinse in cold water and drain. Put the smashed green onion and ginger in a small clay pot or a heavy-bottomed pot, lay 4 cabbage leaves on top, and pour in the chicken stock, the wine and the salt for the stew.
4. Stir the 3 tbsp starch for the surface with 3 tbsp water. Divide the meat mixture into 4 portions, shape each into a ball between your hands, and coat the surface with a thin layer of the wet starch slurry. Heat the broth for about 3–5 minutes, until it bubbles gently at the edge of the pot, then lower the meatballs in one by one with a large spoon.
5. Cover with the other 4 cabbage leaves, put on the lid, and stew over the lowest heat for 2 hours. Keep the surface of the broth at a slight tremble, and do not turn the meatballs once they have set. They are done when the centre reaches at least 71°C (160°F), a spoon pressed lightly parts them, and the cabbage is soft through. If they are still firm, stew 15 minutes more.
6. With a large spoon, lift the meatballs out together with the cabbage and divide them among deep bowls. Remove the green onion and ginger, pour the clear broth back over, leaving the top half of each meatball showing, and eat hot.

## Key points

- **Meatballs are dense and hard**: the meat was chopped too fine, or the cut was almost all lean. Keep small pieces, use pork belly with fat and lean layered, and shape the meatballs once the mixture binds (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
- **Falls apart in the pot, broth full of minced meat**: too much water went in at once, before the mixture had turned sticky. Stir the filling until sticky first (see [salt](/principles/salt/)), then beat in the water in portions; the thin surface slurry only helps the shape set.
- **Cloudy broth, cracked meatballs**: a hard boil broke up the surface and beat the fat into the broth. The protein of the meatballs is especially fragile just after it sets (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), so cover with cabbage, stew gently, and support the meatballs from underneath when you move them.
