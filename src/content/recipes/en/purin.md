Japanese caramel pudding (*purin*) sets soft but firm, and the brown caramel runs down to the plate when it is turned out. It is made with whole eggs, whole milk and sugar, and eaten chilled. The pudding takes its shape from [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/); the bitter-sweet aroma of the caramel comes from the [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) of the sugar as it cooks.

Milk and sugar delay the setting of the custard, but the heat still needs to be controlled. The water bath softens the heating of the mould walls so the centre keeps up with the outer ring (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Prepare six metal moulds of 150 ml each and set them in a deep baking pan that holds them snugly, dividing the custard evenly.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven with top and bottom heat to 150°C (300°F), about 15 minutes. Heat the 1000 ml of water for the bath until boiling, about 5 minutes. Set aside 1 tbsp hot water for the caramel, and keep the moulds close at hand.
2. In a small pan, put the 70 g of sugar for the caramel and 1 tbsp room-temperature water, and cook over medium-low heat for about 5 minutes. Wait until the sugar crystals have fully dissolved, then swirl the pan gently to even out the colour. Take it off the heat when the syrup is deep amber and smells of bitter-sweet caramel.
3. Add the 1 tbsp hot water in portions, slowly, keeping your hand away from the mouth of the pan, and swirl gently so the caramel flows evenly. Immediately divide it among the bottoms of the six moulds and leave about 5 minutes, until the surface sets into a thin layer that does not flow.
4. Heat the milk over medium-low heat for about 3–5 minutes, until fine bubbles form at the edge of the pan and the centre has not come to a boil. Gently stir the eggs and 70 g of sugar together, then slowly stir in one third of the hot milk, followed by the rest.
5. Strain the custard through a fine sieve into the moulds and skim off the foam. Set the moulds in the deep baking pan and pour hot water in to halfway up the moulds. Keep the leftover water in the kettle, and do not let any splash into the custard.
6. Bake at 150°C (300°F) for 35–45 minutes, until the edges are set and the whole centre jiggles slightly, with no liquid sloshing, and the centre has reached at least 74°C (165°F). If it has not set, bake 5 minutes more each time and check again; ceramic moulds usually need 45–60 minutes.
7. Lift the moulds out of the water bath and cool on a rack for about 30 minutes, until only slightly warm and no longer steaming. Cover and refrigerate for 2 hours, until the pudding is cold and holds its shape.
8. Run a thin knife around the inside wall, set a small plate on top, flip them together and shake gently to release. The caramel should coat the top and run down to the plate; if it sticks, pull one edge away to let air in, then invert again.

## Key points

- **Holes in the cut surface, coarse and hard texture**: the water bath boiled too hard or the pudding baked too long, so the protein network tightened and squeezed out water. Take it out of the hot water once it is set (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Caramel is acrid and harshly bitter**: it stayed in the hot pan after reaching deep amber. Have the hot water and moulds ready and take the pan off the heat in time (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)).
- **Collapses when turned out, flowing centre**: the centre had not set while baking, or the pudding was not chilled long enough. Confirm it is cooked by the state of the centre first, then chill it fully (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
