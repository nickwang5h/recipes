Turnip cake, once fried, has a golden cut face and slightly crisp edges, and inside is a steamed cake studded with daikon shreds and cured sausage. It is a Cantonese dim sum dish made from shredded daikon, rice flour and Chinese sausage. The daikon is cooked soft first and goes into the batter with its liquid. The starch in the rice flour takes up water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), holding the daikon shreds and fillings together in one block. The rice flour is just plain white rice flour. Choose a fine one for a smooth cake.

Let the steamed cake cool and set before slicing and frying. Steaming uses steam to cook the inside through. Frying lets the cut face lose water and gives the browned flavour of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). This amount fits a round metal mould about 20 cm across and gives a fairly firm cake in which you can see the daikon shreds.

## Steps

1. Soak the shiitake and dried shrimp separately in warm water for about 2 hours, until the shiitake centres are soft and the shrimp can be cut. Drain and chop, dice the Chinese sausage, grate the daikon, and pour away the soaking water.
2. Put the 30 ml of oil for the fillings in a pot and stir-fry the Chinese sausage, shrimp and shiitake over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the fat of the sausage turns translucent and the shiitake smells fragrant. Mix in the green onion and lift out with the oil.
3. In the same pot, add the daikon shreds and 240 ml of water. Bring to a boil, then cook over medium-low heat for about 10 minutes, until the shreds are soft and slightly translucent. Scoop out the liquid from the pot and measure out 240 ml, then pour it back. Set any extra aside, and if short, top up with water.
4. Mix the rice flour, cornstarch and 120 ml of cold water until smooth and pour into the daikon pot. Add the salt, sugar, white pepper and the stir-fried fillings. Stir over low heat for 1–2 minutes, until the batter falls slowly and the fillings no longer sink to the bottom, then take it off the heat.
5. Brush the mould with 5 ml of oil, pour in the batter and smooth the top. Once the steamer is steaming, set the mould in and steam over medium-high heat for about 40 minutes, until the centre is set and a chopstick comes out without wet batter. Leave room around the mould for steam to pass, and top up with hot water if the pot runs low.
6. Take it out and cool for about 30 minutes, until the cake no longer wobbles and the edge comes away from the mould whole. Cut slices about 1 cm thick with a wet knife, wiping the knife clean whenever batter sticks to it.
7. In a frying pan, add the remaining 30 ml of oil in batches and fry the cake over medium-low heat for about 3–4 minutes per side, until the cut face is golden and the edges slightly crisp. Let the first side set before flipping. Eat hot, with oyster sauce for dipping.

## Key points

- **The cake is soft, falls apart and will not cut**: the daikon liquid was not counted in the total water, or glutinous rice flour was used in place of rice flour (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Measure the liquid after cooking, and wait until the steamed cake has set before cutting.
- **A wet, floury core in the middle**: the mould is too deep or the steam too weak, so the centre heats slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Check the state of the centre. If it still has wet batter, steam for another 5–10 minutes and check again.
- **It breaks when flipped and the surface lacks flavour**: cut too thin or flipped too often, before the steam has escaped (see [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Support the base with a wide spatula and flip only after one side forms a thin crust.
