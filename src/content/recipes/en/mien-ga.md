*Miến gà* is a clear Vietnamese soup of shredded chicken and glass noodles. Some of the ginger, onion and shallots are cooked straight in the broth and some are roasted first, which adds a sweet aroma to the chicken broth; fried shallots are sprinkled on at the table so their fragrance stays on the surface (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The cilantro stems are cooked into the soup, and the leaves are added last.

Soaked glass noodles still take up water when heated, and their starch keeps absorbing it (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Blanching them in a separate pot and eating them once the bowls are assembled keeps the noodles springy and the broth in the bowl clear. The chicken is lifted out once cooked, and the carcass goes back to keep simmering, so the meat is not cooked dry for the sake of the stock.

## Steps

1. Roast the aromatics. Cut one onion in half and put it with half the ginger, the whole shallots and the cilantro stems and roots under the oven broiler for 8–10 minutes, turning partway through, until the surfaces have brown spots and smell sweet. Cool for 5 minutes, until you can hold them, remove any burnt skin, and put the aromatics in a cheesecloth bag.
2. Cook the chicken. Put the chicken pieces and carcass in a pot with the water, the other onion, the remaining ginger, the green onion whites and the salt, and heat over high heat for 10–15 minutes, until the surface boils. Skim the foam and add the bag of aromatics. Simmer gently over low heat for 25–35 minutes, until the meat is white and still springy; insert a thermometer into the thickest part of the breast and the leg, without touching bone, and at 74°C (165°F) they are done. If not, cook 5 minutes more and measure again.
3. Take the meat off the bones. Lift out the chicken, cool for 15 minutes, until you can hold it steadily, then shred it along the grain into coarse strips and cover. Return the bones to the pot and simmer over low heat another 20 minutes, until the chicken aroma is clear and the surface shows only a few small bubbles. Strain out the solids and add the fish sauce. The finished broth is about 2400 ml; if short, add hot water up to that amount.
4. Fry the shallots. Dry the thin shallot slices and put them in the oil. Fry over medium-low heat for 4–6 minutes, until light golden and the bubbles lessen, then lift out. Spread on paper towels and cool for 5 minutes, until slightly darker and crisp to the touch. Keep 1 tbsp of the shallot oil for the bowls.
5. Soak the noodles. Soak the glass noodles in warm water for 10 minutes, until they bend and are easy to cut, drain and cut into lengths of about 15 cm. Blanch them in a separate pot of boiling water for 1–2 minutes, until translucent with no hard core, and divide at once among four bowls. If they are not cooked through, blanch another 30 seconds.
6. Warm the chicken. Return the shredded chicken to the clear broth and heat over low heat for 2–3 minutes, until hot through, then lift out and lay it over the noodles. Add a quarter of the shallot oil, the green onion greens and cilantro leaves to each bowl, then ladle about 600 ml of piping hot chicken broth over it.
7. Finish. Sprinkle on the fried shallots and pepper, and eat at once with lime and chili. The fried shallots go on last so they keep a little crunch on the hot soup.

## Key points

- **Little broth, and the noodles swell into a clump**: the noodles sat in the soup too long and kept absorbing water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Blanch them in a separate pot, and assemble only as much as will be eaten; store leftover noodles apart from the broth.
- **Chicken dry and in bits**: the meat was cooked with the carcass for too long, so the proteins shrank (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take it out once it reaches doneness, shred into coarse strips, and return it to the broth only to warm it before assembling.
- **Bitter fried shallots, burnt taste in the broth**: the shallots came out of the oil too late and the residual heat kept darkening them (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lift them at light golden, and do not use burnt slices.
