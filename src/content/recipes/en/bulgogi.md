*Bulgogi* is thin beef marinated with pear, soy sauce, garlic and toasted sesame oil, mildly sweet and salty, and often eaten wrapped in lettuce. The pear gives the marinade sweetness and has enzymes that help tenderize the meat, but the real first step in making the beef easy to chew is still choosing a tender cut and [slicing it thin against the grain](/principles/knife-work/).

Charcoal gives the most distinct smoky flavour, but at home a heavy-bottomed frying pan works. The surface of the meat has to lose its extra water first before the browned edges from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) can appear. The sugar in the marinade burns easily, so the heat must be high enough and the beef must come off in time.

## Steps

1. Freeze the beef for about 1 hour, until the outer layer is slightly firm and the centre can still be cut; pre-sliced thin beef lets you skip this. Find the grain of the muscle and slice across it into 3 mm slices, as even as you can.
2. Blend the pear, onion, garlic and ginger into a fine purée; with no food processor, grate them fine. Do not add water, since the purée already has enough moisture.
3. Toss the beef with the purée, green onion, soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, honey and black pepper, so every slice is loosened and coated in marinade. Cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes; tender beef already sliced very thin only needs to take up the flavour.
4. Heat a heavy-bottomed frying pan until the beef sizzles the moment it goes in, and wipe on a thin layer of oil. Lift the beef out with tongs, let the extra purée drip back into the bowl, and lay it in the pan in a single layer in batches; leave gaps between the slices and keep the marinade in the bowl.
5. Let the underside brown at the edges before turning, then cook the other side until the slices have all changed colour but are still moist, about 2 minutes, and take them out at once. If the next batch only makes water bubbles when it goes in, wait for the pan to heat up again before continuing.
6. If using an outdoor grill, preheat it and oil the grate, then lay the slices flat in batches and turn until both sides are coloured and the slices are cooked through. Thin meat slips through a wide grate easily, so use a fine-mesh grill basket.
7. Put the cooked beef on a warm plate, sprinkle with sesame seeds and serve right away. Dry the lettuce, wrap a little beef in it, and eat with rice. Do not use the marinade left in the raw-meat bowl as a dipping sauce.

## Key points

- **The beef is cooked but grey, with a pool of water in the pan**: too much beef went in, or the marinade went in with it, and the heat was used to boil water. Take out some of the beef, wait for the water in the pan to cook off, and sear in batches. See [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Burnt outside, not cooked inside**: the slices are too thick, or the sugar and purée burned first at the bottom of the pan. Wipe off the burnt bits, lower the heat a little, and slice the next batch thinner; check for doneness as soon as brown edges appear. See [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **The beef is dry and stringy, or mushy after marinating**: cooked too long, meat loses water, and left too long in the pear marinade the surface turns mushy. Choose a tender cut, marinate briefly in the refrigerator, and take it off once it is cooked through. See [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
