For Henan steamed noodles, the noodles are steamed first, then tossed with a meat and vegetable sauce and steamed again, so the noodles absorb the seasoning and there is no bowl of loose liquid left after cooking. Thin round dried noodles stand in here for fresh thin noodles; dried noodles need more water, and after the first steam they may still be firm, so tossing with the sauce and steaming again finish the [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).

Both steams need the noodles spread loosely so steam can find a path through (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cook the sauce first, then toss it in in stages according to how much the noodles absorb. It is easier to control when the surface is evenly moist and no large pool collects at the bottom of the bowl than when you pour in all the sauce at once. Soy sauce, oyster sauce and the optional bouillon powder already carry salt, so add extra salt only after tasting.

## Steps

1. Add water to the steamer, with the level below the steamer rack. Spread the thin dried noodles loosely, drizzle in 5 ml of the oil in layers and toss, leaving steam channels; once the water is boiling and steam is steady, start timing and steam for 15 minutes.
2. While the noodles steam, cut the pork belly into slices about 2 mm thick, the garlic scapes into 3 cm sections, the green onion into thin slices, the ginger into shreds, and crush the garlic. After the first steam, gently shake the noodles loose with chopsticks.
3. Add the remaining 5 ml of oil to a wok, add the pork slices over medium heat, and stir-fry for about 1 minute until they begin to change colour and release fat. Add the green onion, ginger, garlic and Shaoxing wine, and stir-fry about 1 more minute until fragrant.
4. Add the garlic scapes and stir-fry for about 1 minute, then add the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, thirteen-spice blend and the optional bouillon powder and toss for about 1 minute. Pour in 500 ml of water, bring to a boil and cook about 1 minute, then taste the sauce before deciding whether to add salt.
5. First scoop most of the sauce out into a bowl, and gently toss the noodles with the meat and vegetables left in the wok, then pour the sauce back in stages and toss for about 3 minutes. Let all the noodles become moist and coloured, with no large amount of flowing sauce left at the bottom of the bowl; keep the remaining sauce for later.
6. Spread the noodles and vegetables loosely back on the steamer rack. Once the water is boiling again and steam is steady, start timing and steam over high heat for 15 minutes. Take out a strand to check: take it out of the steamer when there is no dry hard core in the centre and it bites through; if still firm, drizzle in a little of the reserved hot sauce and steam until cooked.
7. While hot, shake the noodles loose with chopsticks, drizzle in the 5 ml of toasted sesame oil, toss until glossy throughout, and divide onto plates at once.

## Key points

- **Noodles are undercooked in the middle**: dried noodles have little water to begin with, and not enough sauce was absorbed (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add a little hot sauce and steam again, and break one open to look at the centre; don't judge by the surface colour alone.
- **Noodles clump and the bottom layer is soggy**: the sauce went in too fast and was piled too densely (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Toss in the sauce in stages, spread loosely before steaming, and don't let the water in the steamer touch the noodles.
- **Too salty**: the salt in the soy sauce, oyster sauce and bouillon powder all stays in the noodles (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Taste the sauce first, and add extra salt only after tasting.
