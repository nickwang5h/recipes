*Pad prik king* (ผัดพริกขิง) is a Thai dry curry in which red curry paste is fried until it coats the meat and beans. It is salty-sweet with some heat, the citrus aroma of the makrut lime leaves is clear, and only a thin layer of glossy oil and paste is left on the plate, with no soup. This version uses pork and green beans.

The key is to cook the pork, the paste and the beans separately. The pork goes into a hot pan first, and the paste is fried afterward over gentler heat for fragrance (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Cooking the green beans first and returning them to the pan to pick up the sauce means the pork does not dry out while you wait for the beans. The thicker the ingredient, the slower it cooks (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Marinate the pork: toss the slices with 2 tsp fish sauce and refrigerate for 15 minutes, until the fish sauce is evenly on the meat. While it waits, prepare the green beans and lime leaves, and measure the sugar and curry paste.
2. Put 1000 ml of water in a pot and heat over high heat for about 5 minutes until boiling. Add the green beans and cook over medium heat for about 5–6 minutes, until cooked through with no raw bean taste but still a little springy to the bite. Drain and spread out for about 1 minute, until no water drops remain on the surface, so they do not thin the sauce when they go back in the pan.
3. Sear the first batch of pork: put 10 ml oil in a large skillet and preheat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows fast but does not smoke. Add half the pork in a single layer and sear for 1 minute, until brown spots show on the bottom. Stir-fry for 30 seconds, until the other side has coloured too, then remove. Cook the remaining pork the same way with another 10 ml of oil.
4. Take the pan off the heat for about 1 minute, until the leftover oil settles and no longer smokes. Add the last 10 ml of oil and the red curry paste, break it up first, then fry over medium heat for 2 minutes, until the raw edge is gone and the paste is a little darker, with no burnt bits on the bottom.
5. Add the palm sugar, the remaining 1 tsp fish sauce and 60 ml of the water. Stir for about 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves and the paste on the bottom is lifted. Add the 5 torn makrut lime leaves and fry for another 30 seconds, until the leaves soften and the citrus aroma spreads.
6. Return the pork and green beans, add the remaining 60 ml of water, and stir-fry over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until the thickest piece of pork reaches 71°C (160°F), the beans are heated through, and the sauce coats everything evenly. Stir-fry for 1 minute more, until no liquid is left on the bottom and a spatula drawn across the pan briefly shows the bottom.
7. Off the heat, stir in the finely shredded lime leaves and transfer to a shallow plate. Serve with jasmine rice. Each bean and slice of pork carries a layer of red paste. First comes salty savouriness, then the roundness of the sugar and the fresh scent of the leaves.

## Key points

- **The red paste is bitter and the leaves have no fragrance**: the pan was too hot after searing the pork, so the paste scorched as soon as it went in. Take it off the heat to cool first, then fry the paste over medium heat. Add the makrut lime leaves into the paste to bring out their aroma. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **The pork is overcooked while the beans are still raw**: meat and beans cook at different speeds. Cook the beans first, cut the pork in slices of even thickness, then combine them in the pan to reduce the sauce. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Too salty once dry**: the curry paste and the fish sauce in the marinade both carry salt, and the saltiness concentrates once the water evaporates. Add only 1 tsp of fish sauce when stir-frying, taste, and decide whether to add more. See [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/).
