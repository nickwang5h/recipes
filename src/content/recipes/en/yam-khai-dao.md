*Yam khai dao* tosses crisp-edged fried eggs, cut into pieces, with onion, tomato, Chinese celery and a sour, spicy dressing. The yolk is fried through, so every piece, once cut, has both white and yolk. It is usually eaten with rice, so the lime and fish sauce should taste bright. The sourness also sets off the richness of the yolk (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).

The crisp edges come from hot oil making the white bubble quickly and then letting the thin edge dry out and colour (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). The yolk cooks more slowly than the thin white, so baste hot oil over the top as it fries to help the centre cook through (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Prepare the dressing and vegetables first and cut and toss only after the eggs are fried, and the crisp edges keep longer.

## Steps

1. Slice the onion, cut the tomato into wedges, and cut the Chinese celery and cilantro into pieces, then wash and drain. Take the eggs out of the fridge and let them sit on the counter during the 10 minutes of prep, until the shells are no longer cold, then crack each into a small bowl.
2. Pound the chilies in a mortar, add the palm sugar and pound to a fine paste. Pour in the lime juice and fish sauce and stir for 1–2 minutes, until there are no obvious lumps of sugar at the bottom of the bowl, then set aside.
3. Add the oil to a small skillet and heat over medium-high heat for about 2–3 minutes. Test the oil with a small piece of the prepared onion; when dense bubbles form around it right away and the oil is not smoking, lift the onion out.
4. Slide one egg in from close to the oil's surface; the white should bubble immediately. Fry for about 2–3 minutes, continuously spooning the hot oil over the top, until the edges of the white are golden and crinkled and the yolk is set. Insert a thin probe into the centre of the yolk from the side; lift the egg out at **74°C (165°F)**.
5. Drain the egg in a single layer on paper towels for 1 minute, until there are no obvious oil droplets on the surface. Before the next egg goes in, heat for about 30 seconds until the test onion bubbles densely again, then fry the remaining eggs the same way.
6. Cut each egg into six pieces around the yolk and put them in a large bowl with the vegetables. Pour in the dressing and toss gently, until the egg pieces and vegetables are coated, then plate, scatter the optional crushed peanuts on top, and eat right away with rice.

## Key points

- **Whites flat and limp, no crisp edges**: the oil is too cool, or several cold eggs went in at once and the temperature dropped. Fry one egg at a time, and add the egg only when the test onion bubbles right away; if the oil keeps smoking, lower the heat first. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Outer ring darkens while the yolk is still runny**: the edge of the white heats quickly while the centre hasn't caught up. Lower the heat slightly, baste the yolk with oil, check every 30 seconds, and cut only after the yolk is cooked through. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Goes soft soon after tossing and tastes dull**: the vegetables carried water, or the egg sat too long in the sour dressing. Drain the vegetables, drain the eggs of oil, and toss just before eating. Taste with a piece of egg to judge the sourness and saltiness of the whole bite. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
