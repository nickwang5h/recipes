*Salade niçoise* is a summer salad from Nice: juicy tomatoes and crisp raw vegetables with hard-boiled eggs, savoury cured fish and olives. The traditional Niçoise version is mostly raw vegetables, with artichokes and young fava beans added in season. French restaurants also serve versions with cooked potatoes and green beans; this recipe is the raw vegetable version.

Everything about this salad depends on when you add the salt (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). A little salt on the tomatoes draws out juice that seasons the whole dish together with the olive oil. Salt added too early makes the other vegetables release water and go soft. So spread the ingredients on a wide platter and season at the last minute. Each serving gets fish, egg and vegetables, and the pieces keep their shape better than if you pile them into a deep bowl and toss repeatedly.

## Steps

1. Cook the eggs until the white and yolk are completely set (about 8 minutes after the water boils), then cool them right away in cold water. Peel, cut into quarters and keep for arranging at the end.
2. Wash the vegetables and spin the lettuce dry. Cut the tomatoes into wedges, the bell pepper into thin strips, and the celery heart and green onion into thin slices. Trim the tough stems from the radishes; leave small tender ones whole and cut larger ones.
3. Use the seasonal vegetables only if you have them. For baby artichokes, peel off the tough leaves, trim the coarse skin and slice only the tender heart thin. Shell the fava beans, and peel off the skins as well if they are thick and coarse. If the artichokes and fava beans are large and mature, do not eat them raw; leave them out.
4. Rub the inside of a wide platter with the cut garlic and take the garlic away. Lay down the lettuce, arrange the tomatoes, sprinkle on a little salt, then add the bell pepper, celery, green onion, radishes and the seasonal vegetables. Spread the thin slices out loosely (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
5. Break the tuna into large chunks and arrange it on the vegetables with the anchovy fillets, egg wedges and olives. Chop or tear the basil just before using and scatter it on top. Do not cut it ahead and leave it under the wet vegetables.
6. Just before serving, drizzle on the olive oil and sprinkle with black pepper. First taste a bite with tomato, fish and oil, and add salt if it is not salty enough. Scoop from the bottom of the platter with a large spoon, so everyone gets several ingredients and some of the juices at the bottom.

## Key points

- **Water pools at the bottom and the lettuce goes limp**: the leaves were not spun dry, or the salt went on too early. Drain the vegetables after washing, and arrange and season at the last minute; a little juice that the tomatoes release naturally can stay (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **It tastes only of salty fish**: the anchovies, olives and tuna already carry salt. Rinse salt-packed anchovies first, add only a little table salt, and taste several ingredients together (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **Raw vegetables are tough and hard to chew**: the artichoke still has tough leaves, or the tender heart is cut too thick. Use only the tender parts and slice celery thin across the grain; if the seasonal young vegetables are not available, leave them out (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
