*Sundubu-jjigae* is a Korean soft tofu stew served at a rolling boil. Pork and kimchi make a sour, spicy soup, large pieces of soft tofu float in it, and an egg is dropped in at the end. Korean sundubu is watery and loose in texture, and you squeeze it straight out of the tube. If you use boxed silken tofu, scooping large spoonfuls keeps a similar texture.

The chili flakes are mixed with oil first and then added to the moist pot, so their colour and part of their aroma disperse into the fat; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The tofu is already set, so in the pot it only needs to be heated. Keep it in large pieces and stir little, so a spoon can pick up both the soup and the soft tofu.

## Steps

1. Mix the chili flakes and vegetable oil into a moist red paste with no dry powder scattered. Do not open the tofu yet; add it when the soup base is cooked.
2. In a small heavy-bottomed pot, put the toasted sesame oil, pork, garlic and onion and stir-fry over medium heat. When the surface of the pork turns white, the onion begins to soften and you smell the garlic, add the kimchi and keep stir-frying until mixed.
3. Pour in the chicken stock and the chili paste, scrape up the browned bits at the bottom, bring to a boil, then turn to medium-low for about 10 minutes. Cook until the onion is translucent and the sour and spicy flavours of the soup have come together. Cut into a piece of pork: it is done when the centre reaches 71°C (160°F).
4. Squeeze in the tofu, or scoop it in with a large spoon. Break up only the especially large pieces and nudge the bottom of the pot lightly to keep it from sticking. Cook until the soup bubbles again and the centre of the tofu is hot, then taste the soup and add salt.
5. Push aside two spaces between the pieces of tofu and crack an egg into each. Keep a small boil and cook until the white has set and the yolk is cooked, with the centre of the egg at 74°C (165°F). Scatter on the green onion and serve right away with rice.

## Key points

- **The red soup is bitter**: the chili flakes hit a dry, hot pot and scorched. Mix with oil first, then add with the stock. If you see black specks or smell scorching, make the soup base again. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **The tofu breaks into crumbs**: constant stirring breaks up the soft tofu. After adding it, push gently with the back of the spoon, and ladle the soup from the edge of the pot. For the structure of the protein network, see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **It gets saltier the longer it cooks**: kimchi and stock already contain salt, and long cooking evaporates the water. Heat the tofu through before adding salt, and serve right after cooking instead of leaving it on the stove to reduce. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
