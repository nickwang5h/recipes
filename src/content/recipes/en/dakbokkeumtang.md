Dakbokkeumtang (also called dakdoritang) cooks bone-in chicken, potato, carrot and onion in a red, sweet and spicy sauce. The skin and the meat near the bone give the broth body, and the whole garlic cloves can be eaten too once they are soft.

Cooking the chicken first and adding the vegetables later brings both to the right doneness in the same pot (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Once the edges of the potato soften they release a little starch, which together with the evaporating water thickens the broth naturally (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The finished dish should keep a broth you can ladle, with the chili sauce clinging thinly to the chicken.

## Steps

1. Separate the chicken legs at the joint into thighs and drumsticks, wipe off surface moisture and trim off the excess fat with scissors. If a thigh is large, ask the butcher counter to cut it into two pieces, so you don't have to chop bone with an unsuitable knife. Cut the vegetables, keeping the potato pieces as even in size as possible.
2. Put the chili flakes, chili paste, soy sauce, sugar, mirin, black pepper and 360 ml of water in a heavy-bottomed pot, stir well and add the chicken. Choose a pot in which the chicken lies in about two layers, and toss until every piece is coated in the sauce.
3. Heat over high heat for about 5 minutes, until the sauce keeps bubbling. Turn to medium heat, cover and cook for 15 minutes, until the outside of the chicken has fully changed colour and chicken fat floats on the sauce. Turn it every 5 minutes so the thick sauce doesn't stick to the bottom.
4. Add the potato, carrot, onion, garlic and ginger and gently stir them into the sauce. Cover and cook over medium heat for 10 minutes, until the onion is translucent and the edges of the potato are soft. Turn once halfway, moving the vegetables sticking out of the sauce to the bottom.
5. Uncover and cook for 5 more minutes, until a chopstick pierces the potato easily and the centre of the thickest part of the chicken reaches 74°C (165°F), with the probe kept away from the bone. If the potato or chicken isn't done, add the 60 ml of water you set aside, cover and cook for 5 more minutes.
6. Add the green onion, sesame oil and toasted sesame seeds, stir and cook for 1 minute, until the green onion softens a little and the sesame oil is dispersed, then turn off the heat. A layer of thick, flowing broth should still be left in the pot. Ladle the broth over the chicken and serve with rice.

## Key points

- **Chicken is done but the potato has a hard core**: the potato pieces are too big and the heat hasn't reached the centre. Cut even pieces, add water in measured amounts and cook covered until done, then decide whether to reduce (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Broth turns pasty and the potato falls apart**: the potato went in too early or was stirred too hard, releasing too much starch. Add the potato later, turn gently from the bottom with a spatula, and stop once it is soft through (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Saltier the longer it cooks, and chili sauce sticks to the bottom**: the water evaporated, but the salt in the soy sauce and chili paste stays. Keep a flowing broth, and when extending the cooking add the water you set aside first. Don't reduce it dry (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
