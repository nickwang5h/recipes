*Khao kha moo* is a common Thai dish of stewed pork leg on rice: skin-on pork leg or hock stewed until soft and sliced over rice, with braised eggs, sour mustard greens and chili vinegar. The braising liquid sets its saltiness and sweetness with soy sauce and palm sugar, and cassia, star anise, cilantro roots and galangal give the aroma. The [collagen in the skin and connective tissue gradually turns into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), which is how the meat goes from firm to soft and moist.

A slow stew at a gentle simmer gives the connective tissue enough time to soften and also keeps a shape that can be sliced. The spices meet the meat through the braising liquid and their flavour blends in slowly (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The sour mustard greens and the chili vinegar spooned on at the table bring [acidity](/principles/acid/), which makes a plate of rich skin, meat juices and rice taste lighter.

## Steps

1. Scrape the hock free of stray hairs, wipe the skin clean, and put it in a deep pot that holds it snugly. Add 2000 ml of water, the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, dark soy sauce and palm sugar, and heat over medium heat for about 15 minutes, until the surface of the liquid begins to bubble.
2. Turn to low heat and simmer gently for 45 minutes, until scum gathers on the surface, then skim it off clean. Add the cilantro roots, garlic, cassia sticks, star anise, both peppers, galangal and shiitake mushrooms, cover with the lid slightly open, and stew another 130 minutes; check every 30 minutes, and when the liquid drops below the meat, top up from the reserved water, 100 ml at a time.
3. While the meat stews, put the eggs into 1500 ml of boiling water and cook over low heat for 8 minutes, until the whites have set and the eggs can be peeled. Lift them out and cool for 5 minutes, until you can hold them steadily, then peel; drain the sour mustard greens, keeping the pieces whole so they are easy to lift out.
4. Add the eggs and sour mustard greens to the braising pot and simmer gently for another 20 minutes, until the egg whites are stained light brown, a fork goes easily into the hock, and the meat separates easily from the bone. If the meat is still tough, stew another 20–30 minutes.
5. Blend the chili vinegar's chilies, garlic, white vinegar, sugar and salt into a fine, pourable sauce. Change to 1500 ml of fresh water in another pot and boil it for about 8 minutes until it is at a boil; blanch the gai lan stems for 2 minutes first, then add the leaves and cook 1 minute, until bright green and the stems can be bitten through, then drain.
6. Lift out the hock, braised eggs and sour mustard greens, and strain the spices out of the braising liquid. Rest the meat for 5 minutes, until it holds steady for slicing, then slice it, skin and all, into bite-sized pieces; cut the braised eggs in half and the sour mustard greens into sections.
7. Divide the rice among 4 plates and top with the pork, a halved braised egg, sour mustard greens and gai lan. Spoon 3 tbsp of hot braising liquid over each plate, and serve the chili vinegar on the side, to be spooned on at the table, 1 tbsp per plate at first.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but still hard to bite**: the connective tissue hasn't softened. Keep the gentle simmer going until a fork goes in easily. See [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **Skin falls apart and the braising liquid is cloudy**: the boil was too hard and the meat was moved too much. Keep a slow bubble, and move the meat gently only when you check the water level. See [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **The whole plate tastes only sweet and heavy**: the braising liquid is rich and the sour side dishes are missing. Serve the sour mustard greens and chili vinegar together, and taste the balance with the meat and rice. See [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/).
