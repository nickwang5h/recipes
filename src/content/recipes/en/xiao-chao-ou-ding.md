Stir-fried diced lotus root is crisp and tender, with the aroma of green onion and bird's eye chili, and a thin sauce on the surface. The dice are [blanched](/principles/blanching/) first so the centres are heated, which means the wok is only for seasoning and tossing and does not have to wait for the centres to cook. Drain well after blanching, or the water carried into the wok will dilute the sauce.

All three sauces are salty, so when there is not much lotus root, hold some back, toss, taste and then add more. Cut size matters too: thick chunks heat slowly in the centre, so the outside browns while the inside is still hard, while small, even dice cook through together and stay crisp and tender; see [knife work and heat](/principles/knife-work/).

## Steps

1. Cut the leek section and bird's eye chili into short pieces and set aside separately. Use as much chili as you can handle, and cut the leek evenly, so fine bits do not burn before large pieces are cooked.
2. Peel the lotus root and cut into even small dice, with the longest side of each piece no more than 3 cm; cut thick pieces smaller. Soak in clean water first so the cut surfaces do not discolour in the air. Pour off the soaking water just before blanching.
3. Bring 500 ml of clean water to a steady rolling boil in a small pot. Leave room in the pot, so the dice are surrounded by water when they go in rather than piled up above the surface.
4. Put the dice in the boiling water and blanch for about 2 minutes. Cut open the largest piece; the centre should be hot through, with the shape intact and still crisp. Lift out and drain thoroughly, so there is no puddle of water on the plate.
5. Pour out the blanching water, wipe the pot dry and add the oil. When the oil is hot, add the leek and chili and stir-fry briefly until fragrant, and add the lotus root as soon as the edges of the leek begin to change colour.
6. Add the lotus root and toss over high heat so every surface is coated in leek and chili oil. If there is clear water in the pan, spread the dice out first and stir-fry off the steam before seasoning; do not let them keep cooking in liquid.
7. Add part of the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and oyster sauce, toss, taste a piece of lotus root, and add more if needed. After seasoning, keep stir-frying for about 2 minutes, until the colour is even, the surface is coated in sauce and there is no large pool of loose sauce at the bottom, then serve.

## Key points

- **Soft outside, hard in the centre**: the pieces are too big or uneven. Cut into even small dice as in [knife work and heat](/principles/knife-work/), and check the largest piece while blanching; do not stir-fry the whole pan longer for the sake of a few thick ones.
- **A plate of water and faint onion aroma**: the dice were not drained after blanching. Drain well after [blanching](/principles/blanching/) and add them to a dry wok, so the sauce clings in a thin coat.
- **Colour is too dark and it is too salty**: too much dark soy sauce, or the lotus root was less than expected (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Taste before adding more, and do not keep adding just to use up the sauces you measured out.
