Steamed pork ribs with black bean sauce are silky and well seasoned, and every piece carries the salty savoury flavour and soybean aroma of the black beans. That flavour comes from fermentation (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Once the beans are soaked soft and lightly chopped, the flavour reaches every piece of meat and the steaming juices.

The thin starch slurry mixed into the meat gelatinizes with heat, making the juices on the plate a little thicker and the bite silkier (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Steam heats the surface of the meat first, then slowly reaches the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). That is why the ribs are cut small and spread in a single layer, which steams more evenly than simply steaming for longer.

## Steps

1. Check the cut ends of the ribs and remove any loose bone chips. Soak them in cold water in the refrigerator for 10 minutes, then change the water and soak once more. Drain and blot the surface dry with paper towels.
2. Rinse the black beans and soak them for about 5 minutes, drain and chop them lightly. Chop the garlic to a mince and cut the ginger into fine pieces. Chop the green onion for garnish separately and set aside.
3. Mix the ribs with the black beans, garlic, ginger, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar and 30 ml of water, grabbing until most of the water clings to the meat. Add 8 g of potato starch and mix again, then finally mix in 10 ml of oil so every piece has a thin coat of batter.
4. Marinate in the refrigerator for 8–30 minutes. Spread the ribs in a single layer on a heat-proof shallow plate, leaving a little room between the bone-in pieces, and pour the marinade over them. If one plate will not hold them, use more plates and do not pile them into a mound.
5. Once the water in the steamer boils, put the plate in, cover and keep the steam steady. Steam for about 18 minutes first. Keep enough water in the pot, and top up with boiling water along the edge if it runs low, so it does not boil dry partway.
6. Insert a probe into the centre of the largest piece, away from the bone. At 71°C (160°F) it is done; if not, steam a little longer and test again. Turn off the heat and leave it covered for about 2 minutes, then lift out the plate with a plate gripper. The juices on the plate should be slightly thick and the surface of the meat glossy.
7. Sprinkle with chopped green onion (optional) and serve. Keep the juices as they are, and do not pour hot oil over.

## Key points

- **Some pieces are cooked and some are raw in the centre**: the ribs vary in size, or are stacked too thick (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Spread them in a single layer and, after 18 minutes, test the largest piece. Do not judge by poking the cartilage with chopsticks.
- **A thick floury coat on the meat**: the ribs were not drained, the slurry was not mixed evenly, or too much starch went in (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Blot dry first and grab the water and starch in separately until even.
- **One bite is very salty, the next has no flavour**: the whole black beans are concentrated in one spot (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Soak briefly, chop and mix thoroughly, and do not add extra salt.
