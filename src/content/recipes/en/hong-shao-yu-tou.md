Red-braised fish head is rich, savoury and fragrant with green onion and ginger: the head and the neck meat attached to it are braised through in a soy sauce liquid, and then the sauce is reduced thick. The ginger slices and green onion sections are fried slowly in oil first so their aroma can enter the fat (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). The finely chopped garlic and dried chilies go in later so they do not turn bitter in the braise.

Once the head is cut up, heat reaches the thick flesh and the joints more easily (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). After it is cooked, move it as little as possible so the meat stays on the bones. The gills and any leftover blood clots must be removed completely first.

## Steps

1. Check the fish head the store has cut up, and remove all gills, leftover innards and blood clots. Rinse clean and drain. Cut the attached neck meat into pieces of similar size, and carefully pick out any sharp bone fragments.
2. Separate the Chinese leek into green leaves and white part: cut the leaves into sections and split them, and cut the white part into 4 cm sections. Slice the ginger about 3 mm thick, smash the garlic, cut the long red pepper into rings, chop two sprigs of cilantro and leave the other two whole.
3. Toss the fish head with 5 g of salt, 10 g of light soy sauce and 10 g of Shaoxing wine, add the leek leaves and about one third of the ginger slices, cover and marinate in the refrigerator for 1–2 hours. Before it goes in the pan, drain off the marinade, remove the leek leaves clinging to the fish, and keep the rest of the ginger slices for frying.
4. Heat 30 ml of oil in the pan, turn the heat to low and add the rest of the ginger, frying slowly until the edges are pale golden, then add the white part of the leek and stir-fry until slightly soft. Add the garlic, star anise and dried chilies and stir-fry briefly for about 5 seconds, just until fragrant.
5. Add the fish head pieces and turn gently for 2–3 minutes, until the surface begins to turn white. Pour in the 500 ml of water and add the braising sauce's light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, black pepper and aged vinegar, plus the optional chicken bouillon powder at this point; do not add the reserved 2 g of salt yet.
6. Add the two whole cilantro sprigs, cover, bring to a boil over high heat, then turn to medium heat and hold at a light simmer. When the liquid has reduced by about half, check the largest piece of neck meat and the thick part next to the bone: the fish is cooked only when the meat is completely opaque and comes away easily from the bone. If not, keep braising gently.
7. When the fish head is cooked, uncover and reduce the sauce. When the liquid has reduced to about one third of the starting amount and still flows, taste and add the reserved salt if needed. If the meat is showing signs of loosening, lift the pieces out first and then reduce the sauce, and pour the hot sauce back over at the end. Sprinkle with the chopped cilantro and the pepper rings.

## Key points

- **Green onion and ginger turn black, the whole pot is bitter**: the oil was too hot and the aromatics burned (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry the ginger only to pale golden, and add the fish right after the garlic and chilies have been briefly fried.
- **Sauce is thick but the part next to the bone is raw**: the pieces were too big or piled too thick, and [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) has not reached the centre. Cut pieces to similar sizes, check the doneness of the thick parts, and if the liquid runs dry first, add a little hot water.
- **Fish meat falls apart into crumbs**: it was stirred hard and repeatedly, or cooked a long time after it was done (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Push the pan gently once the shape has set, and you can take the fish head out while reducing the sauce.
- **Sauce too salty**: the salt in the marinade, the soy sauce and the bouillon powder add up and show more once the sauce is reduced. Do not pour the marinade into the pot, do not over-reduce the sauce, and add salt only after a final taste.
