Ragù alla bolognese is built on meat. Tomato gives the sauce its colour and a little acidity, and it suits [fresh egg pasta](/recipes/fresh-egg-pasta/) cut wide. The finished meat is tender and the sauce thick and glossy, clinging to the pasta while keeping a fine, grainy texture.

In 1982 the Accademia Italiana della Cucina registered the recipe for this dish with the Bologna Chamber of Commerce, and updated it in 2023: it states plainly that there is no garlic or herbs, no flour for thickening, and recommends collagen-rich cuts such as shoulder and brisket.

Browning first and simmering after each have a job. Once the water on the surface of the beef has evaporated, the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) brings brown colour and a roasted-meat aroma. Then a low, moist simmer changes the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) in the shoulder and brisket: collagen slowly turns into gelatin, the connective tissue softens and the sauce gets stickier. Although the meat is ground, this step still takes time.

## Steps

1. Cook the minced pork belly in the olive oil in a heavy-bottomed pot. Keep the heat at medium-low and cook until the fat renders and the lean parts change colour, leaving enough fat in the pot to loosen the vegetables.
2. Add the onion, carrot and celery and cook until soft. Turn the heat to low and cook until the vegetables are tender and the onion is translucent, keeping them pale.
3. Add the beef and brown it. Turn the heat to medium and break up the clumps with a wooden spoon. At first it releases water; keep cooking until the steam lessens, the sound changes from bubbling to sizzling and the meat has browned.
4. Pour in the wine and bring to a boil. Scrape up the brown bits on the bottom, and reduce until no liquid wine is left and the pot sizzles lightly again.
5. Add the tomato paste, tomato purée and hot stock and simmer slowly. Add stock until there is a flowing liquid between the meat, cover, and keep the heat low enough for an occasional bubble. Simmer for about 2–3 hours, stirring up from the bottom, and add a little hot stock if it dries out.
6. Partway through the simmer, add the optional milk. Keep simmering on low until the white liquid disappears into the sauce and the liquid has thickened. Without milk, simmer as usual until the meat is tender.
7. Season with salt and black pepper. The finished sauce is a glossy deep reddish brown, and a spoonful is meat coated in thick sauce; if it still looks like soup, uncover and reduce it further, then toss with cooked wide pasta.

## Key points

- **The meat is grey and weakly flavoured**: the meat sat in its own liquid, so the surface could hardly undergo the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Evaporate the water before adding the wine. If the pot is too small, brown the meat in batches in a wide pan and add it back to the vegetables.
- **Simmered a long time, but the meat is still dry and stringy**: the beef was too lean, or the sauce boiled hard after it had reduced dry. Choose a cut with connective tissue, give the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) time to soften, and keep a moist simmering liquid between the meat.
- **The meat still has hard gristle and the sauce is already thick**: the liquid evaporated faster than the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) softened. Add hot stock, cover and keep simmering on low, and reduce the sauce only once the gristle is soft; do not go by colour alone.
