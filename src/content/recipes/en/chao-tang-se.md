Caramel colour is the source of the reddish-brown colour and caramel fragrance of red-braised dishes. This is the water-and-oil method: the sugar dissolves in water first, and once the water has evaporated the temperature keeps rising and the sugar begins to [caramelize](/principles/caramelization/). The colour moves from golden to reddish brown and the bitterness deepens little by little, so when to stop depends on colour and smell together.

One batch uses 50 g sugar, enough to colour about 1000 g of meat; how much you actually add still depends on the colour in the pan. After diluting, it is a thin, oily caramel colour, and its oil and water count toward the whole dish. Caramel colour keeps darkening off the heat, so have the hot water ready beforehand.

## Steps

1. Boil the water and set aside 400 ml to add at the end. Choose a fairly deep pan in which you can see the colour clearly, have a long-handled ladle ready, and make sure you can lift the pan steadily off the heat when adding the water.
2. Put the other 100 ml hot water, the oil and the sugar in the unheated pan, then turn on medium heat. Stir gently until the sugar grains disappear completely; if using rock sugar, crush it first so large pieces do not stay undissolved while some spots have already coloured.
3. Keep heating to let the water evaporate. At first the syrup is clear with a lot of bubbles, then the colour gradually turns yellow. Swirl the pan gently so the heat is even, and try not to scrape undissolved sugar from the pan wall back in.
4. When the syrup goes from amber toward the colour of black tea, turn the heat down. When you see reddish brown and smell caramel, take it off the heat at once and let the pan's residual heat finish the last bit of colour change (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)); do not wait until it turns black or gives off sharp smoke.
5. Stand back from the pan and pour in the reserved hot water slowly along the edge, in several additions. Wait each time for the violent boiling to subside before continuing; the liquid is very hot, so do not reach in to stir through the steam.
6. When all the water is in, return to low heat and stir gently until the hardened sugar melts again, and stop once it comes to a boil. For red-braising, add a little at first and watch the colour; cool the remaining caramel colour, refrigerate it, and stir the oil layer back in before using.

## Key points

- **Only sweet, colour too pale**: the sugar was still dissolving or concentrating and had not [caramelized](/principles/caramelization/) enough. Wait until the colour has truly turned reddish brown before adding the water.
- **Very bitter, colour black**: the heat was too high, or it stayed in the hot pan after reaching the colour (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Adding water will not rescue a bitter batch; start over.
- **Adding the water sends up a lot of foam**: hot sugar and oil flash the water to steam at once. Have the hot water ready beforehand, take the pan off the heat, and add it in stages; do not pour water into a smoking pan all at once.
- **White gritty grains appear**: the sugar has crystallized (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). If it has not burnt, dissolve it again with the water in the recipe, then continue heating.
