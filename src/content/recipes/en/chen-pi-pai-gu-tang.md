Pork rib soup with aged tangerine peel is clear, not cloudy. The ribs are soft without falling apart, and the soup carries the herbal fragrance of the tangerine peel and a few dried herbs. It is made by double-steaming: the stewing pot sits in the hot water of an outer pot, so the soup inside only gets gently hot and never rolls (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

The ribs are [blanched](/principles/blanching/) first and rinsed of foam, so the finished soup has no grey specks. Then a long, gentle stew slowly softens the connective tissue on the ribs, and part of the collagen turns to gelatin, which gives the soup and meat their soft mouthfeel (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). The pieces vary in size, so once the time is up, check the thickest one. A hot soup does not mean tender meat.

## Steps

1. Choose small pieces of ribs of similar size and put them in a pot with cold water to cover. Bring slowly to a boil. When the surface changes colour and the foam gathers on top, lift the ribs out and rinse off the clinging foam with warm water.
2. Rinse the tangerine peel, American ginseng slices, dendrobium, Solomon's seal slices and ophiopogon root one by one, and drain. A whole piece of tangerine peel has three segments; for one serving of soup, use only one segment.
3. Put the ribs in the bottom of the stewing pot and the tangerine peel and other dried herbs on top. Add hot water so it covers the ingredients as far as possible, but not up to the rim; leave some room.
4. Cover the stewing pot and set it on a stable rack in the outer pot. Add hot water to the outer pot to the level your stewing equipment calls for, so it does not boil over into the pot.
5. Once the water in the outer pot comes to a boil, adjust the heat so it keeps a steady gentle boil, cover the outer pot and stew for about 1.5 hours. Check the water in the outer pot partway through, and top up with hot water along the edge of the pot if it is low. Do not let it boil dry.
6. Open the stewing pot. When a chopstick slides fairly easily into the thick meat and the sinew beside the bone has softened, it is done. If the meat is still tight, keep stewing, and check the water in the outer pot again.
7. Once the meat is tender, add a little salt, stir and taste; add more if needed. Use heat-proof tongs to lift the pot out steadily, set it on a trivet first, then ladle out the soup and meat.

## Key points

- **Grey scum in the soup**: only rinsing the ribs with hot water leaves proteins that seep out of the surface to set as grey scum during the real stew. Blanch the ribs first (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)) and rinse off the foam.
- **The soup is very hot but the ribs are still tough**: after the centre warms up, the collagen still needs time to soften (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Keep stewing gently. Do not make the outer pot boil hard to save time.
- **The outer pot is nearly dry, or water floods into the pot**: too much water in the outer pot floods the stewing pot, and too much heat evaporates it fast, which upsets the double-steaming [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Leave the water level your equipment calls for, check on a schedule, and top up with hot water as needed.
