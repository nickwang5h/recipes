This enoki soup is light, and the taste comes entirely from the mushrooms: the compounds that cook out of the mushrooms into the water give [umami](/principles/umami/). Pulling the mushrooms apart lets hot water reach every small bunch, and cutting them short means they break with a bite instead of tangling in the mouth as a whole bunch; see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/).

Plain water is enough; the MSG and the egg can both be left out. With too much water the mushroom flavour is thin, and too much salt and MSG cover it. The egg drops are optional. They go in after the mushrooms are cooked and [coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) in the hot soup, so they do not cook for long along with the mushrooms.

## Steps

1. Cut off the tight base of the enoki, the part that still carries growing medium, and pull the rest apart by hand into thin, loose bunches. Pick out any slimy or discoloured parts.
2. Wash the enoki under running water and cut it into lengths of no more than about 5 cm. Keep pulling apart the thicker bunches, especially checking whether the area near the root is still joined in a thick block.
3. Put the enoki and water in a pot and loosen the mushrooms so the water can surround them. Heat until the whole pot boils; a few bubbles at the edge of the pot do not count as boiling.
4. Start timing when it returns to a boil and keep it boiling for at least 3 minutes. Push down gently from time to time so floating bunches go under the soup, and cook until all of it has softened and there are no cold, hard clumps.
5. If adding egg, beat it well in a bowl first, then pour it in a thin stream into the gently boiling soup. When the egg turns opaque, push it gently to spread it, and cook until no raw egg remains.
6. Add the MSG and a little salt, stir and taste, then add more salt if needed. Cook for about 30 seconds more so the flavour is even, then turn off the heat.
7. Ladle into bowls, and add a few drops of toasted sesame oil if you like. When the sesame oil floats on the soup, stir lightly so every spoonful carries a bit of fragrance.

## Key points

- **Enoki clumps together and is hard to chew**: the root end was not pulled apart and the long fibres tangled. Following the idea in [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/), pull them apart first, then cut short, and loosen them as they cook; do not put them in as one clump.
- **The soup is salty but has no mushroom taste**: the seasoning is too heavy, or too much water covers the [umami](/principles/umami/). Taste the plain soup before adding salt; if there is too much water, boil it down to a stronger soup first, then do the final seasoning.
- **Egg drops are coarse and tough**: the egg was poured in all at once, set in big pieces and then cooked for a long time, so the [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) went too far. Add the egg after the mushrooms are cooked, and serve once the thin pieces are cooked through.
