*Kai yang* is a common charcoal-grilled chicken in Thailand. Cilantro roots, garlic and white pepper are pounded into a fine paste, which clings to the skin and meat together with the fish sauce. Pounding opens up the plant tissue so the aroma is released more easily (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). It is served with a sweet and spicy vinegar sauce and eaten with cucumber.

The chicken is split in half and flattened first, to even out the thickness. The charcoal has to brown the surface for the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) aroma and also give the heat enough time to reach the meat by the bone (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Golden skin is only a judgment of the surface; the doneness of the meat still has to be confirmed with a thermometer.

## Steps

1. Split the chicken in half along the breastbone and flatten it, and pat the surface dry with paper towels. Scrape and wash the cilantro roots, pound them with the salt, garlic and white pepper into a fine paste, then stir in the fish sauce and palm sugar.
2. Rub the marinade evenly over both sides of the chicken, put it in a covered container and refrigerate for about 3 hours. Make sure the marinade also reaches around the thicker leg meat. After marinating, brush off the big clumps of spices piled on the surface.
3. Pound the cilantro roots, salt, garlic and chili for the dipping sauce. Put the vinegar, sugar and water in a small pot, bring to a boil, add the spice paste and simmer gently until reduced by about half. It should coat a spoon in a thin layer when lifted, and will thicken more as it cools.
4. Prepare a medium charcoal fire, banking the coals to one side and leaving the other side as a cooler zone. Put the chicken on once the coals are covered in ash and no flames are leaping up. Start the chicken over the gentler heat and turn it every few minutes so the skin turns golden brown gradually.
5. Keep grilling for about 25–35 minutes, moving the chicken as the colour develops. Insert a thermometer from the side into the thickest part of the thigh and the breast, away from the bone, and take the chicken off the heat only when both reach 74°C (165°F). If one spot is short, move it to the cooler zone and keep grilling.
6. Rest for about 5 minutes before cutting into pieces, and serve with the sweet and spicy dipping sauce and cucumber. Use a clean knife, board and plate for cutting, and do not put cooked chicken back in a container that held raw chicken.

## Key points

- **Skin is charred but the meat by the bone is not cooked**: the fire was too fierce, and the temperature gap between surface and centre was too large. Move to the cooler zone and keep grilling, turning, and judge doneness by the thermometer (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Garlic paste is black and bitter**: the marinade was piled on thick and the spices scorched first. Brush off the big clumps before grilling, and turn more often once the skin starts to colour (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Skin is wet and pale, with weak aroma**: the surface held too much liquid, or the charcoal had not settled. Blot off pooled liquid and wait for the coals to turn ashy before grilling, giving the surface time to evaporate its water (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
