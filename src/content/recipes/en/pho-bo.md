*Phở bò* puts a clear beef broth, flat rice noodles and thinly sliced beef in one bowl. This version uses cooked beef: the brisket is simmered tender in the broth, then sliced for serving. The roundness of the broth comes from the connective tissue in the bones and meat slowly turning to gelatin; see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Ginger, onion and whole spices give it a clear fragrance.

The broth should taste of beef and also let you see the bottom of the bowl. A gentle bubble lets the flavour seep out slowly, while a hard boil tends to break the fat into the broth. The spices are used in small amounts and kept whole; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). Lime, herbs and chili go on the side so everyone can adjust their own bowl.

## Steps

1. Char the onion and ginger. Put them on a rack under the broiler and turn them as they cook, until the skin is blackened in patches, the inside is soft and there is a sweet smell. Remove the blackened skin and crack the ginger; do not let bitter charred bits into the broth.
2. Blanch the bones clean. Cover the bones with cold water, bring to a boil, boil for another 2–3 minutes, then pour off the water and rinse off the clinging foam, and wash the pot too.
3. Make the broth. Bring the bones and the broth water to a boil, skim, and add the brisket, charred ginger and onion, star anise, cloves, cassia, salt, fish sauce and rock sugar. Turn down to a gentle simmer where the surface only bubbles now and then, and do not cover the pot.
4. Take out the brisket. Check after about 90 minutes: when a knife tip goes in and the meat is still intact, lift it out and cover it so it doesn't dry out. Keep the broth at a gentle simmer for about 3 hours in total. Once the meat has cooled a little, slice it thinly against the grain.
5. Strain the broth. Remove the bones and spices and skim off the excess fat on top, leaving a few small beads of oil. The broth should come to about 1900 ml; if too much has evaporated, top up with hot water. It should taste slightly stronger than you would drink it on its own, so that it can carry the unseasoned noodles.
6. Soften the noodles. Soak the dried noodles until they bend, then cook them in a separate pot of boiling water, following the package directions, until soft with no hard core, and drain into the bowls. Warm the beef slices through in the hot broth and lay them on top, then sprinkle on the onion, green onion, cilantro and pepper.
7. Ladle in the boiling hot broth. Blanch the bean sprouts in a separate pot and serve them with the lime, chili and herbs; the noodles keep absorbing broth the longer they sit, so eat as soon as the bowls are filled.

## Key points

- **Cloudy broth with a greasy surface**: a hard boil disperses the fat and churns the foam back into the broth; see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Wash the bones after blanching, keep the broth at a gentle simmer, and skim off the excess fat after straining.
- **Only cassia and star anise come through**: the spices have overpowered the beef; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). Measure by a short piece of cassia and whole star anise, and lift them out early once the fragrance is enough.
- **Brisket is tough while the broth is still thin**: the meat and bones need different amounts of time; see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Take the meat out when it reaches the right tenderness, leave the bones to keep simmering, and judge the strength of the broth at the end.
