Fresh pork buns have a fluffy wrapper and a soft, juicy filling. Yeasted dough is wrapped around a raw green onion, ginger and pork filling and steamed until done. The pork keeps some fat, the water is beaten in in stages, and then the filling is wrapped in the proofed dough. These amounts make 12 medium buns, 3 each for four people.

Yeast produces gas, and the [gluten network](/principles/gluten/) of well-kneaded dough holds the gas in. After proofing, press out the large bubbles and proof once more, so the steamed crumb has fine pores. The filling is first made sticky with [salt](/principles/salt/) and stirring, so it can hold the added water; when steamed, the meat's [protein coagulates](/principles/protein-coagulation/), forming a filling that holds its shape and is still juicy.

## Steps

1. Stir the sugar and active dry yeast into the warm water and let it stand for 10 minutes, until fine bubbles appear on the surface. Add 300 g of flour and stir into shaggy clumps, then add 1 tbsp of oil and knead for 10–15 minutes, until the dough is smooth, soft but not sticky, and stretches continuously when pulled.
2. Cover the dough and let it ferment in a warm place for 40–90 minutes, until about double in volume with fine, dense holes inside. In cool weather judge by volume; if it has not doubled, check every 15 minutes and keep the surface covered.
3. First mix the ground pork with the light soy sauce, salt, ginger and five-spice powder, and stir hard in one direction for 2 minutes until sticky. Add the 45 ml of cold water in 5 additions, stirring about 30 seconds each time until no loose water is visible before adding the next. Finally mix in the green onion and toasted sesame oil, cover and refrigerate for 20–30 minutes, until the filling is thick and holds together when scooped.
4. Lightly dust the work surface with the reserved 10 g of flour. Knead and press the dough for 2 minutes, until it has deflated and the cut surface has no obvious large bubbles, then roll it into a rope and divide into 12 pieces. Flatten each piece and roll from the edge toward the centre into a round wrapper about 10 cm across, about 4 mm thick in the middle and about 2 mm at the edge; keep the unrolled dough covered.
5. Divide the filling evenly into 12 portions and place one in the centre of each wrapper. Support the bottom, pleat along the edge upward and gather it, then pinch it shut tightly. Keep the sealed top free of meat juice and the bottom intact. Place the buns on steamer paper in the steamer tray, leaving at least 2 cm of space between the buns and from the tray wall.
6. Cover the buns and proof a second time for about 15 minutes, until they are slightly larger and the wrapper springs back slowly when lightly pressed. Add 1500 ml of cold water to the steamer, set the tray in place and heat over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water boils and the steam is steady, then turn to medium heat and steam for 18 minutes, until the wrappers are fluffy and the centre of the filling reaches 71°C (160°F). If not, steam for 2–3 more minutes and check again.
7. Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 5 minutes, until the steam eases, then lift the lid slowly and take out the buns. With a tiered steamer, check the thicker buns; when the steam path is crowded, the surface may be cooked while the centre of the filling is not.

## Key points

- **Wrapper is hard, as if it did not rise**: the yeast was not activated, or proofing was not enough. There is not enough gas in the [gluten](/principles/gluten/), so do not start steaming by the clock alone; look for dough that has doubled, then for wrappers that spring back after wrapping.
- **Filling gives off water and the seal will not stick**: too much water was added at once, before the filling had become sticky. Following the action of [salt on meat protein](/principles/salt/), stir until sticky first and then add the water in stages; if there is water at the bottom of the bowl, keep stirring until it is absorbed and refrigerate.
- **Bottom wrapper is wet and the filling is dry and hard**: a bottom rolled too thin leaks juice, and all-lean meat or long steaming loses water. Keep the bottom thick and keep the fat, and stop the heat in time according to the doneness of [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
