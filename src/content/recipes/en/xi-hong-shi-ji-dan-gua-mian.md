The topping for tomato and egg noodles is sweet-sour and thick, with soft egg curds, tossed with chewy noodles. The topping and the noodles are made separately and mixed in the bowl at the end. The eggs are scrambled only until they set into soft curds and then taken out, so the tomatoes have enough time to soften and release juice and the eggs do not get dry-fried along with them (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

A little sugar balances the tomatoes' acidity (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)). The topping only needs to reduce until it is thick enough to cling to the noodles. Cook the noodles until there is no hard core in the centre; once [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) is complete they are cooked through and chewy. Toss them with the topping as soon as they come out of the pot.

## Steps

1. Slice the green onion, keeping the white and green parts separate. Mince the garlic, cut the tomatoes into small pieces, and cut the optional green bell pepper into pieces. Beat the eggs in a bowl until the yolks and whites are fully mixed.
2. Add the vegetable oil to the pan and heat over medium heat until the oil flows easily and does not smoke, then pour in the eggs. Push them gently into large curds. When all the egg has set but the curds are still soft, take them out and leave the oil in the pan.
3. Add the white part of the green onion and the garlic, and stir-fry briefly until fragrant, then add the tomatoes and the green bell pepper. Stir-fry for about 1–2 minutes, until the edges of the tomatoes soften and red juice collects in the pan. Add the light soy sauce and the optional sugar, and stir-fry for 10–15 seconds.
4. Pour in the water for the topping, just enough to cover the tomatoes. After it comes to a boil, return the eggs and add the oyster sauce. Cook over medium-low heat for about 3–5 minutes until the tomatoes are soft, gently pushing from the bottom of the pan now and then to keep it from sticking.
5. When the sauce is thick enough to cling to the egg curds but there is still flowing liquid in the pan, taste and add salt as needed. Add the green part of the green onion and the optional toasted sesame oil. Scoop the topping with its sauce into the noodle bowl rather than leaving it in the hot pan to reduce further.
6. In the same pan, add the water for the noodles and bring it to a boil, then add the dried noodles and loosen them. Keep the water at a gentle rolling boil and cook for the time on the package (usually 3–5 minutes) until there is no hard core. If it is about to boil over, lower the heat or add water.
7. Taste one strand. When there is no hard core, lift the noodles out, drain off the dripping water and put them into the bowl with the topping. Toss until the noodles are evenly coated and eat right away. If you like it wetter, add a little hot noodle cooking water.

## Key points

- **Eggs are hard with browned edges**: the pan is too hot or the eggs were cooked too long, so the [proteins tighten](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and lose water. Push them into curds over medium heat, take them out as soon as they set, and only stir gently while the sauce reduces.
- **Tomato sourness stands out**: the tomatoes are naturally sour, or the sauce was reduced too far. Balance sweet and sour with the small amount of sugar in the recipe, tasting by the [balance of acidity](/principles/acid/), rather than adding more salt.
- **Topping is very salty**: [salty seasonings add up](/principles/salt/), and dried noodles may carry salt too. Save the salt until after you taste the topping, and if it is still too salty add a little unsalted hot water.
- **Noodles are mushy or still hard in the centre**: [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) needs the right amount of time. Floating noodles are not necessarily done; judge by the package time and by tasting, and lift them out once cooked.
