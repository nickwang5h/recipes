*Yaki soba* is Japanese stir-fried noodles made with pre-steamed wheat noodles, usually paired with pork belly, cabbage, onion and a sweet-and-sour sauce. The noodles are thin and springy. The cooked noodle block is heated first so it loosens, then stir-fried with the meat and vegetables; pulling cold noodles apart by force tends to snap them into short pieces.

A home stove needs room for the food to touch the pan. Meat and vegetables piled thick give off water and the noodles go soft in the steam; in batches, spread out, the surface has a chance to dry and develop the aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The size of the pan and the amount you add decide whether the food heats properly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Stir the 5 yakisoba sauce ingredients together for about 1 minute, until the sugar crystals dissolve. Measure out 80 ml for the stir-fry and set the rest of the sauce aside. Cut the meat and vegetables as described in the ingredients, and drain the washed vegetables well.
2. Preheat a wide skillet over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the surface is warm. Add 2 tbsp of oil and the noodle blocks and heat each side for about 1–2 minutes, until the noodles can be teased apart gently. Loosen them into strands with chopsticks and remove, leaving the oil in the pan.
3. Turn the heat to medium-high and stir-fry the pork belly in two batches, about 3 minutes each, until the slices curl, the surface turns white with light brown edges and the centre reaches 71°C (160°F). Remove the first batch, and return it to the pan once the second is done. Sprinkle with black pepper.
4. Add the onion and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the layers separate and the edges are slightly yellow. Add the carrot and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes more, until the sticks are pliable. Keep the vegetables moving so they touch the pan.
5. Add the cabbage and shiitake and stir-fry for about 2–3 minutes, until the leaves are slightly soft and the shiitake have shrunk, with the thick cabbage stalks still crisp. Add the green onion and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the green parts have just started to wilt.
6. Remove the meat and vegetables and divide them into two portions, together with the loosened noodles. Return one portion of meat, vegetables and noodles to the pan each time, pour in 40 ml of sauce, and toss over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes, until the noodles are evenly coloured, no sauce pools in the bottom of the pan, and the noodles are hot through and glossy.
7. Plate each batch as soon as it is done, scatter aonori and beni shoga over each as you like, and eat hot. The sauce should coat the noodles in a thin layer, the cabbage leaves should come apart, and there should be no pool of liquid on the plate.

## Key points

- **Noodles clump and snap the more you pull**: cold cooked noodles were pulled apart hard before they had heated and softened. Heat both sides first, then tease them apart gently along the strands, and keep them loosely spread while they wait. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Noodles are wet and soft, with no toasty flavour**: the vegetables carried water, or too much went in at once and the pan stayed wet. Drain well and stir-fry in two batches at the end, each with room to touch the pan. See [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Sauce scorches on the pan, noodle colour uneven**: the sugary sauce went in too early, and local spots overheated once the water evaporated. Cook the meat and vegetables through first, pour in the sauce only at the end and toss quickly, then take it off once coated. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
