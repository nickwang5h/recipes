Dak galbi is a one-pan dish famous in Chuncheon: chicken thigh pieces stir-fried with cabbage, sweet potato and rice cakes in a sweet and spicy sauce. The thigh meat has fat and the cabbage gives off water, so a wide skillet can cook everything through and reduce the sauce at the same time.

In the first half, vegetable juices and steam carry heat into the chicken and sweet potato. In the second half, the water evaporates so the sauce can cling (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Rice cakes absorb water and soften with heat (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Even after they and the sweet potato are soft, check the chicken for doneness separately.

## Steps

1. Soak the refrigerated rice cakes in 500 ml of cold water for 20 minutes, until stuck-together slices separate and the surface is no longer dry and hard, then drain. Fresh rice cakes that are already soft can go in as they are.
2. Stir all the chili sauce ingredients together and mix into the chicken pieces. Cover and chill for 15 minutes, until every piece is coated in an even layer of red sauce. Use this time to cut the cabbage, sweet potato, green onions and perilla leaves.
3. Preheat a wide skillet over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes, until a small piece of cabbage sizzles when it goes in. Add the chicken with all the marinade, the cabbage, sweet potato and rice cakes, spread them out, and stir-fry over medium-high heat for 5 minutes, until the outside of the chicken changes colour and the cabbage starts to collapse and give off water.
4. Turn to medium heat and stir-fry for 5–8 minutes, until the centre of the sweet potato is mostly soft and a thin layer of liquid is still left in the pan. Turn from the bottom every 1 minute. If the rice cakes start to stick and the sauce darkens fast, add the 60 ml of water you set aside and scrape the bottom loose.
5. Add the green onions and perilla leaves and stir-fry for 3–4 more minutes, until chopsticks can break the sweet potato and the rice cakes are soft and springy. Put a thermometer in the thickest part of the chicken, away from the bottom of the pan. At 74°C (165°F) it is done. If not, stir-fry for 2 more minutes.
6. If there is still liquid on the bottom of the pan, keep stir-frying over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, until the sauce turns glossy and coats the ingredients in a thin layer, then take it off the heat. Serve while hot, and don't leave the rice cakes in a dry pan to keep heating.

## Key points

- **Sauce is burnt but the sweet potato is still hard**: the slices are too thick, or the water evaporated too fast and the centre hasn't been heated. Keep the sweet potato in thin slices, and when the bottom of the pan dries, add water in measured amounts and lower the heat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Rice cakes hard in the centre or stuck in a clump**: the rice cakes were too dry and the pan lacked water. Soak them first, separate the slices as you stir, and wait until the centres are soft before reducing the sauce (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Dry, stringy chicken**: the sauce was reduced for too long and the meat kept losing water. Use a wide pan if possible, and stop when everything is cooked through and the sauce clings (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
