The three-mushroom dish is savoury and well seasoned, with a thin, glossy sauce and whole broccoli alongside. The three mushrooms are fresh shiitake, brown shimeji and white shimeji, and the savoury flavour comes from oyster sauce and light soy sauce (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)). The mushrooms are blanched briefly first, then stir-fried through; the broccoli is cooked separately to keep the florets whole. The two cook at different speeds, so handling them apart makes the texture easier to control (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)).

At the end only a thin glaze is thickened, so the sauce stays on the surface of the mushrooms. The starch must first be dispersed in cold water and then poured into the boiling sauce; once it absorbs water with heat it thickens and turns clear (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). It doesn't matter if the mixed slurry isn't all used; stop when the sauce clings, and leave a little flowing sauce at the bottom of the pan so each bite is slippery and smooth.

## Steps

1. Trim the stem ends off the mushrooms and rinse them; break the brown and white shimeji apart and cut the shiitake into slices about 0.5–1 cm thick. Cut the bell pepper into small pieces, slice the chili into rings, mince the ginger and chop the green onion.
2. Stir the potato starch with the 50 ml of cold water until there are no lumps of dry starch, and set aside. The starch settles to the bottom, so stir it again before it goes into the pan.
3. Divide the broccoli into small florets and cook them in enough boiling water to cover for about 3 minutes. When a skewer goes into the stem and the florets are still whole, lift them out, drain and set aside.
4. Change the water and bring it to a boil, add the 5 g of salt for blanching the mushrooms, add the three mushrooms, separate them and cook for 1 minute. Lift out and drain well, and discard the salted water.
5. Wipe the pan dry, add the oil, and over medium heat add the ginger, chili and bell pepper and stir-fry for about 30 seconds. When you smell the ginger and chili and the bell pepper is coated in oil, add the mushrooms.
6. Add the light soy sauce, oyster sauce and part of the chicken bouillon powder, and stir-fry until the mushrooms are soft and hot through. The liquid from the mushrooms bubbles with the sauces at the bottom of the pan; taste before deciding whether to add the rest of the bouillon powder.
7. Stir the starch slurry and drizzle a little into the bubbling sauce, pushing the mushrooms around as you pour. Each time, wait for the sauce to turn glossy again and check the thickness; stop when it thinly clings to the mushrooms. You don't need to use it all, and don't cook the bottom of the pan dry.
8. Drizzle in the Shaoxing wine, scatter the green onion, toss briefly, and plate as soon as the raw smell of the wine is gone, with the drained broccoli alongside.

## Key points

- **Glaze is like paste and the mushrooms stick together**: too much starch slurry went in at once (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add it in stages and stop when the sauce clings; if it is already too thick, push it loose with a little hot water.
- **Lumps of starch and the sauce is still cloudy**: the starch wasn't stirred evenly, or the sauce wasn't hot enough (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir it again before pouring, and drizzle it in while the sauce is bubbling, then wait for it to turn glossy again.
- **Saltiness overpowers the mushroom flavour**: the saltiness of light soy sauce, oyster sauce and bouillon powder stacks up (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). Use the 5 g of salt only for blanching, add the bouillon powder in stages, and take a piece of mushroom in the last taste.
