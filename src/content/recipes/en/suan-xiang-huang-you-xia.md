Garlic butter shrimp is tender and juicy, and each shrimp is coated in butter and garlic fragrance. The shrimp are seared on their own first, and the garlic is then fried over low heat, so the heat for each can be controlled separately. When the flesh goes from translucent to opaque, [the proteins are setting](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Keep heating after it is cooked and the water leaves, and the texture turns hard.

The garlic is fried briefly in butter so its aroma passes into the fat, which then coats the shrimp when they return to the pan; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). Lemon juice goes in last, and its sourness makes the butter sauce lighter. The shrimp are kept moist by the buttery juices, so spoon the juices from the bottom of the pan over them when plating.

## Steps

1. Remove the head and shell of the shrimp, keeping the tail shell, make a shallow cut along the back and remove the vein. Rinse and blot dry with paper towels, so there are no water droplets on the surface of the shrimp. Mince the garlic finely, remove the seeds from the lemon beforehand and cut the butter into pieces.
2. Add the olive oil to a frying pan and heat over medium until the oil flows quickly. Add the shrimp and spread them in a single layer. Sear one side for about 1–1.5 minutes, and flip once the side against the pan changes colour. Sear the other side briefly in the same way, and take the shrimp out once the thick flesh starts to turn white.
3. Turn the heat to low and add the butter. If the pan is smoking or the bottom is too hot, take it off the heat to cool a little before melting the butter. Add the garlic and stir-fry for about 30 seconds, until you smell the garlic clearly and it is still pale; the garlic is ready.
4. If using white wine, take the pan off the heat for a moment, pour it in, then return it to the stove and cook for about 1 minute, until the sharp wine smell fades and a little moist sauce remains in the pan. Skip this step if you are not using wine.
5. Return the shrimp to the pan together with the juices that collected on the plate, and toss gently for about 1 minute so the garlic butter coats the shrimp evenly. Look at the largest shrimp: the centre should be completely opaque and pearly white, and then it needs no more cooking.
6. Squeeze in the lemon juice, toss evenly and turn off the heat right away. Move the shrimp to a plate, gather the garlic and the sauce from the bottom of the pan with a spatula and spoon it over, and eat hot. Do not leave cooked shrimp in the hot pan waiting to be served, because the residual heat keeps shrinking the flesh.

## Key points

- **The shrimp curl tightly and are tough to chew**: the first sear and the return to the pan add up, and the proteins keep contracting (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Small shrimp cook faster, so the return to the pan is only to coat them in sauce and glance at the centre.
- **The butter and garlic turn black and bitter**: the pan is too hot and the fine garlic and milk solids scorch first (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lower the heat after taking out the shrimp, and add the shrimp as soon as the garlic smells fragrant.
- **Water pools at the bottom of the pan and the shrimp do not brown**: the shrimp were not dried, or too many went in at once, so heat went into evaporating water first (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Blot the surface dry, sear in a single layer in batches, and add the next batch once the pan is hot again.
