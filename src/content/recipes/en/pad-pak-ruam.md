*Pad pak ruam* (ผัดผักรวม) is a mixed vegetable stir-fry that Thai families often make. The vegetables are crisp, with a little glossy sauce that flavours every piece you pick up and does not collect into a bowl of soup on the plate. Garlic is fragrant first, and oyster sauce, soy sauce and a little sugar make a savoury sauce. The vegetables are not fixed: cauliflower, cabbage, gai lan, pea pods and mushrooms are all common.

The difficulty is that the vegetables cook at different speeds. Carrot and cauliflower go in first, then cabbage, pea pods and shiitake, and the leaves last. Thin-cut hard stems let heat reach the centre faster (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). A little water helps hard vegetables cook, but adding too much at once lowers the pan temperature (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a large wok. If your pan is small, cook the vegetables and sauce in two halves.

## Steps

1. Wash and drain the vegetables and cut them to the sizes in the ingredient list. Put them in three bowls: carrot and cauliflower in one; cabbage, gai lan stems, snow peas and shiitake in another; gai lan leaves on their own. Chop the garlic coarsely and keep some small pieces.
2. Stir the oyster sauce, light soy sauce, Golden Mountain sauce, sugar and the 1 tbsp of water for the sauce until no clear sugar grains are left. Keep the 3 tbsp of water for stir-frying beside the stove.
3. Put the oil in a large wok and heat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily. Add the garlic and stir-fry for 30–60 seconds, until the small bits are pale gold and smell of garlic, then add the vegetables right away.
4. Add the carrot and cauliflower with 2 tbsp of the stir-frying water. Keep the heat medium-high and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the carrot looks glossy, the outside of the cauliflower is a little soft with the centre still firm, and the water on the bottom of the pan steams off quickly.
5. Add the second bowl of vegetables and the sauce and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until the edges of the cabbage soften, the pea pods are bright, the shiitake is soft, and the gai lan stems bite through yet stay crisp. If the bottom gets so dry that vegetables stick, add the remaining 1 tbsp of water along the edge.
6. Add the gai lan leaves and stir-fry for 30 seconds more, until the leaves have just collapsed and are still green. Turn off the heat and transfer to a plate. Pour out the little sauce from the bottom of the pan too. Serve right away with rice.

## Key points

- **A lot of water on the plate and the colours look grey**: the vegetables were not drained, or too many went in at once, so the pan's heat went into evaporating water. Cook in two batches and split the sauce in half. Add the water in stages as written, and look for pooled water on the bottom before each addition. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The leaves are mushy and the carrot is still hard**: hard vegetables and leafy ones went in together, and they cook at different speeds. Cut the hard stems thin, break the cauliflower into small florets, and add the leaves only at the end. See [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **The sauce tastes of burnt garlic**: the garlic was chopped too fine, or it cooked to dark brown before the vegetables went in. Chop it coarsely and add the hard vegetables when the small bits are just golden, since the steam from the vegetables brings the pan temperature down. If the garlic has blackened, pour it out and start again. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
