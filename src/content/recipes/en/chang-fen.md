Cantonese rice noodle rolls have a thin, silky wrapper around fresh shrimp, finished with a savoury, lightly sweet soy sauce. The wrapper is mostly rice flour with some wheat starch added, so once steamed it rolls without easily tearing. Without a rice noodle roll drawer, use a shallow metal pan and steam one roll per pan.

The batter sets into a sheet through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The flour sinks to the bottom, so stir it well before every scoop; an even thickness means every sheet cooks through in the same time. The water must be at a boil first, and the pan needs room around its edges for steam to pass, so the heat can get in; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). The batter makes 6 rolls, using a shallow pan of about 18 × 18 cm.

## Steps

1. Soak the mushrooms in 200 ml warm water for 30 minutes, until the caps and centres are soft, then trim the hard stems and slice. Stir the rice flour, wheat starch, salt, 350 ml water and the oil for the batter until smooth, and rest it for 20 minutes, until the flour is fully hydrated and no dry lumps remain when stirred.
2. Make the soy sauce. Heat 0.5 tbsp oil in a small pot over medium heat and stir-fry the onion, garlic and mushrooms for 3–4 minutes, until the onion is soft and slightly golden at the edges. Add 120 ml water, cover and simmer on low for 3 minutes, until the mushroom aroma is clear. Add the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce and sugar and cook for 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves and the sauce returns to a boil. Strain out the solids.
3. Put 1000 ml water in the steamer and bring it to a boil over high heat for 5–8 minutes, until it boils steadily and there is plenty of steam. Set the shallow pan above the water with a gap around its edges. Have a tool for lifting the pan and a straight-edged scraper ready. Divide the shrimp into 6 portions of 25 g each; butterfly the thick ones thinner and pat dry.
4. Brush the pan thinly with oil. Stir the batter again and divide it into 6 portions. Pour one portion into the pan and tilt it until the batter just covers the bottom. Place it in the steamer, cover and steam over high heat for 1 minute, until the batter has just set, then lay one portion of shrimp along one side.
5. Cover and keep steaming for 2 minutes, until the sheet looks slightly translucent and has small bubbles, and the shrimp are opaque all the way through. If the centre of a shrimp is still translucent, steam for another 1 minute and check again. Lift out the pan and let it sit for 1 minute, until the sheet firms up and lifts off without sticking to the scraper.
6. Loosen the edges of the sheet with the scraper and push it into a roll from the shrimp side, then move it to a plate. Before each next roll, stir the remaining batter well and brush the pan with oil again. Check the water level after every two rolls. Once all the rolls are made, pour the soy sauce over them and eat hot.

## Key points

- **The first roll is thin and the last one is thick and tough**: the starch sinks to the bottom of the bowl, so the batter differs in strength from first to last. Scrape up the layer at the bottom and stir it into an even liquid before every pour, so the texture after [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) stays the same.
- **The sheet is sticky and breaks when lifted**: it is under-steamed, or it was scraped hard straight out of the steamer. First check that the sheet looks translucent, then let it sit for 1 minute; the sheet only has enough structure once [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) is complete.
- **The sheet is thick and the shrimp are sitting in water**: too much batter was poured, or the raw shrimp were pressed onto batter that had not set. Steam the thin batter for 1 minute before adding the shrimp to keep the thickness even; butterflying thick shrimp shortens the path for [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
