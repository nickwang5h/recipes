Pork shumai use a thin wrapper to hold the meat filling, with the top open and the bottom pressed flat so each one stands in the steamer, and the filling inside is tender and juicy. The filling has bamboo shoot and fresh shiitake, and the pork aspic and shrimp are optional. The pork is first stirred with salt until sticky, then stock is beaten in a little at a time, using the [effect of salt on meat protein](/principles/salt/) to improve binding and water retention; in the steamer the [proteins coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and set the soft filling in shape.

Even with a thin wrapper, the filling cooks slowly. Steam reaches the surface first, then heat moves toward the centre (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Make each shumai the same size, leave room for steam to circulate, and check the thickest filling before taking them out. With pork aspic, the extra gelatin in the cold filling melts when heated, giving more broth when you bite in, so the opening and the bottom need to be sealed more firmly.

## Steps

1. Mince the ginger and green onion, drain and chop the cooked winter bamboo shoot, and chop the fresh shiitake after removing the tough stems. If using pork aspic, cut it into small dice; if using shrimp, pat dry and divide into small portions, and refrigerate.
2. Add the ginger and green onion, light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and salt to the ground pork, plus the optional sugar and pepper. Stir hard in one direction until the filling changes from loose grains to a sticky mass that clings to chopsticks.
3. Add the stock in stages, stirring each time until no free liquid is visible before adding the next. After it is all absorbed, mix in the bamboo shoot, shiitake and the optional pork aspic, finally mix in the toasted sesame oil, cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes.
4. Divide the filling evenly among the shumai wrappers and gather the sides with the circle of your thumb and finger, leaving the top open. Press the filling lightly so the wrapper clings to the sides, then press the bottom flat. If there is a lot of filling, add it in several additions without tearing the wrapper.
5. If using shrimp, put a small portion on top of each and press lightly to stick. Arrange the shumai on a perforated steamer liner with space between them; keep the raw shumai waiting for the next batch in the refrigerator.
6. Put them in the steamer once the water boils, cover and keep steam coming continuously. Steam for about 8–10 minutes from the moment they go in, and check the centre of the largest one: at least 71°C (160°F) for a pure pork filling, and at least 74°C (165°F) with shrimp. If it has not reached that, keep steaming, and top up with hot water only.
7. When done, lift them out right away; when you gently shake the plate the shumai should stand steady, the wrapper should be soft and the filling should hold its shape. Eat once slightly cooled; if you steam in batches, first make sure there is still enough water in the pot.

## Key points

- **Filling is loose and there is liquid at the bottom of the bowl**: water was added before the meat was stirred sticky, or too much stock went in at once. Develop the stickiness first with the action of [salt](/principles/salt/), then add water in stages, and do not thin the filling with extra liquid.
- **Outside is cooked but the centre is still cold**: the shumai are too big, packed too closely or the steam is insufficient, so [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) cannot keep up. Go by the centre temperature, not the minutes.
- **Filling is hard and dry**: too much lean meat or long steaming [tightens the protein network](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Choose ground pork with fat and take them out when cooked; once the filling is tight, steaming longer will not rescue it.
