Kyuri no sunomono is crisp and refreshing. The sourness is clear and the sweetness softens it, and it often comes with wakame, served alongside grilled fish or fried foods. *Sunomono* refers to a group of dishes seasoned with vinegar, and cucumber is a common main ingredient. The dressing is rice vinegar, sugar and a little soy sauce (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).

This dish draws out water first and dresses afterwards. Thin slicing exposes more of the cucumber, so water moves out easily after salting; squeezing out that water lets the dressing keep its original strength (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). The slices should be thin enough to bend, but thick enough to stay whole after squeezing (see [knife work](/principles/knife-work/)). Japanese cucumbers and the mini cucumbers common in Canada have small seeds and thin skin; deal with the seed core of a thick cucumber first, or tossing will release a lot of water again.

## Steps

1. Put the rice vinegar, sugar, the 1.5 g of salt for the dressing and the soy sauce in a small pot and stir over low heat for 1–2 minutes, until the sugar and salt dissolve completely and the pot is just steaming at the edge. Take off the heat right away without boiling; pour into a shallow bowl and cool for about 5 minutes, until the bowl feels close to room temperature.
2. If using wakame, soak 3 g of dried wakame in 300 ml of cold water for 5 minutes, until the leaves are fully open with no dry, hard spots. Lift out and squeeze out the extra water, cut large leaves into bite-size pieces, and don't add the soaking water to the dressing.
3. Wash the cucumber and trim the ends. If the skin is thick, peel off alternating long strips with a peeler, leaving some of the green skin; if the seed core of an English cucumber is clearly soft and watery, halve it lengthwise and scrape out the seeds first. Take 300 g after trimming and slice about 1–2 mm thin.
4. Toss the cucumber slices with the 3 g of salt for drawing water, mix gently with your hands, and leave for 5 minutes, until there is liquid at the bottom of the bowl and the slices bend. Don't keep pressing and kneading them into pieces.
5. Divide the cucumber into several handfuls and squeeze gently by hand until it no longer drips continuously and the slices are still whole. Pour away the liquid that has seeped out, and put the cucumber and the squeezed wakame in a clean bowl.
6. Pour over the cooled dressing, adding the toasted sesame seeds if using. Toss gently until every slice is coated, divide among small bowls and serve right away; if you need to prepare ahead, refrigerate the cucumber and dressing separately and toss just before eating.

## Key points

- **It gets blander as you eat and water collects at the bottom of the bowl**: the cucumber or wakame wasn't squeezed, or the salad sat too long after tossing. Get the extra water out of both ingredients and combine just before eating. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **The cucumber is limp and has lost its crunch**: it was sliced too thin, kneaded too hard, or salted too long. Slice to an even thickness and squeeze as soon as the slices bend. See [knife work](/principles/knife-work/).
- **The vinegar is harsh, or it is too sweet or salty**: a sharper vinegar was used, or seasoned sushi vinegar. Use plain rice vinegar, dissolve the sugar first, cool the dressing, and taste it together with the cucumber. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
