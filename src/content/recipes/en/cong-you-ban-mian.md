With green onion oil noodles, the first bite is full of green onion aroma, the noodles are slippery, and the sauce is salty with a touch of sweetness. The aroma is made while the oil is infusing: the green onions lose water slowly in the oil, the whites turn golden and the greens turn crisp, and many aroma compounds move into the oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Lower heat brings the colour more slowly, but it draws out the flavour before the onions burn black.

The sauce can be made as a batch and taken out when you eat noodles. This batch makes enough sauce for several meals; this meal starts with 15 ml tossed in, and you can add more after tasting. Once cooked, noodles have a layer of gelatinized starch on the surface that holds sauce best while hot, so toss as soon as they are lifted out; left to cool and drain, they stick into a clump (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

## Steps

1. Wash the green onions and dry them thoroughly. Cut them into lengths of about 5–7 cm, keeping the whites and greens apart. Stir the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and sugar together in a bowl and set aside.
2. Pour 100 ml of oil into a pan and heat over medium-low heat, then add the onion whites. Keep a gentle bubble going and fry until the edges of the whites turn lightly golden, without letting the oil smoke.
3. Add the green parts, turn the heat to low and keep frying for about 15–20 minutes, stirring a few times so the pieces heat evenly. When the onions are golden, the bubbles have clearly died down and the edges are crisp, lift them out; do not wait for them to turn black.
4. Turn off the heat and let the onion oil cool slightly, then slowly pour in the soy sauce and sugar mixture. Stir well; if the sugar has not dissolved, turn the heat back to low for about 1 minute until the sugar grains dissolve, and turn off the heat once the sauce bubbles gently.
5. In another pot, bring 1000 ml of water to a boil, add the dried noodles and gently loosen them. Cook for the time on the package, about 3–8 minutes; break or taste one strand, and lift the noodles out when there is no hard core in the centre and they are still springy.
6. Drain off the visible water and put the noodles straight into a bowl. Stir the green onion oil sauce from the bottom, measure 15 ml and toss it in, turning until every strand is thinly coated with sauce. Taste, add a little more if it is bland, and top with the fried green onions.
7. Store the remaining sauce and fried green onions separately; cool, then seal and refrigerate. Stir the sauce well before using it next time, and eat the fried green onions soon, as they soften when left too long.

## Key points

- **Green onions black and oil bitter**: the heat was too high, or the onions were lifted out too late. The same frying that extracts aroma also browns the onions, so lift them out when golden; residual heat keeps cooking them (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Violent splattering when the soy sauce goes in**: the water in the soy sauce meets oil that is too hot (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Turn off the heat to let it cool first, then pour slowly; if the sugar has not dissolved, return it to the heat briefly.
- **Saltiness varies each time you take the sauce**: oil and soy sauce separate into layers as they stand (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Stir from the bottom of the container before taking some, and do not scoop only the oil from the top.
- **Noodles stick in a clump and won't toss**: cooked noodles left standing have surface starch that glues them together (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Toss as soon as they are cooked; if they won't loosen, add a little hot noodle water instead of cooking them further.
