*Galbi jjim* is a Korean dish of thick-cut beef short ribs stewed in a sweet and salty sauce. The meat is soft but can still be picked up, and the radish and carrot soak up the meat juices. The home method boils the ribs first to skim them, then adds soy sauce, pear, garlic and ginger; the [savouriness](/principles/umami/) of beef and fermented soy sauce holds up the stewing sauce.

Short ribs have a lot of connective tissue and can still be hard to chew when cooked. Sustained moist heat lets [collagen gradually turn into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), which is what makes the meat tender and the sauce smooth and rich. Vegetables soften more easily than meat, so they go in once the ribs are halfway through stewing, which keeps their blocks intact.

## Steps

1. Trim thick fat from the ribs, put them in 2000 ml of cold water and soak them in the refrigerator for 30 minutes, until the water turns slightly pink, then lift out and drain. At the same time, soak the shiitake in 300 ml of warm water for 30–45 minutes, until the centre of the cap is soft and bends, then remove the stems and cut into pieces.
2. Put the onion, ginger slices, garlic cloves, green onion whites and 1200 ml of water for the first boil in a pot and heat over high heat for about 5–10 minutes, until the water boils.
3. Add the ribs and cook for about 3–5 minutes, until the pot returns to a boil. Turn to medium-high and cook for 10 minutes, skimming off the grey-white foam as it appears, until the surface has changed colour and the foam has clearly lessened, then lift out the ribs.
4. Strain the first-boil broth into a measuring cup, skim off the oil on top, and measure out 600 ml. Mix it with the soy sauce, sugar, honey, mirin, pear purée, onion purée, minced garlic, minced ginger and pepper, and pour it back into the pot with the ribs.
5. Cover and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the sauce boils. Lower to a steady gentle bubble and stew for 30 minutes, until the meat starts to loosen at the edges. Turn the ribs once partway so the pieces sticking out of the liquid also pick up the sauce.
6. Add the radish, carrot and shiitake, cover and keep stewing for 20 minutes, until the radish edges are translucent and a chopstick goes in, and the thickest piece of meat comes apart with a gentle twist of a fork. If the meat is still tough, lift out the soft vegetables first, add 100 ml of the leftover broth and stew for another 15–30 minutes, until the sinew and meat also come apart easily, then put the vegetables back.
7. Uncover and reduce the sauce over medium heat for 10 minutes, until it coats the meat in a thin layer and the meat is still in pieces. Add the green onion greens and toasted sesame oil, toss and heat for 1 minute, until the green parts soften, then serve with rice.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but hard to chew**: the collagen has not softened enough. Keep liquid in the bottom of the pot, stew on in 15-minute stretches, and check the sinew in the thickest piece (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).
- **Sauce is too salty and sticky like thick syrup**: too much water was reduced, and the salt concentrated with it. Dilute with 100 ml of the leftover broth each time, and stop at a thin coat (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
- **Vegetables fall apart into the sauce**: they went in too early and are overcooked by the time the meat is soft. Add the large vegetables later, push gently from the bottom of the pot when stirring, and lift out the soft vegetables first when the meat needs more stewing (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
