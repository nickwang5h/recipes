The broth of tomato and tofu thick soup only needs to cling to the spoon slightly, and still flow when ladled. The tomato is stir-fried first to release its juice, so the sourness and [umami](/principles/umami/) go into the base; the tofu and egg ribbons provide a tender smoothness, and a little starch at the end brings the soup to a light, thick consistency.

The egg and the starch each do one job: the egg sets into thin sheets by [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and the starch thickens the liquid by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Heating the tofu through in the soup first, then drizzling in the egg, keeps cold tofu from dragging down the temperature of the soup. Take it off the heat promptly after thickening, because the tomato's acid and continued heating make the thickening thin out gradually.

## Steps

1. Prepare everything: cut the tomato into small dice, cut the tofu into small pieces of similar size, beat the egg until no streaks of white remain, chop the green onion and slice the ginger thin.
2. Take a little cold water from the water for the recipe and stir the starch into a slurry with no dry powder; bring the rest of the water to a boil. The slurry settles when left standing, so stir it again before it goes in.
3. Add the oil to the pot and stir-fry the ginger slice over medium-low heat for about 5 seconds, then add the tomato when you smell the ginger. Stir-fry for about 30 seconds, and keep going until the edges soften and there is red juice at the bottom of the pot.
4. Add the boiling water and boil for about 60 seconds, then add the tofu. Nudge it gently along the edge of the pot and cook until the soup returns to a boil and the tofu is hot through, without using a spatula to turn and break it.
5. Keep the soup at a light boil and drizzle the egg in a fine stream across the surface of the pot. When the egg has turned into opaque thin ribbons, nudge them apart gently, making sure there is no sticky raw egg.
6. Stir the starch slurry again and drizzle it into the lightly boiling soup in portions, nudging gently as you pour. Stop adding it when the soup turns from cloudy white to bright and just clings to the back of a spoon.
7. Add the chicken bouillon powder and part of the salt, stir, taste, and add more salt if needed. Sprinkle with the green onion, cook for about 30 seconds more and ladle into a bowl.

## Key points

- **The thickening forms white lumps**: the dry starch was not dispersed first, so its outer layer [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) the moment it hit the hot soup. Mix it with cold water first, stir up the settled starch before it goes in, and add it in portions.
- **The egg ribbons are hard chunks**: the egg was poured in too fast, or cooked for a long time after setting, so the [protein network tightened](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Drizzle it in a thin stream, and thicken and take the soup off the heat soon after the egg is cooked.
- **The soup is savoury but too salty**: bouillon powder also contains salt, and together they cover the tomato's [umami](/principles/umami/). Add salt in portions; if it is already too salty, dilute with hot unsalted water and accept that the soup will be thinner.
