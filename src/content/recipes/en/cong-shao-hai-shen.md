Braised sea cucumber with green onions is soft and glutinous, in a sauce with a strong onion aroma and deep savoury flavour. The method is to fry the green onion whites until fragrant first, then braise the sea cucumber in that onion oil. The onion whites meet hot oil and their aroma moves into the oil, and the oil carries the flavour to the surface of the sea cucumber (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The onion whites are fried over low heat until lightly golden and lifted out, then returned at the end, which gives a toasty fragrance without cooking them to mush.

Use sea cucumber that has already been rehydrated, cleaned and softened. Dried sea cucumber must first be soaked and cooked until soft; ready-cooked sea cucumber only needs to be thawed according to its package and drained. The braise is very short, just long enough to heat the sea cucumber through and coat it in sauce. The thin glaze at the end comes from starch gelatinizing (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)), so there must still be liquid in the pan before you thicken.

## Steps

1. Drain the prepared sea cucumber and cut it into pieces about 1 cm long. Use only the white part of the large green onion; wash and dry it, and cut it into pieces about 1 cm long as well. With both cut to a similar size, they coat evenly with sauce when tossed.
2. Stir the oyster sauce, light soy sauce and sugar together in a bowl. In a separate small bowl, mix the starch with 10 ml of cold water until there are no lumps, and keep it within reach. Keep the two bowls apart; the thin glaze is only used at the end.
3. Add the vegetable oil to the pan and fry the onion whites over low heat for about 3–5 minutes, turning them gently halfway so the side against the pan cooks evenly. When the surface is lightly golden and the onion aroma is clear, lift them out and leave the oil in the pan. The onion whites do not need to be fried dark brown.
4. Keep the heat low and pour in the sauce, pushing it across the bottom of the pan. Stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the sugar dissolves and the savoury aroma comes up. As soon as the sauce starts to stick to the pan, go straight to the next step and do not keep dry-frying, or the oyster sauce and sugar will scorch.
5. Add the sea cucumber and turn it gently for about 1 minute so the cut surfaces are coated with sauce, then add 100 ml of water. Cook over medium-low heat for about 5 minutes, keeping the liquid at a gentle simmer and turning occasionally, until the sea cucumber is heated through and the sauce slowly thickens.
6. When the liquid has reduced but there is still flowing sauce in the pan, return the onion whites. Stir the starch slurry again and pour in part of it, pushing as you pour. Once the sauce bubbles again and turns clear, check whether it is thick enough, and add the rest if not.
7. When the sea cucumber and onion whites are both coated in a glossy sauce, and the sauce in the pan slowly closes back when a spoon is drawn across the bottom, turn off the heat and plate straight away. The thin glaze thickens as it cools, so leave a little flow in the sauce when it leaves the pan.

## Key points

- **Green onion turned black and the oil is bitter**: the onion pieces stayed on the hot pan after releasing their water (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry over low heat, lift them out when lightly golden, and throw away any burnt parts.
- **Glaze is lumpy and sets as a thick paste**: the starch was not mixed smooth first, or the sauce was reduced too dry (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Keep liquid in the pan and add the slurry in stages instead of all at once.
- **Outside of the sea cucumber is too salty**: the salt in the sauces gets more concentrated as the sauce reduces (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Use only light soy sauce and oyster sauce with no added salt, and stop when the sauce is thick enough.
