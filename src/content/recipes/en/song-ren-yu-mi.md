Corn with pine nuts puts two textures together: plump, juicy sweet corn kernels and dry, crisp, fragrant toasted pine nuts. A little starch and water make a thin glaze that coats the corn with sugar and salt, which relies on [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The glaze only needs to make the corn shiny; it should not bind the whole dish into a mass you could lift out in one piece.

The pine nuts go in last and come off the heat once tossed: staying only briefly in the wet glaze keeps them from going soft, and the aroma isn't lost in a long stir-fry (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The corn is already cooked and the carrot is cut into small dice, so once in the wok only three things remain: heating, seasoning and glazing.

## Steps

1. Prepare the corn. Drain canned corn in a colander until the liquid is gone and weigh out 200 g. If using fresh corn, cook the kernels first, then drain and weigh them. Don't stir-fry raw and cooked corn together.
2. If using carrot, cut it into even small dice and blanch it in boiling water for about 1 minute. If the carrot still has a hard core, keep cooking until it is done but still has some bite. The cooked corn can be blanched briefly with it at the end. Lift out and drain thoroughly.
3. If the pine nuts are already cooked and crisp, set them aside as they are. To add a little toasted flavour, toss them in a dry pan over low heat until slightly golden and smelling nutty, then pour them out, and don't leave them in the hot pan where they keep darkening.
4. Mix 5 g of starch with 20 ml of cold water until there are no dry lumps. In a separate wok, add 15 ml of oil and heat over medium heat. Stir-fry the carrot briefly first, then add the corn, spread it out and toss until all the kernels are heated through.
5. Add the sugar and salt, and stir-fry until the sugar dissolves and the seasoning is evenly spread. If there is canning liquid or blanching water in the bottom of the pan, cook it off before thickening.
6. Stir the starch slurry again, pour it into the hot wok in several additions, tossing quickly as you pour. Wait until the glaze turns from cloudy to clear and clings to the surface of the corn before deciding whether to add more. Stop once it already coats thinly.
7. Add the toasted pine nuts, toss gently and plate at once. The kernels should separate one by one, the surface should be glossy with no starchy taste, the pine nuts should still be dry and fragrant, and no glaze should run at the bottom of the plate.

## Key points

- **Forms lumps and has a raw starch taste**: the slurry settled and wasn't stirred again, or dry starch was sprinkled directly into the hot wok. Before [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), mix the starch with cold water first, and stir-fry until clear after it goes in the wok.
- **Corn sticks together in a clump**: there is very little water left and all the starch went in at once, so after [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) the glaze is too thick. Add the thin glaze in several additions, stop when the surface turns shiny, and don't force in leftover slurry.
- **Pine nuts are soft or burnt and bitter**: they were stir-fried for a long time in the wet glaze, or the small nuts were overheated. Take the pine nuts out as soon as they smell fragrant while toasting, and stop as soon as they are tossed in at the end. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
