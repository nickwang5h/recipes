Taiwanese red-braised beef noodle soup builds a red-braised soup on chili bean paste and soy sauce. Beef shank is stewed slowly until the tendon is soft and the meat is tender, and served with wheat noodles, greens and stir-fried pickled mustard greens. The tomato and daikon are simmered into the soup, then strained out at the end, so the beef and noodles in the bowl are easier to pick up.

The tendon in the shank needs sustained moist heat to turn from tough into soft [gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Keep stewing after the meat changes colour, and check at the thickest part. The chili bean paste, ginger and garlic are fried first to bring out their fragrance before the water goes in, so the aroma carries into the soup and oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The noodles are cooked separately so that the starch they release doesn't cloud the whole pot of broth.

## Steps

1. Blanch the shank. Put the whole shank in 3000 ml of cold water and heat over high heat for about 15 minutes, until the water is at a rolling boil and the foam gathers. Cook for 1 minute more, until new foam clearly lessens, then lift out, rinse off the foam on the surface, and discard the blanching water.
2. Fry the aromatics. Put 1.25 tbsp of oil in a heavy-bottomed pot, add the green onion sections, onion, carrot, ginger and garlic, and stir-fry over medium-high heat for 5 minutes, until the edges of the onion are slightly golden and the smell is strong. Add the tomato and daikon and stir-fry for 2 minutes more, until the tomato starts to release juice.
3. Fry the paste. Push the vegetables to one side, add the chili bean paste and tomato paste, and stir-fry over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until the oil turns red and the paste smells fragrant. Pour in the Shaoxing wine, scrape the bottom of the pot loose and cook for 1 minute, until the sharp smell of wine fades.
4. Stew the shank. Return the beef and add the light soy sauce, brown sugar, star anise, fennel seeds, Sichuan peppercorns, cassia and 1150 ml of water. Cook over high heat for about 10 minutes, until the soup is at a rolling boil. Cover, turn to low heat and cook for 180–240 minutes, keeping a gentle simmer; stop when a chopstick goes through the thickest part and the tendon is soft. If it is still tough, cook for another 20–30 minutes.
5. Finish the soup and meat. Take out the shank and leave it for 10 minutes, until you can hold it steadily, then slice it across the grain 5 mm thick. Strain the soup through a sieve to remove the vegetables and spices, put the slices back in the soup, heat over low heat for 3 minutes until the meat is hot through, and taste the soup to adjust the salt.
6. Stir-fry the pickled mustard greens. Rinse the pickled mustard greens, squeeze dry and cut into small dice. In another pot, heat 1 tbsp of oil, add the garlic and chilies and stir-fry over medium heat for 30 seconds, until the garlic is fragrant. Add the pickled greens and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, then add the sugar and stir-fry for about 2 minutes more, until there is no pooled liquid and the sour aroma is clear. Remove from the pot.
7. Cook the noodles and assemble. In another pot, heat the noodle water over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until at a full boil. Blanch the bok choy for 1–2 minutes, until the stems bend but are still crisp, then lift out. Cook the fresh noodles in batches for 3–5 minutes, until there is no white core in the centre; if the package gives a different time, follow the package and taste one strand first. Divide the noodles among bowls, pour over the hot beef soup, and add the beef, greens, pickled mustard greens and chopped green onion.

## Key points

- **Beef is cooked but the tendon can't be bitten through**: the collagen in the tendon hasn't softened yet (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Check the thickest part of the whole shank, keep stewing at a gentle simmer, and don't rush to slice while the tendon is firm.
- **Soup tastes burnt and bitter**: the chili bean paste or tomato paste scorched in a dry pot (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry the paste over medium-low heat, and add the wine and water as soon as the red oil and aroma come out.
- **Broth is thick and cloudy and the noodles are sticky**: noodles cooked for a long time in the broth keep releasing surface starch through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) into the soup. Cook the noodles in a separate pot, ladle on the soup as soon as they are lifted out, and add the pickled mustard greens only when assembling the bowl.
