*Chawanmushi* is a savoury egg custard steamed in small cups, with kombu and bonito dashi carrying the flavour of the chicken, shrimp and vegetables. The seasonal fillings can change; the eggs and dashi are the framework. Heat makes the proteins form a network that holds in the liquid, giving a texture that can be scooped and still gives a slight tremble (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).

**The dashi is about three times the volume of the beaten egg**, thinner than many home steamed eggs. Egg size changes this ratio, so measure the beaten egg first; matching the stock to that is more reliable than counting eggs. Steam carries heat to the wall of the cup, and the centre warms up gradually; if the outer layer is heated too hard, it contracts first and forms holes (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cover the cups and keep the steam gentle so the inside and outside set at close to the same time.

## Steps

1. Cut the chicken into pieces about 1 cm, toss with the sake and usukuchi soy sauce for marinating, refrigerate for about 15 minutes, and drain off the marinade. Devein the shrimp, trim and thinly slice the shiitake, and cut the tender spinach leaves short.
2. Blanch the shrimp in boiling water for about 30 seconds, then lift out and drain. Divide the chicken, shrimp and shiitake among four heatproof small cups, spreading the fillings loosely on the bottom without packing the cup full.
3. Gently break up the eggs and measure the volume. Mix three times that volume of room-temperature dashi with the salt, mirin and custard usukuchi soy sauce, and pour slowly into the egg, stirring gently without whipping air in.
4. Pass the egg mixture through a fine sieve to remove the stringy egg white and skim off the foam, then pour into the cups, leaving about 1.5 cm below the rim. Put the spinach on top, and cover each cup with a heatproof lid or aluminum foil to keep out dripping condensation.
5. Put the cups in a steamer that is already steaming, leaving space between them. Turn to medium-low heat so steam rises steadily and the water stays at a gentle simmer, and steam for about 20 minutes. Do not let the cups rattle in violently rolling water.
6. Check the centre of the custard: a thin skewer pushed in should release clear liquid, and the whole surface should tremble slightly when you shake the cup gently. Use a thin probe to confirm that both the custard and the chicken in the centre have reached 74°C (165°F); if there is still raw egg, steam a little longer. Once done, take them out right away and serve with the lids on.

## Key points

- **Honeycomb holes, water pooling, coarse and firm texture**: the steam was too strong or it steamed too long, and the protein network contracted. Reduce the heat, use cups of similar size, and take them out as soon as the centre is cooked (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **A layer of water on top, many dimples**: condensation dripped into the egg, or too many bubbles were beaten in when mixing. Cover each cup separately, stir the egg gently, sieve it and skim off the foam (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Outer ring cooked, centre still liquid**: the cup is too deep, the fillings are packed too tightly, or there is more stock than the egg can support. Match the stock to the measured volume first, then spread the fillings out; keep steaming over low heat until the centre is cooked through.
