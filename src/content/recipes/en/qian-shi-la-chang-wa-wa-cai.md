Guizhou-style cured sausage with baby napa cabbage uses only sausage, cabbage and water. The sausage is boiled first, so its salt and some of its fat pass into the water; the baby napa cabbage then takes up the broth, and the clean sweetness of the leaves goes together with the salty savouriness of the sausage. All of the seasoning comes from the sausage's own [salt](/principles/salt/); taste before deciding whether to add anything else.

The centre of a whole sausage heats more slowly than its outer layer, while thin cabbage leaves soften very quickly. Adding the two at different times makes use of their different thicknesses and [heat transfer rates](/principles/heat-transfer/). "Guizhou-style" in the title means Guizhou-style cured sausage is used; with another sausage, the saltiness and flavour will differ.

## Steps

1. First check the sausage package for a specified cooking time. Thaw frozen sausage in the refrigerator first; a sausage that is still frozen hard cannot use the times below.
2. Remove any bad leaves from the baby napa cabbage, leave a little of the base attached to the leaves, and cut it lengthwise into quarters. Rinse between the leaves and drain. Split especially thick bases further so water can reach the centre of the stems more easily.
3. Bring 750 ml water to a boil in a small pot. The water must be deep enough to cover the sausage; if the pot is too wide, switch to a narrower one or add hot water. Do not let half the sausage hang out of the water and heat only from the lower half.
4. Add the whole sausage, and once the water returns to a boil, turn to medium-low heat and keep it bubbling steadily. Start checking from about 13 minutes, turning the sausage once in between so every part is surrounded by hot water.
5. Insert a thermometer from the end of the sausage into the centre of the thickest part. A pork sausage must reach 71°C (160°F) and also meet the package directions; if it has not, keep cooking. 13 minutes is only the time for the first check.
6. Add the baby napa cabbage with the stems down and the leaves submerged in the broth, and cook for about 2 minutes. Lift it out when the leaves have collapsed and brightened in colour and the stems bend but still have some bite; if the base is thick and still hard, keep cooking briefly.
7. Lift out the sausage and the cabbage and drain the water from between the leaves thoroughly. Let the sausage cool just enough to handle, then slice it; cut the cabbage quarters into bite-size pieces, keep some whole leaves, and arrange everything on the same plate.
8. Taste a slice of sausage together with a mouthful of cabbage and eat while hot. If the cabbage is bland, toss it with a little of the cooking broth, then drain off the excess liquid.

## Key points

- **Outside is soft but the centre is not cooked**: the thickness of the sausage, its starting temperature or the water level in the pot changed the [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Judge by the temperature at the centre of the thickest part and the package directions; do not treat a fixed number of minutes as a guarantee.
- **Baby napa cabbage is limp with no bite**: it was cooked from the start with the sausage, so the leaves were heated too long (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cook the sausage first and add the cabbage last; split thick stems beforehand.
- **Sausage is dry and the cabbage is not seasoned**: the sausage lost water from long cooking, and the leaves carry a lot of plain water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take the sausage out once it is cooked, and drain the leaves before eating them with the sausage slices.
