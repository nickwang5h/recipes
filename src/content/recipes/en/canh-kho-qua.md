*Canh khổ qua* is a Vietnamese home-style clear soup of pork-stuffed bitter melon. The melon flesh stays slightly bitter once soft, the wood ear is crunchy, and short glass noodles sit in the filling. Choose long bitter melon with wide, rounded bumps on the surface, which is usually milder than the small kind with dense sharp points; after the seeds and white pith are scooped out, the soup still keeps the bitterness this dish is meant to have.

When the filling is stirred until sticky and the proteins set with heat ([protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), it joins into one piece inside the melon tube. The soup only barely bubbles, so heat slowly reaches the centre; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). A hard boil knocks the melon pieces apart and also makes the outer layer of meat tighten first.

## Steps

1. Soak the wood ear and glass noodles in 500 ml of warm water for 15 minutes, until the wood ear has fully opened and the noodles bend. Drain and rinse. Cut off the hard stems of the wood ear and mince it, and cut the noodles into short pieces about 1 cm long.
2. Put the ground pork, wood ear, glass noodles, shallot and white part of the green onion in a bowl, and add the filling's bouillon powder, fish sauce, sugar, salt and pepper. Stir in one direction for 2 minutes, until the filling is sticky and can be lifted by chopsticks as a clump.
3. Trim both ends off the bitter melon and cut it crosswise into tubes 4 cm tall. Scoop out the seeds and white pith with a small spoon, keeping the walls intact. Press the filling in a little at a time and level the ends. Shape the leftover filling into meatballs about 3 cm across and add them to the soup too.
4. Put the soup water, bouillon powder, sugar and half the salt in the pot, cover and cook over medium-high heat for about 6–8 minutes, until the surface bubbles continuously. Gently lower in the bitter melon and meatballs, bring back to a boil for about 2 minutes, skim off the foam, and turn to low heat.
5. Cover the pot halfway and cook for 30 minutes, until only scattered small bubbles show on the surface, the melon turns a dull olive green and chopsticks pierce the wall easily. Check the centre of the thickest piece of filling; it should reach 71°C (160°F). If it is still firm or has not reached that temperature, cook 5 minutes more at a time.
6. Add the soup's fish sauce and pepper, taste, and decide whether to add the remaining salt. Cook over low heat for 1 minute, until the flavour of the soup is even, and sprinkle on the green part of the green onion. Lift the melon and filling out with a large spoon into bowls, and serve with white rice.

## Key points

- **Filling scatters into the soup**: the filling was not stirred until sticky, the noodles were too long, or the tubes were packed loosely. Cut the noodles short first and pack the filling in firmly in several additions; once in the pot, move it little, so [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) can form a support.
- **Melon is still firm but the meat is already tight**: the melon pieces were too tall, or the heat was too strong. Cut them all to 4 cm and keep low heat, so the melon wall and the centre of the filling finish cooking together; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Soup is cloudy and the melon edges are broken**: it boiled too hard and the foam was not skimmed in time. Lower the heat right after it comes to a boil, and support the base of the melon when ladling; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
