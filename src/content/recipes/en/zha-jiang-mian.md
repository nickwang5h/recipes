All the flavour of zhajiangmian is in that spoonful of meat sauce. Soybean paste and sweet bean sauce already have the [salty savouriness and sauce aroma that come from fermentation](/principles/fermentation/), and when they are slowly fried open in oil, the steam is driven off and the flavour clings tightly to the noodles. The sauces bring their own salt, so do not add any when frying the sauce.

Here the sauce is a meat sauce with equal parts soybean paste and sweet bean sauce, served with shredded cucumber. The noodles should be on the thick side, cooked until there is no white core and still some bite. The noodles cook through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and if cooked too long they go limp; after cooling in water, drain them so the sauce can cling.

## Steps

1. Rinse the cucumber and cut it into fine shreds on a clean plate. Mince the optional garlic and chop the green onion. Use a separate board for the raw meat, and keep the toppings for the end.
2. Mix the two sauces together. Use wet soybean paste, which can be mixed in directly; dry yellow bean paste has to be thinned with water separately, and cannot be swapped in at the same weight.
3. Heat the oil, add the chopped green onion and ground pork, and break the meat into small bits as you stir-fry. Cook for about 3–5 minutes, until there are no big clumps of meat, the steam has clearly lessened and the pork is cooked through, then turn to low heat. Do not let the green onion scorch black alone in the hot oil.
4. Pour the mixed sauces into the pan and keep pushing and stir-frying, scraping up the sauce that sticks to the bottom. Stir-fry for about 2 minutes, until the sauce smells strongly and is thick yet still flows. When you lift the spatula, the sauce should cling to the meat bits; then lift it out. If the sauce is already very dry, take it off the heat.
5. Cook the noodles in plenty of boiling water (fresh noodles about 3 minutes), nudging them apart gently once they are in. Before lifting them out at the time on the package, break one of the thicker strands: if there is no dry white core in the centre and it is chewy when bitten, lift them out.
6. Rinse the noodles briefly in cold drinking water and lift them out at once to drain. Hold the strainer up for a while, until it stops dripping steadily, then put the noodles in bowls. If you like them hot, skip the cold rinse and just drain.
7. First toss the noodles with part of the sauce, until each strand is coated, then taste for saltiness and add the rest of the sauce. Finally add the cucumber shreds and the optional raw garlic and toss. Keep the toppings crisp and do not let water collect at the bottom of the bowl, or it will dilute the sauce.

## Key points

- **The sauce tastes burnt and bitter**: thick sauce has little water, so the part against the pan overheats in spots (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Turn the heat to low after adding the sauce and keep pushing along the bottom; do not scrape the blackened part in.
- **Noodles are slippery but won't hold the sauce**: the water from cooking or cooling was not drained, which diluted the sauce and washed off the starch on the surface (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Drain first, then toss with a little sauce at a time, instead of making up for it with more sauce.
- **Too salty in one bite**: each brand of wet sauce has a different salt level, and reducing it concentrates the saltiness even more (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Mix the fried sauce in in portions and add the toppings last; if there is sauce left, keep it and do not tip it all in the bowl.
