*Poulet rôti* is the classic home roast chicken: a whole chicken roasted with butter, with golden skin and tender meat, and the juices in the roasting pan reduced separately into a sauce. Breast and legs differ in thickness and muscle, so the chicken is first coloured at high heat, then the heat is lowered and the bird turned, changing the direction of [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) so that the skin and the centre each finish just right.

Dry the skin so the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) can build flavour in the oven. Basting uses fat, and avoid spooning the watery juices from the bottom of the pan over the skin again and again. The golden-brown bits of juice at the bottom become the base flavour of the sauce; the stock dissolves them, and after reducing, butter is stirred in. The sauce is small in quantity but enough for the whole chicken.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). Dry the chicken and its cavity with paper towels; do not rinse the raw chicken. Season the cavity with salt and rub in 15 g soft butter, rub the remaining 15 g on the skin, loosely truss the legs together with kitchen string, and tuck the wing tips behind the back.
2. Place the chicken breast side up in a shallow roasting pan just big enough to hold it, and scatter the carrot and onion around it. Melt the other 30 g butter and mix it with the vegetable oil.
3. Roast for the first 15 minutes, turning the chicken every 5 minutes or so onto its left side, then its right side, and after each turn basting with a layer of the butter and oil. As soon as the skin turns pale golden, lower the oven to 175°C (350°F) and keep roasting on its side.
4. Every 8–10 minutes, quickly baste with fat, and once the butter is used up, spoon up the fat floating on top in the pan. At about the halfway point, turn the chicken onto the other side; near the end, turn it breast side up to colour the front.
5. Start checking at about 70 minutes of total roasting time. Insert the probe into the thickest meat at the top of the leg, away from the bone, and recheck the thick part of the breast, to confirm that the coldest spot of the whole chicken reaches 82°C (180°F); if not, keep roasting. Move it to a plate, cut off the string, and rest for about 10 minutes.
6. Pour most of the fat out of the roasting pan, leaving about 2 tbsp, and cook the minced shallot in it until soft. Pour in the chicken stock, scrape up the golden-brown juices with a wooden spoon, and boil down to about 120 ml. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper, take off the heat, stir in 15 g butter in batches, strain, and serve with the carved chicken.

## Key points

- **The skin is very dark but the meat near the bone is not cooked**: the surface coloured faster than the centre warmed (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lower the heat once it is pale golden, loosely cover with foil when the colour is enough, and let the thermometer decide when it comes out.
- **The breast is dry and hard**: it kept roasting after the centre was done, and the muscle proteins kept shrinking and squeezed out water. Measure at set times and take it out when it reaches doneness; a bigger chicken cannot use the minutes for a small one (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **The sauce is bitter and tastes burnt**: the browned bits on the bottom of the pan have blackened. Golden brown can dissolve into the sauce, black should be avoided; use a pan that is not too large, and add a little hot water if the bottom is about to dry out (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
