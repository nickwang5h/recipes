The ribs are glossy red and soft enough that the meat comes easily off the bone, in a sauce that is rich but not sticky. The sugar is heated in the pan first until it turns a pale amber with a hint of caramel, and that colour is then coated onto the ribs; this is [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/). The soy sauce added afterwards brings saltiness, savouriness and a deeper colour. The caramel must not be cooked to dark brown.

The connective tissue around ribs softens only with moist heat and time; see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Simmer first over low heat until the meat comes easily off the bone, then uncover and reduce the sauce, so tenderness and the thickness of the sauce can be controlled separately. The five-spice powder and the chili are only a background flavour.

## Steps

1. Put the ribs in a pot of cold water with the ginger slices and Shaoxing wine, bring to a boil, skim off the foam and boil for another 2–3 minutes. Lift out, gently wash off the clinging foam with warm water, drain and blot off the surface water.
2. Put 30 ml of oil and 15 g of sugar in the pan and stir over low heat. Once the sugar crystals melt, watch closely. When the liquid turns from pale yellow to a light amber and gives off fine bubbles, add the ribs at once and stir-fry for about 30 seconds to coat them in the caramel. Taking the pan off the heat for a moment also slows the colour change.
3. Add the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, five-spice powder, garlic cloves and the chili, split open, and stir-fry for about 30 seconds more. Add the water as soon as the five-spice powder is mixed in, and do not let the fine powder fry dry against the hot pan for long.
4. Slowly pour hot water in along the edge of the pan, about 500 ml to start; just covering the ribs is enough. Bring to a boil over high heat, then turn to low, cover and stew for 30 minutes, keeping the liquid at a gentle bubble. Check the water level halfway and turn over any ribs that stick out of the liquid.
5. Add the dark soy sauce for colour: pour in half first, stir, then decide whether to add the rest. Keep stewing over low heat for about 10 minutes. Test the thick meat with a chopstick; it is ready when the chopstick goes in fairly easily. If there is still tough sinew, add hot water and keep stewing, and do not rush to dry it out.
6. When the ribs are soft, uncover and reduce the sauce over medium-high heat. Turn gently, and switch off the heat when the sauce is slightly thick and leaves a glossy film on the ribs, with some flowing sauce still in the bottom of the pan. The sauce thickens further as it cools, so do not cook the bottom of the pan to sticky sugar lumps when plating.

## Key points

- **The colour is deep but the taste is bitter**: the caramel was cooked too far, or the spice powder burned (see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) and [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the meat when the sugar reaches a light amber, and add water as soon as the powder is mixed in. Adding more sugar does not fix a burnt bitter taste.
- **Ribs are cooked but still hard to bite**: the meat cooks before the connective tissue, which has not softened yet (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Judge by the resistance of a chopstick and whether the meat comes easily off the bone. Stewing gently is easier to control than a constant hard boil.
- **Five-spice flavour covers the pork**: the powder releases its aroma quickly (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Start with 2 g of five-spice powder for 300 g of ribs, and do not keep adding powder because the liquid looks pale; adjust colour with the caramel and dark soy sauce.
