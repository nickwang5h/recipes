*Pla rad prik* is a whole fried fish covered with a sweet, sour, salty and spicy sauce. The skin is crisp, the sauce clings to the fish, and a little loose sauce is left at the bottom of the plate. Garlic and chili are fried until fragrant first, then palm sugar, fish sauce and tamarind are reduced to a thin syrup. Tamarind provides the sourness, and the sugar and fish sauce hold it up (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).

The crispness of the skin comes from hot oil frying off the water at the surface (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Slashing the fish on both sides lets heat reach the centre of the thick flesh faster; to tell whether it is done, check the thickest piece next to the bone (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The water in the sauce would soften the skin again, so the fish and the sauce are made separately and only combined at serving.

## Steps

1. Cut 3 diagonal slashes into the thick flesh on each side of the fish, down almost to the bone. Press the skin, cavity and slashes dry with paper towels until the paper shows no obvious wetness. Pound the garlic, cilantro roots, white pepper and both chilies together into a coarse paste.
2. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a small pot and stir-fry the chili paste over medium heat for about 2–3 minutes, until the steam lessens and the garlic smell is strong, but the garlic has not browned. Add the palm sugar and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until it starts to melt.
3. Pour in 60 ml of water, the fish sauce and the tamarind, and simmer over low heat for 1–2 minutes, until all the sugar crystals dissolve and the sauce coats the back of a spoon like a thin syrup. Turn off the heat and leave the sauce in the pot.
4. Heat the frying oil in a deep pot for about 6–8 minutes, to 175°C (345°F), when ripples show on the surface and it does not smoke. Slide the whole fish in slowly along the side of the pot and fry over medium heat for about 6–7 minutes, until the skin on the underside is golden and lifts from the bottom of the pot. Do not pry it up with a spatula before the skin has set.
5. Turn the fish with two spatulas to hold it steady and fry another 5–7 minutes, until the skin is crisp on both sides, the flesh at the thickest part is opaque, and the flesh next to the bone flakes easily. If any flesh is still translucent, fry 1 minute more at a time and check again. Lift out the fish and drain on a wire rack for 1 minute, until it stops dripping oil.
6. Reheat the sauce for 30–60 seconds, until it bubbles at the edges, then take it off the heat and stir in the cilantro leaves. Put the fish on a plate, spoon 2 tbsp of sauce over it and pour the rest around, and serve at once with white rice.

## Key points

- **The skin sticks and tears**: the surface was not dried, or the fish was flipped before the skin set. Press the slashes dry before it goes in, and flip only when the skin is golden and releases on its own (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **The skin is deep brown but the flesh by the bone is still translucent**: the fish is too thick, or the oil too hot. Choose a small fish that fits in the pot, slash the thick flesh, and check whether the flesh next to the bone is done (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The sauce is so sour it is harsh**: the tamarind is too strong. Choose the brown, runny Thai kind and measure it accurately; concentrate cannot go in at these spoonfuls (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
