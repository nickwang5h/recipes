*Cà ri gà* is Vietnamese home-style chicken curry. The chicken and potatoes braise in one pot, and the broth carries the aroma of lemongrass, yellow curry powder and coconut milk. Plenty of broth is left, enough to dip a baguette in, or to serve with rice or rice vermicelli. Choose a Madras-style curry powder, so that both the spice and the sweet fragrance of the coconut milk come through.

Mince the lemongrass and fry it briefly with the shallot and garlic in oil, so the aroma spreads through the whole pot with the fat; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The chicken legs cook first and the vegetables go in later, which gives the bone-in meat enough time and lets the potatoes keep their shape. After the coconut milk goes in, keep the pot at a gentle simmer. The broth should be rich and able to flow, and still look like soup, not paste, when you spoon it up.

## Steps

1. Marinate the chicken. Pat the pieces dry and toss with the curry powder, 1 tsp of salt, the optional bouillon powder, the 15 g of sugar from the marinade and the pepper. Cover and refrigerate for 2 hours, until the surface is evenly coated in the yellow marinade. Remove the tough outer leaves of the lemongrass and cut the tender core into fine pieces. Mince the garlic and shallot, and cut the vegetables as listed.
2. Fry the aromatics. Heat the oil in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat for about 1 minute, until it spreads easily but has not begun to smoke. Add the lemongrass, garlic and shallot and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes, until clearly fragrant and the shallot starts to turn translucent. Add the chicken and its marinade right away and toss for 2 minutes, so the surface is coated in the fragrant oil.
3. Braise the chicken. Pour in 720 ml of water and cook over medium-high heat for 3–5 minutes, until the whole pot is boiling. Cover, turn the heat to medium-low and cook for 45 minutes, until the meat by the bone can be pulled loose with chopsticks and the thickest part reaches 74°C (165°F). Turn the pieces once partway through. If it is not cooked through, cook 5 minutes more at a time.
4. Cook the vegetables. Pour in the coconut milk and add the potato, carrot and onion. Leave the lid slightly open and cook over low heat for 10–15 minutes, until a fork pierces the potato easily but the corners are still intact and only small bubbles break the surface of the broth.
5. Season and serve. Add the fish sauce and the remaining 15 g of sugar, stir gently and cook for 1 minute, until the sugar is fully dissolved. Turn off the heat, sprinkle on the optional cilantro, and ladle into bowls with the flowing broth. Serve with a baguette or rice.

## Key points

- **Spices taste scorched and bitter**: the powder and finely minced garlic burn easily on a hot pot bottom (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry briefly over medium heat and add the water soon after the chicken goes in. If the bottom of the pot has already turned black, move to another pot and braise there.
- **Chicken is still tight but the potato has fallen apart**: the two need different cooking times (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Braise the legs until cooked first, add the vegetables afterwards, and check the potato with a fork rather than stirring repeatedly and breaking it up.
- **Broth gets saltier as it cooks**: the salt in the fish sauce, the marinade and the bouillon powder concentrates as the liquid reduces (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Keep the broth; do not reduce it in an open pot until the oil and water separate. Add the fish sauce last and taste it together with the chicken and potato.
