Braised chicken thighs with green onions is diced chicken thigh, browned first and then briefly braised: the outside is savoury and coloured, the inside still tender, coated in a salty-sweet green onion sauce. Only after the surface moisture of the chicken pieces has cooked off do they get the browning of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) and the aroma of seared meat. So build the colour before the sauce goes in, then a short braise is enough to coat the chicken with flavour.

The large green onion and the green onions go in separately. The large green onion is softened in the oil and its aroma moves into the chicken and sauce; the green onions go in last to keep their fresh aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Use kitchen scissors to cut the meat off the bone along the bone, snipping the joints and keeping the skin. Pat the thigh meat dry with paper towels and cut it into 2–3 cm dice. Cut the large green onion and the green onions, and slice the ginger thin.
2. Toss the chicken with the Shaoxing wine, 5 ml of light soy sauce and the white pepper, and marinate in the fridge for 10 minutes. In another bowl, mix the remaining light soy sauce, the sugar, and the optional dark soy sauce and oyster sauce, until the sugar has mostly dissolved.
3. Add 15 ml of oil to the pan and put in the optional ginger and Sichuan peppercorns over low heat. Stir-fry until the ginger is lightly golden and you can smell the peppercorns, then lift them out. If you have neither, go straight to browning the meat.
4. Turn the heat to medium-high and spread the chicken in a single layer; fry for 2–3 minutes first. When the side against the pan has browned and lifts easily, flip, then stir-fry until all sides have coloured. If the pan is small, work in batches so juices do not collect on the bottom.
5. Add the large green onion slices and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the edges of the onion are slightly soft and the aroma is strong while the slices still hold their shape.
6. Pour in the sauce and mix; if the bottom of the pan is very dry, add 1 tbsp of water. Cover and braise briefly for about 2 minutes, making sure there is always a little bubbling sauce in the pan and that it does not cook dry.
7. Uncover and insert a thermometer into the centre of the largest piece of chicken; at 74°C (165°F) it is cooked, and you can reduce the sauce. If it has not reached that, keep heating. When the sauce coats the chicken in a thin layer, add the green onion pieces and stir-fry for 5–10 seconds, taste and add salt, and finish with the optional toasted sesame seeds.

## Key points

- **Chicken only turns white and never browns**: the surface is too wet or the pan too crowded (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Pat dry, spread in a single layer, and wait until the bottom has coloured before turning.
- **Onion and sauce taste scorched and bitter**: the sugar and onion were baked too long in a dry pan (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Check that there is sauce in the pan before the short braise, and add water if there is not.
- **Chicken is dry**: the chicken kept reducing in the sauce after it was cooked, so the proteins kept contracting and squeezing out water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Stop as soon as the sauce coats the chicken.
