Sweet braised wheat gluten with mushrooms is a sweet and salty cold dish common in Shanghai: kaofu with shiitake, wood ear, daylily buds and peanuts, cooked until the sauce colour is even and every piece holds liquid. Kaofu comes from wheat [gluten](/principles/gluten/), and once cooked it has sponge-like pores. Soak it fully, rinse it and squeeze out the clear water, then fry and simmer it, so the seasoning gets into the pores and is released when you bite.

Shiitake and soy sauce together support the [umami](/principles/umami/). Daylily buds are supple, wood ear is crisp and peanuts are soft, and these textures suit the springiness of the kaofu. The slow simmer cooks the dried ingredients through, and reducing at the end decides how sweet and salty the dish is. The [salt](/principles/salt/) in soy sauce does not evaporate with the water, so don't season it all the way to the final saltiness at the start.

## Steps

1. Put the wheat gluten in 1000 ml of warm water and soak for 30–60 minutes, until the centre is soft when you pinch it open, with no dry hard core. Soak the shiitake, wood ear and daylily buds each in 400 ml of warm water for 60 minutes, until the shiitake can bend, the wood ear has fully opened and the daylily buds have no hard strands. They can be soaked at the same time. Pour out the soaking water.
2. Rinse the wheat gluten under running water and squeeze gently until it stops dripping, then cut it into squares about 2.5 cm. Remove the hard stems from the shiitake and halve them, remove the hard bases from the wood ear and tear it into small pieces, and remove the hard tips from the daylily buds. Rinse them and drain, and rinse the peanuts.
3. Put the oil and ginger slices in the pot and fry over medium heat for 30 seconds, until the ginger smells fragrant and small bubbles form at the edges. Add the wheat gluten, spread it out, and fry for 2 minutes, until the parts touching the pan are slightly golden. Turn gently so you don't crush the pores.
4. Add the shiitake, wood ear, daylily buds and peanuts, and toss for 1 minute, until everything is coated in oil. Add the Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, sugar and 710 ml of water, and heat over medium heat for about 4 minutes, until it bubbles steadily at the edge of the pot and the sugar has dissolved.
5. Cover and simmer over low heat for 60 minutes, until the wheat gluten is the colour of the sauce inside and out and the peanuts bite open easily. Every 15 minutes, turn it gently from the bottom of the pot, and keep it at a light simmer. If the bottom of the pot is about to show, cover tightly and lower the heat a little more.
6. Uncover and reduce the sauce over medium heat for 3–5 minutes, until only a shallow layer of glossy sauce is left at the bottom of the pot, the surface of the wheat gluten is moist, and pressing it lightly shows liquid. To serve cold, spread it in a shallow tray to cool for 20 minutes, until it no longer gives off much steam, then cover and refrigerate for 2 hours, until the centre is also cold, and serve.

## Key points

- **Soft outside, hard inside, and no flavour in the centre**: the wheat gluten went into the pot before it was soaked through, and the dry, hard centre never connected with the simmering liquid. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/). While soaking, break open a piece to check, then squeeze out the water and cut.
- **Only sweet and salty, with no savoury shiitake taste**: the dried ingredients were not soaked through or too much soy sauce was used, so the salt covers the ingredients. See [umami](/principles/umami/). Soak the shiitake until they bend, add the simmering sauce by the measure, and don't try to add savouriness by adding more soy sauce.
- **Very salty when cold, and the wheat gluten is dry**: the sauce was reduced completely, so the salt concentrated and the water in the pores went down. See [salt](/principles/salt/). Stop the heat while there is still glossy sauce in the pot, and cover it when refrigerating.
