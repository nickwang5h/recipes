*Pasta alla Norma* comes from Catania in Sicily. The classic combination is tomato pasta, fried eggplant, basil and ricotta salata. Salted, aged ricotta is firm enough to grate into crumbs, which add savouriness when scattered over hot pasta.

The eggplant should be fried in hot oil until golden outside and soft inside, and drained promptly (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Cook the tomato sauce down first, and add the eggplant only after the pasta is tossed so the slices keep their shape. Basil goes in last so its aroma stays clear (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Peel and slice the eggplant, sprinkle the layers lightly with salt, put them in a sieve with a plate on top as a weight and leave about 1 hour. Rinse off the surface salt quickly and dry thoroughly with paper towels, leaving no water droplets on any slice.
2. Put the tomatoes, the olive oil for the sauce, the onion, a little salt and pepper in a cold pan. Cook uncovered over medium-low heat until reduced to about a third of the starting volume, with a thick, even sauce and no ring of thin liquid around the edge of the pan.
3. Pour the frying oil into a deep pot and heat to about 175°C (350°F). Fry the eggplant slices in batches, leaving space around each batch, and turn them until both sides are golden and the centre bends easily. Wait for the oil to come back up to temperature before the next batch.
4. Lift the fried eggplant onto a rack to drain, then press gently with paper towels to soak up the surface oil. Spread the slices out: stacked, the surface will soften from steaming.
5. Cook the pasta in boiling salted water and lift it out when the centre still has a slight bite. Set aside a cup of the cooking water, and toss the pasta in the tomato sauce. If the sauce is too thick, add only a little cooking water, and toss until the sauce coats the pasta evenly.
6. Off the heat, stir in part of the basil and about half of the ricotta salata. Divide onto plates, top with the fried eggplant, grate the remaining cheese over it, add the remaining basil and eat right away.

## Key points

- **The eggplant is greasy and pale**: too much went in at once and the oil temperature dropped, stretching the frying time. Fry less per batch, keep the oil at about 170–180°C (340–355°F) after the slices go in, and drain promptly (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **The oil spatters violently**: the salted eggplant was still wet on the surface, and water turns to steam quickly in hot oil. Dry both sides after rinsing and lower the slices gently into the oil (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The eggplant falls apart into the sauce and the pasta is soft**: the fried eggplant was cooked for a long time in the sauce along with the pasta. Toss the pasta first and plate the eggplant last, and cook the pasta only until it has bite (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **The basil is dark and its aroma weak**: the leaves were heated too long. Tear the leaves in after turning off the heat and keep a few for the plate (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
