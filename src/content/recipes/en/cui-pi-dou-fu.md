Crispy fried tofu has a thin golden shell outside and tender tofu inside, coated in a thick, salty-sweet sauce. The tofu is dredged in starch, dipped in egg, then fried. The protein in the coating sets with heat, the starch absorbs water and holds its shape, and the water on the surface is fried off, leaving a thin shell (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Use firm tofu: the slices hold their shape and don't break easily when flipped.

The fried shell stays crisp only briefly. Once it meets the sauce it absorbs water and softens, so serve it hot as soon as it is plated. The sauce thickens by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Turn off the heat as soon as it just clings to the tofu.

## Steps

1. Beat the eggs so the white and yolk are fully mixed, with no strings of white left. Put them in a shallow bowl, so each tofu slice can be dipped whole and lifted out whole.
2. Stir together the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, dark soy sauce, sugar, the 10 g of cornstarch for the sauce and 200 ml of cold water. There must be no dry lumps at the bottom of the bowl. Set it aside, and stir again before it goes in the pan.
3. Drain the firm tofu and cut it into slices about 1.2 cm thick, then blot the surface gently. Remove only the surface water, and don't press hard enough to crush it. Slices of similar thickness set at the same time in the pan.
4. Put the 50 g of cornstarch for dredging on a plate. Coat each tofu slice in a thin layer of starch, shake off the loose starch, then dip it in the egg and let the extra egg drip back into the bowl. Coat the next slice just before it goes in the pan, because a coating left to stand clumps.
5. Add 18 ml of oil to a skillet and heat until the oil spreads easily and the corners of a tofu slice sizzle lightly the moment they touch it. Turn the heat to low and lay the tofu out in a single layer. If the pan is small, fry in batches and don't stack.
6. When the underside is golden, the coating has joined into one piece and a spatula can lift it, flip it and fry the other side until golden. If the tofu is still soft and loose before flipping, wait longer and don't keep prying it back and forth.
7. Stir the sauce so the starch that settled on the bottom is lifted, and pour it in along the edge of the pan. Raise the heat and cook for about 3 minutes, shaking the pan gently so each slice picks up sauce. When the sauce returns to a boil, turns thick and glossy and clings to the tofu, turn off the heat and plate.

## Key points

- **Coating falls off, slices break when flipped**: the surface is too wet, the coated slices stood too long, or the egg coating hasn't set (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Blot off surface water, coat just before frying, and flip only when the underside has set.
- **Dark burnt spots on the golden surface**: loose starch and egg gathered in one place and burnt first, or part of the pan is too hot (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Drip off extra egg while coating, fry over low heat, and clear the burnt bits before the next batch.
- **Sauce clumps or sets into jelly-like lumps**: the starch settled and wasn't stirred in, or the sauce kept cooking after it thickened (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir well before it goes in the pan and stop when it is thick enough. If it is too thick, loosen it with a little hot water.
