Da zhu gan si is a Huaiyang dish: white tofu shreds simmered in fresh chicken broth until the shreds are soft but still springy, each one soaked with broth. White pressed tofu is first sliced into thin sheets, then cut into fine shreds, and cooked with shredded chicken, ham, bamboo shoots and shrimp. The finer the shreds, the more surface they give the savoury broth (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). At home you can buy ready-made plain white tofu shreds and skip the slicing. Blanch and cook them the same way.

Pressed white tofu is firmer than soft tofu and stays springy even when cooked soft. Blanching in hot water first washes off some of the beany taste and the crumbs on the surface (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). The chicken broth and ham lay down a salty, savoury base, and the shrimp are cooked only at the end. The broth's [umami](/principles/umami/) clings to the shreds, and some broth is still left in the dish when it reaches the table.

## Steps

1. Lay the pressed white tofu flat and, with the knife parallel to the board, slice it layer by layer into sheets about 1 mm thick. Stack the sheets neatly and cut them into fine shreds. Set the thick edge pieces aside so a bundle doesn't have thick strips mixed in. Cut the ham and cooked bamboo shoots into fine shreds, tear the cooked chicken into shreds and wash the bok choy hearts.
2. Bring 1000 ml of water to a boil, which takes about 5 minutes, until the surface keeps rolling. Put the tofu shreds in a bowl, pour the boiling water over them, separate them with chopsticks and soak for 2 minutes, until the shreds turn supple, then drain off the cloudy water. Bring the other 1000 ml of water to a boil for about 5 minutes and soak for another 2 minutes. This water comes out clearer than the first. Drain the shreds well.
3. Put the lard in a pot and heat over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until it melts. Pour in the chicken stock, add the ham shreds, bamboo shoot shreds and tofu shreds, and cook over medium heat for about 5 minutes until it barely simmers. Gently push with the back of a spoon so the broth seeps into the bundles of shreds, and don't press the tofu shreds into a lump.
4. Cover, turn the heat to low and cook for 10 minutes, until the shreds are soft and hang down on their own when lifted, and the broth tastes of ham and soy. Nudge them gently once at the 5-minute mark, and keep the bottom from sticking and the broth just barely bubbling.
5. Add the shrimp, cooked chicken shreds and bok choy hearts, and cook over low heat for 3–4 minutes, until the shrimp are completely opaque and curled into a relaxed arc and the bok choy stems can be bitten through. Taste some shreds with broth, add salt, and ladle everything with the broth into a deep dish. The shreds should be heaped slightly above the broth.

## Key points

- **Shreds are coarse and hard, like strips of pressed tofu**: the slices were cut too thick and the thickness is uneven, so they heat unevenly (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut the sheets evenly first. Cook the thick strips separately, and don't overcook the fine shreds while waiting for the thick ones to soften.
- **Cloudy broth, beany taste overpowering the chicken broth**: crumbs on the shreds and the blanching water went into the pot (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Pour away both rounds of blanching water, drain well before adding to the broth, and nudge gently while cooking.
- **Broth is so salty it covers the soy flavour**: the salt from the ham and the stock add up, and it concentrates further as it cooks (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). Choose a low-sodium stock, leave plenty of broth, and taste a shred with broth at the end before deciding whether to add salt.
