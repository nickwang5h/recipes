*Cantucci* are Tuscan sliced-and-rebaked almond biscuits, dry and crisp, often dipped in sweet wine. The recipe is an old-style one, with a little butter, almonds mixed with pine nuts, and aniseed flavour. The logs are baked twice: the first bake goes only until they can be sliced, and the second dries out the inside. One batch is enough as an after-dinner treat for twelve people.

Thin slices lose water faster than a whole log, and the cut surface lets heat and steam move in and out; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). So even a first bake that is deeply coloured does not mean the inside is crisp all the way through. The dough only needs to come together in one piece; with too much kneading, [gluten](/principles/gluten/) grows strong and the biscuits turn hard and tough. Keep the almonds whole, so the cut shows distinct pieces of nut.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F) and line a baking sheet with parchment. Beat the fifth egg and set it aside; mix the other four eggs with the sugar, softened butter, salt and aniseed.
2. Add the flour and mix until no dry flour remains, then stir in the almonds and pine nuts. The dough should be soft and slightly sticky but able to gather into logs. Only if there is dry, crumbly dough should you add the reserved beaten egg a little at a time, keeping some for brushing. If the dough sticks to your hands, do not keep adding flour.
3. Divide the dough into four logs and press them gently to about a finger thick and a palm wide. Place them on the baking sheet with space between them. Brush the surface thinly with egg, and do not let the egg pool on the sheet.
4. Bake for about 25 minutes, checking partway that the colour is even and turning the sheet if needed. Take them out when the surface is light golden, the log holds its shape when pressed lightly, and there is no wet batter when you cut an end open. This bake is only to cook them through enough to slice, not until the whole log is dry and hard.
5. Move the logs to a rack. When they are warm and the knife does not squash them as it cuts, slice them gently on the diagonal into pieces about a finger thick with a serrated knife. Support the log and let the knife saw through the almonds, rather than chopping down with the knife.
6. Lay the slices flat on the baking sheet and bake again at 180°C (350°F) for about 5 minutes, turning them partway through, until the cut surfaces are dry and the edges lightly coloured. If thick slices are still moist and soft, bake a little longer, extending it bit by bit, rather than waiting until the surface scorches to find the centre is not dry.
7. Cool completely on a rack, make sure the broken surface is dry and the biscuit snaps crisply when bitten, and then pack them in an airtight box. Do not seal hot biscuits in right away, or steam will condense in the box and soften them.

## Key points

- **Cracks when sliced, almonds fall out whole**: the logs are too cold and dry, or the knife was pressed down hard. Slice while warm with a serrated knife, supporting both sides, and bake the first time only until set. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Very hard but tough to bite**: overmixing, or too much flour added to stop stickiness. Add the egg from the original recipe in stages, stop mixing once combined, and get the crispness from the water lost in the second bake. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Centre is soft after cooling, and it softens again in the box**: the second bake did not dry them enough, or hot biscuits were sealed in and trapped steam. Return the slices to the oven to dry, and seal them only after they have cooled completely. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
