Laba congee is a thick, soft sweet congee made with eight things: rice, glutinous rice, black rice, adzuki beans, mung beans, peanuts, jujubes and longan. The starch in glutinous rice and rice [absorbs water and gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), which is what thickens the congee; the beans need to take up plenty of water first, so they too are soft by the time the rice grains burst open.

The ingredients soften at different speeds, so they go in at different times. The rice is 150 g in all and the beans 50 g in all, and the rest is peanuts and dried fruit. The finished congee should be scoopable and flow back into the bowl, the beans should crush under light pressure, and the nuts should still have a little soft chew.

## Steps

1. Pick over and wash the adzuki beans, mung beans and raw peanuts, put each in plenty of water, and soak in the refrigerator for at least 8 hours. The beans swell as they take up water, so leave room in the container; after soaking, pour off the soaking water and rinse once more.
2. Wash the rice, glutinous rice and black rice, add water and soak in the refrigerator for about 3 hours, then drain off the soaking water just before they go in the pot. Dried beans and the rice can't go into the pot together, so plan these two steps ahead of time.
3. Put 2000 ml of clean water, the two soaked beans and the raw peanuts in a heavy-bottomed pot, bring to a boil, turn to low, leave the lid ajar, and cook for about 30 minutes first. The bean skins begin to loosen and the centres have taken up enough water; if the adzuki beans are still hard, cook a while longer.
4. Add the three kinds of rice, stir gently along the bottom, bring back to a boil and continue at a low simmer for about 60 minutes. Stir the bottom every so often and check whether the black rice is soft. If the water level drops and the congee thickens while the rice centres are still hard, add boiling water a little at a time.
5. Pit the jujubes and cut into small pieces, and rinse the longan. Add both dried fruits to the congee and simmer on low for about 60 minutes more, until the beans crush under light pressure, the rice grains have burst open, and the dried fruit is soft all the way through. The congee is thicker toward the end, so stir gently along the bottom more often.
6. Add the rock sugar, stir until completely dissolved, then taste for sweetness. Scoop some up to look: the congee should flow back into the pot in a continuous stream, with rice and beans evenly distributed. If it is too thick, add boiling water and stir in; if too thin, keep simmering gently. Turn off the heat at the thickness you like and divide into bowls.

## Key points

- **The rice has turned to mush but the adzuki beans are still hard**: different ingredients take up water and heat at different speeds (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Soak the beans first, cook them first, and add the rice once the bean centres begin to soften.
- **The congee dries into a lump or scorches on the bottom**: the ingredients absorb water and the liquid evaporates, so there is less and less. [Starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) makes the congee thicker and thicker, so start with plenty of water, then add water later and stir along the bottom.
- **The beans and rice are slow to soften and it is too sweet**: sugar added too early interferes with the starch taking up water. Add the dried fruit in order, and add the rock sugar once the beans and rice are all soft, so the sweetness doesn't mask an undercooked texture.
