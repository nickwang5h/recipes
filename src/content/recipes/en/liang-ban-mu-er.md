Cold wood ear salad is about crisp, springy texture and the garlicky, hot, sour fragrance. Soak the dried wood ear open, cook it through, then let the seasoning work into its folds. The cooking step follows [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/). Soaking only restores the shape of the dried fungus, and it still has to go in the pot.

Smashed raw garlic is sharper (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The vinegar and a little sugar make the dressing of light soy sauce and toasted sesame oil taste clearer (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Wood ear has little flavour of its own, and draining matters more than adding soy sauce. Water left in the folds dilutes the dressing.

## Steps

1. Rinse the dried wood ear briefly under running water, put it in a clean bowl and soak it in about 400 ml of water for about 45 minutes. Soak until the pieces have fully opened and no hard centre can be felt. If any are still hard, keep soaking, for no more than 2 hours in total at room temperature. For longer, put it in the refrigerator.
2. Remove the hard knots at the base and rub and wash each piece clean. Tear large pieces into bite-sized ones. Wood ear that is already soaked starts here.
3. Bring enough water to cover the wood ear to a boil in a pot, add the wood ear and spread it out. After the water returns to a boil, keep it on high heat and cook for about 1.5–2 minutes, until every piece has opened out and the thick parts are hot through.
4. Lift the wood ear into a clean strainer, shake off the water, and spread it out to cool until it no longer feels hot. Leave it folds-down to drain for a while so the bottom of the mixing bowl stays dry. Do not rinse it in raw tap water to cool it.
5. Peel the garlic, smash and mince it. Rinse and chop the bird's eye chilies. Put them in a large bowl with the sugar, light soy sauce and vinegar and stir until the sugar dissolves. Then mix in the toasted sesame oil and the optional small amount of wasabi.
6. Add the wood ear and fold from the bottom of the bowl again and again so the dressing works into the folds. Taste a piece before deciding whether to add salt. If the light soy sauce is salty enough, add no extra salt.
7. It can be eaten as soon as it is mixed. For more flavour, cover and refrigerate for about 15 minutes, then take it out and fold once more to coat the wood ear with the dressing that settled at the bottom.

## Key points

- **A hard knot in the mouthful**: it was not soaked through, or the base was not fully removed. A short cook cannot fix a dry, hard centre (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Feel each piece before it goes in the pot, and soak until it is soft all through.
- **A puddle at the bottom and the wood ear tastes of little**: water held in the folds has diluted the sour and salty flavour (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Drain well before mixing, coat the pieces with the dressing you have, then taste.
- **Slimy and off-smelling after soaking**: wood ear soaked too long at room temperature lets bacteria grow, and the toxins they make are not destroyed by cooking (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Throw out the whole bowl. Soak at room temperature for no more than 2 hours, and cook it soon after soaking.
