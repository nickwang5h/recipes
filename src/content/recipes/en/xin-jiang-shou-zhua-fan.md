In Xinjiang lamb pilaf, the grains stay separate and are coated with the scent of lamb fat, the carrots and onion simmer down to sweetness, and the ribs are soft enough to come off the bone. The raisins are optional. Lamb, carrot, onion and rice go in one pot: the base is fried first, water is added to simmer the meat, then the rice is laid on top and cooked through, and everything is turned from the bottom only at the end.

This dish waits on two things, the meat and the rice. Moist heat first softens the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) in the ribs, and the rice goes on only once the meat comes off the bone. The rice needs enough broth and steam to complete its [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). A heavy-bottomed pot, a tight lid and low heat let the rice absorb the water slowly, which keeps whole grains better than frequent stirring.

## Steps

1. Brown the lamb. Pat the ribs dry, add the oil to a heavy-bottomed pot and fry over medium-high heat for 5 minutes, until every side has changed colour and some edges are golden. Keep the rendered lamb fat and don't trim off all the fatty parts.
2. Fry the vegetables. Add the onion and 1 tsp of the salt and fry over medium heat for 1 minute, until the onion is soft and translucent. Add the carrot sticks and fry for another 3 minutes, until the surface is coated in oil and starting to bend, but the sticks keep their shape.
3. Simmer the meat. Add 720 ml of water and boil over high heat for 5 minutes, until it is at a full boil, then skim off the foam. Add the ground cumin, cover, turn the heat to low and simmer for 90 minutes, with small bubbles gently breaking the surface. Add the remaining salt at the 60-minute mark. Check every 30 minutes, and if the liquid falls below the meat, add 60 ml from the hot water you've set aside.
4. Soak the rice. At the 50-minute mark of the simmer, rinse the rice, add 1000 ml of cold water and soak for 30 minutes, until the grains have absorbed water and can be snapped with a fingernail. Drain for 5 minutes, until it no longer drips continuously from the strainer.
5. Lay the rice. Check the ribs: only continue if a fork goes in easily and the meat peels away from the bone. If it is still tough, simmer another 15–20 minutes. Push the meat toward the edge of the pot, leave a layer of vegetables on the bottom, and spread the rice and raisins over it. The liquid should show between the grains and come close to the surface of the rice. If you can't see any liquid, add the reserved hot water in portions of 60 ml.
6. Reduce the liquid. Cover and cook over medium heat for 5 minutes, until steam rises through the rice layer. Uncover and cook for another 5 minutes, until no flowing liquid is visible on the surface and small air holes appear. Only part the rice gently to check; don't keep turning the base up from underneath.
7. Steam the pilaf. Cover tightly and cook over the lowest heat for 25 minutes, until the grains have swollen and have no hard centre. If there is still a hard centre, drizzle 60 ml of the reserved hot water down the side of the pot and steam another 5 minutes. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 10 minutes, until the steam on the surface settles, then turn the pilaf over from the bottom up.

## Key points

- **Lamb is tough but the rice is already mushy**: the rice went in too early. Collagen takes longer to soften in the meat than rice takes to cook (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Test the meat with a fork first and lay the rice once it is soft.
- **Scorched bottom, raw top**: heat is concentrated at the bottom or the visible liquid cooked off too far (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use a heavy-bottomed pot and the lowest heat, and cover tightly as soon as air holes appear in the rice, so it doesn't keep dry-cooking.
- **Rice clumps with no whole grains**: too much liquid or repeated stirring stirred out the gelatinized starch on the surface. Drain the soaked rice well, judge by the liquid level when laying the rice, and turn it only when cooked (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
