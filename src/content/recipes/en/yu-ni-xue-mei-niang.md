The wrapper of these taro mochi cream balls is soft and chewy with a milky flavour, and inside is starchy taro paste and whipped cream. The wrapper becomes sticky and stretchy through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/): glutinous rice flour is steamed through, butter is kneaded in, and it can only be rolled out once it has cooled. Glutinous rice flour has no wheat gluten, so all the stretch comes from cooked starch.

The taro paste is starchy and soft, and the whipped cream holds air, so it feels lighter in the mouth. Let both the wrapper and the taro paste cool completely before they touch the cream, because warmth makes the cream collapse (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). This batch makes about ten. The sugar is added in three places, 76 g in all; the toasted dusting flour is only for preventing sticking, and whatever is left over is not kneaded into the wrapper.

## Steps

1. Cut the taro into pieces and boil in water for about 40 minutes, until a chopstick goes through easily. Lift out and drain. Add 30 g of milk and 25 g of whipping cream and blend into a paste, then add the purple sweet potato powder and 18 g of sugar and blend until smooth. Spread out to cool, then refrigerate.
2. Stir together 75 g of glutinous rice flour, 22 g of cornstarch, 135 g of milk and 50 g of sugar, and sift into a shallow heatproof bowl. Cover with a heatproof lid, or with plastic wrap suitable for steaming, leaving a vent hole. Once the water boils, steam over medium heat for about 30 minutes.
3. Take it out once the centre of the dough has set and there is no white batter left. While it is hot, add 30 g of butter and press and mix with a spatula. When it is no longer too hot to touch, knead until the butter is absorbed and no oil shows, then cover and refrigerate for about 1 hour.
4. Put 50 g of glutinous rice flour in a dry pan and stir constantly over low heat, until the raw-flour smell is gone and it smells toasted, with only a slight yellowing of colour. Tip it out to cool for dusting. Keep turning it so no part scorches.
5. Add 8 g of sugar to 120 g of cold whipping cream and whip until the ridges are clear and the peaks stand up when you lift the whisk, with a smooth texture and no graininess. Return it to the refrigerator once whipped.
6. Take out the dough and knead for about 5 minutes until supple. Divide into ten equal pieces of about 30 g each. Dust the work surface thinly with the toasted flour and roll each piece into a round, thicker in the middle and thinner at the edge. Too much dry flour makes the wrapper hard.
7. Divide the cream and the taro paste into ten portions each. Place cream on each wrapper, then taro paste, lift the edges and pinch to seal. Brush the extra flour off the seam and press lightly to stick it. Dust the flour on only thinly. Set each one seam side down in a paper cup, wrap and refrigerate before eating.

## Key points

- **White core in the wrapper, and it tears easily**: the centre has not [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) fully. Steam it in a shallow bowl and check the centre for raw batter, instead of only looking at whether the surface has set.
- **Wrapper thick and hard, seam won't stick**: too much dusting flour, or the surface dried out. Cover the wrapper dough after kneading, dust thinly as you work, and brush the flour off the seam before pinching it closed.
- **Cream leaks out and the whole ball collapses**: the taro paste or wrapper was still warm and softened the cream. Both must be completely cold, and the cream kept refrigerated; use the same amount of filling in each wrapper and leave room for the seam.
