*Onigiri* are a Japanese home staple, rice lightly pressed into shape around a little salty, savoury filling. They are usually triangular, and sometimes oval. The filling is bonito flakes mixed with soy sauce, and the outside is wrapped with plain toasted nori. The rice keeps its natural flavour, and the saltiness comes from a little salt on the palms and the filling in the centre.

Once cooked, the [starch](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) that has gelatinized on the surface of short-grain rice lets the grains join easily. Pressed gently while still warm, the onigiri holds its shape. If you press too hard, the grains are squeezed into a dense lump and it bites hard. Bonito flakes with soy sauce give a clear [umami](/principles/umami/) in a small amount, so the filling does not need to fill the whole centre.

## Steps

1. Prepare warm short-grain rice. If you start from raw rice, wash it, cook it according to the white rice directions on the bag or rice cooker, and after steaming, cut through it with a paddle and fluff it gently. The grains should be soft and able to stick together, with no free water at the bottom of the pot. Weigh out the cooked rice in the ingredient list.
2. Mix the bonito flakes with the soy sauce and loosen any clumps. The filling should be slightly moist and easy to pick up with a small spoon, with no sauce pooled at the bottom of the bowl. Cut the nori into long strips about 2.5 cm wide and keep them dry.
3. Divide the rice into ten portions of about 100 g each and cover them loosely so the surface doesn't dry. Let it cool a little until it is safe to hold and still warm, and start shaping; don't wait until the rice is completely cold and hard.
4. Wash your hands and dampen them lightly, then rub on a small pinch of salt. Spread one portion of rice across your palm, press a shallow well in the centre, put in about 1 tsp of bonito filling, and bring the rice around it to enclose the filling.
5. Cup one hand under the base and curve the other hand into an angle, press one face lightly, then turn the onigiri and shape the three corners in turn. Press only until it can be set down without falling apart and you can still see whole grains on the surface. If your hands get sticky, dampen them lightly again before making the next one.
6. Before eating, wrap the nori with the shiny side out, across the base of the onigiri and up both sides. To keep the nori crisp, wrap it just before eating; if you like soft nori, you can wrap it ahead. Put the onigiri on a clean plate. If you won't eat them right away, seal them in clean wrapping, refrigerate promptly, and keep them cold when carrying them.

## Key points

- **It falls apart when you pick it up**: you used loose long-grain rice, or the rice has gone cold and hard. Choose Japanese short-grain white rice and shape it while warm; closing your palms is enough, so don't rescue it by kneading it into mush (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Hard to bite, like pressed rice cake**: you pressed too hard and squeezed out the gaps between grains. Press lightly, turn the face each time, and stop as soon as it holds its shape; don't cook the rice to a mush either (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **One bite is very salty and another bland**: there is too much salt on your hands, or the filling has runny sauce. Rub the salt on thinly and evenly, and use soy sauce only to moisten the bonito flakes so it doesn't seep into one part of the rice (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
