*Croque-monsieur* is a hot ham and cheese sandwich found in French cafés: crisp outside, with hot ham and soft cheese inside, and golden-brown gratin spots on top. This is the version gratinéed with béchamel, using white sandwich bread, cooked ham and Gruyère. The béchamel must be thick enough to spread on bread. Spread it thinly in the middle and evenly on top, so the cut sandwich doesn't flood the plate with sauce.

In the béchamel, the butter first coats the flour. Once the milk goes in, the starch in the flour absorbs water and thickens, which is [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The toasty flavour comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/), once the bread edges and cheese surface have dried out in the oven. The oven first heats the whole sandwich through, and only at the end does the top element add colour (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 210°C (400°F). Lay the 8 slices of bread flat and pick 4 for the bottoms and 4 for the tops. Grate the cheese and set aside.
2. Melt the butter in a small pot over low heat, add the flour and stir until even and finely bubbling. Keep cooking for about 2 minutes, until the raw flour smell fades and the colour is still pale. Don't let it turn brown.
3. Add the milk in three additions, whisking the paste smooth each time before adding the next. Once smooth, bring to a boil, then keep it at a gentle simmer until the sauce coats the back of a spoon thickly and falls from the spoon in a wide ribbon.
4. Season with a little salt, white pepper and the optional nutmeg, and take off the heat to cool slightly. The sauce should be thick and spreadable, still movable with the back of a spoon. Don't let it cool into a hard block.
5. Spread the mustard on the bottom slices, lay on the cooked ham, then spread a thin layer of béchamel on top and cover with the remaining slices. Spread the filling thinly, and keep the rest of the béchamel for the tops.
6. Divide the remaining béchamel among the tops of the sandwiches and spread it thinly, then sprinkle on the Gruyère. Bake on the upper-middle rack for about 10 minutes, until the filling is hot through, the edges are dry and crisp, and the cheese is melted and bubbling.
7. If the top is still pale, switch to the top element and stay by the oven. Take the sandwiches out as soon as golden-brown spots appear. Let them rest for about 1 minute, cut on the diagonal and eat hot.

## Key points

- **Soggy bread, sauce runs out when cut**: the béchamel is too thin, or the filling layer is too thick. Cook the sauce until it coats a spoon, spread the filling thinly, and keep one layer for covering the top (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **White lumps of flour in the sauce**: the milk went in too fast, and the outside of the flour gelatinized first and sealed in dry flour. Add the milk in additions and whisk well. If needed, strain, then cook again (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Burnt on top, cold inside**: only the strong top heat was used from the start, so the heat stays on the surface. Bake normally first so the filling heats through, and brown briefly at the end (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
