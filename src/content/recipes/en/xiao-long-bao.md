Xiaolongbao have fresh pork and a mouthful of hot soup inside a thin skin. The soup starts as aspic: pork skin is simmered for a long time, its [collagen turns into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), and it sets into a jelly when chilled, which is why it can be diced and mixed into the filling; when steamed, the jelly melts again and becomes soup.

The dough is not leavened. Knead it evenly and let it rest so the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) relaxes and stretches easily, so it can be rolled thin without tearing. Boiling water first cooks part of the flour, and cold water is added after, which makes a skin that is soft with some chew. This makes 20 buns at a time; make the aspic the day before, and steam as soon as the buns are shaped.

## Steps

1. Bring the pork skin and pork neck bones to a boil in water and boil until scum collects, then drain at once and rinse. Scrape any remaining fat off the pork skin, cut it into strips about 2 cm wide and return it to a small pot.
2. Add 950 ml of water, the ginger and green onion for the aspic and the wine, bring to a boil, cover and simmer over low heat for 2 hours, until the skin is soft and the broth feels thick and silky. Strain out the solids and pour the broth into a shallow container. Cool in a cold water bath for 20–30 minutes, until it stops giving off a lot of steam, then refrigerate for 8 hours so the centre also sets into one piece; cooling should take no more than 2 hours in total.
3. Mix the flour with 60 ml of boiling water first, then mix in 35 ml of cold water to make a dough. Cover and rest for 10 minutes, then knead for about 15–20 minutes, until fine and smooth. Cover and rest again for 30–60 minutes, until it hardly springs back when rolled out.
4. Add all the filling seasonings to the pork and mix for about 2 minutes, until even and sticky. Scrape off the layer of fat on top of the aspic, take 250 g and cut it into small dice about 0.5 cm, and fold it gently into the pork filling. Refrigerate for 30 minutes, until the filling is cold and firm with no loose liquid.
5. Divide the dough into 20 pieces of about 14 g each. Dust thinly with cornstarch and roll each piece into a round wrapper about 12 cm across, slightly thicker in the centre, with the edge faintly translucent. Wrap as you roll, keeping the remaining pieces covered.
6. Put about 35–40 g of filling in the centre of each wrapper, and take care that the sealing edge does not touch the meat juices before pleating. Lift the edge and gather it in pleats, then pinch the top closed. If you are new to this, fewer pleats are fine, as long as the bottom wrapper is intact and the seal is tight.
7. Line the steamer basket with perforated baking parchment and leave about 3 cm between buns. Bring the steamer water to a full boil, about 8–10 minutes, then put the basket on and cover, with no more than 10 buns per basket. Steam at a steady steam for 8 minutes, until the wrapper turns semi-translucent, the filling holds together inside, and the bottoms are still intact.
8. Insert a thermometer into the centre of the filling of the largest bun; at 71°C (160°F) it is done. If not, steam 2 more minutes. For the next basket, wait 1–2 minutes after the water in the steamer returns to a boil before timing.
9. Put the black vinegar and ginger shreds in a dipping dish. Hold a bun by the top of the pleats and support the bottom with a spoon, bite a small opening to let the soup out, and rest it for 1–2 minutes. Taste the soup first, and eat the skin and filling once it is no longer scalding.

## Key points

- **Soup does not set after chilling**: the broth is too thin and the concentration of [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) is too low. Return it to the pot and reduce over low heat for 10–15 minutes, until the volume has dropped by about a quarter, then cool and chill it to set, and wrap only once it has set.
- **Dough will not roll out and the edge keeps shrinking back**: the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) is still tight, or the surface has dried and formed a skin. Cover and rest another 15 minutes, and dust only thinly with flour instead of kneading it in.
- **Soup leaks out when wrapped, and there is none after steaming**: the aspic warmed and melted, or the thin wrapper was stretched until it tore. Keep the filling ice-cold, wrap as you roll so the aspic melts only during steaming, and lift each bun by its whole bottom.
- **Skin is cooked but the filling is still cold**: there is not enough steam, or the basket is too crowded, and heat has not fully [reached the centre](/principles/heat-transfer/). Start timing only once the water is boiling, and leave space between the buns.
