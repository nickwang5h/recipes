Stir-fried bitter melon and eggs puts the clean bitterness of the melon and the richness of the egg curds in one mouthful. The bitter melon is cut into thin slices and [blanched](/principles/blanching/) briefly, which takes away some of the bitterness and makes the stir-frying afterwards finish faster. The eggs are cooked separately and combined at the end, so the melon's water doesn't rush into the eggs just as the beaten eggs hit the pan.

When eggs are heated, the proteins denature and coagulate (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), turning runny egg into soft curds. Take them out first, then toss the seasoned bitter melon back in, so the heat on each can be controlled separately. A little salt, sugar and oyster sauce only hold up the flavour; the finished dish should still taste of the bitter melon's own bitterness.

## Steps

1. Wash the bitter melon, cut off both ends and halve lengthwise. Scoop out the seeds and soft white pith with a spoon, then slice on the diagonal about 2 mm thick. Keep the thickness as even as you can; thick slices stretch out the stir-frying time for the whole pan.
2. Put the water and 1 tsp of the salt for blanching in a wok and bring to a rolling boil over medium-high heat, about 5–7 minutes. Add the bitter melon and blanch about 0.5 minutes, until the colour brightens and the slices are slightly soft, then lift out right away and pour away the blanching water.
3. Spread the bitter melon in a colander and drain for 2–3 minutes, until it stops dripping, then shake the colander lightly to get rid of water between the slices. Don't tip wet bitter melon straight back into the wok; water pooling at the bottom of the plate dilutes the seasoning.
4. Beat the eggs with 0.25 tsp salt, the white pepper and toasted sesame oil until the whites and yolks are blended and there are no clear clumps of egg white at the bottom of the bowl. Mix the light soy sauce, oyster sauce and sugar in a small bowl so they go in at once when the two are combined.
5. Wipe the wok dry and preheat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the 2 tbsp of oil you add flows freely. Pour in the eggs and scramble for about 0.5–1 minute, until the bottom sets in sheets and the surface is still moist, push into large pieces and transfer to a clean bowl.
6. Add the other 1 tbsp of oil to the same wok and stir-fry the bitter melon over medium-high heat for about 0.5 minutes, until the steam has gone from the slices. Drizzle the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the wok and stir-fry about 0.25 minutes more, until the wine boils and there are no puddles at the bottom, then pour in the small bowl of sauce and toss.
7. Return the eggs to the wok and fold gently for about 0.5–1 minute, until the egg is fully set and the bitter melon still has some bite, and the centre of the egg pieces reaches 74°C (165°F). Lift and fold with the spatula, keeping the egg in large pieces, and plate as soon as it is done.

## Key points

- **The bitter melon is too bitter and raw in the thick parts**: the white pith wasn't cleaned out, or the slices are uneven. Scrape out all the soft white pith, slice thin, then [blanch briefly](/principles/blanching/) on time to keep the clean bitterness while cutting down the raw, hard edge; adding more sugar turns the whole dish sweet.
- **Water at the bottom of the plate and the egg tastes diluted**: the blanched melon wasn't drained, or it cooked for a long time after combining and gave off water. Let the surface water go before adding the eggs; add the [salt](/principles/salt/) and sauce together at the end, and don't leave it seasoned and waiting.
- **The egg is broken up and hard**: it was stirred too often, or stayed in the hot pan after it was cooked. With [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/), eggs that keep heating tighten and lose water; cook into large pieces and take it out as soon as it is heated through at the end.
- **The bitter melon goes limp in a lunch box**: it was stir-fried too soft before packing, or hot steam condensed into water in the closed box. Keep some bite in the melon and cook the eggs through, divide into shallow containers, refrigerate once they stop steaming, within 2 hours of leaving the wok, and cover tightly only when fully cold. Reheat the next day until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
