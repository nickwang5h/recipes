Kinpira gobo is a sweet and salty side dish of burdock and carrot matchsticks, common on home tables and in lunch boxes. It should have a little bite, but the burdock strips must not be hard enough to prick the mouth. The vegetables are stir-fried in oil until cooked, then a little seasoning liquid is cooked down to dry, so the strips stay separate and glossy and there is no pool of sauce on the plate.

Burdock roots vary a lot in thickness, so measuring by weight is more reliable than going by "1 root". The skin only needs a scrub and a light scrape, to keep the flavour just under it. Cut the burdock and carrot into thin strips of similar size so the path for heat to the centre is short and a small amount of liquid is enough to finish the dish (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Once the seasoning goes into the pan, the water evaporates and the saltiness concentrates, so the vegetables should be stir-fried until nearly cooked first (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).

## Steps

1. Scrub the burdock clean and lightly scrape the rough skin with the back of a knife. Slice it on the diagonal into pieces about 5 cm long and 2 mm thick, then stack them and cut into strips about 2 mm wide. Cut the thick end and the thin end separately to keep the thickness as even as possible.
2. Put the burdock strips in 500 ml cold water and soak for 5 minutes, until the water turns slightly brown. Change to the other 500 ml cold water and soak for 5 more minutes, until the strips have no visible grit on the surface. Lift out and drain well; do not keep soaking.
3. Peel the carrot and cut it into strips similar to the burdock. Mix the sake, mirin, soy sauce and sugar. If using the chili, snip off the stem, shake out the seeds and cut it into rings.
4. Put the sesame oil in a large frying pan and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily but is not yet smoking. Add the burdock strips and stir-fry for 2–3 minutes, until they bend and look slightly translucent, with no liquid on the bottom of the pan.
5. Add the carrot strips and keep stir-frying over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until both vegetables bend easily, and a taste of the burdock shows no obviously raw hard core but still some crunch.
6. Pour in the seasoning liquid and the chili rings and stir-fry over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until the liquid no longer collects at the bottom of the pan and every strip is coated in a thin shiny glaze. Scatter on the toasted white sesame seeds, mix, turn off the heat and plate.

## Key points

- **Burdock is hard while the carrot is mushy**: the burdock was cut too thick and the heat has not reached its centre. Make the strips the same thickness first, and add the burdock to the pan before the carrot (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).
- **Like boiled vegetables, with limp strips**: the burdock was not drained after soaking, or too many vegetables were crowded into a small pan, so the heat went into evaporating water first. Use a large pan where the strips spread out, and stir-fry until no liquid is left before adding the seasoning (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Too salty and bitter once reduced**: the soy sauce went in while the vegetables were underdone, and the long stir-frying afterwards cooked off all the liquid. Cook the vegetables through before seasoning, stop the heat as soon as the glaze has coated them, and do not add extra salt at the end (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
