Century egg tofu puts the springy firmness of century egg and the delicate softness of tofu on one plate, with a sour, salty sauce linking the two textures. Silken (lactone) tofu is made by slow acidification, which makes the soy milk proteins form a water-filled gel (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). It holds a lot of water and has a tender structure, so after cutting it into pieces it has to be moved gently or it turns into a plate of crumbs.

The vinegar makes it fresher to eat and the sugar softens the sharp acidity (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). The light soy sauce provides savouriness, and toasted sesame oil and chili oil are chosen to taste. Before pouring the sauce, drain off the free water in the box, and eat right after dressing, so water that seeps out over time does not dilute the seasoning. Sprinkle the peanuts on last to keep them crisp.

## Steps

1. Peel the century eggs and cut each into quarters with a clean knife. If the yolk sticks to the knife, wipe a thin film of the optional toasted sesame oil on the blade; wipe the knife clean after each cut and the cut surfaces will be neater.
2. Open the tofu package, pour off the water in the box and unmould gently. Cut into blocks or slices and place in the centre of a plate, with the century egg arranged around it. If the tofu is marked as needing heat, cook it as the package directs and let it cool before assembling the plate.
3. Put the light soy sauce, sugar and vinegar in a small bowl and stir until the sugar dissolves. Add the toasted sesame oil and chili oil as desired and stir again. The oil and vinegar separate as they stand, so stir again before pouring.
4. Tip off any water that has newly collected at the bottom of the plate and spoon the sauce over the tofu and century egg. Cover everything thinly, without stirring hard.
5. Sprinkle on roasted peanuts, green onion and cilantro to taste. Wash and drain the green onion and cilantro first, scatter the peanuts at the last moment, and eat soon after plating.

## Key points

- **The tofu breaks into mush**: the delicate [protein gel](/principles/protein-coagulation/) cannot take repeated tossing. Arrange the plate first and then pour on the sauce, and push the pieces gently with a spoon instead of grabbing and mixing as you would firm tofu.
- **Water collects at the bottom of the plate and the sauce is weak**: the water in the box was not fully drained, or the dish sat too long after mixing. The water dilutes the salt already there (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Drain before pouring the sauce, assemble the plate just before eating, and do not rush to add more soy sauce.
- **The acidity is sharp and the oil is heavy**: the ratio of vinegar to the optional oils does not suit your taste (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Dissolve the sugar first, reduce or omit the oil, and keep the flavours of the century egg and tofu themselves.
