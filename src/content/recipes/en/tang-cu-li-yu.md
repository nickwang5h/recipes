Sweet and sour carp needs two things done separately: the fish fried until the outer layer is crisp, and the sweet-sour sauce cooked until it flows and clings to the fish. This version adds ketchup to the sauce, which gives a tomato colour besides the sweet-sour taste. The batter first [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when heated, then the hot oil takes water from the outer layer to form a crisp shell (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).

The thick back of a whole fish cooks more slowly than the thin belly, and scoring shortens the distance heat has to travel into the flesh (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Even when the shell has coloured, look again at whether the thick parts are cooked; the sauce is poured on only just before serving, so water doesn't soften the crisp shell too early.

## Steps

1. Wash the prepared fish and pat it dry. Lay it steady and score both sides at intervals: first cut down about 1 cm, then cut along the flesh crosswise for about 3–4 cm, leaving the base of each slice attached. Don't cut through the body.
2. Squeeze and rub the leek and ginger so their juice touches the fish, add 5 g of salt and 25 g of Shaoxing wine and rub in, then cover and refrigerate to marinate for 30 minutes. Take out, discard the leek and ginger, and blot the water from the skin and the cuts.
3. Mix the flour, the starch for the batter, 180 ml of water and 2 g of salt, then stir in the egg. Refrigerate and rest for about 30 minutes, then stir again. The batter should fall in a continuous stream and cling to the skin. Coat both sides of the fish and the cuts, then drain off any heavy excess batter.
4. Add the frying oil to a deep pot big enough for the whole fish; the oil plus the fish should come to no more than half the height of the pot. Keep the oil at 170–180°C (340–355°F) with a thermometer. Support the fish with two long-handled heat-proof tools and lower it slowly; ladle oil over the parts that stick out of the oil to set them.
5. Once the shell has set, gently support the fish and turn it over, and fry the second side for about 2 minutes first, then continue according to doneness. When both sides are golden and the flesh at the thickest part is completely opaque, lift out, supporting the head and the body, and drain on a rack. Leave the hot oil in the pot off the heat to cool, and start a small pot for the sauce.
6. Mix the 50 ml of water, ketchup, sugar and white vinegar and bring to a boil. In a separate bowl, stir the starch with the 10 ml of water into a slurry and pour in a little at a time, slowly; stir for about 20 seconds and watch, and stop adding slurry once the sauce flows and coats the back of a spoon thinly.
7. Move the fried fish to a plate and pour the hot sauce evenly over it only just before serving, garnishing with cilantro if you like, and eat right away. Leave out the rest of the slurry once the sauce flows and coats a spoon; too thick a sauce wraps the fish in a gluey layer.

## Key points

- **Burnt outside, raw inside**: the oil is too hot and the outside colours faster than the inside cooks (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep the frying oil at 170–180°C (340–355°F), and check the thick parts for doneness before lifting out.
- **Batter falls off, shell is thick and soft**: if the fish surface is too wet or the batter coat too thick, there is too much steam for the shell to fry crisp (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Blot dry after marinating, coat the cuts, and drain off excess batter.
- **Sauce is like paste**: too much starch went in at once and [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) too thick. Add the slurry in stages and stop when it thinly coats the back of a spoon; if it is already too thick, loosen it with a little hot water.
