*Bún thịt nướng* lays hot grilled pork over room-temperature rice noodles, lettuce, cucumber and herbs, with crushed peanuts on top and a sweet-sour fish sauce dressing poured over and tossed as you eat. The pork is marinated with garlic and five-spice flavour, and the noodles and fresh vegetables spread out the flavour of each bite and catch the juices that run down.

Pork shoulder carries a little fat, so sliced thin and grilled to a brown surface it stays juicy, and the grilled flavour comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Cook the rice noodles and rinse them in cold water to wash off some surface starch so they do not clump (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The final [acid](/principles/acid/) makes the pork, peanuts and fresh vegetables taste clean; hold the dressing back until just before the first bite.

## Steps

1. Mix the garlic, shallot, five-spice powder, oil, fish sauce, salt, sugar and a little pepper, add the pork strips and rub until every strip is coated in marinade. Cover and refrigerate for 1–2 hours.
2. Make the dressing in the order given for [Nước Chấm](/recipes/nuoc-cham/): stir together the lime juice, sugar, water and the optional rice vinegar first, then add 75 ml fish sauce, taste it with the fresh vegetables and decide whether to add the rest. Add the chili last; this dressing gets no extra garlic, since the marinated pork already has garlic flavour.
3. Boil a pot of water and blanch the bean sprouts until the whole sprout is scalding hot and the bean part has no raw taste, then lift out, cool and drain. Cook the noodles in a separate pot, checking against the package time, which is usually about 3–5 minutes, and lift them out when there is no hard core and they bend gently.
4. Rinse the noodles with cold water until they are no longer warm, drain them thoroughly, and gently loosen them with chopsticks. Wash and drain the vegetables and herbs, divide them among four deep bowls, and lay the noodles on top, leaving room for tossing.
5. Preheat the grill to medium-high, scrub the grate clean and wipe on a thin layer of oil. Spread the pork strips out on the grill and turn until both sides are browned and fragrant with slightly charred edges. Strips that are too narrow can go in a grilling basket so they do not fall into the fire; do not pour the raw marinade over the cooked meat.
6. Test one of the thicker strips from the side, and take the pork off only when the centre reaches 71°C (160°F); with thin meat, do not go by the charred colour alone. Divide it over the noodles, sprinkle with crushed peanuts, and serve the dressing on the side. As you eat, pour it in a little at a time and lift the vegetables from the bottom of the bowl to toss.

## Key points

- **The pork is pale with no grilled flavour**: the grill is not hot enough, or the strips overlap. Spread the pieces out, and let the contact side colour before turning; drain off the extra liquid from the heavily marinated meat first (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **The noodles clump together**: they were overcooked, not rinsed in cold water, or pressed into a pile while draining. Lift them out as soon as there is no hard core, and loosen them gently after rinsing (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **A pool of water at the bottom of the bowl and a bland taste**: the noodles and vegetables were not drained, or too much dressing went in at once. Drain them thoroughly first, then add the dressing in small amounts; taste a whole mouthful of noodles, meat and vegetables (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)).
