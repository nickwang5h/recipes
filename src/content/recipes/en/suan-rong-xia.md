For garlic shrimp, the shrimp are split along the back, topped with garlic paste and steamed. Opening up the thick flesh shortens the distance heat has to travel to the centre and lets the sauce touch the meat; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). The tail is left connected, so the shrimp is still whole after steaming and easier to pick up.

Steam cooks the meat gently, and the hot oil afterwards briefly heats the garlic and adds a more obvious garlic fragrance; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). The saltiness of garlic sauce varies a lot, so taste it first and then decide whether to add all the light soy sauce at the end. The juices the shrimp release stay on the plate and can be used for dipping.

## Steps

1. Snip the long whiskers, legs and sharp points off the shrimp. With kitchen scissors, cut the shell along the back, then cut open the meat along that cut with a knife, stopping about 1 cm from the tail so the tail stays connected. Keep the shrimp steady and work with the blade facing away from you.
2. Remove the vein and the visible sand sac in the head, and rinse clean. Blot water from the cut and the shells with paper towels. Open the meat out to both sides and lay the shrimp on a heatproof shallow plate, in a single layer as much as possible so the thick parts do not overlap.
3. Stir the garlic sauce and spread it evenly in the cut of each shrimp, pressing it lightly open, without piling it into a thick lump on any one shrimp. Divide the oil and the minced garlic in the sauce together, and leave a gap at the edge of the plate so steam can circulate.
4. Bring water to a boil in the steamer, put in the plate of shrimp, cover and steam over high heat. Once the water is boiling again and the steam is steady, time about 3 minutes, then lift the lid and check the thick part of the largest shrimp. The meat should be pearly white and completely opaque; if it is still translucent, keep steaming until cooked through.
5. Take the plate out with a plate gripper and set it on a heatproof surface. Heat the vegetable oil in another pot and take it off the heat when ripples appear on the surface but before it smokes. Pour a little over the garlic first; it should bubble finely and smell of garlic. Then pour the rest over in several additions until even.
6. Pour the light soy sauce into the steaming juices along the edge of the plate and tilt the plate gently to mix, so it does not all land on one shrimp. Use less if the garlic sauce itself is salty. Serve hot, and take some garlic and a little of the juice from the bottom of the plate with each shrimp.

## Key points

- **The shell is red but the thick flesh is still translucent**: the shell and thin parts cook first and the centre heats more slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Judge by the largest shrimp. The 3 minutes is only when to begin checking.
- **The meat is tough and the cut has closed up**: it kept heating after it was cooked (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take the plate out as soon as it is done. Do not leave it in the steamer while waiting for other dishes.
- **Black specks and bitterness in the garlic**: the hot oil was too hot and scorched the finely minced garlic (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Pour the oil in several additions, watch the bubbling and colour first, and let oil that is smoking cool down.
