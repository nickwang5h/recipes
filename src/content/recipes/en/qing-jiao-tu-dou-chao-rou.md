Pork, potato and green pepper stir-fry puts the fat of pork belly, the softness of potato and the crispness of green pepper in one pan. The three ingredients cook at different speeds, so they go in one after another: the meat first, the potato slices fried through next, and the peppers stir-fried briefly at the end. Cutting the potato into even thin slices lets the centre cook through before the surface turns brown (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

The potato brings its own starch, which is already enough to make a little sauce cling; the final starch slurry just adds a thin glaze. Starch swells in hot liquid, so the sauce turns glossy and thicker (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The whole pan should have a thin film of sauce, and the potato slices should still separate from one another.

## Steps

1. Wash and dry the peppers and cut them into pieces, slice the potato 2 mm thin and the pork belly 4 mm thin. Cut the green onion into short pieces and mince the ginger and garlic. If the potato has been soaked in water to keep it from darkening, drain it well before it goes in the pan; mix 5 g starch with 15 g water into a slurry and set aside.
2. Put the oil in the pan and heat over medium heat until it flows easily, then add the pork slices and spread them out. Stir-fry until the slices are opaque and the edges of the fat curl slightly and fat has rendered at the bottom of the pan, then add 3 ml soy sauce and 2 g salt and toss to colour.
3. Lower the heat slightly, add the green onion, ginger and garlic and stir-fry for about 5 seconds. As soon as the aroma comes up, add the potato; do not wait for the fine minced garlic to darken. Spread the potato out as much as you can so the slices take turns touching the bottom of the pan, then add 5 ml soy sauce and 2 g salt.
4. Turn to medium-high heat and stir-fry, alternating with brief pan-frying, until the edges of the potato slices turn slightly translucent and the surface has pale yellow browned spots. Press a slice with the spatula; when it cuts fairly easily and has no hard white core in the centre, move on. If the pan is too dry and the potato is still hard, add a little hot water down the side of the pan and toss, letting the centre cook through first.
5. Add the green peppers and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the surface of the pieces is evenly hot, bright in colour and starting to soften. The potato should be intact and the pork cooked through.
6. Stir the starch slurry again, since it settles at the bottom, and pour half down the side of the pan, keeping hot liquid in the pan, then toss quickly. Stop once the sauce is glossy and lightly clings to the potato; if you need it thicker, add a little more slurry. Taste to decide whether to use the remaining salt, and plate immediately.

## Key points

- **Potato is browned outside and hard inside**: the slices were too thick or uneven (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Make the thickness uniform first; slices already in the pan can be cooked through with a little water, then stir-fried uncovered to drive off the excess.
- **Clumped starch glaze stuck to the bottom of the pan**: too much slurry went in at once, or it went onto a dry pan bottom (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir the slurry first, add it in portions, and stop once the sauce turns glossy; you need not use up all the slurry you made.
- **Few browned spots, the dish looks boiled**: the potato and peppers were too wet, or the pan was too full (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Drain before they go in, leave brief contact time with the pan while stir-frying, and cook in separate pans if you double the amount.
