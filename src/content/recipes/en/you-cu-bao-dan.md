Crispy fried eggs in garlic sweet and sour sauce is made by frying whole eggs in hot oil: the edges are charred and fragrant and the middle sets slowly. After the eggs are broken into large pieces, the surface carries a sour and spicy sauce while the inside stays springy and tender. The eggs are fried until set before the sauce is added. This order depends on [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/); if you stir before they set, the eggs break up.

Black vinegar brightens the rich flavour, and sugar softens the sourness (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Garlic and chili go in only after the eggs have set, are stir-fried briefly until fragrant, and then the sauce goes in; the wet sauce cools the pan down just enough. The green onion goes in last, to keep a little fresh green onion aroma, instead of frying with the eggs until it is dry.

## Steps

1. Crack the eggs one at a time into small bowls and keep them at hand, unbeaten. If the pan is small, fry in batches and leave space between the eggs to spread; otherwise the outer layer is already charred while the eggs piled in the middle are still raw.
2. Cut the green onion into sections about 3 cm long, and mince the garlic and chili, keeping them separate. Minced garlic is fine and burns easily in oil, so mix the sauce first rather than adding the garlic and then improvising the sauce.
3. Stir together the vinegar, light soy sauce, optional oyster sauce and sugar with 30 ml of water. Stir until the sugar dissolves and the oyster sauce is loosened, so no big lumps stick to the bottom of the bowl. If you use more soy sauce, the whole sauce will be saltier.
4. Add the oil to the pan and heat over medium heat until it flows smoothly but is not smoking. Slide the eggs in slowly close to the pan surface. When you hear the frying sound, do not move them for about 2 minutes, until the whites are set on the bottom and the edges are golden.
5. Flip and fry for about 2 minutes, until the whites have no clear raw liquid and the yolks are cooked through, then cut them into large pieces with the spatula. Keep the middle soft; do not crush the pieces that have already set again and again, and do not dry-fry them for too long.
6. Push the egg pieces aside to make a little room, add the garlic and chili, and stir-fry briefly until you can smell them. Lower the heat and pour the sauce in slowly along the egg pieces, avoiding the pooled oil; when the fierce bubbling has calmed, turn the heat up to reduce.
7. Gently turn the egg pieces so both sides are coated. When the liquid at the bottom of the pan goes from a large pool to a little thick sauce, add the green onion and toss. Take it off the heat and plate as soon as the green onion has just softened, leaving a glossy sauce on the surface.

## Key points

- **Egg pieces break and have raw liquid inside**: the bottom was stirred before it had [set](/principles/protein-coagulation/), or the pan was overfilled. Fry until set, flip and cook through before cutting into pieces, and work in batches in a small pan.
- **Bitter garlic and lost green onion aroma**: the garlic and green onion went in too early. As in [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/), add the sauce once the garlic has been stir-fried briefly until pungent, and keep the green onion for the end.
- **Hard eggs and faint vinegar aroma**: the reducing dragged on too long, so the proteins kept tightening and heat drove off the vinegar aroma. Use the [acid](/principles/acid/) to judge the finish, and take the pan off once a thin sauce can cling to the eggs; do not reduce it dry.
