Saba no misoni simmers skin-on mackerel fillets gently with ginger, sake and a sweet, salty miso sauce. It is a Japanese home-style simmered dish. Mackerel is a rich, fatty fish, and the savoury saltiness of miso supports its flavour (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)). A brief blanch of the surface reduces slime, blood and foam (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)).

The liquid does not need to cover the fish. A drop lid (*otoshi-buta*) sits right on the fish and lets the bubbling liquid fall back onto it, so even a small amount of liquid flavours the fish evenly. Keep the skin side up and move the fish as little as possible. Keep the heat low enough for a gentle simmer, because a rolling boil knocks the fillets apart (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). The miso goes in in two portions, to add aroma at the end.

## Steps

1. Pull out the pin bones and cut the fish into 4 pieces. Score a shallow cross in the skin over the thick part, just cutting through the skin. Slice some of the ginger and cut the rest into fine strips, and keep them separate.
2. Bring the 1000 ml of blanching water to a boil over medium heat, about 5–8 minutes, until it boils steadily. Blanch the fish one piece at a time for 15 seconds, until the surface turns white and the slime on the skin has set. Lift each piece straight into the chilled water and cool for 30 seconds, until the surface no longer feels hot. Gently rinse off the blood and the set bits, and blot dry with paper towels.
3. Put 60 ml of water, the sake, mirin, sugar, 2 tbsp of the miso and the sliced ginger in a small shallow pot. Heat over medium for about 2–3 minutes, stirring until the liquid boils and there are no lumps of miso.
4. Arrange the fish skin side up in the pot, turn to medium-low and put on the drop lid. If you have no drop lid, use a round of parchment paper with small holes cut in it, set close to the fish. Simmer gently for 13–15 minutes, until the fish is completely opaque, flakes easily at the thickest part and only small bubbles rise at the edge of the pot.
5. Lift off the drop lid and move one piece of fish to a plate for the moment to make room. Scoop some of the simmering liquid into the remaining 2 tbsp of miso and stir it smooth, then pour it back into the pot, return the fish and add the soy sauce. Swirl the pot gently and spoon the liquid over the fish, keeping the heat low for 1 minute, until the sauce is even and there are no miso lumps.
6. Turn off the heat, put on a regular lid and rest for 30 minutes, until the pot has stopped bubbling and the fish pieces are still whole. Then reheat over medium-low for 2–3 minutes, until the liquid is at a gentle simmer again and the centre of the fish is hot. Spoon the liquid over the fish as it heats, and watch the bottom of the pot especially if the liquid has already thickened.
7. Lift the fish out with a wide spatula and plate it skin side up. Spoon the liquid over it and top with the ginger strips. The sauce should cling to the skin, with some left on the plate.

## Key points

- **Cloudy liquid and a strong fishy smell**: the blood and slime on the surface were not cleaned off. Blanch briefly, rinse gently in cold water, then add the fish to the simmering liquid (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)).
- **Fillets fall apart and the fish is dry**: the liquid boiled hard, or the fish was turned repeatedly or cooked too long. Keep the skin side up, flavour with the drop lid and a gentle simmer, and take the pot off the heat once the fish is cooked through (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Sauce too salty or scorched on the bottom**: the fish was salt-cured, or the miso sauce was reduced too far. Choose unsalted fresh fish, use a small pot that fits the amount of fish, and keep the liquid flowing when you reheat (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)).
