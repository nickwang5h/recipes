Ponzu is clear, with a bright sour and salty taste and a citrus scent, good for dipping hot pot meat or spooning over tofu and grilled fish. At home it usually means a citrus dipping sauce with soy sauce, also called *ponzu shōyu*: the juice of Japanese *yuzu* with Japanese dark soy sauce, mirin, kombu and katsuobushi. Japanese yuzu is a different fruit from the common large pomelo; its juice is sour with a concentrated aroma, and the juice is used for seasoning.

[Acid](/principles/acid/) makes oily food taste fresher, and the sweetness of mirin softens the sharpness on the first taste. Soy sauce and kombu supply glutamate and katsuobushi brings inosinate, which together strengthen the [umami](/principles/umami/). Keeping the yuzu juice cold while the kombu and katsuobushi steep slowly in the liquid preserves the fresh fruity scent. The finished sauce does not need to be reduced; pour a little into a small dish at the table and dip lightly.

## Steps

1. Pour 2 tbsp hon mirin into a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 1–2 minutes until the liquid starts to boil, then cook over low heat for 30 seconds, until the sharp raw alcohol smell fades while the liquid is still thin. Pour into a small bowl and cool for about 5 minutes, until it no longer steams.
2. Wipe visible grit from the kombu with a clean damp cloth and leave the white powder on it. Put the kombu and katsuobushi in a clean, dry glass jar with a lid, then add the soy sauce, yuzu juice and the cooled mirin.
3. Shake the jar gently so the katsuobushi is soaked and the kombu is fully submerged. Cover and refrigerate for 24 hours, until the katsuobushi has sunk and softened and the kombu has absorbed liquid and unfolded, and when you open the jar you can smell citrus and a light smokiness from the katsuobushi.
4. Strain the sauce through a fine sieve into a clean container and let the solids drip for about 2 minutes, until the liquid no longer runs continuously, without pressing the kombu and katsuobushi hard. The strained sauce should be clear and dark brown with no obvious bits.
5. Pour the sauce back into a clean, dry jar and refrigerate; it can be used as soon as it is strained. Pour out only what you need for the meal each time, and leave sauce that has touched food in the small dish rather than pouring it back into the jar. When spooning it over tofu or fish, start with 1 tsp, taste, then add more.

## Key points

- **It tastes only sour and salty and is thin**: the steeping was skipped, or the katsuobushi and kombu did not steep through. Add them in the right amounts and refrigerate a full 24 hours, so their umami gets into the sauce (see [umami](/principles/umami/)).
- **Weak citrus scent and a harsh alcohol smell**: the yuzu juice was boiled with the rest, or the mirin was used raw. Boil the mirin briefly by itself and cool it before combining; the umami can be steeped out slowly, but the fruity aroma should not be extracted with heat.
- **Too salty or sharply sour in the mouth**: a salted yuzu juice was used, or the substitute juice is more sour. Prefer unsalted pure juice and taste it with the food you dip; a lemon substitute changes the sour aroma (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
