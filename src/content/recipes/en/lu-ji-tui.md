Soy-braised drumsticks get a salty-sweet flavour from soy sauce, sugar and whole spices. The skin takes on colour, and the meat stays whole and does not fall apart when picked up. This pot builds on star anise, bay leaf, green onion and ginger, fried until fragrant before the liquid goes in, so the fragrance gradually passes into the braising liquid (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

Drumsticks are close in thickness, so they suit braising whole. Keep the surface of the liquid at a gentle bubble so the heat has time to reach the meat beside the bone (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The braising liquid is saltier than a sauce to pour over rice, so use only a small part of it once the chicken is cooked. Reducing the whole pot concentrates the salt too.

## Steps

1. Blot the surface of the drumsticks dry with paper towels. Choose a small pot about 18–20 cm across that fits the drumsticks snugly, so you do not add extra liquid to fill a wide pot.
2. Put the oil, green onion pieces, ginger slices, star anise and bay leaf in the pot and fry over low heat for 2 minutes, until the edges of the ginger turn slightly yellow and you smell the fragrance, with the green onion not yet black.
3. Pour in the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, rock sugar and water. Cook over medium heat for 3–5 minutes, until the rock sugar dissolves and the surface bubbles steadily.
4. Add the drumsticks in a single layer, with the liquid covering about two-thirds of them. Cook over medium heat for 2 minutes, until it bubbles again at the edge of the pot, then turn the heat to low and cover.
5. Braise over low heat for 25 minutes, keeping the surface gently moving. Turn the drumsticks over and braise for another 10 minutes, until a chopstick goes into the thick meat fairly easily. Insert a probe from the side into the thickest part, avoiding the bone, and the centre should reach 74°C (165°F). If not, cook 3 minutes more and test again.
6. Lift the drumsticks onto a clean plate and drain off the excess liquid. Strain the green onion, ginger and spices out of the pot, and spoon 2 tbsp of the braising liquid over the drumsticks. The skin should look glossy with only a thin layer of sauce at the bottom of the plate. Put the rest of the liquid in a shallow container and refrigerate promptly.

## Key points

- **The skin is already dark but the meat by the bone is not cooked**: soy sauce colours faster than the inside heats. Keep the heat low, turn on time and measure the centre temperature (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **The braising liquid is bitterly salty**: too much evaporated while cooking, or the whole pot was reduced into a sauce. [Salt concentrates as the liquid reduces](/principles/salt/), so braise gently under a lid and serve only a little liquid with the chicken once it is cooked.
- **The bottom of the lunch box is full of sauce**: drain the sauce before packing and keep just a thin layer, so the rice does not go soggy. For how water and saltiness relate, see [salt](/principles/salt/). Divide into shallow boxes and refrigerate promptly, cover tightly once fully cold, and reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) at the centre.
