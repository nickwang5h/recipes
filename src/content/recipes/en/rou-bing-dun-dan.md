For steamed pork patty with eggs, seasoned ground pork is spread in a shallow dish with an egg set in the middle and steamed over water until done; it eats soft yet can be picked up with chopsticks. Both the meat and the egg set through [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). The egg mixed into the pork binds the ground meat together, and the egg in the centre keeps its own texture; the juice in the dish is what the meat and egg release as they heat.

A patty spread shallow and even cooks through more easily than one in a deep bowl (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). This dish takes no added water. Mixing only needs to disperse the seasonings and egg, and don't leave it waiting in the hot steamer after it is done.

## Steps

1. Put the ground pork in a large bowl and add the Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce, white pepper, toasted sesame oil and one of the eggs. Mix with chopsticks until no egg collects separately at the bottom and the mixture is evenly moist, without beating it for a long time.
2. Spread the meat in a shallow heatproof dish in a layer about 1.5 cm thick, flattening it out to the edges. In the centre, press a shallow well with the back of a spoon big enough to hold the whole egg, leaving a layer of meat at the bottom of the well.
3. Crack the other egg into the well in the centre, keeping the yolk whole. To steam in smaller dishes, spread the meat to the same thickness; for more servings use more shallow dishes, and don't pile one dish thick.
4. Put water and a rack in the steamer, with the water level below the dish. Once it boils, put the dish in, cover, and turn to a medium heat that keeps steam coming steadily; time about 15 minutes from when steam returns.
5. Insert a thermometer probe into the thickest part of the patty and into the egg in the centre, without touching the bottom of the dish. It is done when the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), the egg white has set and there is no runny egg in the centre. If not, keep steaming and measure again; a set surface does not mean the middle is cooked.
6. As soon as it is done, take it out at once and serve with the savoury juice in the bottom of the dish. Use a spoon to separate the patty from the edge and scoop up meat with juice. If you have to wait for other dishes, don't put it back on the heat to steam.

## Key points

- **The patty is hard and a lot of water collects in the dish**: it kept steaming long after it was cooked through, and the protein network tightened and squeezed out water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Spread the patty shallow, turn off the heat when the centre is cooked, and eat it promptly after taking it out.
- **The outer ring is cooked but the meat in the centre is still raw**: a deep bowl, a thick layer of meat or meat piled in the well slowed the [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) to the centre. Spread an even thin layer and check the meat under the egg instead of going by the colour of the edge.
- **The egg spills over to the edge and cooks unevenly**: the well is too small and the egg runs to the thinner spots and sets first (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Press the well wider first, keep the dish level, and don't dig through the middle.
- **Too salty and too peppery**: light soy sauce already carries salt, and too much pepper covers the egg flavour (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add no extra salt, use a little pepper at first, and since the raw meat can't be tasted directly, steam a small piece separately to check the seasoning.
