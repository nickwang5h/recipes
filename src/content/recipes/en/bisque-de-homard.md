*Bisque de homard* is a smooth, rich soup made from lobster shells, meat and aromatic vegetables. The classic method thickens with rice: the softly cooked rice and the soup base are blended fine, and butter and cream are added at the end. The cream gives a round mouthfeel, but the main flavour is still lobster; the reserved tail meat is cut into small pieces and placed in the bowl as a garnish.

Once the rice is soft it undergoes [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the soup thickens naturally; blending it fine is what avoids a gritty texture. At the end, dispersing cold butter into the warm soup is a smooth [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/). Do these two things separately: first cook the rice through and draw the flavour out of the shells, then adjust the thickness and the fat.

## Steps

1. Take the cut-up raw lobster, crack the claw shells, cut the tail into sections, and remove the sand sac from the head, the intestinal vein and the green tomalley inside the head, keeping the clean shell and meat. Keep the lobster refrigerated until needed and put it in the pot right after preparing it; wash the counter and tools that touched raw meat at once.
2. In the pot, sauté the carrot and onion in 20 g butter until the vegetables are slightly soft and smell sweet. Add the lobster pieces in their shells and stir until the shells turn bright red and the outside of the meat turns white, taking care not to blacken the bottom of the pot.
3. Move the pot off the heat for a moment, add the brandy and white wine, without flambéing. Return to medium heat and cook until the wine has clearly reduced, then add 250 ml chicken stock and the bagged parsley stems, thyme and bay leaf. Simmer for about 10 minutes, until the meat is white and opaque and the thickest part reaches 74°C (165°F).
4. In another pot, cook the rice in 750 ml chicken stock, stirring gently to stop it sticking, until the grains are completely soft and fall apart when pressed. Keep the rice and the stock it cooked in together for use.
5. Take out the lobster, pull off the tail and claw meat, set aside about 100 g for garnish and return the rest of the meat to the soup base. Crack the shells and put them back in the pot to simmer gently for about 10 minutes, then strain through a fine sieve, pressing out the liquid clinging to them, remove the spice bag and discard the hard shells.
6. Blend the strained soup with the soft rice and the remaining lobster meat until fine, then strain through a fine sieve again. Add the remaining 500 ml chicken stock, stir well and heat until it has just come to a boil; the soup should coat a spoon thinly and still pour in a continuous stream. Use an immersion blender for hot soup, since steam builds up in a closed jar.
7. Add the reserved lobster meat to warm through, take off the heat and stir in the cream and cayenne, then stir in 150 g cold butter in several additions. Add the next only when each has melted in, season with salt and pepper and divide among hot bowls; do not boil again once the butter is in.

## Key points

- **Rough soup with hard bits**: the rice was not cooked soft, or fine pieces of shell got into the soup. Strain out the shells first, then blend the rice and soft ingredients, and strain again at the end; do not put hard shells in an ordinary blender (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Butter floating on the surface and a greasy mouthfeel**: the finishing temperature was too high and the butter was not dispersed evenly. Stir in the cold butter in batches off the heat; if it has already separated, whisk in a little warm chicken stock until even (see [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).
- **Lobster meat like rubber**: the garnish meat was simmered a long time with the shells. Take it off and keep it as soon as it is just cooked, and put it back only at the end to warm; long cooking makes the meat shrink and lose water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
