Chirashi sushi is the sushi Japanese families make for celebrations: a big platter of sushi rice mixed with sweet-savoury shiitake, burdock and kanpyo, covered with shredded egg crepe and dotted with shrimp and snow peas, for everyone to share.

As with [sushi rice](/recipes/sushi-meshi/), the vinegar goes in while the rice is hot: the [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) grains soak it up, and the sweet-sour taste becomes the base of the rice (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Everything must be cool and the mix-in squeezed dry before it goes in, or the rice turns soggy.

## Steps

1. Wash the rice 3 times and drain.
2. Put the rice in the rice cooker pot with the 540 ml water and the kombu on top, soak 20 minutes, then start it on the white rice setting. To cook it in a pot, follow the [sushi rice](/recipes/sushi-meshi/) recipe.
3. While the rice cooks, make the mix-in: soak the dried shiitake in the 120 ml water until fully soft.
4. Squeeze out the shiitake, strain the soaking water and keep it; cut off the stems and slice the caps thinly.
5. Scrape the skin off the burdock with the back of a knife, then shave it into thin slivers, turning it as if sharpening a pencil. Keep the slivers in water so they don't brown, changing the water once or twice.
6. Rinse the kanpyo quickly, rub it with the salt and rinse it clean.
7. Boil the kanpyo for 3 minutes, transfer to iced water, squeeze dry and cut into short pieces.
8. Cut the carrot into fine julienne.
9. Put the shiitake, drained burdock and kanpyo in a medium saucepan, add the dashi, shiitake soaking water, sake, mirin, 1 tbsp sugar and soy sauce, bring to a simmer over medium heat and skim off the foam.
10. Turn the heat to low, set a drop lid (a small lid resting right on the food) on top and simmer; when the liquid is nearly gone, add the carrot and cook until almost no liquid is left, about 20–25 minutes in all. Spread it on a plate to cool; if you won't use it right away, cover and refrigerate it.
11. Make the sushi vinegar: heat the rice vinegar, 3 tbsp sugar and salt, stirring until the sugar dissolves, then take it off the heat to cool slightly.
12. Whisk the eggs, mirin, salt and starch slurry together and strain through a fine sieve.
13. Heat a nonstick pan over medium heat, wipe it with a paper towel dipped in the oil, turn to medium-low, pour in just enough egg to cover the bottom and tilt the pan to spread it. Cover and cook about 1 minute, until the surface is no longer wet. Take it out to cool and cook the rest the same way.
14. Stack the crepes, roll them up tightly, slice into fine shreds and loosen them with your fingers.
15. Pull the strings off the snow peas, blanch them for 1 minute in boiling water with a little salt, and cut them on the diagonal.
16. Devein the shrimp in their shells. Bring water to a boil, add a little salt and the sake, and cook the shrimp for about 2–3 minutes, until pink and 74°C (165°F) in the centre; lift out and peel.
17. Once everything else is done and the rice is cooked, pick out the kombu, tip the hot rice into a sushi tub that has been wetted and wiped dry, and pour the sushi vinegar evenly over it along the paddle.
18. Slice through the rice with the paddle held at an angle, fanning it as you go, until the grains are glossy and about body temperature.
19. Squeeze the liquid out of the mix-in and fold it into the sushi rice with the paddle.
20. Cover the rice with the egg shreds, arrange the shrimp and snow peas on top, sprinkle with sesame and pile the shredded nori in the centre.

## Key points

- **The rice is soggy and clumped**: the mix-in wasn't squeezed dry, or something went in while still warm (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Let everything cool completely and squeeze the liquid out of the mix-in first.
- **The sweet-sour taste sits only on the surface**: the rice was seasoned after it had cooled, so the grains couldn't take it in (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Have the sushi vinegar ready and season the rice the moment it is done.
- **The egg shreds are browned**: the pan was too hot. Turn to medium-low before pouring in the egg; if the pan is too hot, lift it off the heat for a few seconds.
- **Leftovers**: once the mix-in, egg shreds and shrimp are cooked, their total time at room temperature should stay under 2 hours. Refrigerate what is left and eat it within 2–3 days.
