*Jeyuk bokkeum* is a Korean home-style spicy stir-fried pork. Thin pork is coated in a marinade of gochujang, chili flakes, garlic and ginger, then stir-fried with onion and green onion. The finished dish has a thick, scant sauce, and the heat comes with sweetness and salt. Marbled pork shoulder suits it, and [slicing thinly against the grain](/principles/knife-work/) shortens the muscle fibres and makes seasoning and cooking more even.

Once the marinated meat releases liquid, spread the slices out first and wait for the sauce to tighten, then let the edges brown through the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Gochujang is red to begin with, so you cannot tell by colour whether the pork is cooked. Check the centre temperature. Take the meat off the heat as soon as it is done so the juices do not cook away.

## Steps

1. If a whole piece of pork shoulder is too soft to slice, freeze it for 1 hour, until the outside is slightly firm and the centre can still be cut. Then slice against the grain into pieces about 3 mm thick and cut them into chunks about 5 cm wide. If you bought pre-sliced pork, skip the 1 hour in the freezer.
2. Mix the gochujang, chili flakes, soy sauce, mirin, sugar, honey, toasted sesame oil, garlic and ginger. If you are using the apple, mix it in too. Press out the lumps of paste with the back of a spoon until the sugar and paste are evenly dispersed.
3. Separate the slices one by one and toss them with the marinade, the onion and the green onion. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes. When you take it out, every slice should carry a thin coat of sauce, including the folds, with no clumps of meat stuck together.
4. Preheat a heavy skillet over medium-high heat for 2–3 minutes, until the meat sizzles the moment it goes in. Add half the meat and vegetables and spread them into one layer with tongs. The marinade already has sesame oil, so add no other oil and no water.
5. Cook for about 2–3 minutes, until the slices loosen and the onion starts to soften. Keep stir-frying for 3–4 minutes more, until the free liquid at the bottom of the pan has nearly cooked away and the edges of the meat are lightly browned. Push a thin probe from the side into the centre of the thickest slice, and take the meat out at 71°C (160°F).
6. Wipe the burnt sauce out of the pan and preheat for 1 minute, until the next batch still sizzles clearly when it goes in. Cook the other half the same way. If the meat has browned but the centre is not done, turn the heat to medium and cook 1–2 minutes more, then probe again.
7. Combine the two batches of cooked meat on a plate and toss so every portion gets onion and green onion. Serve at once with rice. Dry the lettuce and use it to wrap the meat. Serve the cooked meat on a clean plate, not in the container that held the raw marinated meat.

## Key points

- **A pan of meat in liquid**: too much went in at once, and the water from the meat and onion pulled the pan temperature down. Cook in two batches, spread out, and brown briefly once the free liquid has cooked away. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Sauce is burnt black but the meat is not cooked through**: the slices are too thick or the heat is too high. Cut them evenly to about 3 mm, lower the heat once brown edges appear and check the centre for 71°C (160°F), and wipe off the burnt bits before the next batch. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Dry, tough lean meat**: a very lean cut was used, or the meat was kept cooking for a darker colour. Use marbled pork shoulder, and take it out once it is cooked through and the sauce clings. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
