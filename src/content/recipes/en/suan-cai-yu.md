Fish with pickled mustard greens uses fermented sour mustard greens and freshwater fish for a sour and spicy base. The whole fish is divided into bones and slices: the head and bones are fried fragrant and simmered into a broth, and the slices go in last. This gives the broth time to develop, and lets the tender flesh come off the heat in time. Buy a mustard green product: the thick stems have a crunch and the leaves carry the sour aroma into the soup.

The slices are thinly coated, and when heated the starch forms a smooth coating (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Whether the slices are tender depends mostly on how briefly they are cooked: cooked longer, the protein keeps shrinking and squeezes out water, and the flesh turns dry (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). At home you can have the fish counter fillet the fish and take the bones and flesh home in separate bags.

## Steps

1. Pull out the pin bones you can feel in the flesh and cut it on the diagonal into large slices about 3 mm thick. Cut the head and bones into pieces and pat them dry. Mix the slices with the salt, white pepper and egg whites, then with the cornstarch, and refrigerate for 15 minutes, until the surface has a thin coating and no liquid pools at the bottom of the bowl.
2. Rinse the pickled greens and squeeze them dry, and cut into pieces about 3 cm long, slicing the thick stems thin on the diagonal. If they taste so salty they are harsh, soak in cold water for 5 minutes, until they are still sour but less salty, then squeeze dry.
3. Put 2 tbsp oil in the pot and stir-fry the ginger, sliced garlic, white parts of the green onion and pickled chilies over medium heat for about 1 minute, until you smell the ginger and garlic. Add the pickled greens and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until there is less steam and a clear sour aroma, then take them out.
4. Add 2 tbsp oil to the same pot and fry the fish head and bones over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, turning once, until the surface turns golden. Return the pickled greens, pour in 1600 ml hot water and boil over high heat for 2 minutes, until the soup is rolling all over; turn to medium-low and cook for 3 minutes, until the soup turns cloudy and has a clear taste of fish and pickled greens.
5. With a slotted spoon, lift the bones, head and pickled greens into the bottom of a deep bowl, taste the soup, then add salt. Keep the soup at a gentle simmer and add the fish slices one by one in 2 batches, cooking each batch for 30 seconds, until the thickest part is opaque and the flesh separates into pieces when lightly pinched with chopsticks, then lift them onto the pickled greens at once. Do not stir with a spatula.
6. Pour the hot soup into the bowl and spread the minced garlic, green part of the green onion, dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns in a pile over the fish slices. Add the remaining 4 tbsp oil to a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until ripples appear on the surface. Pour a small spoonful first, and when the spices immediately start to form fine bubbles, pour the rest of the oil evenly over them. Serve hot.

## Key points

- **The slices fall apart and the flesh is dry**: they were cut too thin, moved too much or cooked too long. Cut to an even thickness, cook in batches at a gentle simmer, and lift out each batch as it is done (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **White lumps of coating float in the soup**: the egg white and starch did not stick to the slices, and leftover coating in the bowl went into the pot. Drain the slices before coating, and put only the slices into the soup (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **The sourness is weak and the pepper aroma is bitter**: soaking the pickled greens too long washes out the sour aroma, and oil that is too hot scorches the chili pieces. Taste first and then soak briefly, and pour a small spoonful of oil first to watch for bubbles (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
