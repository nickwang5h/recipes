Typhoon shelter shrimp is a crisp, fragrant seafood dish common in Hong Kong: shell-on shrimp are fried first to bring out their flavour, then tossed with minced garlic, chili and bread crumbs. The crumbs are loose and golden, cling to a layer on each shrimp when you pick it up, and leave dry, fragrant garlic crisps on the plate.

The method heats the shrimp and the crumbs separately. The shrimp first lose surface water in hot oil (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)); the garlic and bread crumbs then colour slowly over lower heat to build the aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). The shrimp are already cooked, so at the end they only need to be mixed in. Stop when the garlic smells done; do not toss the shrimp and crumbs together for long in pursuit of a darker colour, or the shrimp shrink small and hard.

## Steps

1. Snip off the shrimp's feelers, sharp points and legs, cut open the shell along the back, pull out the vein and dry the shrimp. Toss with 1 tsp Shaoxing wine and 0.25 tsp white pepper and marinate in the refrigerator for 15 minutes so the seasoning coats the shrimp evenly; pat the surface dry again before they go in the oil.
2. Pour the vegetable oil into a small wok and heat over medium heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the oil reaches 150°C (300°F), flowing lightly on the surface but not smoking. Fry the shrimp in two batches, about 15 seconds per batch, until the shells turn red and the exposed meat on the back is fully white and opaque, then lift onto a rack to drain.
3. Between batches, bring the oil back up over medium heat for about 1 minute, until it again reaches 150°C (300°F). Return each batch of fried shrimp to the oil for 5–10 seconds, until the shells are dry and the bubbles lessen, then lift out at once and spread them out.
4. Turn off the heat, pour off the extra oil and leave 80 ml in the wok. Turn to medium-low heat, add the ginger and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until fragrant; add the garlic and chili and stir-fry another 30 seconds, until the edges of the garlic are just pale yellow.
5. Add the bread crumbs and keep stirring over medium-low heat for about 2–3 minutes, until the crumbs are loose and evenly golden, with no damp clumps at the bottom of the wok. If the garlic is colouring quickly, lower the heat at once.
6. Return the shrimp and add the salt, sugar, the remaining white pepper, green onion and the remaining Shaoxing wine. Toss for about 30–60 seconds, until the shrimp are coated in crumbs and the wok is dry. Transfer to a wide plate at once, spread out, and serve.

## Key points

- **Soft shells and greasy crumbs**: the shrimp were not dried, or too many went in at once and the oil temperature dropped, so the surface dried slowly. Fry in batches, letting the oil recover between them; spread the shrimp out to drain after frying (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Garlic burnt and bitter while the crumbs are still pale**: the garlic was cut too fine or the heat was too high, so the garlic burnt first. Cook the two together over medium-low heat and keep stirring (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Shrimp curled into tight rings, tough**: fried too long, or tossed together for too long at the end, so the proteins in the meat contracted and the water was lost. Lift the shrimp out as soon as the meat is fully white, and at the end toss only until the crumbs stick (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
