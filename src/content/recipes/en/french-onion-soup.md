French onion soup (*Soupe à l'oignon gratinée*) is built on browned onions and beef stock. Toasted bread goes on top of the soup, is covered with cheese and is baked until it gratinées. The top is crisp, the layer below soaks up the soup, and the onions leave sweetness and a deep roasted flavour.

**Browning sets the depth of the soup.** The onions must first release their water and soften before they take on colour easily. The colour comes from both [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/), in which sugar takes part, and the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/), in which sugar reacts with amino compounds. The brown film on the bottom of the pot is part of the flavour too, so scrape it back into the soup when you add the stock.

## Steps

1. Melt the butter over medium heat in a heavy-bottomed pot, add the oil and onions, stir, and cover with the lid. Braise covered until the onions collapse and turn translucent, then take off the lid.
2. Brown the onions. Add the sugar and a little salt, turn the heat to medium-high, stir often and scrape up the bottom, and cook until the whole pot is a deep walnut brown and smells sweet. As the moisture cooks off, lower the heat gradually, and no black spots should appear at the edges.
3. Sprinkle the flour evenly over the onions and stir over low heat for about 3–4 minutes, until the flour is fully coated into the onions and no dry flour is left in the pot.
4. Build the soup base. Take the pot off the heat first and pause briefly, then pour in some of the hot stock, stir to disperse the flour and scrape up the brown film from the bottom. Then add the rest of the stock, the vermouth and the brandy.
5. Simmer the soup base. Bring it to a bare simmer, then leave the lid slightly open and keep it bubbling gently. Taste after about 90 minutes. The soup should have a rich onion sweetness. If the liquid has dropped too much, add water, then season with salt and black pepper.
6. Toast the baguette until it is dry on both sides and lightly golden, and get ovenproof soup bowls ready. Toasting the bread dry first lets it hold up in the soup and lets the cheese spread evenly.
7. Gratinée the top. Ladle in the hot soup, set the bread on top and cover it generously with cheese. Move to the oven under the broiler and bake until the cheese melts, bubbles and shows golden spots. Serve in the bowls. The bowls are very hot, so carry them with care.

## Key points

- **The soup is pale and the flavour thin**: the stock went in before the onions had released their water, so the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) was not done. Next time, wait until the whole pot of onions is a deep brown before adding the stock and simmering.
- **A burnt, bitter taste**: some spots overheated, and the [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) of the sugar went on toward burning. Scrape the bottom often while cooking the onions. If the film starts to turn black, lower the heat and add a little water to loosen it, and don't let burnt bits get into the soup.
- **The cheese is burnt but the soup is not hot**: the broiler only colours the surface and doesn't heat through to the soup below. Bring the soup to a scalding heat before you ladle it into bowls, and take the bowls out when the cheese bubbles and shows golden spots.
