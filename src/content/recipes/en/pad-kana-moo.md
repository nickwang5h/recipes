*Pad kana moo* (ผัดคะน้าหมู) is a Thai home-style stir-fry of gai lan and pork. The pork slices carry the sweet-salty oyster sauce, the gai lan stems stay crisp and the leaves are just soft, and a little thin sauce is left on the plate to spoon over rice. The garlic and chili are not chopped fine but pounded coarsely, so you can still see pieces in the finished dish.

The dish depends on fast heat. Thin pork slices cook in seconds (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Gai lan stems are cut into thin diagonal slices and the leaves into large pieces, so they need only about 30 seconds in the pan (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Garlic and chili are turned in the hot oil for a few seconds first so their fragrance goes into the oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Wash the gai lan and drain it, and cut off the tough bottom ends. Slice the stems thinly on the diagonal and cut the leaves into large pieces. Put the garlic and chili in a mortar and pound to a coarse texture; do not pound too fine.
2. Heat a wok over medium-high heat, add the oil, and stir-fry the garlic and chili for a few seconds. Add the pork slices right away.
3. Keep stirring until the pork is almost entirely white. Cut into the thickest slice: it should no longer be pink inside, and the centre should reach 71°C (160°F).
4. Add the oyster sauce, light soy sauce and sugar and mix. Stir-fry until the sugar dissolves and the liquid in the pan has reduced a little.
5. Add the gai lan and stir-fry for about 30 seconds. If the pan is too dry, drizzle in a little water to loosen the sauce on the bottom, and leave a little thin sauce to coat the meat and greens. Take it off the heat and plate right away. Serve with rice.

## Key points

- **The stems are hard but the leaves are already limp**: the stems were cut too thick or stayed in the pan too long. Slice the stems thinly on the diagonal, cut the leaves large, stir-fry for about 30 seconds and turn off the heat, since residual heat keeps cooking. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The pork is hard to chew**: the slices were cut too thick, or cooked a long time after the sauce went in. Cut the pork thin, stir-fry until just cooked, then season. See [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Too much liquid on the plate and the greens are watery**: the greens went in before the sugar had dissolved and the sauce had reduced. Cook the sauce until it thickens first, then add the gai lan and stir-fry quickly.
