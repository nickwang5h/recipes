*Risi e bisi* is a Venetian thick soup of rice and peas, with grains cooked through but still with some bite, surrounded by a spoonable broth. Young peas give a clean sweetness, and a small amount of prepared tender pods goes into the pot too. Butter, onion and meat stock hold up the sweetness of the peas, and Parmigiano finishes it.

The thickness of the soup comes from starch released from the surface of the rice (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The rice keeps absorbing water after cooking, so if it waits in the pot the soup gradually turns into a lump of rice. The cheese brings salt and aged [umami](/principles/umami/), so stir it in only after the rice is cooked and taste for salt then.

## Steps

1. Shell the fresh peas and keep all the peas, then pick about one cup of whole, tender pods. Break the pods open, snap off one end and pull to strip off the tough inner membrane and strings; cut off any part you can't clean, keeping only tender pieces that snap easily.
2. Rinse and drain the peas and prepared pods, and heat the stock in a separate pot. Find a lidded pot big enough that the rice and peas spread out rather than piling up in the middle.
3. Melt the butter in the pot, add the finely chopped onion and cook over medium heat until soft and lightly golden. Add the peas and pods, season with a little salt, and stir for about 2–3 minutes so they are evenly coated in butter.
4. Pour in 710 ml of hot stock, cover, and cook at a gentle boil for about 10 minutes. When the surface of the peas is soft and the centres still give a little resistance, add the rice; the pod pieces will slowly soften.
5. Add the rice and the remaining 120 ml of stock, stir well, cover, and cook at a steady gentle boil for about 20 minutes. Stir from the bottom now and then; stop when the rice is cooked in the centre with a slight bite, and the pot should hold a thick soup that flows between the grains.
6. Stir in the parsley and cheese, taste and add salt, and ladle straight into shallow soup plates. A spoonful should carry rice, peas and soup together as it falls back; if it has dried so it piles in a lump, add a little hot water and stir it in before serving.

## Key points

- **It turns into dry rice on the plate**: the rice kept absorbing water, or too much evaporated during cooking. Cook covered at a gentle boil, serve as soon as it is done, and keep a flowing broth at the end. Add hot water to restore the thickness, then taste for salt again. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Stringy pod fibres in the mouth**: the pods were too old, or the inner membrane was not stripped off. Keep only tender parts you can strip; if in doubt, throw the pods away and keep the peas. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Too salty once the cheese goes in**: the stock already has salt, and the cheese adds another portion. Leave room while cooking and taste after the cheese is stirred in; salt will also concentrate as the liquid reduces. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
