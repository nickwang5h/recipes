*Sugo di pomodoro al burro* is a tomato sauce with a gentle balance of sweet and sour and the richness of butter. Tomatoes, butter and a halved onion simmer together slowly, the onion gives its flavour to the sauce, and it is taken out before serving. The method comes from Hazan's recipe, and the sauce suits [potato gnocchi](/recipes/gnocchi-di-patate/) as well as ordinary dried pasta.

The key is a slow, uncovered simmer that lets the extra water evaporate. As the tomatoes soften and are crushed, the sauce thickens. The butter adds the smooth feel of fat and makes the acidity taste softer; see [acid](/principles/acid/). Low heat keeps the bottom and the top of the pot exchanging heat, and an occasional stir stops the thickening sauce from scorching at the bottom first; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).

## Steps

1. Pour the whole canned tomatoes with their juice into a heavy-bottomed pot. Cut them into a few pieces with scissors or crush them with a wooden spoon. Add the butter and the halved onion. Trim the root of the onion, but keep enough of it that each half holds together.
2. Heat over medium until the butter melts and the tomato juice starts to bubble, then turn to low right away. Slow small bubbles should keep appearing at the edge and in the centre of the pot, with the sauce moving gently. Cook uncovered to leave a way out for the steam.
3. Simmer slowly for about 45 minutes, stirring from the bottom now and then and pressing any tomato pieces that are still whole with the back of the spoon. Leave the onion in large pieces and cook it until soft with translucent edges. If anything sticks to the bottom, scrape it up gently right away and lower the heat.
4. Look at the sauce: it should have gone from a thin liquid to a thick pulp that falls slowly from a spoon, with a little butter visible floating on the surface or at the edges. If it is still watery, keep simmering uncovered, and turn off the heat once it reaches that state.
5. Take out the two onion halves, taste the sauce and add salt. For pasta, heat the sauce first, add the cooked pasta and a little pasta water as needed, and toss until the tomato sauce coats the pasta evenly. Portion any sauce you cannot use at once, cool it, and refrigerate or freeze it.

## Key points

- **Still thin after a long simmer**: the lid held in the steam, or the pot is too narrow, so the water evaporates slowly. Cook uncovered at a visible slow boil and judge it done by the thickness of the sauce. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Scorched and bitter at the bottom**: once the sauce thickens it moves more slowly, but the heat has not been reduced, so a patch loses water and scorches first. Use a heavy-bottomed pot and stir the bottom promptly. If it has already burned black, move the top layer into a clean pot and leave the black bottom behind. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Sharp acidity and heavy saltiness**: the tomatoes themselves have a poor flavour, or too much salt was added before the sauce thickened. Choose whole canned tomatoes made from ripe tomatoes, let the butter melt fully into the pulp, and taste for salt at the end. The acid comes from the tomatoes, and more fat will not remove it. See [acid](/principles/acid/) and [salt](/principles/salt/).
