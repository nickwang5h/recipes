Red-braised pork belly is made differently from place to place. This is the Hunan version, Chairman Mao's red-braised pork: the colour comes from caramel colour rather than dark soy sauce, with a little dried chili and cassia for fragrance. The sugar is heated in the pan until reddish-brown, which gives colour and a caramel aroma. Then the meat is added and coated, water is poured in and the pork is braised; see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/). The caramel colour has to be finished before the water goes in.

The softness of skin-on pork belly comes from another process: the collagen in the skin and connective tissue gradually converts to gelatin in moist heat; see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). The meat changing colour is only the beginning. The pork is nearly done when chopsticks pass easily through skin and meat. Braise until soft first, then reduce the sauce, and you get both tender meat and a glossy sauce.

## Steps

1. Blanch the pork belly. Put the whole piece into boiling water and cook for 3–4 minutes, until the outside turns white and foam rises. Lift it out and let it cool until you can hold it firmly.
2. Cut it into pieces of similar size, with the skin on, and drain off the surface water. Wet meat makes the oil spatter when it goes into the caramel, so once it is cut, set out the wine, water and spices first.
3. Make the caramel colour. Put the oil and the sugar for the caramel in a clean, heavy-bottomed pan, heat over low heat and move it gently. Once the sugar has melted, keep watching: when the syrup goes from pale yellow to reddish-brown and smells of caramel, immediately turn the heat to low or take the pan off the heat.
4. Coat the pork with the caramel. Carefully add the drained pork pieces and turn them until every side is coated with the syrup, then add the Shaoxing wine down the side of the pan. Do not leave the pan on the heat waiting for the sugar to darken further, and do not lean over the pan.
5. Braise the pork. Add enough water to just cover the meat, along with the ginger, star anise, dried chilies, cassia and most of the green onion sections. Bring to a boil, then cover, turn the heat to low and braise for about 45 minutes. Keep the surface at a gentle bubble and turn the pieces now and then. If the water drops below the meat while the skin is still firm, add more water and keep braising.
6. Test for tenderness. Push a chopstick into the thickest piece. When it passes through both skin and lean meat and the piece still holds its shape, uncover the pot. If the skin still has clear resistance, keep braising over low heat and do not reduce the sauce yet.
7. Reduce the sauce and season. Turn the heat up to medium-high and turn the pieces gently, reducing until the sauce is glossy and clings to the meat, with a little still-flowing sauce left in the bottom of the pan. After tasting, add light soy sauce, salt or sugar as needed, sprinkle on the remaining green onion sections and serve.

## Key points

- **Colour is dark and it tastes bitter**: the caramel colour went too far, and [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) turned into burning. Throw out a burnt, bitter caramel and start over; more sugar will not cover it.
- **The meat is cooked but the skin is still hard to chew**: [collagen converting to gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) needs moist heat and time. Add water and keep braising over low heat, and reduce the sauce only once the skin and meat are soft.
- **The sauce has dried up but the meat is tough**: the sauce was reduced too early over too high a heat, so the water evaporated before the meat was soft. Check the skin and meat first, as in [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/); while reducing, turn the pieces often and stop when the sauce is glossy and clings to the meat.
