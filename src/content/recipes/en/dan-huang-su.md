Salted egg yolk pastries are small domed pastries: a salted duck egg yolk wrapped in red bean paste, with layers of flaky pastry around it. When you cut one open, the crust falls apart in thin sheets, the bean paste is fine and moist, and the yolk at the centre is salty and rich. Using lard in both the water dough and the oil dough gives the texture of traditional Chinese flaky pastry.

The water dough has [gluten](/principles/gluten/) and stretches, which is what lets it wrap an oil dough made of only flour and fat. Rolling out and rolling up layers the two doughs one inside the other. In the oven, heat reaches each layer, the water evaporates and the dough sets (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Before the filling goes in, every layer must be whole and thin enough for the baked layers to separate.

## Steps

1. Mix the flour, sugar and lard for the water dough with 85 ml of warm water into a dough and knead for about 10 minutes, until the surface is smooth and the dough slowly stretches into a thin sheet. Cover and rest for 30 minutes, until it springs back less when rolled.
2. Press the cake flour and lard for the oil dough into a fine dough. Cover and rest for 30 minutes, until both doughs are soft and neither cracks nor leaks oil when pressed. Divide the bean paste into 8 portions of 25 g each, flatten each one, wrap a cooked salted duck egg yolk in it, then pinch it closed and roll into a ball.
3. Weigh the water dough and the oil dough separately and divide each into 8 equal portions. Press each water dough portion into a small round that is thick in the middle and thin at the edge, put in an oil dough portion, then gather and pinch tight. Cover each one as you finish, so the surface doesn't form a skin.
4. With the seam side up, roll lightly into an oval about 12 cm long and roll it up from the short end. Cover and rest for 10 minutes, until the roll no longer springs back much. Then roll it lengthwise into a long sheet again, roll it up, cover and rest for another 10 minutes.
5. Set the oven to 175°C (350°F) and preheat it for about 15 minutes, until the oven shows that it has reached the set temperature. Press the middle of a roll, fold both ends toward the middle, flatten and roll it out into a round wrapper that is thick in the centre and thin at the edge. Place a filling ball in it and slowly close the wrapper with the web of your hand, then pinch the seam tight.
6. Arrange them seam side down on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper, 3 cm apart. Brush on a thin layer of egg yolk and leave for 5 minutes, until the surface is slightly dry. Brush on a second layer and sprinkle with black sesame seeds.
7. Bake on the middle rack for about 15–20 minutes, until the tops are golden, the bottoms are dry and the layers show on the sides. Move to a rack and let cool for 15 minutes, until the crust has set when picked up and sheds flakes with a light touch, then eat.

## Key points

- **Dough tears when rolled and the oil dough shows through**: the two doughs differ in softness, or the gluten in the water dough can't stretch (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Keep the doughs soft and pinch any crack together right away. Roll and fold with light pressure, and don't pull hard.
- **Dough keeps shrinking back and the layers are crushed**: the gluten hasn't relaxed after kneading and rolling (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Cover and rest for another 10 minutes, and keep rolling once it hardly springs back, without pressing hard.
- **Top is golden but the bottom is still damp and soft**: the surface colours faster than the inside dries (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cover the top with foil, bake for 5 more minutes and check the bottom. Use bean paste that can be formed into a ball, since a paste with too much water makes the bottom damp.
