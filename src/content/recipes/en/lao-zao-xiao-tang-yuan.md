Mini tangyuan in fermented rice soup is a glutinous rice dessert soup with the sweet fragrance of fermented rice. The tangyuan must be cooked through in the water first, so the glutinous rice starch absorbs water and heat and turns soft and chewy; see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Adding the fermented rice later lets you control how done the tangyuan are and how long the fermented rice is heated separately.

Fermented rice (*laozao* or *jiuniang*) is glutinous rice that has already fermented. It has the sweetness of converted starch, a faint sourness from fermentation and a hint of wine; see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/). It only needs to be heated and stirred in at the end; a long boil drives off some of the aroma, as described in [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). Ready-made fermented rice varies in sweetness, so hold back the sugar until you have tasted the soup. Use both the rice grains and the liquid so the flavour is complete.

## Steps

1. Pour 300 ml water into a small pot and bring it to a boil. Stir the rice grains and liquid of the fermented rice gently together before weighing it out, and rinse the goji berries. If the tangyuan are frozen, take them out only when you are about to add them.
2. Tip the tangyuan into the boiling water and push them gently along the bottom with the back of a spoon so they come away from the bottom and separate from one another. Keep the water at a gentle roll, and don't press on them with the spoon or the skins will split.
3. Cook for the time on the package and check them at about 8 minutes. Once they float, still make sure the centre is cooked: cut one open or let one cool and taste it. The inside should be evenly soft and chewy, with no white dry-flour core.
4. If the tangyuan are larger and the package calls for more time, keep cooking. If the water is so low that they can't move freely, add hot water, bring it back to a boil and check again.
5. Add the fermented rice and the optional goji berries and stir them in gently with a spoon. Cook for about 2 minutes more, until the soup is back at a faint simmer, the rice grains have loosened and the goji berries have swelled slightly, then turn off the heat. Do not boil hard.
6. Taste the soup first, then add the reserved sugar in portions and stir until dissolved. If the fermented rice is sweet enough, add no sugar.
7. Divide between two bowls and eat while warm. Don't leave them in the pot, where they keep swelling and stick together.

## Key points

- **Soft outside but still a floury core**: the heat was turned off because they floated, or the tangyuan were larger than expected. [Starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) needs water and heat to reach the centre. Cook by the package and eat one before taking the pot off the heat.
- **The tangyuan stick to the pot or split**: the surface turns sticky after [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), combined with too little water or too hard a boil. At the start nudge them along the bottom, keep enough water for them to move, and keep a gentle roll later on.
- **The wine fragrance is faint**: the fermented rice went in too early, and continued heating drove off the volatile aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add it after the tangyuan are cooked, simmer briefly and turn off the heat.
- **The soup is too sweet and cloying**: the fermented rice already brings sweetness, and all the sugar was added at once (see [Fermented Seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). Taste the soup before adding sugar, and if it's too sweet, thin it with hot water.
