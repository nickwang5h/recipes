*Nước chấm* is Vietnamese for "dipping sauce". This is the most common version, a sweet and sour fish sauce dip: lime, sugar and water form the base, fish sauce brings the saltiness and [umami](/principles/umami/), and chili and garlic add pungency. You can dip spring rolls in it, or toss it through [Bún Thịt Nướng](/recipes/bun-thit-nuong/) to tie together the rice noodles, meat and fresh vegetables.

This sauce works through the proportions of its flavours. Sugar softens the bite of the acid, and water lowers the saltiness of the fish sauce (see [acid](/principles/acid/) and [salt](/principles/salt/)). Brands of fish sauce differ, and so do the juice yield and sourness of limes, so balance the sour and sweet first and add the salt after. The finished sauce should be fluid and fresh, with sour and sweet first, then a clear salty savouriness, and the heat coming last.

## Steps

1. Roll the limes lightly on the counter, then cut them open and squeeze the juice, pick out the seeds and measure 80 ml. Do not wring the peel into the juice. A little pulp can stay in, but remove the membranes and seeds from the juice.
2. Stir the lime juice, sugar and warm water until you can feel no sugar grains at the bottom of the bowl. Taste this layer on its own; it should be like a sourish lime sugar water. If you use rice vinegar, add it at this step, and check that you can taste the sour and sweet together.
3. Add 75 ml of fish sauce and stir, then test the taste with a small bite of cooked rice noodles or lettuce. If the main ingredients still taste bland, add the reserved fish sauce 1 tsp at a time, stirring and tasting again. The sauce can be a little strong on its own, and should come out just right once tossed with unseasoned rice noodles.
4. Add the minced garlic and the first portion of chili rings, stir and taste the heat again. The garlic can be left out; if you are wary of heat, serve the remaining chili separately. Don't try to make up for the aroma of chili or garlic by adding more fish sauce.
5. Divide among dipping dishes, or put it in a small pitcher for tossing with noodles. Use a wide, shallow bowl for dipping large lettuce wraps, so the whole bite can be dipped into the sauce; for rice noodles, pour it in in stages and toss to loosen them. If you make it ahead, cover and refrigerate, and stir again before serving.

## Key points

- **Very salty, with an obvious fishy smell**: the fish sauce is too concentrated and the sweet and sour base is too weak. Take a small portion, add water and a little lime juice to adjust it by taste, then correct the whole bowl in the same proportion; sugar cannot cover up saltiness (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Only sharp sourness, with no roundness**: the limes are quite sour, or the sugar is not fully dissolved. Stir until the sugar grains are gone, then add sugar in small amounts; sugar changes how the sourness is perceived but does not remove it (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
- **Tasty for dipping but bland when tossed with noodles**: a whole bowl of rice noodles and fresh vegetables dilutes the sauce. Drain the vegetables and noodles, add the sauce in stages, and judge with the main ingredients; tasting the sauce alone does not represent the whole bite (see [umami](/principles/umami/)).
