Dark jelly made from ready-made guilingao powder has a mild herbal bitterness, which the sugar softens. The setting agent in the powder decides how it forms: products containing starch need heat to [gelatinize](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and products using other gelling agents have their own cooking and cooling requirements. Read the ingredient list and instructions first, then decide whether this ratio of water to powder applies.

Use an unsweetened powder that needs cooking: 25 g of powder to 620 ml of water in total, divided into four small bowls. Cold water disperses the powder first, then hot sugar syrup is added gradually, so the outside of the powder does not set into a shell on contact with heat while the inside stays dry. While cooking, keep scraping the bottom of the pot so the thick liquid [heats evenly](/principles/heat-transfer/), and in the end it cools into a complete jelly.

## Steps

1. Put 25 g of guilingao powder in a small pot and add 120 ml of cold water in portions. Stir along the bottom and corners until no dry clumps are visible and the liquid is even; if the package says to soak first, do that step as directed.
2. In another heatproof container, put 100 g of white sugar, pour in 500 ml of boiling water and stir until the sugar grains are completely dissolved. Mix the syrup in while it is still hot.
3. Stir the powder mixture in the pot again and slowly pour in the hot syrup while stirring constantly. Mix each addition in before continuing, to reduce the chance of lumps forming between the cold and hot parts.
4. Heat over medium-low, scraping across the bottom and sides of the pot constantly, until the whole pot thickens evenly and small bubbles start to rise. If the package says to keep cooking for a while after boiling, cook for the stated time; don't stop just because bubbles appear at the edge.
5. While hot, divide into four clean small bowls or moulds, scrape off what is left at the edge of the pot, and tilt the containers gently to level the surface. Set them somewhere stable, and once they stop steaming heavily, cover and refrigerate for about 1–2 hours.
6. Cut only when the centre is cold through and the whole bowl has set. The knife should leave a clean cut face, and when you shake it gently, one soft block of jelly moves, not ripples of liquid.

## Key points

- **Hard lumps of powder, rough cut face**: the powder was not dispersed in cold water first, and the hot water thickened the outside of the clumps first. Stir the cold powder mixture smooth and add the hot syrup slowly; for the mechanism in starch-based products, see [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Still runny after chilling**: the ratio of water to powder does not suit the brand, or it was not cooked enough. Check the package for water amount and heating requirements, an undercooked mixture won't set however long it chills.
- **Scorched bottom, thin top**: the thick liquid flows slowly, and the bottom keeps getting heat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lower the heat, keep scraping the bottom, so the whole pot thickens evenly.
- **Too sweet, with the bitterness masked**: a powder that already contains sugar was used, and the full amount of sugar for unsweetened powder was added too. Confirm first whether the product contains sugar, reduce the added white sugar according to the package.
