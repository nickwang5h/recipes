Sogogi muguk is a common Korean clear soup: thin beef, radish, green onion and garlic are all it takes to make a pot. You can see the shape of the radish in the soup, and the first taste is the savoury beef, followed by the clean sweetness of the radish. It is a soup to eat with rice.

The beef is seasoned with soup soy sauce and toasted sesame oil, stir-fried until it changes colour, and then simmered in water to make the broth. The savoury compounds in the meat pair with the glutamate from the fermented soy sauce (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). Soup soy sauce is light in colour but full in saltiness, which keeps the soup clear. The beef is sliced thin, so it is easy to eat after a short simmer (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the beef against the grain into bite-size slices about 2 mm thick, and mix in 1 tbsp of soup soy sauce, 1 tsp of minced garlic, the toasted sesame oil and black pepper. Refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the seasoning is evenly coated on the slices. While the beef marinates, cut the radish into slices about 3 cm square and 6 mm thick, and cut the green onion into pieces.
2. Set a soup pot over medium heat, add the beef with its marinade, and stir-fry for about 3 minutes, until the slices separate and the whole surface has changed colour. Add the water as soon as the slices change colour, so the minced garlic doesn't scorch.
3. Pour in 1900 ml of water and heat over medium-high heat for about 7–10 minutes, until the surface of the soup bubbles steadily. Skim off the foam, turn to medium heat, cover and cook for 10 minutes, keeping a steady stream of small bubbles in the soup. The beef should be completely cooked and bite through easily.
4. Add the radish slices, cover and cook for 10–15 minutes, until the radish gradually turns translucent at the edges and the centre, and a chopstick pierces it easily. Taste a slice: the centre should have no raw, crunchy radish texture, and the edges should still be whole.
5. Add the green onion pieces, the remaining 1 tsp of minced garlic and 1 tbsp of soup soy sauce, then taste the soup and season with salt and black pepper. Cook for 5 more minutes, until the green onion is soft and the raw sharpness of the garlic has gone, then turn off the heat and serve.

## Key points

- **Beef is too tough to chew**: a large piece of stewing meat with a lot of membrane was used, or the slices were cut with the grain and too thick. Choose a brisket with little membrane and slice it thin against the grain; if it is cut into thick chunks, it needs a longer simmer. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Radish is soft outside and hard inside**: the slices are too thick, and the centre heats slowly. Make the thickness even at about 6 mm and taste once the whole slice is translucent; radish pieces of different sizes cooked together tend to break apart in some places while still raw in others. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Soup is dark and the savoury taste is covered by saltiness**: regular dark soy sauce was used, or full salt was added early and then cooked for a long time. Use soup soy sauce, added in two parts, one with the beef and one before the pot comes off the heat; leave salt adjustments until the radish is soft and the volume of soup is steady. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
