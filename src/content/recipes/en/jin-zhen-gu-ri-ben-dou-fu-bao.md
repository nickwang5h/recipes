The star of this clay pot dish is tube-packed egg tofu: it is fried until the surface is golden, then braised in the sauce, so each bite has both a fried fragrance and a silky tenderness. Before frying, let the moisture on the bottom surface drive off first so the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) can happen easily on the surface.

The enoki mushrooms are stir-fried until soft first. Their volume shrinks and they hold up the tofu, and the sauce reaches it more evenly. The final thickness comes from evaporation, and reducing until the sauce can cling to the tofu is enough. The braised outer layer softens again but the fried fragrance remains. The gel structure of egg tofu is brittle, so lift it from underneath when moving it, and do not chop it up by sliding a spatula back and forth.

## Steps

1. Prepare the main ingredients. Cut off the roots of the enoki, pull them into small bunches, rinse and drain. Gently squeeze the egg tofu out of its packaging and cut it into rounds of similar thickness. Chop the chilies and mince the garlic. If the tofu surface has obvious drops of water, blot them gently with paper towels.
2. Fry the tofu. Add 10 ml of oil to a frying pan, heat over medium-low heat, and spread the tofu in a single layer. Wait until the bottom is golden and the edges have set, then lift and turn with a wide spatula. Fry both sides to a light golden colour and take them out. If the pan is small, fry in batches, and do not crowd the soft tofu together.
3. Mix the sauce. Stir the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, dark soy sauce, sugar and 100 ml of water together until the sugar has mostly dissolved, and set it beside the stove.
4. Stir-fry the enoki. Add the remaining 5 ml of oil to the pan, add the chilies and garlic, and stir-fry until fragrant and before the garlic turns brown. Add the enoki and stir until the stems soften and the whole pile has clearly collapsed, with a little mushroom liquid in the bottom of the pan.
5. Braise the tofu. Lay the fried tofu flat over the enoki and pour the sauce in along the edge of the pan. Bring to a boil, cover, turn to low and braise for about 5 minutes, keeping the pan at a gentle bubble. During this time only shake the pan gently, and do not stir-fry.
6. Reduce the sauce. Uncover and turn to high heat, shaking the pan lightly or spooning the liquid from the edge over the tofu. Turn off the heat when a glossy layer of sauce is still in the bottom of the pan and it can coat the tofu thinly, and lift it onto the plate together with the enoki.

## Key points

- **Tofu sticks to the pan and breaks when turned**: it was moved while the contact surface was still wet and soft (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Give the bottom more time to set, lift from underneath with a wide spatula, and only shake the pan while braising.
- **Still pale after long frying**: the surface is too wet or the pan is crowded with tofu, so the heat goes into evaporating water first. Dry the surface, fry in batches, and let the surface dry enough for the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) to happen.
- **The inside of the tofu turns tight and the pan is dry**: the sauce was reduced too long, and continued heating tightens the network of [denatured and coagulated protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Stop once the sauce clings to the surface; if the pan is nearly dry, add a little water first.
