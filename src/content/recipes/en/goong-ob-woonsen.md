*Goong ob woonsen* braises shrimp and glass noodles together in one pot, with ginger, garlic and pepper as the main fragrance. The oil first warms the aromatics in the bottom of the pot, then the sauce goes in so the noodles take on these flavours (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). When done, the noodles are glossy brown and springy, with almost no loose liquid left in the pot.

The dried mung bean glass noodles are soaked until they bend, then finish cooking in the pot as they take up the sauce. Soaking softens them but does not cook them; the heat and liquid in the pot [gelatinize](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) the starch. The lid holds in the steam, so the shrimp on top are heated too (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Choose a small heavy-bottomed pot that just fits the noodles and shrimp; a clay pot must be a type made for direct use on the stove.

## Steps

1. Soak the noodles in 1000 ml of room-temperature water for 10 minutes, until they bend easily and the bundle loosens. Drain and cut into two or three lengths with scissors, so lifting a bite at the table does not bring up the whole pot.
2. Snip the long feelers and the sharp spine off the shrimp heads, cut open the back shell and remove the vein, keeping the shells on. Smash the garlic, bruise the cilantro stems and slice the ginger. Crush the black peppercorns and set them with the ground white pepper.
3. Stir the light soy sauce, oyster sauce, black soy sauce, sugar, toasted sesame oil and 120 ml of stock until no sugar grains are visible. Add the noodles and shrimp and toss, then leave for 5 minutes, until the noodles are evenly coated with the brown sauce, tossing once partway.
4. Put the oil, ginger, garlic, cilantro stems and pepper in the small pot and heat over medium for about 2 minutes, until the bottom keeps up a light sizzle and the ginger and garlic smell strongly, with the garlic still pale. Add the noodles and all the braising sauce, and lay the shrimp flat on top of the noodles.
5. Cover tightly and heat over medium-high for 1–2 minutes, until you hear continuous boiling and steam escapes around the lid. Turn to medium-low and braise for 3 minutes, then lift the lid and turn the noodles up from the bottom to spread around any sauce not yet absorbed.
6. Cover again and braise for 2–3 minutes, until the noodles are translucent and springy with no hard core when bitten through, the shrimp are opaque with the centre at 74°C (165°F), and no liquid pools at the bottom. If the noodles are still hard, add 15 ml of the held-back water at a time, cover and braise 1 minute more.
7. Take off the heat, scatter the green onion pieces over, cover and leave for 1 minute, until the green onion softens but is still bright green. Serve straight from the pot, lifting out noodles together with shrimp and braising sauce. Leave the ginger slices in the pot and serve with white rice.

## Key points

- **Hard noodles, dry pot**: the water evaporated too fast, or the lid leaks steam. Use a small pot with a tight lid and add the held-back water in portions (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Dry-heating the whole time will scorch the oyster sauce and sugar first.
- **Noodles collapse into a lump**: they were soaked and cooked too long and then turned over and over. Use fine mung bean noodles, soak only until pliable and stop the heat once braised (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Bitter ginger and garlic**: the aromatics scorched in a dry pot. Add the wet noodles and braising sauce as soon as the fragrance comes up and the garlic is still pale (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
