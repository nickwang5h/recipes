Stir-fried flat rice noodles should be dry and fragrant, with each strand separate. The noodles are already steamed, so the pan only reheats them, cooks off the extra surface water and coats them in soy sauce and oil. If there is standing water in the pan, the heat goes first into evaporating it; drain the vegetables and stir-fry the pork separately before combining, and you get a dry, fragrant result (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

Refrigerated fresh rice noodles turn hard, because the [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) starch has retrograded. Soften them before separating the strands and they are less likely to break. To judge doneness, check that the strands are hot through, soft and still springy.

## Steps

1. Gently separate the fresh rice noodles. If they have turned hard in the fridge, warm them briefly until they bend, then pull apart the strands that are stuck together; if they are already soft and supple, separate the strands directly, without rinsing off the oil on the surface.
2. Blanch the bean sprouts in boiling water for 60 seconds, then lift out and drain. Cut the cucumber into shreds and lightly press the water out of the cooked gluten; set each aside with the sprouts, and pour off any water that collects on the plate.
3. Coat the pork. Before beating the egg, set aside a little egg white, and mix it with the pork, starch and pepper until each strand of pork has a thin coating and there is no pool of starchy liquid in the bowl. Beat the rest of the egg and set aside.
4. Using the oil for the pork and egg, stir-fry the pork first, spreading it out and then turning, until the strands separate and even the thickest piece is cooked through, then remove. Pour in the remaining beaten egg and stir-fry until set with no liquid egg, then remove and set aside.
5. Add the oil for the noodles to the pan, add the green onion whites and garlic, and stir-fry until fragrant. Add the noodles, drizzle in the dark soy sauce, and toss gently until the colour is even, then sprinkle in the cumin and the optional MSG.
6. Toss the pork, gluten and light soy sauce into the noodles, then add the bean sprouts, cucumber and cooked egg. Lift from the bottom of the pan and toss until the noodles are hot all the way through and there is no liquid left at the bottom; taste a bite, then decide whether to add salt.
7. Turn off the heat, toss in the green onion greens and plate immediately. For several servings, fry in separate batches, and give the noodles room to spread in each batch, or the stir-fry turns into steamed noodles.

## Key points

- **Wet and sticky, not fragrant**: the bean sprouts, cucumber or gluten carry water and drag down the pan temperature (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain everything well before it goes in, stir-fry briefly after combining, and do not add extra water.
- **Noodles break into short pieces**: the refrigerated noodles were still hard when pulled apart, or were cut and chopped repeatedly in the pan (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Warm them to soften first, and lift and spread instead of pressing and cutting.
- **Colour is right but it is too salty**: the dark soy sauce, light soy sauce and salt have stacked up (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Mix the two soy sauces first and taste at the end before adding salt; do not add any more salty stir-fry mixes.
