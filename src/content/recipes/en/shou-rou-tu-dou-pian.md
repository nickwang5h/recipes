In lean pork and potato slices, the potato is soft but whole and the pork is smooth and tender. Both are cut into slices about 2 mm thick, the potato is blanched first, and then the two are stir-fried together. The centre of a thin slice is close to the surface, so it cooks quickly (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Because the potato is blanched in advance, it does not have to wait in the wok, and the lean pork gets less time over heat.

The pork is coated in a little light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and starch. When the starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat, it gives the pork a smooth surface. Lean pork still loses water as its [proteins coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/), so even after the starch marinade the stir-fry must be short. In the finished dish the potato slices are soft and whole, the pork is cooked through, and there is no thick, pasty sauce at the bottom of the pan.

## Steps

1. Peel the potato, halve it, and cut it into slices about 2 mm thick. Rinse off the loose surface starch briefly after cutting. Wash the garlic shoots and cut them into pieces about 1 cm long, keeping the tougher white parts apart from the green leafy parts.
2. Trim the tough membrane off the pork and cut it against the grain into slices about 2 mm thick. Add the starch, 5 g of the light soy sauce and the dark soy sauce, and toss lightly until every slice is evenly coated. Marinate for 10 minutes. Don't let a pool of juice sit at the bottom of the bowl and thin out the coating.
3. While the pork marinates, bring a pot of water to a boil. Add the potato slices spread out, and nudge them gently so they don't stick. Lift them out when the slices bend, have no hard core when bitten, and are still whole. Don't boil them for a fixed 5 minutes.
4. Spread the potato slices out to drain. Don't pile them thick in the colander. Wait until the surface stops dripping before they go into the wok, so no blanching water gets into the pan and the hot slices don't steam each other to mush.
5. Heat the wok, add 15 ml of oil, and when the oil flows easily but has not started to smoke, add the pork. Spread it out and let the surface set, then stir-fry until both sides change colour. If the wok is small, work in batches and don't press the pork into a lump.
6. Add the white parts of the garlic shoots and stir-fry for about 20 seconds until fragrant. Then add the potato slices, the green leafy parts and the remaining light soy sauce. Toss gently, taste, and add salt in small amounts, keeping the potato slices whole.
7. Stir-fry together for about 3 minutes. When the thickest pork slices are cooked through, the potato can be bitten through and the garlic shoots are just cooked, plate it. Take it off the heat as soon as it is ready, and don't stir-fry the potato apart.

## Key points

- **Potato breaks down into a paste**: the slices were blanched too long and then stirred repeatedly (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Lift them out as soon as they just bend, and toss gently when they go back in the wok.
- **Pork is dry and tough**: the pork waited with the potato to cook, or the stir-fry together ran too long (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Blanch the potato fully first, and take the dish off the heat as soon as the pork is cooked through.
- **Potato has a hard core while the pork is already overcooked**: the slices are uneven in thickness (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Blanch the thicker slices separately until done before adding them to the wok, and cut the slices to an even thickness next time.
