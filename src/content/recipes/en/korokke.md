Korokke has a crisp shell and a soft mashed-potato centre scented with beef and onion; it is an old favourite of Japanese *yoshoku* cooking. The home-style potato and ground beef version mashes boiled potatoes, mixes in cooked beef, onion and a little vegetable, then coats the patties in bread crumbs and deep-fries them. The potatoes cook through by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), and draining off extra water before shaping gives a filling that is soft yet able to hold the coating.

The crisp shell comes from the bread crumbs losing water in hot oil (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Cool the filling first and make the patties an even thickness: a warm filling is soft and cracks easily, and a patty that is too thick may still be cold in the centre when the coating is already golden. Plan on 2 per person.

## Steps

1. Put the potatoes in cold water and heat over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water boils, then cook 15–20 minutes more, until a chopstick goes through the centre easily. Drain all the water, return the potatoes to the pot over low heat and shake for 1–2 minutes, until the surface is dry and floury and no water is left in the pot, then take off the heat and mash, leaving a few small lumps.
2. Add 2 tbsp of oil to a wok and stir-fry the onion over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until translucent; add the carrot and shiitake and stir-fry about 3 minutes, until the fine pieces are soft. Add the ground beef, break it up and stir-fry about 5–7 minutes, until the meat is fully cooked and the centre reaches 71°C (160°F).
3. Drain the juices from the bottom of the pan from the beef and vegetable filling, and mix it into the mashed potato with the salt and both peppers. Spread out to cool for about 10 minutes, until warm but not hot to the touch, then mix in the 1 egg for the filling.
4. Divide into 12 portions and press into ovals about 1.5 cm thick, smoothing out cracks at the edges. Refrigerate in a single layer for 30 minutes, until the surface is cool and a patty can be picked up without bending or collapsing.
5. Put the flour, the 3 eggs beaten into egg wash, and the panko in three separate plates. Coat each patty in turn: a thin layer of flour, a dip in the egg, then covered all over with bread crumbs, pressing lightly so the coating sticks and covers the edges fully.
6. Heat the oil in a deep pot over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until a thermometer reads 175°C (345°F). Fry 2–3 at a time for about 4–6 minutes, turning gently once, until both sides are golden and the coating is crisp; measure the centre from the side, and lift them out only when it reaches 74°C (165°F).
7. Wait about 1–2 minutes between batches, until the oil returns to 175°C (345°F) before adding more, and skim out burnt crumbs. Rest the finished korokke on a wire rack for 2 minutes, until they stop dripping oil, keep them in a single layer to let steam escape, and dip in tonkatsu sauce while hot.

## Key points

- **They crack and the filling leaks in the oil**: the potato or the cooked filling was wet, the patty too soft, and the coating has gaps. Drain, dry the potatoes in the pot, and chill until a patty can be lifted steadily before coating; don't keep poking them in the hot oil. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **The shell is greasy and soft**: too many went in at once and dropped the oil temperature sharply, or they were stacked after frying and held in steam. Fry in batches and spread them on a wire rack. See [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **The coating is deep brown but the centre is still cold**: the patty is too thick and heat hasn't reached the centre. Keep the thickness uniform when shaping; the filling has raw egg mixed in, so still check the centre temperature. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
