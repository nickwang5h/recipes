*Pasta e ceci* is a chickpea and pasta soup from central and southern Italy, with garlic and rosemary as the usual base flavours. Versions with anchovy, with tomato or with mixed short pasta exist in different places. This one uses garlic oil, a little tomato and meat stock. Some of the chickpeas stay whole and others are crushed, so the thick soup still has the texture of whole beans.

The garlic takes colour in the oil first, then the rosemary briefly infuses its aroma (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The bean purée thickens the soup, and the short pasta is cooked directly in the soup, releasing starch that binds it further (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). The tomato goes in only after the beans are soft, so the acid does not keep the beans from softening.

## Steps

1. Cover the chickpeas with plenty of cold water and soak in the fridge for 8–12 hours, then drain off the soaking water. Bring fresh water to a boil, add the chickpeas, reduce to low heat and cook about 1.5–2 hours or longer, until a bean mashes to a paste easily with no hard bits in the centre. Drain and set aside.
2. Put the olive oil and whole garlic cloves in a soup pot and heat over medium heat, turning the cloves from time to time. When the surface is light golden brown, take them out, add the rosemary and stir briefly until you smell its aroma, then add the tomatoes and their juice.
3. Cook the tomatoes over low heat for about 20–25 minutes, until the pieces are soft and a little oil rises at the edges. Add the cooked chickpeas and stir for about 5 minutes, so the beans are evenly coated in the juices in the pot.
4. Add 240 ml of the beef stock first, cover and simmer gently for about 15 minutes, then remove the rosemary. Scoop out about a third of the chickpeas, mash them with a little soup to a fine purée and stir it back into the pot.
5. Add the remaining beef stock, return to a gentle simmer and add the short pasta, stirring gently from the bottom of the pot often. Add the reserved hot water in stages according to thickness, and cook until the pasta is tender but still has bite and the soup flows slowly. Do not let it reduce to a dry pot.
6. Stir in the butter and cheese, taste and add salt and black pepper, and serve hot in bowls. The pasta keeps absorbing soup, so if it is not going to the table right away, make the bean soup first and cook the pasta just before eating.

## Key points

- **The garlic oil is bitter and the soup tastes scorched**: the garlic got too dark and the bitterness has gone into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Take the garlic out when it is light golden brown, and pass the rosemary through the oil only briefly. If the oil is already scorched and bitter, start the pot over.
- **The beans are soft outside and hard inside**: acidic tomato went in before the beans had fully softened (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Cook the beans in plain water until they mash to a paste, then add them to the tomato base. Older beans may need longer.
- **The soup is too thick and the bottom layer is scorched**: the short pasta absorbs water, and the bean purée and starch thicken the soup, so the bottom can overheat in spots (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cook over low heat, stir gently along the bottom and add hot water again and again. The soup should still flow when it goes into the bowls.
