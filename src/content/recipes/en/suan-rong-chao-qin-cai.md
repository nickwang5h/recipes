Garlic stir-fried celery uses thin-stalked Chinese celery and is eaten for the fresh fragrance and crispness of the stalks. Low heat first lets the garlic aroma into the fat, then high heat cooks the celery through. Thin stalks heat quickly, and taking them out in time keeps the juice you get when you bite. For how the garlic is handled, see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

The heat control in this dish starts before the celery goes into the wok. Splitting thick stalks keeps thin ones from going soft while thick ones are still raw, and draining the wash water well means the wok's heat goes into the celery instead. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/) and [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Lard brings a meaty richness, while vegetable oil lets the smell of the celery itself stand out.

## Steps

1. Pick off old leaves and tough stalks, keeping a few tender leaves. Wash the celery. Split the thicker stalks lengthwise so the pieces are similar in thickness, then cut into pieces no longer than 5 cm.
2. Drain the celery in a colander and shake off the water droplets between stalks and leaves. There should be no water dripping steadily before it goes in the wok. Shake apart any leaves that are wet and clumped.
3. Mince the garlic. Put the celery, garlic and salt by the stove and have a plate ready, so you do not have to leave the wok to look for ingredients during the quick stir-fry.
4. Heat the wok and add the lard. When it has fully melted and spreads smoothly over the bottom, turn to low heat. Sprinkle in the salt and then add the garlic right away. Stir for about 10 seconds. When you smell the garlic and it is still pale, go to the next step.
5. Turn to high heat, add the celery, spread it out quickly and toss from the bottom up. After about 30 seconds, check: the stalks are bright in colour, the tender leaves have just shrunk, a stalk you pick up is still upright, and it is hot, crisp and not noticeably raw-tasting.
6. Plate it as soon as it reaches that state. If you use thicker regular celery, cook until the centre is hot all the way through instead of going by 30 seconds. Chinese celery does not need to be cooked until limp.

## Key points

- **Water pools at the bottom and the celery wilts**: lots of wash water, or an overfull wok, means heat goes into evaporating water first (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain well, and cook in batches when you scale the recipe up. Do not wait for the water to cook off by lengthening the time.
- **Some stalks are raw and some are soft**: uneven thickness heats the centres differently (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Split thick stalks first. Regular celery is thicker than Chinese celery and needs a little longer.
- **The garlic turns black and the whole plate tastes bitter**: finely minced garlic scorches quickly in hot oil (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry the garlic over low heat and add the celery as soon as you smell it. Garlic that has already blackened should be thrown out and redone.
