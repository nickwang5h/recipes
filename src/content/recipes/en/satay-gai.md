*Sate kai* is Thai chicken satay, served with a warm peanut sauce and a cucumber pickle. The marinade is built on turmeric, coriander seed and cumin, and the coconut milk carries the aroma to the surface of the meat (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The chicken is cut into thin strips so it cooks quickly, and brushing the skewers with coconut milk as they grill keeps the outside moist.

The peanut sauce is cooked from red curry paste, coconut milk and finely ground roasted peanuts. It is sweet and salty, with a touch of sourness from tamarind. The cucumber pickle is fresh and uses [acid](/principles/acid/) to cut the richness of the skewers, so the next bite tastes clean.

## Steps

1. Cut the chicken thigh into strips about 7 cm long, 2 cm wide and 0.6 cm thick. Toss with the turmeric, coriander seed, cumin, palm sugar, 240 ml of the coconut milk and the pineapple juice, cover and refrigerate for 2 hours. Soak the bamboo skewers in water for at least 30 minutes.
2. For the cucumber pickle, heat the sugar, vinegar, water and salt until the sugar dissolves completely, then take off the heat and let cool. Put the cucumber, shallots and chili in a separate bowl, and pour the fully cooled sweet-sour liquid over them only just before serving.
3. In a small pot, heat the oil, red curry paste and 60 ml of the coconut milk, stirring as you fry, until it smells fragrant and a few droplets of oil appear at the edge of the pot. Add the rest of the sauce ingredients and cook over low heat, stirring, for about 2–3 minutes, until the sauce is evenly thick and smooth and coats a skewer. Take off the heat.
4. Thread the marinated chicken onto the bamboo skewers with the strips lying flat, not bunched into thick wads. Mix the coconut milk meant for brushing with the water. Discard the raw-meat marinade.
5. Oil the grill. Put the skewers over medium charcoal heat, brush lightly with the coconut milk and water, and turn. After about 8–10 minutes, brown spots appear on the surface; when the centre of the thickest piece reaches 74°C (165°F), they are done. Check every batch.
6. Let the peanut sauce cool until warm before spooning it into small dishes, and serve the cucumber tossed with the sweet-sour liquid in a separate bowl. Eat the skewers as soon as they come off the grill. Do not use the leftover coconut milk and water that touched raw chicken as a dipping sauce.

## Key points

- **Meat charred outside, raw inside**: the strips were bunched into thick wads on the skewer, so the centre heats slowly. Lay thin strips flat, and move them to a cooler part of the grill if the surface browns too fast (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Surface turns mushy after marinating**: the protease in fresh pineapple acted for too long. Marinate for 2 hours and grill, and do not marinate overnight (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **Peanut sauce scorches on the bottom and the spices turn bitter**: the thick sauce sat against the bottom of the pot for too long. Keep the heat low and scrape the bottom as you stir, and stop when it is thick enough to coat a skewer (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Cucumber weeps water and the sour liquid thins out**: it was soaked in the sweet-sour liquid too early. Cool the liquid first and toss only just before eating (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
