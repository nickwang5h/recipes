*Bò lúc lắc* is a Vietnamese dish of beef cut into cubes and seared in a hot pan, shaking it as it cooks. "Lúc lắc" is the back-and-forth shake of the pan. The cubes are like dice, but they still need to sit on the bottom of the pan first to form a brown crust before you turn them. Garlic, black pepper and a salty, savoury marinade coat the surface, and the hot beef goes onto sweet-sour watercress so the juices run down the leaves to the bottom of the plate.

A contact surface that is hot and dry enough gives a roasted aroma (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)), but if you cook too long the meat protein tightens and the beef turns dry (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). So choose a steak cut with a little marbling, cut the cubes even, and leave gaps in the pan. The sour note from the vegetables goes in last (see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/)).

## Steps

1. Mix the garlic paste, sugar, cornstarch, 1/2 tsp of the black pepper, oyster sauce, light soy sauce and fish sauce, and toss with the beef cubes so the marinade coats every side in a thin layer. Refrigerate for about 20 minutes.
2. Rinse the shallot slices briefly in cold water and drain, then put them into the mixed rice vinegar, water, sugar, salt and a little pepper. Keep the watercress separate and do not toss it with the dressing yet, so it does not wilt too early.
3. Heat a large frying pan and add half the oil. When the oil shows ripples, add half the beef in a single layer with gaps between the cubes. The marinade should stay on the meat; leave any extra liquid at the bottom of the bowl.
4. Leave the cubes alone for about 1 minute, until the underside is clearly browned and lifts easily, then turn them with a spatula. After that, change the side about every 30 seconds until the outside is browned and fragrant. Insert a thermometer from the side into one of the larger pieces and take the batch out when the centre reaches 63°C (145°F); this usually takes 3–4 minutes per batch.
5. Reheat the pan and sear the second batch with the remaining oil. If the garlic bits have blackened, wipe off the black residue before adding the beef. Do not put the first batch back in for more cooking.
6. Only just before serving, toss the watercress with the shallot and sweet-sour dressing and spread it on a plate. Pour both batches of beef and the cooked juices from the plate over the top and serve right away with rice; the leaves should only be lightly wilted by the hot meat.

## Key points

- **The beef is grey and the pan is full of water**: too much went into the pan at once, or the marinade liquid went in with it. Sear in batches and in a single layer, and wait for the contact side to brown before turning (see [The Maillard Reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)).
- **Burnt outside, cold inside**: the cubes are uneven, or the sugar and garlic burned first. Cut even cubes, lower the heat a little once the crust has formed, and check the centre temperature; burnt garlic bits will make the next batch bitter too.
- **The beef has colour but is hard to chew**: a stewing cut was used, or the meat was reheated again and again after searing. Use a steak cut and take it off the heat as soon as it is done (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **The leaves go limp and there is a lot of liquid**: the vegetables were wet, or the dressing was tossed in too early. Drain the leaves thoroughly and dress only just before serving (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
