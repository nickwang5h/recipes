The creaminess of century egg and pork congee comes from the rice grains absorbing water and [gelatinizing](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) in the water. Keep cooking until the grains burst open and the starch enters the congee liquid, and the rice and liquid become one. Lean pork needs much less heating time, and adding it after the congee base is done reduces the [water loss caused by protein contraction](/principles/protein-coagulation/).

This is the home-style version with soy sauce, oyster sauce and lettuce, so the congee comes out slightly darker. The century egg is cut into small pieces so it keeps a whole texture in the congee. Ginger shreds and pepper provide the pungent aroma, and the sauces are stirred in last so the saltiness is easy to control.

## Steps

1. Measure 150 ml of dry rice with a measuring cup, rinse it, put it in a rice cooker with 1000 ml of clean water and the ginger shreds. Select the congee setting, not the rice setting, and do not let the rice and water go over the congee fill line.
2. Cut the lean pork into thin shreds and mix with the vegetable oil, then refrigerate until needed. Peel the century eggs and cut into small pieces, chop the green onion and cilantro, shred the lettuce finely, and use a clean cutting board for the ready-to-eat ingredients.
3. When the congee program finishes, check that the rice grains have burst with no hard core and that rice and liquid hold together when scooped up. If the rice grains are still sitting at the bottom with a layer of clear water above, keep cooking the congee. If it has become so thick that it is hard to stir, loosen it first with a little boiling water.
4. Pour the finished congee into a soup pot and heat to a gentle bubble. Add the pork shreds scattered, pushing them lightly to prevent clumping, and cook until the pork is cooked through (about 3 minutes) and the centre of the thickest piece reaches 71°C (160°F). If the congee is very thick, add water first so the pork shreds can spread out in it.
5. Add the century egg and shredded lettuce, fold gently, and cook until the century egg is heated through and the lettuce has wilted (about 30 seconds). The pieces of century egg should be whole, the pork shreds separate, and the surface of the congee still able to flow slowly.
6. Stir in the light soy sauce, oyster sauce and white pepper, then add the reserved salt in stages, tasting as you stir. Turn off the heat and sprinkle with green onion and the optional cilantro, then divide into bowls immediately, keeping in mind that the congee keeps thickening after leaving the heat.

## Key points

- **The congee and liquid separate and the rice centres are hard**: [gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and the breakdown of the grains are not complete. Keep heating gently and stir the bottom; add boiling water if there is too little.
- **The pork shreds are dry and tough**: the meat cooked for a long time with the rice, and the [muscle proteins keep contracting](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Add the pork only after the congee base is done.
- **It is too salty as soon as the sauces go in**: century egg, soy sauce and oyster sauce are all salty. Stir well and taste before deciding whether to add [salt](/principles/salt/), and do not pour in the whole reserved 2 g at once.
