*Flammekueche* is the Alsatian "flame cake": a very thin crust topped with cultured cream, onion and smoked bacon. The cream base is often mixed with fromage blanc, though some traditional versions use only thick cultured cream. Here it is only the cream: slightly tangy and rich, with a crisp edge and a centre that still folds gently.

The dough is flour, water and a little oil, kneaded together. After resting it rolls out thinner (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). The baking pan preheats with the oven too, so heat enters the dough from underneath at once (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Spread only a thin layer of cream and scatter the onion and bacon, or the topping will weigh the crust down into one wet sheet.

## Steps

1. Mix the flour, salt, water and oil, and knead for about 3–5 minutes, until the dough is fairly smooth and no dry flour shows when you tear it open. Cover and rest for 30 minutes, so the surface doesn't dry out. The wait also makes the rolled dough shrink back less.
2. Put a thick metal baking sheet or baking stone on the lower-middle rack and preheat the oven to 250°C (475°F). Start preheating while the dough rests. The pan must be heated through, and it is not enough for the air in the oven to reach temperature.
3. Cut the bacon into thin strips. Put the bacon in a cold pan and heat over medium until the fat turns translucent and a little fat collects in the pan. Take the strips out while they are still soft and drain off the fat. Slice the onion thin and separate it by hand, so no clump of rings overlaps.
4. Stir together the crème fraîche, nutmeg and black pepper. Divide the dough in two. On a lightly floured counter, roll each piece into a rectangle about 1–2 mm thick. Roll from the middle outward, and make the corners and edges thin too, with no thick rim left.
5. Move one sheet of dough onto a thin metal tray and spread half the cream evenly over it, leaving about 1 cm around the edge. Scatter half the onion and bacon over it without piling it high. Dust the tray with only a thin layer of flour to stop sticking, then slide the dough onto the preheated pan.
6. Bake for about 8–10 minutes, until the base is dry, the edge is golden with a few dark spots, the onion is soft and the bacon is cooked through. Lift a corner with a spatula to look at the underside. If it is still wet and white, bake longer, and don't go by the edge colour alone.
7. Move the first flammekueche to a rack and cut it into pieces to serve right away, then assemble and bake the second. Don't stack freshly baked ones or leave them on the hot pan, because steam from the underside softens the crisp crust again.

## Key points

- **The dough shrinks back as soon as you roll it**: the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) is still tight. Cover it and rest it a few more minutes, then roll gradually from the centre outward. Keep forcing it and the middle tears while the edge is still thick.
- **The edge is burnt but the bottom is still soft**: the pan wasn't heated through, or the topping is too thick. Let the pan store heat beforehand, spread the cream thin and scatter the toppings. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The bacon is black and the onion is bitter**: the strips are too small, or the baking went on until deep brown. Take it out when the base is cooked through and the edge is golden. The toasty aroma from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) turns acrid and bitter if you go further.
