Tengzhou vegetable jianbing has a crisp, browned outside and a hot vegetable filling inside. Thin pancakes wrap napa cabbage, glass noodles, tofu and a layer of egg, and are griddled on a hot *ao* griddle or in a flat-bottomed pan, then folded into a strip that is easy to hold. The pancake is thin and browns quickly against the pan; the filling sits behind two layers of pancake, so the centre warms slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

Cook the glass noodles through and stir-fry the filling until no water pools, then sandwich and griddle: once the pancake is crisp, the filling is hot all the way through. Napa cabbage gives off water once it is mixed with salt (see [salt](/principles/salt/)), so do not season the filling and let it sit. Choose a ready-cooked, thin, foldable Shandong pancake with a diameter slightly smaller than the bottom of the pan, so there is room to flip it.

## Steps

1. Soak the dry glass noodles in warm water for about 10 minutes, until they bend without snapping, then cook as the package says, until there is no hard core in the centre. Drain and cut into short pieces no longer than 5 cm.
2. Cut the napa cabbage and carrot into fine shreds and the tofu into cubes no bigger than 1 cm. Cut the optional Chinese chives into short lengths and the ham sausage into small dice. Drain all the vegetables first, so no washing water gets into the filling.
3. Add 15 ml of oil to a flat-bottomed pan and stir-fry the cabbage, carrot and tofu over medium heat, until the shreds are soft and there is almost no water pooled in the pan. Add the glass noodles, the optional Chinese chives and ham sausage, and toss to combine and heat through.
4. Add the salt, cumin powder, black pepper and the optional MSG and chili flakes, mix well and taste. Move the cooked filling out of the pan; if it still gives off vegetable liquid, leave that in the pan and do not put it between the pancakes.
5. Wipe the pan clean and brush with part of the remaining oil. Pour in the beaten egg over medium heat and spread it into a thin sheet. When the surface of the egg has just set, lay one pancake on top, flip so the egg sheet faces up, then turn the heat to low.
6. Spread the cooked filling evenly, leaving a margin of about 1 cm at the edge, and cover with the second pancake. Press gently so the layers stick together, and brush the bottom of the pan or the pancake lightly with the remaining oil.
7. Griddle over medium-low heat for about 2 minutes, until the bottom is lightly golden, then lift with a wide spatula, flip and griddle for another 2 minutes. Turn to the lowest heat and griddle for about 1 minute more, until both sides are golden, the pancake edge is crisp and the filling in the centre is hot. If not, keep flipping over low heat.
8. Fold both sides toward the middle into a strip about 10 cm wide, cut it in half and eat while hot.

## Key points

- **Outside is burnt and the centre is still cold**: the pancake and the filling heat at different speeds (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cook the filling first, spread it flat and griddle over low heat; do not pile it in the centre.
- **Glass noodles have a hard core**: soaking soft only means they took up water, so still check that they are cooked through (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cook them as the package says before adding them to the filling.
- **Pancake is wet and soft, and the filling leaks when flipped**: the cabbage gave off water, or the filling is too wet and piled too thick (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Stir-fry until no water pools in the pan, leave a margin at the edge, and eat right after griddling.
