*Sườn ram mặn* is a sweet-salty Vietnamese home-style pork rib dish. Short pieces of ribs are coated in caramel, simmered soft with fish sauce and a little water, and then the sauce is reduced. The dark colour and toasty aroma come from the [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) done before the meat goes in; reducing the sauce concentrates the colour and flavour onto the meat.

How tender the ribs get depends on time. The [collagen slowly turns into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) in the tendons and connective tissue, which lets the meat come away from the bone and gives the sauce a slight stickiness. Braising covered first to soften the meat, then uncovered to evaporate the liquid, lets you control softening and concentrating separately. The finished dish is glossy, with a little flowing sauce left on the plate.

## Steps

1. Blanch the ribs. Put 1500 ml of water in a pot and heat over medium heat for about 8 minutes, until boiling. Add the ribs and cook for 2–3 minutes more, until the surface turns white and the foam gathers. Lift out the ribs, drain for about 2 minutes until they stop dripping, and pat dry with paper towels.
2. Marinate the ribs. Toss with the garlic, shallot, salt, pepper and the optional chicken bouillon powder. Cover and refrigerate for 15 minutes, until the seasoning clings evenly to the meat. Set aside the 120 ml of hot water for braising and the fish sauce.
3. Make the caramel. In a heavy-bottomed pot about 18–20 cm wide, heat the oil and sugar over medium-low heat for 3–5 minutes, swirling the pot gently, until the sugar melts to an amber colour and smells of caramel. Take the pot off the heat and add the ribs, then return to medium heat and stir-fry for 3–4 minutes, until the meat is evenly coated in brown.
4. Braise covered until tender. Pour in the 120 ml of hot water and the fish sauce and cook over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, until it returns to a boil. Cover, turn to low heat and cook for 25 minutes, keeping a gentle trickle of small bubbles. The meat near the bone should start to loosen, and a fork goes in with a little resistance. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece; it is cooked at 71°C (160°F).
5. Uncover and reduce the sauce. Keep cooking over medium-low heat for about 25 minutes, turning the ribs every 5 minutes, until a fork slides easily into the meat and the sauce coats the meat in a thin layer. If the meat is still tough but the bottom of the pot is nearly dry, add 25 ml from the reserve water and cook for 5 minutes more; reduce the sauce once the meat is tender.
6. Plate. Skim off the excess fat on the surface with a spoon and turn the ribs through the remaining sauce. When the pot holds only a little glossy, thick sauce and the ribs coat easily as you turn them, turn off the heat and plate. Serve with white rice.

## Key points

- **Bitter and blackened**: the caramel went too far (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Take the pot off the heat and add the meat at amber; if the pot already smells sharply smoky, throw out the caramel and start again.
- **Sauce is thick but the meat is still tough**: evaporation outran the collagen softening (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Use a narrower pot with a suitable lid; braise with the measured hot water until tender, and only then uncover to reduce.
- **Ribs stick to the bottom and blacken in spots**: once the sauce thickens, the pot bottom heats up quickly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lower the heat at the end and keep turning the meat; stop once the sauce coats the meat, without waiting for the pot to dry out.
