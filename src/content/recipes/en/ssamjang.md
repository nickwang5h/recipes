*Ssamjang* is the small dab of thick sauce in Korean lettuce wraps of rice or meat, and it is also used for dipping raw vegetables. Doenjang gives a salty, savoury base, gochujang adds heat and sweetness, and green onion, garlic and toasted sesame oil make the flavour sharper. It is ready to use once mixed and goes well with [grilled pork belly](/recipes/samgyeopsal-gui/) and [grilled galbi](/recipes/la-galbi/).

Doenjang develops its flavour through [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/), and the free amino acids released as its proteins break down also bring [umami](/principles/umami/). Both pastes already contain salt, and the sweetness of honey and the aroma of sesame bring them together. The finished sauce should stay on a lettuce leaf, and you judge the saltiness by the whole bite of meat, rice and vegetables (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).

## Steps

1. Chop the green onion, onion and garlic into fine bits of about 2 mm. Rinse the onion, then pat it dry before chopping so it does not bring extra water into the sauce; crush the garlic before chopping, so it spreads more easily when mixed.
2. In a bowl, put 4 tbsp doenjang and 1 tbsp gochujang. Press out the small lumps of doenjang with the back of a spoon and stir until the two pastes are an even colour with no separate red streaks. The original soybean pieces in the doenjang can stay.
3. Add the honey and sesame oil and stir until the surface is slightly glossy and a scoop holds a small mound. Scrape the honey fully off the spoon; the sesame oil only needs to be dispersed, not beaten into a completely smooth paste.
4. Add the green onion, onion, garlic and toasted sesame seeds, and mix until the bits are evenly spread. Scrape the bottom of the bowl and turn up the paste that has sunk, and check for any honey or lumps of paste that are not mixed in.
5. Divide the sauce into small dishes and use it at that meal. Spread about 0.5 tsp on each lettuce wrap, wrap in plain cooked meat or rice and taste; dip for more flavour, and do not pile a whole spoonful on a single leaf.

## Key points

- **So salty it covers the meat flavour**: both fermented pastes carry salt, and tasting them straight makes it easy to misjudge the amount. Spread a little in the wrap, taste it with meat or rice, and add no more salt. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **The sauce is thin and runs out of the leaf edges**: the onion and wet leaves brought in water, or the sauce was thinned out like a dipping sauce. Pat things dry before chopping, press the base smooth with the back of a spoon, and keep it thick enough to hold a mound. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Only sweet and hot, with a weak soybean flavour**: too much gochujang is covering the salty, savoury base of the doenjang. Mix doenjang to gochujang at 4:1 by volume, then add aroma with green onion, garlic and sesame. See [umami](/principles/umami/).
