Large pork and shepherd's purse wontons are a common vegetable-and-meat wonton in Shanghai and the Jiangnan region. White square wrappers enclose a filling of shepherd's purse and pork, plump at the bottom with two corners overlapped. Once cooked, the wrapper is silky, the filling is tender, and the clear broth carries the greens' fragrance. The shepherd's purse is squeezed dry first, and the pork is stirred with salt and soy sauce until sticky. [Salt](/principles/salt/) draws some of the protein out of the meat, which binds the chopped greens, fat and meat together.

The filling cooks in water through [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). The wrapper is thin, but the meat at the centre heats slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep the water at a gentle simmer so the wrapper and filling finish together. Wontons of an even size also make it easier to judge doneness for the whole batch.

## Steps

1. Thaw frozen shepherd's purse in the fridge for about 12 hours ahead of time, until there is no ice in the centre and it comes apart. For fresh shepherd's purse, or frozen that has not been blanched, bring 1500 ml of water to a boil, which takes about 5 minutes, then blanch it for 1–1.5 minutes, until the leaves wilt. Rinse with cold water, squeeze until no water drips from your fist, and chop finely. Frozen shepherd's purse that is already blanched only needs squeezing and chopping.
2. Stir the pork with the salt, light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and white pepper in one direction for 3–5 minutes, until the filling is sticky and clings to the chopsticks. Stir in the shepherd's purse and 2.5 tbsp of the toasted sesame oil until it holds together in one mass. Cover the wrappers with a damp cloth so the edges do not dry and crack.
3. Put about 2 tsp of filling in the centre of each wrapper. Dab the edge with sealing water and fold in half into a rectangle. Press out the air around the filling and press the three edges tight. Bend the two corners of the filled side toward each other, overlap them with a dab of water and pinch firmly. Make about 48 wontons and set them apart on a tray lined with parchment paper. If you wrap in batches, keep the unused filling in the fridge.
4. Heat the chicken stock over medium heat for about 6 minutes, until small bubbles rise steadily around the edge. Add the 2 tsp toasted sesame oil, the salt and the pepper for the broth, and keep it hot. In another pot, heat 3000 ml of water for about 10 minutes until boiling.
5. Cook the wontons in two batches, nudging the bottom of the pot so they do not stick. Each batch takes about 5–7 minutes in total, including 1–2 minutes at a gentle simmer after they float, until the wrapper is soft but still whole. If the water is boiling hard, add 120 ml of cold water each time to calm it; you will use 240 ml in total. Lift out the largest wonton and measure the centre of the filling: at 71°C (160°F) they are done; if not, cook 1 minute more.
6. Lift the wontons out with a slotted spoon, drain off the cooking water and divide among four bowls. Ladle a quarter of the hot broth into each bowl, sprinkle with green onion and serve right away, because the wrappers turn too soft if left to sit.

## Key points

- **Filling is watery and the wrapped skins turn soggy**: the shepherd's purse was not squeezed dry, and the water thinned the sticky meat filling (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Squeeze out the water and chop, and add the greens only after the meat has been stirred sticky.
- **Filling is hard and crumbles into grains of meat**: the meat is too lean, or it was not stirred until sticky, so it tightens separately when heated (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Use fatty ground pork and stir until it clings to the chopsticks before wrapping.
- **Wrappers burst while the filling is not cooked**: a hard rolling boil tore the wrappers, or floating was taken as a sign the meat was done (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Leave plenty of room in the pot, keep a gentle simmer, and go by the centre temperature of the largest wonton.
