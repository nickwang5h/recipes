*Pesto alla genovese* is a raw basil sauce from Liguria: green, fragrant and rich, where one spoonful is enough to coat pasta in basil flavour. The traditional seven ingredients are basil, garlic, pine nuts, Parmigiano, Sardinian sheep's milk cheese, olive oil and salt. Choose sweet basil, whose tender leaves have a fresh aroma. Do not use Thai basil, which smells different. The pine nuts are not toasted, which keeps their soft, raw flavour.

Grinding breaks the leaves, and the aroma stored inside is released and dissolves into the oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The finished sauce is loosened with hot pasta, and a little pasta water helps the fat spread (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). If the sauce is heated in a pan the whole time, the basil's fresh aroma is lost, so the pasta is tossed with the pesto in a bowl. Hazan's version adds soft butter; here the traditional Genoese seven ingredients are used.

## Steps

1. Wash the basil, spread it on a clean cloth and pat it gently dry, discarding hard stems and bad leaves. Grate the two cheeses separately into fine crumbs. The pine nuts should smell lightly nutty, with no stale oil smell.
2. Put the garlic and a little coarse salt in a mortar and crush with the pestle to a fine paste. Add the pine nuts and keep pounding until no large pieces remain.
3. Add the basil in small handfuls, pressing and turning the pestle along the wall of the mortar, adding the next handful only when the previous one has turned to paste. Scrape any leaves stuck to the wall back in. Pound until the leaves are a fine, moist green paste; it need not be a perfectly uniform purée.
4. Stir in the two cheeses, then add the olive oil slowly in stages, mixing with a spoon as you add. Stir until the sauce is smooth and slides slowly off the spoon, then taste and add salt.
5. When ready to use, put the amount of pesto you need in a large bowl and loosen it with a tablespoon of warm pasta water, then add freshly cooked pasta and toss. If the sauce is still clumped, add a little more water and toss until every strand is evenly coated. Pesto can also be stirred into a vegetable soup after it comes off the heat.

## Key points

- **The aroma is dull and the colour dark**: the ground leaves sat in the air for a long time, or the sauce was heated while mixing (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Have all the ingredients ready before you begin grinding, use the pesto soon after making it, and toss the pasta in a large bowl off the heat.
- **The sauce sets into hard lumps and will not coat the pasta**: it has a lot of cheese and fine solids, and not enough water when used (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Loosen it with pasta water a spoonful at a time before tossing the pasta. Do not add only oil, or oil will pool on the plate.
- **Garlic or saltiness overwhelms the basil**: the garlic cloves were too big, or salt was added as usual after switching to the saltier Romano (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Choose small cloves, and taste and season with salt after mixing in the cheese.
