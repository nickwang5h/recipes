*Ratatouille* is a Provençal summer vegetable stew. Eggplant, zucchini, bell pepper and tomato are cooked in olive oil until soft, with each vegetable still keeping its shape and the reduced juices clinging to the surface. It goes with roast meat or can be eaten on its own, hot or cooled.

The dish depends on dealing with each vegetable's water separately (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The eggplant and zucchini are fried first and the tomatoes reduced first, then everything is combined. The [acid](/principles/acid/) in tomatoes slows the softening of vegetable tissue, so cook the other vegetables to nearly done first, and at the end you don't have to stew the whole pot to mush waiting for the eggplant to soften.

## Steps

1. Toss the eggplant and zucchini with salt and leave for about 30 minutes. Pour off the liquid that comes out, and pat each slice dry with paper towels; the surface should have no visible droplets.
2. Put the tomatoes briefly in boiling water, and lift them out as soon as the skin loosens. Peel, cut crosswise, squeeze gently to remove the seeds and thin juice, and cut the flesh into strips.
3. Using the olive oil set aside for frying, fry the eggplant first, then the zucchini. Fry only one layer at a time, about 1 minute per side, until the edges have a little colour, then remove them; they will keep cooking later.
4. Cook the onion and peppers in the remaining oil over low heat for about 10 minutes, until the onion is translucent and the pepper strips are soft, then stir in the garlic. Add the tomatoes and cover for about 5 minutes, until the flesh gives off juice; uncover and reduce until there is almost no free liquid on the bottom.
5. In a shallow heavy-bottomed pot, spread one third of the tomato mixture and parsley, then half of the eggplant and zucchini. Repeat with another layer, and finish with the remaining tomato and parsley.
6. Cover and simmer over low heat for about 10 minutes, then uncover and simmer for about 15 minutes more. Tilt the pot now and then to spoon the juices back over the vegetables. Stop when the eggplant cuts easily and only a little oily, thick juice is left on the bottom, then taste and adjust the salt and pepper.

## Key points

- **The pot looks like vegetable soup**: the vegetables were too wet or the pot too crowded, so the heat went into evaporating water first. Pat them dry before frying, spread them out in batches, and reduce the tomato juice first. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The eggplant is still firm while the tomatoes have fallen apart**: the acidic juices slow softening. Fry the eggplant on its own first, and after combining, check the thickest slice instead of stopping by the clock alone. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
- **The whole pot breaks down to mush**: long stewing and repeated stirring broke up tissue that had already softened. Layer them in a shallow pot, spoon the juices over, and disturb the vegetables as little as you can. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The finished dish is too salty**: it was salted ahead and then seasoned at the usual amount, and reducing the juices concentrates the salt further. Taste after the juices have reduced and add only a little more at the end. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
