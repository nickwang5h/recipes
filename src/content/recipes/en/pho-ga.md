*Phở gà* is a bowl of clear chicken broth, narrow flat rice noodles and cooked chicken. Roasted ginger and onion bring a sweet aroma, and coriander seed and clove lightly support the chicken flavour (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The chicken bones are simmered slowly, and the connective tissue gradually turns into [gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), which gives the broth a rounded mouthfeel.

The chicken and the broth are judged separately. The breast and legs come out as soon as they are cooked, while the carcass keeps simmering. This looks after both the tenderness of the meat and the richness of the broth. Only fine droplets of fat are kept in the broth, and the green onion, cilantro and lime are left for assembling the bowls so their aromas are bright.

## Steps

1. Roast the ginger and onion. Broil them in the oven for 15 minutes, turning midway, until the surface is charred in spots and the inside slightly soft. Cool for 5 minutes, until you can hold them steadily, peel off the burnt skin and crack the ginger. Toast the coriander seeds separately in a dry pan for 1 minute, until the aroma is strong.
2. Blanch the chicken wings. Put the wings in the water for blanching and heat over high heat for 10–15 minutes, until the pot boils, then let it boil for another 3 minutes, until grey-white scum gathers. Pour off the water, rinse off the scum clinging to the wings and wash the pot clean. The pieces of the whole chicken are not blanched.
3. Cook the chicken. Put all the chicken pieces and the carcass with the broth water and heat over high heat for 15–25 minutes, until the pot boils. Skim the scum and add the roasted ginger and onion, coriander seeds, cloves, cilantro bundle and the broth seasonings. Simmer gently uncovered for 25 minutes, until the meat turns white and springy. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part of the breast and legs, away from the bone: at 74°C (165°F) it is done. If not, cook 5 minutes more, then test again.
4. Simmer the bones for the broth. Lift out the breast and legs and cool for 15 minutes, until you can pull the meat off. Refrigerate the meat whole in a covered container, and return the bones you pull out to the broth pot. Simmer the broth gently for another 90 minutes, until the chicken flavour is full and the soft tissue around the carcass has loosened, with the surface only ever bubbling lightly.
5. Strain the broth. Strain out the solids and skim off any excessively thick fat, then measure out about 3800 ml of broth; if short, top up with hot water to that amount. Soak the onion for the bowls in cold water for 30 minutes, until the sharpness eases, then drain. Slice the chicken thinly.
6. Blanch the noodles. Soak the dried noodles in warm water for 15–20 minutes, until they bend but are still milky white, then blanch them in a separate pot of boiling water for 10–20 seconds, until soft with no hard core. If still hard, blanch another 15 seconds. Blanch the bean sprouts separately for 30 seconds, until heated through with the stems still crisp.
7. Assemble the bowls. Warm the chicken in the hot broth over low heat for 2–3 minutes, until heated through. Divide the noodles among eight bowls, arrange the chicken on top and sprinkle with onion, green onion, cilantro and pepper. Ladle about 475 ml of boiling hot chicken broth into each bowl and eat right away, with lime, chili and mint on the side.

## Key points

- **The chicken is dry and tough**: the meat kept simmering in the broth after it was cooked, and the proteins contracted and lost water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take out the breast and legs first and keep simmering the bones.
- **The broth is cloudy and heavy and greasy**: a hard boil rolled the scum and fat into the broth (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Keep it at a gentle simmer, and do the skimming and the straining as separate steps.
- **The broth tastes scorched and bitter**: the charred skin of the roasted ginger and onion went into the broth (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Keep only the roasted soft inside, and wash off the black bits before they go into the pot.
