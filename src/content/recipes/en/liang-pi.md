Liangpi is refreshing with a garlic note. The gluten soaks up the dressing, the cucumber and sprouts are crisp, and the sesame paste is rich. This version uses ready-made liangpi and a fairly thick sesame paste. The paste is stirred with water a little at a time, so the water, the fat and the fine sesame particles blend into a smooth sauce (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

Crushed garlic releases its sharpness more easily (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Both the dressing and the sesame paste already contain soy sauce and oyster sauce, so decide on salt after everything is mixed (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Prepare the ready-made liangpi and gluten as their packages say. The water for the garlic water, the paste and the mixing must be drinkable.

## Steps

1. Read the serving instructions on the liangpi and cooked gluten packages. Cut the liangpi into wide strips and the gluten into small pieces. Anything that needs heating is prepared as directed, then cooled. Ready-to-eat liangpi does not need repeated soaking to wash off oil.
2. Bring the water for the sprouts to a boil, add the washed bean sprouts and cook over high heat for about 60 seconds, until they are hot through and the stems are slightly soft. Lift out, cool with cooled boiled water and drain.
3. Wash the cucumber, cut it into fine shreds and put it on a clean plate. If the cucumber and sprouts carry a lot of water, mixing them in will dilute the dressing.
4. Mash the garlic to a fine paste and stir in the water for the garlic water. Separately mix the water for the dressing, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, oyster sauce, vinegar and chicken bouillon powder until no grains are left.
5. Stir the matching light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and oyster sauce into the sesame paste first, then add 10 ml of water at a time, stirring until even before the next. It will tighten at first, then keep stirring until it runs slowly off the edge of the spoon.
6. Toss the liangpi with the dressing first, then add the sprouts and gluten, drizzle on the sesame paste and lay the cucumber shreds on top. Fold gently so the sauce clings to the liangpi and the gluten takes up the dressing too.
7. Taste a bite with liangpi and the other ingredients, and add salt only if it is clearly bland. Add chili oil if you like heat, mix and eat.

## Key points

- **The sesame paste clumps and will not loosen**: the water went in too fast and the paste was not yet mixed through (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Stir one portion of water in fully before adding a little of the next.
- **Every layer is salty and the whole bowl is too salty**: the garlic water, the dressing and the sesame paste all carry [salt](/principles/salt/). Season with soy sauce and oyster sauce first, judge after everything is mixed, and do not season each sauce on its own to a strong taste.
- **Uneven garlic flavour, with the odd sharp chunk**: the garlic was not mashed, so the sharpness of the [aromatics](/principles/aromatics/) sits in coarse pieces. Mash it to a paste before making the dressing and mix it well as you pour it in.
