The home-style clear version of *galbitang* uses bone-in beef short ribs and radish. The meat comes easily off the bone, the soup is pale, and it is usually served with white rice. Choose thick pieces cut along the bone; the membrane next to the bone softens with long cooking, which is what makes the bite tender (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).

A clear broth starts with cleaning the ribs. [Blanching](/principles/blanching/) takes away the surface exudate and debris, and when you simmer in clean water you still skim the foam and control the boil. The radish is cooked until it can be cut, lifted out, then returned to the pot, so you keep both chunks of radish and soft ribs. The soup soy sauce supplies the base seasoning, and the final saltiness is left to [salt](/principles/salt/).

## Steps

1. Remove bone chips from the saw cuts on the ribs, put them in 2000 ml of cold water, and soak them in the refrigerator for 30 minutes, until the water turns clearly red, then drain. In another pot, heat 2000 ml of water for about 10 minutes, until it boils steadily.
2. Put the ribs in the boiling water and, after it returns to a boil, cook for 10 minutes, until the surface turns grey and the foam rises in clumps. Pour off the blanching water, rinse the grey foam off each piece, and wash the bottom of the pot.
3. Return the ribs to the pot with 5760 ml of water, the large pieces of radish, the onions, green onion whites, whole garlic cloves, ginger and 1 tsp of salt. Heat over medium-high for about 10 minutes, until the surface boils. Skim the foam, then keep the heat at medium-high and cook uncovered until this step has run 60 minutes in all, keeping the surface bubbling steadily.
4. Lift out the radish, and discard the onions, green onion whites, ginger and whole garlic cloves. Leave the radish on a plate to cool for 5 minutes, until it is no longer hot to the touch, then cut it into bite-size pieces about 2 cm thick. Add 500 ml of hot water and the soup soy sauce to the pot.
5. Turn to medium-low and stew covered for 1 hour, until a chopstick goes through the thickest piece of meat, the membrane next to the bone is soft and the meat comes apart easily with a gentle twist. If it is still tough, cook for another 15 minutes; keep the surface at a gentle bubble.
6. If you are adding noodles, soak them in 500 ml of warm water for 20 minutes during the last 20 minutes of the ribs' stewing, until they bend, then drain. Return the radish, minced garlic and noodles to the pot and cook over medium heat for 4 minutes, until the noodles are transparent with no hard core when bitten and the radish is hot at the centre.
7. Skim off the excess fat on the surface of the soup with a spoon, taste, and add salt and black pepper. Divide the ribs, radish and soup among bowls and scatter with green onion; the meat should still be in pieces, and the bones should pull out gently.

## Key points

- **Grey specks and bone chips in the soup**: the ribs and pot were not washed clean after blanching. Rinse the saw cuts and the gaps between the bones, then simmer in fresh water; particles that have already got into the soup can be strained out with a fine sieve (see [Blanching and Removing Off-Odours](/principles/blanching/)).
- **Meat has changed colour but is still tough by the bone**: the meat is cooked but the membrane is not yet soft. Keep stewing over low heat, and check whether the membrane at the thickest part is soft and the meat leaves the bone (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).
- **Soup gets saltier the longer it cooks**: evaporation concentrates the salt. Season lightly early on, and decide the saltiness by tasting after the noodles and radish go back in (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
