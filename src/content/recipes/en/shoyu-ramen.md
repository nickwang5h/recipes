Shoyu ramen has a clear, savoury broth with a distinct soy sauce flavour and a slightly thick, smooth body. The unsalted broth and the soy sauce tare are only combined in the bowl. Both provide [umami](/principles/umami/), and the [gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) from the long simmer adds the thick, smooth body. The broth is a Tokyo-style clear pork-bone broth, and the pork belly is cooked in the broth first, then simmered in the soy sauce liquid to become chashu.

A batch for eight bowls needs a stockpot of at least 12 L. The broth simmers for 6 hours, and each bowl takes about 360 ml.

## Steps

1. Put the pork bones, onion, garlic, ginger and 9500 ml of water in the pot with no salt, and heat uncovered for 20–30 minutes until boiling. Tie the pork belly tightly with food-safe cotton string every 1 cm. Skim off the grey-brown foam, then add the pork.
2. Simmer uncovered for 2 hours, keeping the broth boiling without boiling over, and skim the foam as it appears. Once there is room in the pot, add 950 ml of water twice; each time, wait 3–5 minutes for it to return to a boil. 10 minutes before the 2 hours are up, ladle out 480 ml of broth and set it aside.
3. In a separate pot, combine the ladled-out broth, soy sauce, sake, mirin and salt. Cook over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until all the salt has dissolved and bubbles form at the edge of the pot, then take it off the heat.
4. After the broth has simmered for the full 2 hours, add the chicken fat and the leek greens, and boil uncovered for another 1 hour, until the pork belly is tender, a bamboo skewer passes through it, and the centre reaches at least 71°C (160°F). Lift the pork out, supporting it, and move it into the pot of soy sauce liquid.
5. Cover the soy sauce pot with a drop lid (a lid that rests directly on the pork) and simmer gently over low heat for 2 hours, until the pork is easily pierced and the surface is deep brown. Every 30 minutes, spoon the liquid over the pork. At the same time, turn the bone broth down to low and keep simmering uncovered for 3 hours, until the flavour is concentrated and you can still clearly see liquid.
6. Lift the pork out onto a shallow tray and cool it by setting the tray in a larger bowl of ice water for about 20 minutes, until it no longer steams. Put it in the freezer for 30–60 minutes, until the fat is firm but the pork can still be cut. Snip off the string and cut the pork into slices about 3 mm thick. Strain the cooking liquid, skim off the fat, and use it as the tare.
7. After the bone broth has simmered for a total of 6 hours, pass it through a fine strainer and measure out 2880 ml. Keep any extra broth separately; if there is less, top it up with hot water to that amount. Before filling the bowls in batches, heat the broth for about 3–5 minutes, until it steams steadily and bubbles form at the edge of the pot.
8. Cook the noodles two bowls at a time. Add the noodles once the water is at a rolling boil and cook for about 1–1.5 minutes, until there is no starchy core and they are still springy; follow the package for the exact time. Put 1 tbsp of tare and 1 tbsp of chopped leek whites in each bowl first, then pour in 360 ml of hot broth.
9. Lift the noodles out, shake off the water, and put them in the broth at once. Warm the sliced chashu in the pot of hot broth for about 1 minute, until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F), then arrange it on the noodles, with the menma, green onion and nori. Taste, add at most 1 more tbsp of tare per bowl, and eat immediately.

## Key points

- **The broth is full of grey scum and bits of meat**: the foam was not removed during the boil. It is mostly coagulated protein and can't be removed by stirring it apart (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **The broth is too salty**: the whole pot of soy sauce liquid was used as the broth base. Measure 1 tbsp per bowl first, then add the unsalted broth (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Noodles are soft and the broth is weak**: the noodles sat and soaked up broth, or too much cooking water was carried over. Cook in small batches, shake the noodles dry, and fill the bowls at once.
