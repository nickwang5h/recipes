*Hōrensō no goma-ae* is a Japanese home-style side dish. White sesame is toasted and ground, then mixed with soy sauce, sugar and a little [dashi](/recipes/dashi/) and tossed with cooked spinach. In the mouth comes the toasted aroma of sesame first, then the savoury soy sauce and a slight sweetness; the sugar only needs to support the aroma, not make the dish sweet.

The dish first softens the leaves by [blanching](/principles/blanching/), then lets the dressing cling by squeezing out the water. Once sesame is ground open, its oil and aroma spread into the dressing more easily (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). The finished dish should have fine bits of sesame clinging to the leaves, no pool of water at the bottom of the bowl, and spinach that can still be picked up in bunches.

## Steps

1. Wash the spinach, especially the soil and sand where the stems meet the roots. Keep the tender stems of the whole plants and cut off the tough roots.
2. Put the white sesame in a dry, heavy-bottomed skillet and toast over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, for 2–3 minutes. When it is pale golden and smells nutty, tip it into a mortar; do not leave it in the hot pan.
3. Grind the sesame while it is still warm, until most of the seeds are broken open and begin to look oily. Add the sugar and grind to mix, then add the soy sauce and dashi and stir hard into an even, thick dressing. Leave a few fine granules; it does not need to be ground into a fully smooth sesame paste.
4. Boil a large pot of water and add a little salt. Add the spinach in batches; for thick stems, dip the stem ends in first, then push the leaves under and blanch for about 45 seconds. Lift out as soon as the leaves have just wilted and the tender stems bend, and cool quickly under cold water; do not cook until the leaves fall apart.
5. Drain the spinach, divide it into small bunches and squeeze out the water gently by hand until it no longer drips continuously. Cut whole plants into sections about 4 cm long, then loosen them gently; baby spinach can be left as whole leaves. Do not wring it into a tight ball.
6. Put the spinach in the dressing and toss gently so the sesame clings evenly to the leaves and stems. Divide into four small servings and eat as soon as it is mixed. If preparing ahead, refrigerate the spinach and the dressing separately and mix just before eating.

## Key points

- **Water at the bottom of the bowl, sesame all sunk**: the spinach was not drained and squeezed, and the leftover water thinned the dressing. Next time, cool it thoroughly after blanching and then squeeze it in bunches (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)).
- **Sesame specks but no fragrance**: the sesame was under-toasted, or the granules were not ground open. Toast fresh to pale golden and grind while warm to release more aroma (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Whole seeds sprinkled on top give only scattered flavour.
- **Leaves grey and limp, clumped together when lifted**: blanched too long, cooled too slowly, or squeezed and tossed too hard. Blanch in batches and lift out as soon as the leaves soften; when tossing, fold rather than press (see [blanching](/principles/blanching/)).
