Black sesame tangyuan wraps a sweet filling of sesame, sugar and lard in a white glutinous rice wrapper. The cooked wrapper is soft and slippery, and when you bite in, the filling flows out slowly. Roast the sesame first and then grind it fine so the fragrance is even; the lard helps the filling hold its shape when cold and melts when hot, drawing the sugar and sesame into a soft, smooth paste.

The soft, sticky wrapper comes from [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Glutinous rice flour has no wheat gluten, so it stretches only a little when wrapping; scald part of the flour with a little boiling water, then add room-temperature water, and the dough is softer. This makes 20 tangyuan, 5 per person; they must be the same size to cook through at the same time in one pot.

## Steps

1. Roast the sesame. Put raw black sesame seeds in a dry pan and stir over low heat for 4–6 minutes, until the seeds are slightly plump and smell nutty and one can be crushed easily between your fingers. Spread out to cool for 10 minutes, until it no longer feels warm. If you bought roasted sesame, go straight to the next step.
2. Grind the sesame filling. Put the sesame and sugar in a small food processor and blend in 15-second bursts, stopping to scrape down the sides, 4–6 times, until the powder is fine and slightly shiny. Mix in the soft lard until there are no white lumps of fat, then press it smooth against the side of the bowl.
3. Chill the filling. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes, until the filling can be pinched into a ball by hand without spreading into a puddle. Divide into 20 portions of about 8 g each, roll round and return to the refrigerator. Take out only a few at a time for wrapping and keep the rest cold and firm.
4. Make the glutinous rice dough. Pour 45 ml of boiling water into the glutinous rice flour and mix with chopsticks into wet flakes, then gradually add 60 ml of room-temperature water. Let it sit for 2 minutes, until cool enough to handle, then knead for 3 minutes, until the surface is smooth and the edges do not crack when you flatten it. If it is stiff, use the extra water, adding 5 ml at a time, kneading evenly before checking again.
5. Wrap the tangyuan. Divide the dough into 20 equal portions and keep the unused ones covered. Take one portion and press it into a small bowl with the base and sides about the same thickness, then put in one ball of filling. Support the base with one hand and push the wrapper upward with the other, pinch the seam tightly shut, then roll gently round. If there are cracks on the surface, smooth them over with a wet fingertip.
6. Cook the tangyuan. Heat 2000 ml of water over high heat for about 8 minutes, until at a full boil, and add the tangyuan in two batches. Push once gently along the bottom of the pot with the back of a spoon, turn the heat to medium and cook for 3–4 minutes, until they have all floated up and the wrappers look slightly translucent. Cook 1 minute more, until the wrappers are soft and the shapes are intact, then ladle into bowls with a little of the cooking water.

## Key points

- **Edges crack while wrapping**: the dough is short of water, or was left uncovered and the surface lost moisture. Add 5 ml of water at a time, knead evenly and keep it covered (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Filling is coarse and gritty**: the sesame was only broken up, not ground fine. Roast until fragrant and cool it, then grind in several rounds until fine and shiny (see [extracting aroma](/principles/aromatics/)); sugar cannot cover the bitterness of burnt sesame.
- **Split open while cooking, black filling leaks into the water**: the seam was not pinched shut, the wrapper was uneven in thickness, or the tangyuan cooked a long time after floating. Check for cracks after wrapping, keep the water at a gentle bubble, and lift them out 1 minute after they float (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
