*Haemul-pajeon* is a Korean savoury pancake with green onion and seafood fried into a batter. The first bite is a crisp edge, then soft green onion and seafood. The batter only holds things together; you should clearly see the green onion and shrimp, or the pancake turns into a heavy lump of dough. Cut into sections, the green onion is also easy to spread thin in a home frying pan.

The starch in the flour absorbs water and gelatinizes with heat, fixing the loose ingredients in place (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Then heat from the bottom of the pan and loss of water at the surface form the crisp crust (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Mix the batter only until the dry flour is gone; stirring on strengthens the [gluten](/principles/gluten/) and makes the pancake tougher.

## Steps

1. Stir the soy sauce and white vinegar together and set aside as the dip. Drain the thawed squid and shrimp, blot dry with paper towels, then cut into small pieces of similar size.
2. Beat the egg and mix it with the water and salt. Add the flour and stir gently until no dry flour remains. The batter should run continuously off a spoon, and the trail left after lifting should close up quickly.
3. Mix the green onion sections, squid and shrimp into the batter, so each section of green onion is thinly coated. Mix the seafood in last and fry right away; don't let it sit in the batter.
4. Put 1 tbsp of the oil in a wide frying pan and heat over medium-high heat until the oil flows smoothly. Pour in half the batter, spread the green onion and seafood evenly with a spatula, and move thick spots apart to form a thin pancake.
5. Turn to medium heat and fry about 3–5 minutes, until the bottom is golden, the edge is set and a spatula can lift the whole pancake, then flip. Add 1 tbsp oil around the edge of the pan and lift the pancake edge slightly so the oil runs underneath.
6. Fry the other side until golden, pressing lightly on thick spots to help them touch the pan. Take it out when the shrimp is opaque, the squid is cooked through, no wet batter remains in the middle and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
7. Fry the second pancake the same way with the remaining 2 tbsp oil. Lay the finished pancakes out flat, and cut into pieces and dip when eating, to keep the crisp edge; don't stack two hot pancakes.

## Key points

- **Burnt outside, gummy inside**: the pancake is too thick, or the heat is too high. Make two pancakes and spread out the spots where seafood is dense; once the bottom has coloured, keep frying over medium heat until cooked through. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Tough and rubbery**: the batter was mixed too long and too much gluten developed. Stop as soon as the dry flour is gone, and don't aim for the smoothness that long stirring gives. See [gluten](/principles/gluten/).
- **Goes soft soon after leaving the pan**: the seafood brought in too much water, or stacked hot pancakes trapped steam. Blot the seafood dry first, lay the pancakes out and eat right away, and add the dip only just before eating. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
