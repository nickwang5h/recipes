Roujiamo is a common street food in Shaanxi. This version is *la zhi rou* (spiced braised pork) in *baiji mo* (white flatbread): the meat is simmered tender in a spiced liquid, the bread is a yeasted dough griddled dry, and the bread is split open and filled while hot. The pork belly is cooked with its skin on, so the skin and connective tissue soften gradually and the hot juices turn silky (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Chop the meat but keep small pieces, so you still taste the mix of fat and lean.

The flatbread has to hold the juices, so the dough is fairly stiff. Knead it evenly, let it ferment, then roll it into rounds. Its chew comes from [gluten](/principles/gluten/). The outside is griddled until it has light golden spots and the inside is cooked through and springy, so it can be split into one whole pocket. The traditional version is partly oven-baked; at home, use a heavy-bottomed pan with a lid and cook it dry.

## Steps

1. Cut the pork belly into 2 pieces and put them in the blanching water. Heat over medium-high for 8–10 minutes, until the water boils, foam gathers and the outside of the meat turns white. Lift out the meat, rinse off the foam, discard the water and wash the pot.
2. Return the meat to the pot with 240 ml of braising water and the rest of the ingredients in the braised pork group. Cook over medium heat for 5 minutes, until the liquid boils. Cover, turn to low and simmer for 90–120 minutes, until a chopstick slides easily through the skin and the lean meat but the meat still lifts out in one piece. Turn the meat once along the way and keep the liquid at a gentle simmer.
3. Make the dough while the meat simmers. Mix the flour, instant yeast, sugar for the dough, water and oil, and knead for 8–10 minutes, until the dough is fairly stiff and smooth, with no dry flour. Cover and ferment for 40–90 minutes, until about double in size and full of fine holes when you pull it apart.
4. Knead the risen dough for 3–5 minutes to push out the large bubbles. Divide into 4 pieces. Roll each into a long oval, roll it up from the short end into a tube, stand it on end and press it flat, then roll it into a round about 10 cm across and 1 cm thick. Keep the other pieces covered so they do not form a skin. Cover the shaped rounds and rest them for 20 minutes.
5. Preheat a heavy flat-bottomed pan over low heat for 2 minutes, until a round makes only a faint sizzle when it goes in. Add the rounds with no oil. Cook covered for 2 minutes per side, then uncovered for about 1 minute per side, until both sides have golden spots and the edges are set. Cut one open to test: the centre should have no wet, sticky dough. If it is still raw, cook 1–2 minutes more per side, covered.
6. Lift out the hot meat and chop it into small pieces. Mix in 2 tbsp of the braising liquid from the pot, until the filling is moist and no liquid pools at the bottom of the bowl. Split each hot flatbread open from the side, leaving one edge attached, divide the filling among them and eat while hot.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but the skin is still tough**: the collagen has not softened enough. Keep the heat low, and simmer 15 minutes more at a time, testing with a chopstick each time (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).
- **Bread is scorched outside and sticky in the centre**: the pan is too hot, so the surface colours before the inside cooks. Lower the heat and cook covered and slowly, following [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Judge doneness by cutting one open, not by the brown spots.
- **Bread falls apart when you split it**: the dough was too soft, over-proofed or rolled too thin. Keep the dough fairly stiff and shape it once it has doubled (see [Gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Add only enough braising liquid to moisten the filling.
