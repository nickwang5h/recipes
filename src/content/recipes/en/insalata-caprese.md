*Insalata caprese* is a salad of tomato and fresh cheese from the island of Capri. Slices of tomato and mozzarella are laid out alternately, basil and olive oil add fragrance, and what you taste is the sweet-sour tomato and the soft, milky cheese. Both cow's-milk fior di latte and water buffalo mozzarella work, and the buffalo one is richer.

What decides the result is water, and when you add salt. Cut tomatoes run with juice and fresh cheese comes with packing liquid; drain the water off first, so the olive oil can stay on the surface of the food. Salt makes the flavours clearer, but if it sits too long the tomatoes keep giving off water (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Basil's aroma evaporates quickly, so tear it just before eating (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Pick ripe tomatoes that smell like tomato and give slightly when pressed gently. Wash and dry them, remove the stem end, and cut crosswise into slices about 1 cm thick. Pour off any juice that runs onto the board and keep only the whole flesh.
2. Take the mozzarella out of its packing liquid, drain it in a sieve, and cut it into slices about as thick as the tomato. If water beads on the cut surfaces, blot gently with paper towels until there is no flowing water on the surface.
3. Arrange the tomato and cheese slices alternately on a flat platter, overlapping slightly. Spread the slices out on a wide plate rather than piling them up, because the tomatoes at the bottom will be pressed and give off juice.
4. Tuck the dried basil leaves between the slices, tearing large leaves by hand; use them as soon as they are torn, when their fragrance is at its brightest.
5. Just before serving, sprinkle a little salt over the tomatoes, then drizzle the olive oil evenly over the tomatoes and cheese. Taste a bite with both tomato and cheese; the cheese itself is salty, so add more salt only if needed, then serve.

## Key points

- **A pool of water at the bottom of the plate**: the packing liquid was not drained well, or the salad waited too long after salting, so the oil and salt were washed to the bottom by the juices (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Drain the cheese, blot the tomatoes dry and save the salt for last; if you prep ahead, keep things separate and assemble just before eating.
- **Cheese firm and lacking milky flavour**: you bought low-moisture pizza mozzarella (for cheese structure see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). This salad needs fresh cheese packed in liquid; on the shelf look for fresh mozzarella, fior di latte or plain bocconcini, which should be soft and juicy when cut.
- **Basil blackened and faint in fragrance**: the leaves were pressed and cut repeatedly, or left too long after tearing (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Blot them dry after washing, tear them only when plating, and serve once the oil is on.
