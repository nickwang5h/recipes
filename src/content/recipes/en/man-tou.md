Mantou are soft steamed breads, and the home northern method uses yeast to loosen the dough. The [gluten](/principles/gluten/) network built by kneading traps the gas the yeast produces. In the steamer the bubbles expand and the starch absorbs water and gelatinizes (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)), and the dough sets into a soft bun.

For a fine crumb, push the gas out fully between the two proofs. Let the dough rise the first time, knead out the large bubbles, then shape it and let small bubbles grow. The dough should be fairly firm with a smooth surface. Ordinary Canadian flour absorbs water differently, so start with 150 ml of water and add the extra 20 ml only if the dough is still dry and cracking after kneading.

## Steps

1. Stir the yeast into 150 ml of warm water and leave for about 5 minutes, until the yeast granules disperse and fine bubbles appear on the surface. The water should feel warm, not hot to the hand.
2. Stir the yeast water into 300 g flour a little at a time until there is no loose flour, then add the oil. Knead for about 10 minutes, until the dough is smooth, elastic and does not stick to the bowl. If it is still dry and cracking, add the reserved water 5 ml at a time, kneading it in before checking again.
3. Cover the dough and proof in a warm place for about 60–90 minutes, until it is about double in volume with an even honeycomb inside. A dent pressed with a finger springs back slowly, and the dough still holds up and has not collapsed flat.
4. Dust the board thinly with flour and knead the dough for about 5–8 minutes to press out the large bubbles. Cut it open to check that the cut face is fine, with no obvious holes. After kneading, roll it into an even log and cut into 8 pieces.
5. Set each piece on a square of parchment paper in the steamer basket, leaving about 3 cm between them. Cover and proof for about 15–20 minutes, until the buns have clearly puffed, feel lighter than when just cut, and slowly spring back when the side is pressed lightly.
6. Put cold water in the steamer, set the basket on top and heat over high heat for about 5–8 minutes, until steam is steadily coming out. Turn to medium heat and steam 15 minutes more, until the buns stand firm and the skin is dry. The steam should not stop at any point.
7. Turn off the heat and wait 5 minutes before lifting the lid, so condensation from the lid does not drip onto the buns. Break one open: the centre should be soft with no wet, raw layer. If there is a raw layer, steam them again for 5 minutes and check.

## Key points

- **Coarse crumb with large holes**: the large bubbles were not fully pushed out after the first proof (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Knead until the cut face is fine before shaping; do not just flatten once and cut.
- **Small and hard to bite**: dead yeast, an underproofed dough, or a dough that is too dry (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). First check that the yeast makes fine bubbles, then judge by volume and spring-back whether the proof is done.
- **Collapsed and wrinkled after steaming**: overproofed, or the steam was interrupted so the structure did not set (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Proof until light but still holding up, do not lift the lid often while steaming, and wait 5 minutes after turning off the heat before taking them out.
