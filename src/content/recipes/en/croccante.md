*Croccante* is Italian almond brittle, made all over the centre and south of Italy around Christmas: toasted almonds held in a layer of amber sugar, spread thin and left to cool until it snaps.

It works by [cooking sugar](/principles/sugar-syrup/). Sugar and honey cooked to about 170°C (340°F) have lost almost all their water, so once cool they set into a hard, crisp sheet with no crystals. At this temperature the sugar also starts to colour and turn toasty (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)); a few degrees more and it turns bitter, so watch both the thermometer and the colour. A few drops of lemon juice keep the sugar from turning grainy, and the honey stands in for glucose syrup and also helps stop crystals. Lemon juice splits some of the sucrose into glucose and fructose, which is stickier than sucrose in a hard candy, so wrap the brittle as soon as it is completely cool.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F), conventional setting with no fan. Line another baking sheet with parchment and keep it by the stove.
2. Spread the almonds on a baking sheet and toast them in the oven for a few minutes, until lightly coloured and fragrant; don't let them burn.
3. Put the honey, sugar and a few drops of lemon juice in a stainless steel saucepan, not an enamelled one, and stir to mix.
4. Melt the sugar over medium heat and clip a kitchen thermometer to the pan.
5. When the syrup reaches 140°C (285°F), tip in the still-warm almonds and stir to coat.
6. Keep cooking and stirring, and take the pan off the heat at 170°C (340°F). If the colour turns light amber before it gets there, take it off the heat right away.
7. Pour it onto the parchment-lined sheet and spread and flatten it with a wooden spoon. You can also use a lemon as a rolling pin to press it thinner. The sugar is around 170°C (340°F): keep your hands off its surface, and don't taste it until it is completely cool.
8. Leave it at room temperature until completely cool.
9. Turn it out onto a cutting board, peel off the parchment and cut it into bars.
10. Wrap what you don't eat in waxed paper and keep it in a tin or a lidded jar somewhere cool and dry.

## Key points

- **The brittle is bitter**: the sugar was cooked too far and burned (see [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Take it off the heat as soon as it turns light amber; you don't have to wait for the thermometer.
- **The sugar is grainy and cloudy**: there was no lemon juice, and the sucrose crystallized again (see [sugar syrup](/principles/sugar-syrup/)). Add a few drops of lemon juice before cooking.
- **The brittle isn't crisp enough**: the almonds were damp, and their water spoiled the texture of the syrup. Toast the almonds first and add them while warm.
- **It goes soft and sticky after a few days**: it was kept somewhere warm or damp, and the caramelized sugar softens. Wrap it once completely cool and store it somewhere cool and dry.
