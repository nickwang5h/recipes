Yangshuo beer fish has charred, fragrant skin, intact flesh and a thick, rich sauce that is good over rice. The fish is a river fish, fried until fragrant first, then braised in beer, with tomato bringing sourness and juice. This version uses carp with scales on, on a base of Guilin chili sauce. The water on the fish surface is fried off first, which gives a golden skin and the roasted aroma of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/); after the beer goes in, the heat comes down to cook the flesh gently.

The scales are crisp after frying and soften again after a while in the braising liquid. The beer, tomato and sauces already have plenty of flavour, so when reducing at the end leave a little liquid that still flows, which is just right poured over rice.

## Steps

1. Clean the carp of gills, guts and the blood line inside the belly, then cut it open from the belly side and lay it flat, keeping the back joined. Mix it with the Shaoxing wine, half of the ginger slices and 3 g of salt and marinate in the refrigerator for 15 minutes. Drain off the marinade before it goes in the pan and blot the fish dry with paper towels.
2. Cut the tomato and the green and red bell peppers into chunks, cut the green onion into sections, and keep the white and green parts apart. As soon as the fish is fried, go straight on to the sauce, or the empty pan will burn the oil left in it.
3. Heat the wok, pour in 30 ml of oil and turn to medium heat. Lay the fish in skin side down, and turn it over when the edges are golden and a spatula slides under it easily. Fry the other side until golden too, then lift the fish out, supporting its body.
4. Using the oil left in the wok, stir-fry the white part of the green onion, the remaining ginger and the garlic until fragrant, then add the chili sauce and stir-fry until the oil turns red. Add the tomato and stir-fry until the pieces are still whole and there is red juice at the bottom of the pan.
5. Pour in 330 ml of beer, add the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and sugar, and gently scrape up the brown fond at the bottom. Return the fish, bring to a boil, then turn to medium-low, cover and braise for 15–20 minutes, keeping a light bubbling at the edge of the pan.
6. Add the green and red bell peppers and cook for another 3–5 minutes. Look at the thickest part of the fish: when the flesh turns opaque and separates along the grain, it is done. If there is still raw flesh, keep braising gently, and do not stir or turn the fish.
7. Uncover and reduce the sauce until it coats the fish skin in a thin layer and still flows when you tilt the pan. Taste, then add the rest of the salt, sprinkle with the green part of the green onion, and serve the fish with its sauce.

## Key points

- **Skin sticks to the pan and breaks when flipped**: there is too much water on the skin, or it was not fried until it set (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Blot it dry after marinating and flip only when the skin loosens.
- **Flesh breaks up and is dry**: the braise was too vigorous or the reducing dragged on too long (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). If the fish is done and there is still a lot of liquid, take the fish out first and reduce the sauce on its own.
- **Sauce gets saltier as it reduces**: the salt from the sauces and from marinating the fish all went into the liquid, and it concentrates as the water evaporates (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Have 5 g of salt in total for the whole dish; after marinating the fish, add only what is left.
