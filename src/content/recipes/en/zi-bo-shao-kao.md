Zibo barbecue is eaten by rolling grilled meat in a small pancake with green onion and sauce: the meat is charred and savoury, the pancake soft, the green onion white crisp. With pork belly, ready-made cooked pancakes and sesame sweet bean sauce, you can roll them at home. Pork belly gives off fat as it heats and its surface slowly dries, and that is what lets the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) build the charred flavour.

The heat of the charcoal reaches the surface first and then slowly moves to the centre of the meat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). So a thick piece can be browned outside and not cooked inside. Cut the pieces to the same size so they all finish together (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cook the meat through first, then grill it to the char you like.

## Steps

1. Remove the skin from the pork belly, trim off tough sinew, and cut into small pieces no thicker than 2 cm. Keep the pieces on each skewer a similar thickness, with a little space between them. If it is hard to cut, freeze it for about 30 minutes first, until slightly firm and still cuttable.
2. Wash and drain the green onion whites and cut them into sections of about 5 cm. Add 10 ml of warm water to the sesame paste in portions and stir until it spreads smoothly, then mix in the sweet bean sauce. Warm the cooked pancakes as the package directs and keep them covered so they do not dry out.
3. Light the charcoal outdoors, somewhere with ventilation. Grill only when the charcoal is glowing red with no open flame; check after about 15 minutes. Spread the charcoal into a hotter zone and a gentler zone, leaving a place to move the skewers aside.
4. Lay the skewers on the grate and flip when the underside is golden and fat is seeping out. If dripping fat causes flames, move the skewers to the gentler zone, and grill again once the flames die down. Do not let flames lick the meat directly.
5. After flipping, grill for about 2–3 minutes more, then turn the other sides by thickness. Once the surface is golden and the fat at the edges has shrunk, measure the centre of the thickest piece with a thermometer and take it off only at 71°C (160°F). If it has not reached that, move it to the gentler zone and keep grilling.
6. Put the cooked skewers on a clean plate, and use a pair of tongs that has not touched raw meat to pick up the cooked meat. Take a warm small pancake, spread about 3–4 g of sauce on it, add the meat and 1–2 sections of green onion white, and roll it up to eat. Use the remaining sauce for dipping.

## Key points

- **Charred black outside, raw in the centre**: the heat is concentrated in the outer layer and thick pieces warm slowly inside (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Move to the gentler zone to cook it through. The centre temperature is more reliable than the colour of the surface.
- **Bitter meat with a burnt, smoky taste**: flames from dripping fat have scorched the surface (see [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Move the skewers in time and stop at golden with a good aroma, and do not chase large black edges.
- **Some pieces on one skewer dry and some raw**: the pieces differ too much in thickness (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Cut them even first, grill pieces with an obvious thickness difference on separate skewers, and check doneness skewer by skewer.
