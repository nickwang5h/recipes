Shanghai thick stir-fried noodles uses thick, round wheat noodles with shredded pork, shiitake mushrooms and greens. The soy sauce colour is deep, but the noodles must still stay in separate strands. Light soy sauce gives the saltiness, dark soy sauce gives the colour, and a little sugar ties the flavours together. Fresh shiitake sliced thin can go straight into the wok, so the shredded pork, greens and mushrooms make a whole meal in one pot.

The noodles go through two stages of heat, boiling and stir-frying. [Starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) cooks the core of the noodle, but the starch released at the surface makes the strands stick together. Boil until a slight core remains, rinse off the sticky film, drain, and only then put them in the wok, so the noodles can stand up to tossing. On a home stove especially, drain the greens well so that steam does not use up the heat in the wok (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Toss the shredded pork with all the seasonings in the pork marinade group, cover and refrigerate for 10 minutes, until the surface has a thin coating and there is no obvious flowing marinade at the bottom of the bowl. Meanwhile, cut the mushrooms and greens, and mix the two soy sauces and the sugar for the stir-fry.
2. Boil water in the same deep wok you will use for stir-frying the noodles, over high heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water keeps rolling. Add the thick noodles and cook for about 3–4 minutes, until they bend and are pliable and have only a slight core when bitten; brands cook differently, so start tasting 1 minute before the package time.
3. Pour the noodles into a colander and rinse with cold water until they no longer feel hot to the touch. Drain for 2 minutes, until the bottom of the colander is no longer dripping steadily. Toss with 0.25 tbsp of the oil and loosen gently. Pour out the water in the wok and wipe the same wok dry.
4. Add 0.5 tbsp of oil to the wok and heat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows freely. Spread in the pork and stir-fry for about 2–3 minutes, until the strands separate and change colour on the surface and the centre of the thickest piece reaches 71°C (160°F). Transfer to a clean plate.
5. Add the remaining 0.75 tbsp of oil to the same wok and add the shiitake mushrooms. Stir-fry over medium-high heat for 2 minutes, until the slices soften and the edges brown slightly. Add the bok choy stems and stir-fry for 1 minute, until the cut edges look slightly translucent.
6. Add the noodles and the stir-fry sauce and toss over medium-high heat for 2–3 minutes, until the noodles are evenly brown and there is no pooled sauce at the bottom of the wok. Loosen them with chopsticks and a spatula; do not chop at the noodles with the edge of the spatula, which would cut them.
7. Return the pork, add the bok choy leaves and stir-fry for 1–2 more minutes, until the leaves have just wilted and the centre of the noodles is hot with no hard core. Serve at once.

## Key points

- **Noodles clump together and break up as they are stirred**: they were overcooked, or the sticky film was not rinsed off (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Before they go in the wok they should loosen easily, leaving room for the final stir-fry to finish cooking them.
- **Colour is deep but it eats like boiled noodles**: the noodles and greens carried too much water and pulled the wok temperature down (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain first, add the soy sauce once there is no pooled water at the bottom of the wok, and do not keep adding dark soy sauce to chase colour.
- **Shredded pork is tough**: the cooked meat stayed with the noodles for a long stir-fry, and water kept being squeezed out (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook the pork through first and take it out, and return it to the wok only at the end to heat evenly.
