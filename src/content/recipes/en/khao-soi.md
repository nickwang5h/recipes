*Khao soi* is a northern Thai coconut curry egg noodle soup, common in Chiang Mai. The bottom of the bowl holds soft boiled noodles, the top a handful of crisp fried noodles, and beside it sit pickled mustard greens, raw shallots and lime. The curry broth is rich and savoury, and the sour toppings keep the next bite fresh (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).

Tsaoko and coriander seeds are toasted first, and the ginger and shallots are charred in a dry pan until spotted brown. They are pounded into a paste and fried in coconut milk (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The fried noodles rely on hot oil to drive off water. When the noodles turn golden, watch the bubbles too: the crisp texture comes once the bubbles have almost gone (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).

## Steps

1. Toast the tsaoko seeds and coriander seeds: put them in a dry pan over medium heat and stir for 1–2 minutes, until slightly darker and clearly fragrant. Tip them out and grind. Separately, snip the seeded dried chilies into pieces and grind them, then mix the two powders together.
2. In the same dry pan, char the ginger slices over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes per side, until brown spots appear. Add the shallots for the paste and char them for a few seconds, then keep turning until the edges also have brown spots. Pound the ginger, shallots and coarse salt for about 8–10 minutes, until there are no obvious hard lumps. Add the chili and spice powder and the turmeric in several additions and pound to an even, wet paste.
3. Fry the curry paste: put 120 ml coconut milk in a pot and heat over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until it boils. Stir in all the paste and stir-fry for 3–5 minutes, until thick and fragrant; fine beads of oil may appear at the edge. Add the remaining coconut milk, the water, both soy sauces, the palm sugar and the chicken drumsticks.
4. Cook over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the broth bubbles again, then turn to low and simmer gently for 40 minutes with the lid cracked open. The drumsticks are done when a fork pulls the meat apart and a thermometer in the thickest part, away from the bone, reads 74°C (165°F). The surface of the broth should show only small bubbles; do not reduce it too far. At the end, taste and add the fish sauce from the ingredients as needed.
5. Fry the noodles while the chicken simmers: take 85 g fresh noodles, shake off the loose flour and snip them into lengths of about 10 cm. Heat the oil over medium heat for about 5–7 minutes to 175°C (350°F). Add a small pinch at a time, press them under the oil and fry for about 1–2 minutes, until golden and the bubbles have almost disappeared. Lift out, spread them out and drain. Wait about 1 minute for the oil to return to 175°C (350°F) before frying the next batch.
6. Make the optional chili oil: put the chili flakes and 10 ml oil in a small pot and stir over medium-low heat for about 2 minutes, until slightly darker and strongly fragrant. Pour it at once into a small dish.
7. Boil the remaining 255 g fresh noodles: heat the water over high heat for about 8–10 minutes to a full boil, add the noodles and cook for about 3 minutes, checking from the 2nd minute, until there is no hard centre and the noodles are still springy. Drain and divide among 4 bowls. Put 2 drumsticks in each bowl and ladle in the hot curry broth.
8. Put the fried noodles and the optional cilantro on top, and serve with the pickled mustard greens, shallots, lime and optional chili oil. Eat while the fried noodles are still crisp, and stir the sour toppings into the broth.

## Key points

- **Thin-tasting curry broth**: the spices were not toasted or the paste was not fried long enough. Take the dry spices out as soon as they darken slightly, and fry the paste until thick before adding liquid (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Fried noodles golden but chewy**: the surface coloured first and the inside still held water. Fry a little at a time, lift out when the bubbles have almost gone, and lower the heat if they darken too fast (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Heavy and greasy by the end**: there are too few pickled mustard greens and too little lime, so the rich coconut milk has no sour balance. Serve the toppings in the bowl so every bite picks up a little sour crunch (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
