Oi muchim is a fresh, quickly tossed side dish common on Korean tables. The cucumber wears a thin coat of red dressing, and each bite is crisp first, then garlic, sesame and heat. The dressing is only soy sauce, Korean chili flakes and toasted sesame oil, and all the fresh juice of the cucumber is kept.

The [salt](/principles/salt/) in the soy sauce gradually draws water out of cut cucumber, so this dish is seasoned just before serving. Thin slices are easy to coat with the dressing, and you only need to toss until it is evenly distributed. The garlic is cut fine and mixed with the sesame oil so its aroma spreads more easily (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Wash the cucumber, wipe off the water drops on the surface and cut off both ends. Halve the long cucumber lengthwise, then slice on the diagonal about 3 mm thick. The seeds of an English cucumber are small and tender and can be eaten, and the cut faces should be whole and juicy.
2. Cut the onion into thin strips, the green onion into fine rounds and mince the garlic. Make the onion strips as thin as you can, so that each bite of cucumber has only a light onion bite. Pull apart any onion slices stuck together and put them in a large bowl.
3. In a small bowl, stir the soy sauce, chili flakes, sesame oil and garlic together. If you add sugar, stir for about 30–60 seconds, until no obvious sugar grains are left and the chili flakes are evenly moistened. Then you won't need to toss for long once it is poured over the cucumber.
4. Just before serving, combine the cucumber, onion and green onion in a large bowl and pour the dressing over. Use two spoons to lift gently from the bottom and toss for about 30 seconds, until every cut face has picked up red chili flakes and no spot at the bottom of the bowl has a pile of dressing.
5. Add the toasted sesame seeds, toss gently, and transfer to a small plate with the thin layer of dressing. Eat right away with rice or grilled meat. If you will eat it over two meals, refrigerate the unseasoned cucumber slices and the dressing separately, and mix only the portion you are about to eat.

## Key points

- **Water collects at the bottom of the plate and the flavour gets weaker**: the cucumber sat too long after being dressed, and the salt keeps drawing out water. Cutting ahead is fine, but season just before eating; drain off any liquid already released, then plate (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **The cucumber goes limp and the edges break**: the slices are too thin, and you tossed as hard as if kneading dough. Keep slices thick enough to pick up, toss gently from the bottom and stop when the seasoning is even (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Only a nose-stinging heat, and the sesame aroma is covered**: you swapped Korean chili flakes for a very hot plain chili powder, or used too much sesame oil. Look for gochugaru, mix it by the stated amounts and then taste; keep the toasted sesame seeds for the finish (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
