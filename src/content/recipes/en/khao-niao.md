*Khao niao* is the sticky rice eaten across northern and northeastern Thailand. It is made with long-grain white glutinous rice that is soaked in water and then steamed over it. The steamed grains stick together but you can still see each one's outline, and you can pull off a small ball to dip in sauce. It goes well with [Moo Ping](/recipes/moo-ping/), [Larb Moo](/recipes/larb-moo/) and [Som Tam](/recipes/som-tam/).

Glutinous rice is high in amylopectin, which makes it very sticky once cooked. Soaking first lets water into the inside of the grains, and the steam then heats that water so the starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). The rice gets most of its water from soaking, and the rice on the tray never touches the water below. If it is spread too thick or the steam path is blocked, the centre cooks slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Put the rice in a large bowl and rinse it 3–4 times with fresh water, until the poured-off water is mostly clear. Pour off the rinse water and add 1000 ml room-temperature water. Use a bowl where the water stands about 3 cm above the rice.
2. Soak for 4 hours, until the grains have swollen and, when you press one open with a fingernail, the centre is no longer hard. Pour off the water and drain the rice in a sieve for 5 minutes, until it no longer drips continuously. If you plan to soak overnight, soak it in the fridge for 8–12 hours instead.
3. Add 1500 ml water to the steamer and keep the water level below the tray. Cover and heat on high for about 5–8 minutes, until the water boils steadily and there is plenty of steam. Line the tray with a thin cotton cloth that has been soaked and wrung out until it does not drip.
4. Spread the rice loosely on the cloth in a layer about 3 cm thick, leaving a hole about 3 cm wide in the middle for the steam to rise. Fold the edges of the cloth over the rice, leaving a gap all around so the cloth does not seal the tray.
5. Cover and keep the water at the bottom boiling, and steam for 25–30 minutes. Taste a few grains from the middle of the rice layer; they should be slightly translucent and chewy, with no chalky white hard centre. If the centre is still hard, loosen the rice, sprinkle the reserved water evenly over it 30 ml at a time, cover and steam for 10 more minutes, until the hard centre is gone.
6. Tip the rice onto a large plate and fluff it to let off steam for about 1 minute, until the surface has no wet, shiny layer of condensed steam. While it is still warm, put it in a lidded container. At the table, cover it with a clean cloth or a lid so the top does not dry out.

## Key points

- **Soft outside, hard inside, and drier the longer it steams**: the soak was too short, so the centres of the grains lack water. Soak long enough, then taste the grains in the middle. A hard centre is caused by a lack of water; add water and steam again so the starch can [gelatinize](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), because steaming longer dry will not make up the water.
- **Wet and sticky at the bottom, still raw on top**: the rice layer is too thick, or the rice touches the water below. Keep it over the water rather than in it, spread it thin and leave the hole in the middle so steam passes through the rice (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Hard as soon as it reaches the table**: the rice was left spread out too long and the surface lost water, and as it cools the starch also [retrogrades](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Cover it once the surface steam has let off, and time the steaming to the meal.
