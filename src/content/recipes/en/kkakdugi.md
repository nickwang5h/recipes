Kkakdugi is a common Korean kimchi in which neat cubes of radish are coated in red chili flakes, and it goes well with a light beef bone soup or rice. Just mixed, it is fresh and spicy with a clear radish flavour. After fermenting it gains a sour fragrance, and it should still be crisp to bite.

[Salt](/principles/salt/) first draws out some of the water so the seasoning clings to the radish, and then lactic acid fermentation gradually builds the sourness (see [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/)). Korean radish is dense; cubes sour more slowly than strips, so judge ripeness by the sour fragrance and the texture.

## Steps

1. Use a clean knife and board. Wash the soil off the radish, peel off the tough skin and any damaged spots and dry it. Cut it into cubes about 2–2.5 cm across and put them in a large bowl. Cubes of the same size salt and take up seasoning more evenly.
2. Sprinkle on the coarse kosher salt and the sugar, toss, and let stand for 30 minutes, until a clear amount of liquid has gathered in the bottom of the bowl, the radish surface is wet and the corners are slightly softened. Toss once halfway so the bottom and top layers swap places.
3. Pour the liquid that has come out into a small bowl, measure out 80 ml to keep, and discard the rest. Do not rinse the radish; put it back in the mixing bowl.
4. Add the fish sauce, chili flakes, garlic, ginger, green onion and the reserved radish liquid, and toss with clean gloved hands. Mix until every cube is evenly red with the fine seasoning clinging to the cut faces, and a little wet red liquid is left at the bottom of the bowl.
5. Pack it into a clean food-grade fermenting container. Press the radish down lightly to remove the large gaps between the cubes and pour in the mixing liquid. Leave space at the top of the container and use a lid that can vent, since the liquid will increase.
6. Ferment in a cool spot at room temperature for about 48–72 hours, looking every 24 hours. Move it to the fridge when fine bubbles appear and you can smell a clear sour fragrance; if the sour smell shows up earlier, refrigerate earlier. A spot next to a heater or a stuffy kitchen is a poor place for this wait; you can put it straight in the fridge and let it sour slowly.
7. After moving it to the fridge, leave it for another 168–336 hours (7–14 days), until the sourness has blended into the radish and the raw sharpness of the garlic has faded, and eat it when a taste of a cube is still crisp. Each time, use clean utensils to take out a small dish and leave the rest in the fridge.

## Key points

- **So salty it stings**: coarse and fine salt weigh differently by volume, and the fish sauce adds salt too. Measure coarse salt, discard the extra salting liquid, and do not pour all of it back (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).
- **Just mixed and it seems to have "no kimchi taste"**: the red comes from the chili flakes, and the sourness has to come from fermentation. After refrigerating, let it sour slowly and taste for the sour fragrance and the texture (see [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/)).
- **Mould on the surface**: throw out the whole jar. Keep the container and serving utensils clean, and refrigerate once it has matured (see [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/)).
