Singapore noodles are curry-scented stir-fried rice vermicelli: thin noodles coated golden, with a dry plate bottom, served with shrimp, char siu, egg and vegetables. It is a common dish in Cantonese restaurants in Hong Kong.

The noodles first absorb water and turn pliable, then finish cooking in the wok under heat (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Soaked too soft, they break easily when tossed; too firm, and you have to add too much water, which also cooks the other ingredients soft. The curry powder meets hot oil as you toss and spreads over the noodles (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)), so the finished dish isn't strong in one bite and bland in the next.

## Steps

1. Put the soaking water in the wok and heat over medium-high heat for about 4–5 minutes, until dense small bubbles form on the bottom and steam starts to rise from the surface, then turn off the heat. Add the vermicelli and soak for about 30 minutes, until it bends and separates by hand but still has a little resilience in the centre. If the thin noodles reach this point sooner, lift them out so they don't turn mushy.
2. While the noodles soak, cut the vegetables, char siu, garlic and green onion, and beat the eggs. Mix the curry powder, sugar, white pepper and 0.5 tsp of salt. Lift the noodles into a colander and drain for about 3 minutes, until they no longer drip continuously, then snip them into lengths of about 20 cm.
3. Pour the soaking water out of the wok and wipe the wok dry. Preheat over medium heat for about 1 minute, add 1 tbsp of oil, pour in the beaten eggs and scramble for about 1 minute, until the edges puff and the egg sets. Push into wide strips and remove.
4. Add the remaining 1.5 tbsp of oil to the same wok and stir-fry the garlic over medium heat for about 0.25 minutes, until you smell the garlic. Add the shrimp and char siu and stir-fry for about 0.25 minutes, until the shrimp start to change colour on the surface. Drizzle the wine around the edge of the wok and stir-fry for about 0.25 minutes more, until the wine boils.
5. Add the dried chili peppers, napa cabbage and carrot and stir-fry over medium-high heat for about 0.5–1 minute, until the cabbage wilts slightly and the carrot shreds can bend. Then add the noodles and toss from the bottom for about 1 minute, until the noodles and fillings are loosely mixed.
6. Sprinkle the curry seasoning evenly over the pan and toss from the bottom for about 2 minutes, until the whole pan is one colour. If the noodles still have a hard centre, add the prepared 60 ml of water in portions of 15 ml, stir-frying for about 0.5 minutes each time. Once the noodles are pliable and there is no liquid pooling at the bottom, stop adding water.
7. Add the light soy sauce, toasted sesame oil and eggs and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the centre of the shrimp is opaque, the egg strips are hot through, and the centre of the pan reaches 74°C (165°F). Taste and add the rest of the salt as needed, then add the onion and green onion and stir-fry for about 20 seconds, until the onion is slightly soft but still has some bite. Plate.

## Key points

- **Noodles break into short crumbs**: they were soaked too soft and then pressed and tossed repeatedly in the wok. Rice vermicelli softens after absorbing water and heat ([starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Drain it once it bends, and toss with a lifting, folding motion.
- **A lump of noodles sticks to the bottom of the wok**: the pan is too crowded, or the noodles are so dry that spots overheat. Following the logic of [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), keep the fillings loose, lift the bottom layer first, then add water in portions as in the steps.
- **Curry clumps, colour uneven**: the powder fell in one spot. Mix it with the salt and sugar first and scatter it widely, so every lift of noodles meets the seasoning powder. Don't press dry powder on the bottom of the wok and burn it for a long time (see [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)).
