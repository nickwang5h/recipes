The sweetness in longan and red date congee comes from the dried fruit, and the glutinous rice turns plain water into a smooth, silky congee. The rice grains keep absorbing water and heat, the starch swells, and some of it escapes into the liquid (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Glutinous rice is high in amylopectin, so it turns especially sticky and soft when cooked. The congee is ready when the grains have burst open and fall apart under light pressure.

This pot has a lot of water, so the congee comes out thin, enough for four bowls. Judge the thickness only once the rice is cooked: while the rice is still hard the liquid is clear, and that does not necessarily mean too much water. In a rice cooker, use the congee setting. Its heat is gentle and the grains open up slowly; a regular rice program may stay on high the whole time, or switch off too early. If the heat is too high and the liquid boils too hard, the congee can also boil over (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Shell and pit the longans, keeping only the flesh. Wash and pit the red dates, and tear open any large ones. Soak the two separately in clean water for about 10 minutes, then drain off the soaking water and pick out any hard shell and bits of pit.
2. Rinse the glutinous rice lightly twice and drain off the rinsing water. Put the rice in the rice cooker, add the congee water from the ingredient list, then add the red dates and longan flesh and stir gently, so the rice does not clump in one spot.
3. The liquid level must stay below the congee maximum in the manual. Then press the congee button. If the pot is too small, cook in two pots, keeping the ratio of rice, water and dried fruit the same.
4. After about 1 hour, check the rice, and if your model allows opening the lid, open it and stir. The congee is done when the rice has swollen and burst open and falls apart under light pressure, the longan is soft and the date flesh mashes easily; if there are still hard cores, keep cooking.
5. With no congee setting, use a pot of enough capacity: bring the same rice, water and dried fruit to a boil, then turn the heat to low, leave the lid ajar and keep a gentle simmer, stirring gently from the bottom now and then. Check the rice after about 1 hour.
6. When cooked, stir well and scoop out a bowl to check the thickness. To thicken it, uncover the pot and reduce over low heat; to thin it, add hot water and stir until even. The congee also thickens as it stands off the heat, so once it is right, divide it into bowls instead of boiling it down further in the pot until it sticks to the bottom.

## Key points

- **Rice is hard and the liquid is still clear**: the grains have not absorbed enough water, [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) is not finished, or the program stopped early. Keep cooking gently and don't rush to pour off the water.
- **Bubbles boil over, bottom is scorched and sticky**: the pot is too full or the heat is too high, so foam rises and the rice sinks to the bottom and overheats in spots. Following [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), lower the heat and leave headroom, and stir gently from the bottom in a pot; in a rice cooker, stay within the congee capacity.
- **Clearly thicker after standing**: the grains keep absorbing water, and the structure formed by [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) also changes as it cools. Leave a little looseness when serving, add a small amount of water when reheating, and break up any clumps before tasting for sweetness.
