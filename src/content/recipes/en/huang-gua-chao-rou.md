In stir-fried pork with cucumber, the cucumber is crisp and the pork slices are tender, and the two only meet briefly in the pan. The cucumber is first salted with a little [salt](/principles/salt/) to draw out surface water, then drained before stir-frying, so the pan does not fill with liquid. The light soy sauce already seasons the pork, so decide on extra salt only after everything is tossed together.

Slice the pork thin against the grain and slice the cucumber on the diagonal to a similar thickness. Good [knife work](/principles/knife-work/) makes the pork heat more evenly, and lets the cucumber soften slightly in a short time while staying crisp. The pork is cooked first and the cucumber added after, so the two have separate stopping points, and the cucumber does not have to stay in the pan while you wait for the pork.

## Steps

1. Slice the lean pork thin and evenly against the grain, toss with 5 g of cooking oil and 5 g of light soy sauce, and marinate in the refrigerator for 10 minutes. Separate the slices so several are not stacked in a clump; this lets them heat evenly in the pan.
2. Wash the cucumber and cut off only the hard stem end and any bad tip. Weigh out 100 g of trimmed cucumber and slice it on the diagonal about 5 mm thick. If the seeds are especially large and the flesh around them soft, remove the soft part first, then weigh.
3. Toss the cucumber with 0.5 g of salt and let it sit for 5 minutes. When a little water has collected at the bottom of the bowl, pour it off and blot the surface gently with paper towels; do not wring it out hard, so the cucumber keeps its crispness.
4. Smash and mince the garlic, and stem and cut the chili. Set the cucumber, pork and aromatics by the stove; once the pork goes in, you must finish the quick stir-fry that follows without stopping.
5. Heat the pan and add 10 g of oil, then heat over medium heat until the oil flows and there is no smoke. Add the garlic and chili, stir a few times quickly, and add the pork when you smell the garlic but it is still pale.
6. Turn the heat to medium-high, spread the pork out and break up the slices, then toss until every side has changed colour. Insert a thermometer into the thickest slice; at 71°C (160°F) it is done.
7. Add the drained cucumber and stir-fry over high heat for about 30 seconds, so the slices pick up the garlicky pork juices. On a weaker burner this can stretch to about 50 seconds; stop when the cucumber is hot through, slightly translucent at the edges and still crunchy when bitten.
8. Taste a slice of cucumber, then decide whether to add the remaining salt, toss quickly and plate immediately. Do not leave it in the hot pan while you wait for the rice; the residual heat keeps softening the slices and drawing out water.

## Key points

- **Lots of water on the plate, cucumber limp**: the water drawn out by the salt was not poured off, or the cucumber was stir-fried too long when it went back in (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Drain well after salting, add the cucumber last and stir-fry it only briefly.
- **Pork tough, and a few slices undercooked**: the slices vary in thickness, so thin ones have lost water while the centres of thick ones have only started to cook (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Slice evenly against the grain, and cook the pork through first.
- **Garlic tastes bitter**: the oil was hot enough to scorch the garlic first (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Stir-fry the aromatics before the oil smokes, and add the pork as soon as you smell them.
