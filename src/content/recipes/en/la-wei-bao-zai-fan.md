Cantonese clay pot rice with cured meats cooks Cantonese sausage, cured pork and rice together in one small clay pot. The cured meats sit on top of the rice, and the fat and sweet-salty flavour they steam out seep down; at the end the soy sauce and green onion are stirred in. The thin, crisp layer of crust at the bottom is the other half of what makes this rice.

The rice first takes up water, then the heat gelatinizes its starch (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)), and that is how it goes from hard grains to cooked rice. The crust has to wait until the water at the bottom is mostly gone; then [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) through the base of the pot dries and browns the rice that touches it, and that toasted aroma comes from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). So cook the rice through first, then make the crust: two steps, each watched on its own.

## Steps

1. Rinse the rice and drain it, put it in a clay pot about 19 cm across that can go over direct heat, add 240 ml of water and soak for 1 hour, until the grains swell slightly and look white on the surface. Don't pour away the soaking water; cook with it.
2. Wipe the sausage clean with a damp paper towel, wipe the cured pork and cut into slices about 0.5 cm thick. Stir together the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine, toasted sesame oil, sugar and white pepper for about 30 seconds, until no grains of sugar are obvious, and set aside.
3. Put the pot with the rice and water over medium-low heat and cook uncovered for about 4–6 minutes, until the water has dropped to the level of the rice and small holes appear in the surface. The bottom should still be moist; stay by the pot, and don't wait until the water has boiled away to add the cured meats.
4. Lay the whole sausages and the cured pork slices on the rice, in a single layer if possible, without stirring them into the rice. Cover, turn to the lowest heat and cook 10–12 minutes, until the grains are plump, a grain tastes of no hard core, and the fat of the cured pork has turned translucent. The centre of pork cured meats should reach 71°C (160°F), or follow the package if it says otherwise; if not, cover and cook 3 more minutes and measure again. If the rice is still hard, add 30 ml of hot water around the edge, cover and cook on low for 3–5 more minutes; do this at most twice.
5. When the rice is cooked through, drizzle 2 tsp of vegetable oil around the inside wall of the pot, put the lid back on and cook over medium-low heat for 2–4 minutes to make the crust, until a faint crackling comes from the bottom and there is a smell of toasted rice. On a gas stove, turn the pot every 30 seconds so the bottom heats evenly; if there is a sharp burnt smell or heavy smoke, take it off the heat at once.
6. Take out the sausage, cut it into slices about 0.5 cm thick on the diagonal, and lay them back on the rice. While the pot is still hot, pour in the mixed soy sauce, sprinkle with green onion, and mix the cured meats with the rice on top, then use a rice paddle to lift the thin crust from the edge. The grains should be distinct and the crust golden.

## Key points

- **The rice is undercooked and the bottom has already burnt**: not enough water or too much heat, so the pan went dry before the rice had gone through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/). Finish cooking it covered with the reserved hot water, and make the crust only after the rice is cooked through.
- **No crust and the rice is wet and sticky**: oil was added before the water had cooked down, or the heat was too low when making the crust (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Make sure the rice is cooked and there is no obvious loose water in the pot, then drizzle oil around the edge and toast briefly.
- **The crust is burnt and bitter**: heating continued after it dried, and the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) went too far. Check the heat when you hear a faint crackle and smell toasted rice; it also works in a heavy-bottomed pot, starting with a shorter 2 minutes.
