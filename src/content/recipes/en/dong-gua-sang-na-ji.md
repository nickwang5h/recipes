Steamed chicken on winter melon is a layered steamed dish: winter melon on the bottom, soaked cordyceps flowers in the middle and marinated chicken pieces on top. As it steams, the chicken juices run down, and the melon cooks soft, taking on the [umami](/principles/umami/) of the chicken and the seasoning liquid. The juices at the bottom of the plate can be spooned up and eaten with the melon.

"Sauna" describes the steaming method. Steam carries heat to the plate and the surface of the food, and the heat then has to travel inward through each layer of ingredients (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cutting the melon thin, cutting the chicken evenly and spreading it in a single layer shortens the wait at the thickest spot; take the dish out once the centre is cooked, so the chicken's [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) don't keep contracting and losing water.

## Steps

1. Marinate the chicken. Cut the chicken into small pieces of similar size, add the shredded ginger, light soy sauce, oyster sauce, oil and half the salt, and mix until each piece is evenly coated in the marinade. Refrigerate for 20 minutes.
2. Soak the cordyceps flowers. Rinse the dried cordyceps flowers and soak them in clean water until soft and bendable, then lift out and drain. You can soak them while the chicken marinates. Before laying them on the plate, separate them into a loose layer and don't press them into a thick clump, or the chicken underneath heats more slowly.
3. Lay the winter melon. Remove the hard skin and soft pith from the winter melon, cut it into thin slices of similar thickness and spread them over the bottom of a rimmed heatproof plate. The rim has to hold the juices released in steaming; overlap the slices as little as possible, so the bottom layer also heats through easily.
4. Lay the chicken. Spread the cordyceps flowers over the winter melon first, then arrange the marinated chicken pieces on top in a single layer, pouring the marinade over as well. Leave a little space between the pieces, don't pile the thickest pieces in the centre of the plate, and leave room around the plate for steam to flow.
5. Steam. Fill the steamer with enough water, bring it to a boil, put in the plate and cover. Time about 15 minutes from when the steam returns. Keep the steam coming steadily and don't lift the lid often; when the water is about to run dry, add hot water along the edge of the pot.
6. Check and remove. Measure the centre of the thickest piece of chicken with a food thermometer, keeping the probe away from the bone, and take it out once it reaches 74°C (165°F); if not, cover and keep steaming. The winter melon should be translucent and easy to pierce through; if the chicken is done while the melon is still hard, take the chicken out first and keep steaming the melon.
7. Serve. Take out the plate with a heatproof gripper and bring it to the table together with the juices at the bottom. Taste a piece of the cooked melon with the chicken juices, and add the remaining salt if needed.

## Key points

- **Surface is cooked but the part near the bone is not**: the chicken pieces are too thick or stacked, so the centre heats slowly. Cut the pieces evenly and spread them in a single layer (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), and measure the temperature of the thickest piece before removing, instead of going only by 15 minutes.
- **Chicken is dry and tough, and there is a lot of liquid on the plate**: it kept steaming after it was done, and as the proteins in the meat contracted, the water was squeezed out (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Take it out promptly once the centre is confirmed done, and steam hard melon separately.
- **Melon is bland and the chicken is salty**: the marinade was concentrated in the top layer and unevenly distributed when the plate was laid. Pour the marinade over along with the chicken, and eat with some of the chicken juices from the bottom of the plate (see [umami](/principles/umami/)); taste first, then add salt.
