In this claypot the lamb belly is braised until the skin and tendon are soft and tender, and the tofu skin sticks soak up the meat juices. The Cantonese name *zhi zhu* means tofu skin sticks; fried ones go in, along with shiitake, shallots and a little chili oil, for a fairly strong savoury flavour. The lamb needs to be braised steadily in moist heat so the [collagen turns into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). How long that takes depends on the size and cut of the meat.

Chu hou paste, white fermented bean curd and red fermented bean curd each bring their own salty, savoury and aged aromas. The braising sauce is fried first and a second portion of fermented bean curd sauce goes in at the end, which keeps the fermented bean curd flavour distinct (see [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/)). The fried tofu skin sticks go in late, once softened, so they soak up the sauce without breaking down into crumbs.

## Steps

1. Soak the dried shiitake in cold water for 2–3 hours, until the centre has no hard core, then trim off the stems. Soak the fried bean curd sticks as the package directs; after about 30 minutes check whether they bend. Once soft, cut them into 5 cm lengths and squeeze out the water gently.
2. Mash the red fermented bean curd, chu hou paste, 20 g of the white fermented bean curd and the dark soy sauce together into the braising sauce. Mix the remaining 20 g of white fermented bean curd, the sugar and the chili oil into the finishing sauce. Slice the shallots, crack the garlic, and cut the green onions into 5 cm lengths, keeping the white and green parts separate.
3. Put the lamb in a pot of cold water with 3 slices of ginger. After it comes to a boil, skim off the foam. When the surface has changed colour and the foam has gathered at the top, lift the lamb out. Rinse off the scum, drain, pat the surface dry, and put it in the pot while it is still warm.
4. Add the oil to the pot and stir-fry the remaining ginger, the shallots, the white parts of the green onions and the garlic over medium heat until fragrant. Add the lamb and stir-fry for about 2–5 minutes, until the side touching the pot is lightly golden. Turn the heat to medium-low, add the braising sauce and stir-fry for about 2 minutes, so the meat is evenly coated in the sauce.
5. Pour in 500 ml of hot water and move everything to a small stewpot that fits the lamb snugly. The water must cover the meat; add hot water if it does not. Add the shiitake, rock sugar, bay leaf, star anise and cassia bark. Bring to a boil, cover and turn to low heat.
6. Braise for 90 minutes first, keeping the surface of the liquid gently bubbling, and top up with hot water if it runs short. Check the thickest piece of meat. If a chopstick goes in and the skin and tendon are close to soft, add the tofu skin sticks. If the lamb is still firm, keep braising and hold off on the sticks.
7. After adding the sticks, braise for about 20 minutes more, until they have soaked up enough sauce to be soft and the skin and tendon of the lamb bite through easily. Nudge the bottom of the pot gently to keep it from sticking, and do not cut the sticks up by pushing a spatula back and forth. Take out the whole spices, pour in the finishing sauce, and stir until the sugar melts and the whole pot is bubbling again.
8. Reduce the liquid until it clings to the tofu skin sticks and there is still thick, flowing sauce at the bottom. Add the green parts of the green onions and transfer to a claypot. Before serving, taste a piece of lamb with some sauce.

## Key points

- **Meat is still tough and the tofu skin sticks have broken into crumbs**: the sticks went in too early. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/). Wait until the lamb is nearly tender, then add the sticks.
- **Spices overpower the lamb**: whole spices steeped for a long time taste more concentrated once the sauce reduces. See [spice extraction](/principles/aromatics/). Use a little less, and lift them out once the aroma is enough.
- **Sauce is thick but bitter, with black bits stuck to the pot**: the fermented bean curd paste stuck and burned. See [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/). Fry the paste over medium-low heat, leave enough water for the long braise, and nudge the bottom of the pot at the end.
