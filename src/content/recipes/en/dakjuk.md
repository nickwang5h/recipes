Dakjuk is a Korean rice porridge made with chicken stock and shredded chicken, often with finely cut carrot, celery and mushrooms added. The soaked rice is first stir-fried in sesame oil, so the grains pick up oil and a sesame aroma, and then stock is added to cook them soft. The [umami](/principles/umami/) of the chicken goes into the rice broth, and the seasoning stays light.

The thickness of the porridge comes from [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/): the grains swell in the hot stock, and the starch they release makes the liquid smooth. At the start you can still tell the grains apart. At the end they are soft through with the edges bloomed, and the porridge still runs slowly off the spoon. The vegetables and cooked chicken go in late, so the chicken doesn't dry out while the rice cooks.

## Steps

1. Rinse the rice, add 600 ml of cold water and soak for 60 minutes, until the grains have absorbed water and look whiter, then pour into a strainer to drain.
2. Put the chicken stock in a pot and heat over medium heat for about 6–8 minutes, until it boils. Add the chicken pieces, turn to low heat and cook for 12–15 minutes, until the centre of the chicken reaches 74°C (165°F) and is fully cooked when cut. Lift out the chicken and keep the stock.
3. Spread out the chicken for 5 minutes, until you can handle it, then tear it along the grain into fine shreds. Mix in the minced garlic, 0.5 tbsp of sesame oil, half the salt and the black pepper. Measure the stock the chicken was cooked in again and top it up to 1400 ml with the hot water you set aside.
4. Add 2 tbsp of sesame oil and the drained rice to a heavy-bottomed pot and stir-fry over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until the grains turn glossy, the edges become slightly translucent and you can smell the sesame. Keep the grains pale, then pour in the chicken stock right away.
5. Heat over medium heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the stock comes back to a boil, then turn to medium-low and cook for 20–25 minutes. Stir along the bottom every 3–5 minutes, until the grains are soft with no hard core in the centre and the liquid starts to thicken.
6. Add the carrot, celery and shiitake, cover and cook over low heat for 10–15 minutes, until the diced vegetables can be crushed soft with the back of a spoon. Every 2–3 minutes, lift the lid and scrape across the bottom so nothing sticks.
7. Stir in the shredded chicken and cook for 3 more minutes, until the shreds are hot again and the whole pot reaches 74°C (165°F). If the porridge is too thick, add 50 ml of the hot water you set aside at a time and stir gently until it flows slowly off the spoon. Season with the remaining salt, ladle into bowls and sprinkle on the optional green onion.

## Key points

- **Hard centres in the rice, yet the porridge is already very thick**: the water evaporated too fast and the grains didn't absorb enough. Add hot water and lower the heat, and cook until the centres are soft before thickening (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Scorched bottom while the top is still bubbling**: once the porridge thickens, the grains sink and overheat locally. Lower the heat gradually and scrape the bottom as you stir. If it already smells burnt, move the top layer to a clean pot (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Dry chicken shreds, faint sesame oil flavour**: the cooked chicken went in too early, or the sesame oil was heated at high heat for a long time. Add the chicken last, and add the stock as soon as the stir-fried rice smells fragrant (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
