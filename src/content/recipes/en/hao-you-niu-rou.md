Beef with oyster sauce is a common Cantonese velvet stir-fry. Thin beef slices are coated with a little marinade, stir-fried until the surface sets, then returned to the pan to take up the oyster sauce mixture. Gai lan lines the edge of the plate, so each bite has crisp greens, slippery tender beef and a thin layer of savoury glaze.

Tenderness starts with the cut. Cutting against the grain shortens the long fibres, so each bite chews apart more easily (see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). The starch in the thin marinade [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat and adds slickness at the surface; the beef still needs a short stir-fry, in batches, so the pan bottom recovers its temperature in time (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the beef into slices about 3 mm thick against the grain and toss with the 2 tbsp water and the marinating cornstarch, oil and oyster sauce; add the baking soda if the meat is tough. Marinate in the refrigerator for 30 minutes, until there is no obvious pooled water at the bottom of the bowl and the slices have only a thin wet coating.
2. Mix the chicken stock with the oyster sauce, sugar, cornstarch, dark soy sauce and white pepper for the sauce. Split the thick gai lan stems lengthwise and keep stems and leaves separate; cut the onion into wedges, tear the oyster mushrooms into small pieces, and cut the ginger and garlic. Have everything ready before it goes into the pan.
3. Put 1500 ml of water and 1 tsp of oil in a pot and boil over high heat for about 5–7 minutes, until the water is boiling all over. Blanch the gai lan stems for 30 seconds first, then the leaves for 30 seconds, until the leaves are bright green and the stems can be bitten through yet are still crisp. Lift out and drain for 1 minute, until no more water drips, and arrange around the edge of the plate.
4. Pour out the blanching water, wipe the pot dry, and preheat over medium-high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is dry. Add 1 tbsp of the oil and cook the beef in two batches: spread each batch and sear 30 seconds, until it sets against the pan, then stir-fry about 45 seconds, until most of the surface has changed colour. Set aside; the centre is left to finish cooking when it returns to the pan.
5. Add the remaining 2 tbsp oil to the pan, turn to medium heat and stir-fry the ginger for 30 seconds, until the ginger aroma is clear; add the garlic and stir-fry for 15 seconds, until it has just become fragrant. Turn to high heat, add the onion and oyster mushrooms and stir-fry about 15 seconds, until the onion edges colour slightly and the mushrooms soften. Drizzle the Shaoxing wine down the side of the pan and stir-fry another 30 seconds, until the strong smell of alcohol has gone.
6. Stir up the sediment at the bottom of the bowl, pour in the oyster sauce mixture, and cook over medium heat for about 30–60 seconds, until the liquid bubbles and turns from cloudy to clear. Return the beef and stir-fry for 30–60 seconds, until each slice is coated in a thin glaze and the thickest slice, when cut, has no raw, translucent meat, then take off the heat at once.
7. Pour off the water that has collected again on the gai lan plate, spoon the beef with its sauce into the centre, and serve hot. The glaze should cling to the slices, with only a little flowing sauce left on the plate.

## Key points

- **Beef won't chew through**: cutting with the grain left long fibres, or the slices vary in thickness (see [knife work](/principles/knife-work/)). Find the parallel grain first, and cut thin slices across it.
- **Pan floods with water and the meat gets tougher the more you stir-fry**: the pan is too full and the heat goes into the pooled water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Stir-fry the meat in batches, drain the vegetables first, and take the beef out promptly once it is coated.
- **Glaze clumps or is like paste**: the starch settled at the bottom of the bowl and was not dispersed when poured in (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir it up again before it goes in, push it around in the bubbling sauce, and don't sprinkle dry starch in separately.
