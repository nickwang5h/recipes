A tamago sando (egg sandwich) is a boiled-egg salad in soft white bread with the crusts removed, a common breakfast and lunch box item in Japan. The whites are chopped into small bits and the yolks mashed to a paste, then mixed with Japanese mayonnaise into a spreadable filling. The first bite should be soft bread, then rich, smooth yolk and slightly springy whites.

The eggs are cooked hard through [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and cooled before mixing. Mayonnaise is itself a stable [emulsion](/principles/emulsification/), which carries fat and water through the chopped egg; a little milk helps loosen the mashed yolk. A thin spread of butter on the inside of the bread slows the moist filling from soaking into it. With all three kept soft, the cut face doesn't squeeze apart.

## Steps

1. Put the eggs in a narrow pot where they fit in a single layer and pour in 1000 ml of water, which should cover the eggs by about 2.5 cm. Heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until the water has steady large bubbles; counting from the boil, turn to medium-low heat and cook for 12 minutes, keeping the water at a gentle roll.
2. Move the eggs into 1000 ml of chilled cold water and soak for 10 minutes, until the shells feel cool and the centre of the egg is no longer warm once peeled. Peel and dry the surface; the yolk should be fully set, with nothing fluid in the centre.
3. Mash the boiled eggs with a fork, breaking up the yolks first, then pressing the whites into small pieces about 3–5 mm. Add the milk, sugar, salt, black pepper and mayonnaise and mix until the yolk is smooth and the whites evenly distributed, and the filling gathers into a mass when scooped.
4. Spread 2.5 g of butter on one side of each of the four slices of bread, all the way to the edges. Divide the egg filling between two of the slices in an even layer, leaving about 0.5 cm at the edges, and top with the other two slices, butter side in.
5. Sandwich between two flat plates and press lightly with the weight of the top plate for 5 minutes, until the bread and filling stick together and no filling squeezes out of the four sides. Saw off the crusts gently with a sharp knife, then cut diagonally; the cut face should be neat and the filling should hold its shape.

## Key points

- **Filling is dry and crumbly, with big pieces of yolk**: the yolks weren't mashed fine, or the eggs sat in hot water for a long time after cooking. Cool them promptly, then mash the yolks thoroughly; add the milk by measure. See [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Filling is runny and slides out when cut**: the boiled eggs were wet on the surface, or too much milk or mayonnaise went in. Dry them after peeling and mix with the given amounts first; mayonnaise gives a rich, smooth texture, but the more you add, the softer the filling gets. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **Bread is soggy and the four sides are squashed**: the filling was still warm, the butter was missed, or extra weight was used. Wait until the eggs are completely cool, spread the butter thinly to the edges, and press lightly with just one flat plate. If you're not eating it right away, box it and refrigerate.
