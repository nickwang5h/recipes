*Phanaeng neua* is a curry of sinewy beef with peanuts and thick coconut cream. It is clearly salty and sweet, the thick sauce clings to the meat, and it is finished with makrut lime leaves and Thai sweet basil and eaten with hot rice. The brisket is stewed first and then sliced, so the slices stay whole and the sinew is soft.

It is not enough for the brisket to be cooked through. It needs moist heat and time for the collagen in the connective tissue to turn slowly into gelatin (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). When frying the curry paste, first cook the thick coconut cream until the oil separates, and the oil carries the aroma of the spices (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The sauce is thickened by reduction and by the fine peanut bits in the paste. With the water controlled, no separate starch slurry is needed.

## Steps

1. Boil the raw peanuts in water for at least 30 minutes, until they crush when pinched with no hard bits in the centre. Drain and cool. Soak the dried chilies until soft and squeeze out the water. Chop the lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallot and cilantro roots separately.
2. Pound the chilies with the salt to a fine paste first, then add the cilantro root, galangal, lemongrass, shallot, garlic and the toasted nutmeg one at a time, pounding each fine before the next, and pound in the peanuts last. Measure out 4 tbsp of paste and portion and freeze the rest right away.
3. Put the brisket in a pot of cold water, bring it just to a boil until the scum rises, then lift it out and rinse off the scum with water. In another pot, bring 1000 ml of coconut milk to a boil, add the meat, turn to low and simmer for about 2 hours, until a fork goes in easily and the sinew has softened.
4. Let the brisket cool slightly in its liquid until it can be cut, and slice it against the grain. Measure out 750 ml of the stewing liquid and set it aside, adding water if there is not enough. Cover the beef to keep it warm.
5. Cook the thick coconut cream in a large pot over medium heat until bright beads of oil rise to the top. Add the 4 tbsp of curry paste and stir-fry for about 10 minutes, until the aroma deepens and the paste darkens. Add the palm sugar first and stir for about 1 minute, then add the fish sauce.
6. Pour in the reserved stewing liquid and reduce uncovered over low heat until the sauce coats the back of a spoon in a layer. Add the sliced beef and the chilies and cook briefly, until the meat is hot through, the sauce clings to it, and fine bright beads of oil show on the surface.
7. Stir in the makrut lime leaves and sweet basil, and take it out as soon as the leaves have just wilted. If the sauce is still very thin, take the meat out first and keep reducing the sauce. The meat is already soft, so do not cook it longer.

## Key points

- **The beef is cooked but chewy**: the collagen has not softened all the way, and high heat only makes the muscle lose water and turn dry. Simmer slowly with small bubbles, and judge by whether a fork goes easily into the sinewy meat. See [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **Coarse peanut bits in the sauce**: the peanuts were not cooked soft, or the paste was not pounded fine, so the pieces stay in the sauce. Cook the peanuts until soft, cool them and pound them in last. If the paste scorches and turns bitter, make it again. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **A bowl of oily soup that does not cling to the meat**: the sauce was not reduced after the stewing liquid went in, or it was boiled hard until only oil was left. Control the water first and keep a thick sauce that still flows. See [Emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
