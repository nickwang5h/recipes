Honey char siu is a Cantonese roast meat. Pork shoulder with fat marbling is marinated all the way through, roasted to a sweet-salty outer layer with a charred aroma, then brushed with honey for a shine. The home method mixes the marinade from char siu sauce with oyster sauce and five-spice powder, and raises the meat in the oven so the excess juices drip down and every side of the meat is exposed to heat.

The roasted aroma comes entirely from the surface: once the moisture has gone, the sugar and the amino acids in the meat undergo the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) and the aroma develops. Honey is high in sugar, so it goes on last, which is what lets it shine. When the strips are cut to an even thickness, the centres cook through at about the same time (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Cut the pork shoulder into long strips about 2.5 cm thick, keeping the fat that runs through the lean. Mix the char siu sauce, oyster sauce, light soy sauce, five-spice powder, garlic and ginger. Take out 2.75 tsp into a clean small bowl, cover and refrigerate it to use as the honey glaze, and toss the remaining marinade with the pork.
2. Put the pork in a lidded container and refrigerate to marinate for 6–12 hours, until every strip is evenly coated in the sauce, turning once partway through. Spread the strips out, and do not press several pieces of different thickness into one clump.
3. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F) with top and bottom heat, about 15 minutes, until the preheat signal is done. Put 500 ml hot water in a baking pan and set a rack above it, with the water level below the rack. Lift the pork out of the marinade, pick off the clinging ginger and garlic, and lay the strips in a single layer with space between them.
4. Roast on the middle rack at 220°C (425°F) for 15 minutes, until the sauce on the surface stops running and the edges begin to colour. Take out and turn over, brush on a layer of the marinade from the original container, and roast 10 minutes more, until the second side has also dried. Check the drip pan: if the water is nearly gone, add 250 ml hot water so the drippings fall into water.
5. Mix the honey with the reserved 2.75 tsp of clean marinade. Take the pork out and brush a thin coat of the honey glaze on both sides. Raise the oven to 240°C (475°F) and roast for 5 minutes, until the surface is shiny and the edges show dark brown. Turn the pork over, brush with honey again and roast 3 minutes, until the honey layer is sticky and glossy but not blackened.
6. Insert a probe from the side into the thickest part; the pork is done at 71°C (160°F). If it has not reached that, lower the oven back to 220°C (425°F) and roast 5 minutes at a time, measuring after each. Cover areas that are dark enough with foil so they do not burn.
7. Rest the pork for 5 minutes after it comes out, until the surface stops steaming strongly, then slice about 0.5 cm thick and serve with a little of the juices from the plate. Do not use leftover marinade that touched raw meat as a cold dipping sauce.

## Key points

- **Burnt outside, undercooked centre**: the strips are too thick, or the honey was brushed on thickly too early, so the surface colours faster than the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep the thickness even, save the honey for the last stage, and cover and keep roasting once the colour is right.
- **Roasts into a pan of liquid, and the surface lacks aroma**: the meat sits in the juices at the bottom of the pan, so the surface cannot lose water (see [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Raise it on the rack and keep the water in the drip pan.
- **Dry, hard lean meat**: the cut was too lean, or the pork kept roasting after it was done, and protein shrinkage pushes out water (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Choose pork shoulder with fat marbling, judge the final stage with a probe, and take it out once cooked.
