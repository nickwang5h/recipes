Emperor Qianlong's napa cabbage is a cold dish of tender cabbage leaves tossed in a sweet-and-sour sesame dressing. The sesame paste gives body, the vinegar makes the richness of the sesame sharper, and the sugar rounds off the sourness (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). The tender leaves are crisp and sweet on their own, and with the thick stems removed, each bite picks up a similar amount of dressing.

Sesame paste contains oil and ground sesame solids; only when liquid is added and mixed in thoroughly does it become a smooth dressing (compare [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The paste thickens when the vinegar first goes in; keep loosening it in portions.

## Steps

1. Take the tender yellow leaves from the cabbage heart and cut off the thick white stems. Rinse under running water, then drain thoroughly and blot water droplets off the leaves with clean paper towels. Open the leaves and check the folds, where water collects most easily.
2. Tear the dry tender leaves into pieces about half the size of a palm and put them in a wide bowl. The pieces need not be even; avoid tearing them too small, or each mouthful tastes only of dressing and the leaves wilt more easily.
3. Stir the oil floating in the sesame paste jar back into the paste at the bottom, then take 30 g into a bowl. Add the 45 ml vinegar in portions, stirring each time until no dry lumps of paste are visible before adding the next.
4. Add the sugar and salt and stir until the grains dissolve, then stir in the toasted sesame oil. When you lift the spoon, the dressing should fall in a continuous stream and leave a thin coating on a leaf dipped in it; if hard lumps remain, press them apart with the back of the spoon first, then stir.
5. Pour the sesame dressing over the cabbage and fold gently from the bottom of the bowl upward until both sides of the leaves are coated. Avoid squeezing and kneading; the leaves should stay open, with no large pool of thin liquid at the bottom of the bowl.
6. Sprinkle on the optional toasted white sesame seeds and plate. You can let it stand for about 5 minutes before eating; if you like the crispest texture, eat it right after tossing. If you need to prepare ahead, refrigerate the cabbage and the dressing separately and toss them together before serving.

## Key points

- **Hard lumps in the dressing**: the vinegar has not been mixed into the paste (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Add the rest of the vinegar in portions and press lumps apart with the back of the spoon; do not add a lot of extra oil.
- **Water collects on the plate quickly**: the leaves were not drained, or sat too long after tossing; salt draws water out of vegetables (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Blot the droplets dry after washing and toss with the dressing only just before serving.
- **Sourness overwhelms the sesame aroma**: the acidity of vinegar varies by brand, and sugar can only round the sourness off, not suppress it (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Taste a coated leaf first; if it is clearly too sour, add more dry leaves instead of adding sugar again and again.
