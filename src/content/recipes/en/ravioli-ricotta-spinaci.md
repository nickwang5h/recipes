*Ravioli ricotta e spinaci* are spinach and cheese dumplings wrapped in thin egg pasta, with a filling built on ricotta, spinach, Parmigiano and nutmeg. The aroma of butter and sage is light and lets the milky cheese come through. The spinach is cooked soft and squeezed, and the cheese is drained, so the filling doesn't soak the thin pasta.

The dough needs kneading to develop [gluten](/principles/gluten/) and then resting to relax, so it can be rolled thin without tearing. When sealing, press out the air so the two sheets touch directly; flour and filling caught in the seam keep it from sticking. Makes about 24.

## Steps

1. Knead the flour with the eggs and yolk into a ball, and keep kneading until the cut surface is even and the outside fairly smooth. If there is dry flour that won't come together, add a little water; wrap and rest for 30 minutes so a skin doesn't form.
2. Put the washed spinach in a pot with the water clinging to its leaves, cover and cook until completely wilted. Let it cool, squeeze until it stops dripping, and chop fine; drain the liquid that has collected under the ricotta.
3. Mix the spinach, ricotta, Parmigiano and nutmeg, and add a little salt and black pepper. The filling should hold in small mounds with no liquid in the bowl; put it back in the fridge once mixed.
4. Roll the dough in batches into long sheets about 1 mm thick, covering the dough you aren't using. Thin it a setting at a time, until you can faintly see your hand through it; flour only enough to prevent sticking, and brush off loose flour at the seal.
5. Put small mounds of filling on half of a sheet, leaving wide seams. Fold the other half over, press outward from the filling to push out the air, then press the edges firmly. If the seam is too dry, brush on a thin layer of water. Cut into squares about 4 cm.
6. Lay the ravioli flat on a plate lightly dusted with flour. Bring salted water to a boil and add them in batches, keeping a gentle boil, until the pasta is cooked and the sealed edges have no hard, floury core. Taste one; floating is not enough to tell.
7. Melt the butter and sage over low heat and cook until the leaves release their fragrance and the butter is still pale. Add 1 tbsp of the ravioli cooking water, then the lifted-out ravioli, and shake the pan gently until a thin sauce coats the pasta, then plate right away.

## Key points

- **The dough keeps shrinking back after rolling**: the gluten is still tight, or the surface of the dough has dried out (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Cover and rest it again and thin it a setting at a time, instead of pressing it out with ever more flour.
- **Filling leaks when it goes in the water**: the filling was too wet, trapped air, or the seal had flour on it (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Squeeze the spinach dry before filling, and press from the filling outward to drive the air out.
- **The pasta is cooked but the plate is full of oil**: the butter had no water to bind with and was not tossed enough (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). Add a little cooking water, take the pan off the heat and shake it to gather the fat into a thin sauce; don't stir thin-skinned ravioli hard.
