Meaty crab claypot puts the sweetness of crab, the soft starchiness of potato and the chew of rice cake into one pot of sauce. The crab pieces are lightly coated in starch and fried first, which sets the outer layer of the cut surfaces; the later simmering then cooks the thick parts through (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Keep the coating thin, because a thick starch crust turns into a sticky paste in the sauce.

The potatoes go in first and the rice cakes last, because they need different amounts of time. Rice cakes are already cooked through, so in the sauce they mainly soak up liquid and soften, and the starch released from their surface thickens the sauce (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Finish with a layer of thick sauce that still flows; it keeps thickening after it goes into the serving dish.

## Steps

1. Take the cleaned crab and remove the gills, stomach, intestines and other inedible parts. Cut it into pieces of about 50–80 g and pat dry. Cut the potatoes into 3 cm cubes, the onion into wide pieces and the bell peppers into slices. Cut the rice cakes into slices about 1 cm thick, rinse them apart in cold water and drain. If using shrimp, snip off the sharp points, cut along the back and remove the vein, and pat dry.
2. Heat the frying oil in a deep pot to 180°C (350°F). Coat the cut surfaces of the crab thinly in starch and shake off the loose starch. Fry in batches for about 1 minute each, lifting the pieces out once the outer layer has set. Wait for the oil temperature to recover between batches.
3. Add the oil for sizzling the aromatics to a stewpot, then add the garlic, ginger, dried chilies and onion. If using shrimp, add another 10 g of garlic and 10 ml of oil here. Stir-fry over medium-low heat until the edges of the onion soften slightly and the smell is clearly fragrant. Then add the sauces you chose, the rock sugar, chicken bouillon powder, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and Shaoxing wine, and stir over low heat until the sugar starts to dissolve.
4. Add the crab pieces, potatoes, beer and water. If using shrimp, add the extra 50–100 ml of beer and 200 ml of water here. Bring to a boil, then simmer over low heat for 12 minutes. Only go on when a chopstick slides into the centre of a potato; if it is still firm, keep simmering, and if the liquid drops too fast, add a little hot water.
5. Add the rice cakes and bell peppers. If using shrimp, add them now too. Cook until the rice cakes are soft and both the shrimp and the thick parts of the crab are completely opaque, then turn to high heat to reduce the sauce, nudging the bottom of the pot gently so the rice cakes do not stick.
6. When the sauce clings to the crab and potatoes and some flowing liquid is still left in the bottom of the pot, sprinkle in the white pepper, toss and serve. The sauces are salty on their own, so taste the liquid after it has reduced.

## Key points

- **The frying oil spits violently**: there is water or ice on the crab (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Thaw frozen crab in the fridge first, then pat dry before coating. Fry in batches, and do not preheat to 200°C (400°F) to make up for a small pot.
- **Rice cakes stick to the bottom and the sauce turns pasty**: the rice cakes sat at the bottom for a long time, and the starch gets more concentrated as the sauce reduces (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add the rice cakes late, nudge them often and stop as soon as the sauce clings.
- **The sauce overpowers the crab**: the salt from several sauces adds up, and it is more noticeable as the liquid reduces (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). A substitute replaces only the sauce it stands in for, so do not add it on top of the original sauce.
