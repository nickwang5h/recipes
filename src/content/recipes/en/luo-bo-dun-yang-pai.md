Daikon and lamb stew is a clear-broth stew. The lamb is stewed until the meat comes off the bone, and the daikon soaks up the broth. The sinew and connective tissue in lamb ribs need long, slow, moist heat to turn into gelatin gradually, which is what lets the meat come off the bone (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Daikon softens fast, so it goes in once the lamb is nearly tender, and the two finish together.

[Blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/) takes away the foam first, and then fresh water is used for a gentle simmer so the broth stays clean. Blanching the daikon separately takes away some of its sharpness, and it does not need to be cooked soft. The Sichuan peppercorns, ginger and green onion only support the lamb, and whole spices are easy to fish out before serving.

## Steps

1. Peel the daikon and cut it into rolling pieces of 3–5 cm. Cut the large green onion into sections and slice the ginger. Have the butcher chop the lamb ribs, look at the cut ends and clear away any loose bone chips.
2. Put the lamb ribs in a pot of cold water with half the green onion and ginger and 15 ml of Shaoxing wine. Cook for about 10 minutes, until the surface of the meat changes colour and the grey-brown foam has all risen. Lift out, wash off the foam and pour away the blanching water.
3. In another pot, heat the daikon with 2 g of rock sugar and cold water. After it boils, cook for about 5 minutes. Lift out and drain for later.
4. Put the lamb ribs in the stewing pot, and add the remaining green onion and ginger, Shaoxing wine, rock sugar, Sichuan peppercorns and the optional angelica root. Pour in the prepared 1000 ml of water, adjusting so it just covers the lamb. Bring to a boil and skim off the new foam.
5. Cover with an ordinary lid and simmer gently for 40–60 minutes, with small bubbles rising slowly at the edge of the pot. Check the lamb. When a chopstick goes into the thicker meat and the sinew still has a little resistance, add the blanched daikon.
6. Cover and simmer for about 20 minutes more, until a chopstick goes through the centre of the daikon and the lamb loosens along the bone. If the broth has clearly reduced, add hot water to near the level of the ingredients and return to a gentle simmer.
7. Taste the broth, add a little salt, fish out the Sichuan peppercorns, green onion and ginger, and serve. If you use a pressure cooker, pressure-cook the lamb for about 15 minutes after it comes up to pressure, release the pressure fully, add the daikon and pressure-cook again for about 10 minutes. Follow the cooker's manual for liquid and fill, and do not add an extra 300 ml of water.

## Key points

- **The lamb is cooked but still tough**: the collagen has not softened through (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Simmer the lamb longer before adding the daikon.
- **The daikon breaks up and the broth is cloudy**: the daikon went in too early, or the whole pot kept boiling hard (see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/)). Stew in stages and stir little once the daikon is in.
- **The broth gets saltier the longer it cooks**: heavy salt went in first and the broth reduced afterwards (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add the salt last, and do not add a big portion at each stage of stewing.
