Water spinach has thin leaves and hollow stems, which suit quick tossing in a hot wok until the leaves are soft and the tender stems are still crisp. The minced garlic is heated briefly in the oil first, which carries its aroma onto the vegetable; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). With light soy sauce and a little sugar, the dish tastes savoury, with a clear garlic flavour.

A quick stir-fry needs the whole handful to touch the hot wok. If the leaves are piled on top, those at the bottom are already soft while those on top have not been heated. Keep lifting and shaking them apart, and the leaves and stems cook more evenly. Wok temperature also depends on how well the vegetable is drained and how full the wok is; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). The fibres in old stems are coarse and tough, so pick them off. That works better than stir-frying for a long time after they are in the wok.

## Steps

1. Pick off the rotten leaves and the old stems that will not snap. Wash the water spinach. Cut the long stems into two or three pieces, shake the leaves apart and drain well in a colander.
2. Separate the cloves from half a head of garlic and mince them. Measure the salt, sugar and light soy sauce and place them by the wok. Water spinach softens quickly once it is in the wok, so the cooking has to be done in one continuous run.
3. Heat the wok, pour in a little vegetable oil and tilt the wok to coat the bottom, then add the rest of the oil. The oil should flow smoothly with ripples on the surface. Avoid heating it until it smokes steadily. For a nonstick pan, preheat over medium heat.
4. Turn to low heat, add the garlic and stir for 10–15 seconds. As soon as the garlic aroma comes out and the garlic is still pale, add the water spinach right away and turn to high heat.
5. Lift the vegetable from the bottom of the wok and keep shaking the leaves apart, so the fluffy handful gradually shrinks. Cook until all the leaves are soft and bright in colour, and the tender stems are still springy when picked up.
6. Keep stir-frying for 15–20 seconds so the stems and leaves heat evenly. Sprinkle in the sugar and some of the salt, drizzle in the light soy sauce and stir-fry for about 10 seconds. Taste a small piece of the cooked vegetable, add the rest of the salt as needed and plate it right away.

## Key points

- **The top is still raw and the bottom is soft**: the vegetable was piled too high and the heat did not pass through evenly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lift from the bottom and shake it apart. For a bigger amount, cook in separate batches instead of cramming it into the wok.
- **The garlic tastes scorched and bitter**: the oil was too hot when frying the garlic, and the fine mince burned quickly (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry the garlic over low heat until fragrant, add the vegetable straight away, and keep the garlic moving with the vegetable off the bottom of the wok.
- **Dull colour after plating and water at the bottom of the plate**: cooked vegetable left in the hot wok keeps softening, and salt also draws water out of vegetables (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Plate it as soon as it is seasoned and eat it hot. The light soy sauce already has saltiness, so add the salt in parts.
