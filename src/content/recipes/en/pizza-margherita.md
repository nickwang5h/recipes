The classic toppings for *pizza Margherita* are tomato, fresh mozzarella, basil and olive oil. The tomato is sweet-tart, the cheese milky, and the basil goes on top. This version uses a home oven and a baking stone, and the dough has a little olive oil: the rim is golden and the base is crisp, unlike the soft crust baked quickly in a very hot oven.

Kneading builds a [gluten](/principles/gluten/) network that can hold gas, and fermentation makes the dough rise. The stone soaks up heat first, then delivers it straight into the base. If wet toppings are piled on too thick, the heat goes first to evaporating water and the base turns soft and gummy (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Stir the yeast into 60 ml of the lukewarm water from the recipe and let stand for about 10 minutes. Add part of the flour and mix, then add the remaining water, the remaining flour, the oil for the dough and the salt in batches, and mix into a soft dough that holds together.
2. Knead on the counter for about 10 minutes, until the surface is smooth and the dough does not tear easily when stretched. Put it in the oiled bowl, cover, and let rise at room temperature for about 3 hours, until about double in volume.
3. Blanch the tomatoes until the skins slip off, peel and seed them, cut into strips, and drain in a sieve until they no longer drip steadily. Cut the cheese into strips and drain it in another sieve. Both need to be done before you top the pizza.
4. Put the baking stone in the oven, set it to 230°C (450°F), and preheat for at least 30 minutes. The stone heats up along with the oven; do not slide a cold stone into a hot oven.
5. Divide the dough in two. Take one piece and press it out from the centre, then gently stretch it over the backs of your hands into a round about 30 cm across, leaving the rim thick. If it springs back when pulled, cover it and let it rest a while before stretching again.
6. Set the dough on a pizza peel lightly dusted with flour, and shake gently to check that it moves. Spread half the tomatoes and cheese over it, sprinkle with a little salt and the optional Parmigiano, add the basil, drizzle with half the topping oil, and slide it straight onto the stone.
7. Bake for about 15–20 minutes, until the rim is golden with brown spots and the base has set when you lift it with the peel. Take it out and eat at once. Top the second pizza just before it goes in, and bake it the same way.

## Key points

- **The dough shrinks back or tears**: the gluten is still tight, or the centre was stretched too thin (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)). Cover and let the dough relax, then stretch outward from the thicker part.
- **The cheese is cooked but the base is still wet and soft**: the stone was not fully hot, or the tomatoes and cheese carried too much water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Drain them well, top in a thin layer, and put the pizza in the oven as soon as it is topped.
- **The dough sticks to the peel**: after topping, water slowly seeps into the flour and the dough (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Check that the bare dough slides before you top it, and top only just before it goes in; too much dusting flour tends to burn and turn bitter.
