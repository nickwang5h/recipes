*Dashi* is the soup base for miso soup, simmered dishes and steamed egg custard. This pot makes **ichiban dashi**, taking flavour from kombu and bonito flakes together. The broth is pale gold and clear, tastes savoury and smells of bonito. Kombu supplies glutamate and bonito supplies inosinate, and together they reinforce each other's [umami](/principles/umami/). The flavour is already complete, so don't add salt while making the broth, and leave the seasoning to the next dish.

The amounts are on the strong side. Kombu needs slow heating, while thin bonito flakes give up their flavour quickly in water and shouldn't boil for a long time together with it. The order of heating decides how long each ingredient stays in hot water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Whether the broth is clear also depends on not squeezing fine fish crumbs into it while straining.

## Steps

1. Lightly wipe the grit off the kombu with a slightly damp cloth. Leave the white powder on the surface and don't rinse or brush it off. Put the kombu in a pot with 1000 ml of cold water, so it is submerged.
2. Turn on low heat and heat slowly, uncovered, for about 10 minutes. When fine bubbles start to appear at the edge of the pot and the water is about to boil, lift out the kombu. It should be soft by now. If the centre is still hard, turn the heat even lower and steep a little longer, keeping the surface from rolling.
3. Bring the broth in the pot to a boil, add the 60 ml of cold water you set aside and put in the bonito flakes at once. Gently wet the flakes floating on the surface, and avoid stirring them up and breaking them with a spoon.
4. Turn off the heat as soon as the broth starts to boil again and move the pot off the burner. Let it stand for about 1 minute until the bonito flakes sink, then skim off the foam on the surface.
5. Line a fine strainer with a clean damp cheesecloth and strain the broth slowly, letting it drip through on its own. Don't wring the cloth or press the bonito flakes. The strained broth can go straight into [miso soup](/recipes/miso-shiru/) or [chawanmushi](/recipes/chawanmushi/). If you won't use it right away, cool it quickly and refrigerate.

## Key points

- **Kombu flavour is strong and the broth feels slimy**: the kombu boiled in the water for too long and gave up too much gelatinous matter and seaweed taste. Next time heat slowly and lift it out just before boiling (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Cloudy broth, harsh bonito flavour**: the bonito was cooked for a long time, broken up by stirring or squeezed while straining. Steep briefly in the right order, then let the broth drip through on its own. Fine crumbs that have already got in can be strained again through damp cheesecloth.
- **Salty but not savoury**: salt can't make up for missing umami. Check the amounts and freshness of the two dried ingredients first, and only then season the finished dish with salt (see [umami](/principles/umami/)).
