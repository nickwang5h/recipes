The base flavour of *cánh gà chiên nước mắm* is fish sauce, garlic and sugar. The wings are fried to a thin crust first and only coated with the sauce at the end, so the first taste is salty-sweet, followed by the toasty flavour of the skin. Fish sauce supplies both [salt](/principles/salt/) and [umami](/principles/umami/), so there is no need to layer soy sauce or more salt into the marinade.

Steeping the garlic in water and straining out the solids carries the garlic flavour into the marinade without letting minced garlic scorch in the hot oil. Only a thin layer of rice flour is kept, and the crust forms by the dehydration in hot oil described in [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/). What is reduced at the end is the sugar and fish sauce liquid; stop the heat when it is thick enough to cling to the wings.

## Steps

1. Mix the garlic paste with 45 ml of water and steep for 5 minutes, until the water clearly smells of garlic. Strain the garlic water through a fine sieve, press the solids and discard them. Stir in the fish sauce, both sugars and the white pepper, and stir for about 1 minute, until the sugar is mostly dissolved.
2. Dry the wings with paper towels, put them in a food bag with the marinade, rub to coat, seal and refrigerate for 2 hours, until the surface is evenly tinted light brown. Take them out and drain for 5 minutes, keeping all the marinade in a separate wide pot, then blot the film of liquid off the wings with paper again.
3. Pour the oil into a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 6–8 minutes, until the oil reaches 175°C (350°F) and its surface has not yet begun to smoke. Thinly coat the wings in rice flour piece by piece and shake off the loose flour. Coat only the batch about to go in, and leave the rest to be coated for the next batch.
4. Fry the wings in 3 batches, about 8–10 minutes per batch, turning partway through, until the crust is golden and the bubbles have lessened and the centre of the thickest part reaches 74°C (165°F), with the probe kept away from the bone. Lift each batch onto a rack and drain for 1 minute. Before the next batch goes in, wait about 1–2 minutes for the oil to return to 175°C (350°F).
5. The marinade has touched raw chicken, so it must be cooked through. Add the chili garlic sauce to the wide pot holding the marinade and heat over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the whole pot boils, then keep it boiling for 3 minutes, until the liquid is slightly thick and the bubbles turn dense.
6. Add the fried wings and toss over medium heat for about 1–2 minutes, until every piece has a sticky, glossy thin coat and almost no flowing liquid is left in the bottom of the pan. Turn off the heat, sprinkle on the optional green part of the green onion, and plate right away.

## Key points

- **Soft crust, greasy surface**: too many wings went in at once, the oil recovered slowly, and the rice flour coating could not dry out (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Fry the wings in batches and spread the fried ones on a rack, not piled in a bowl.
- **Dark brown outside, meat by the bone not cooked**: the sugary marinade was not blotted dry, or the oil was too hot, so the outside coloured first (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Dry them before coating, and measure the centre temperature in the thickest drumette.
- **Sauce is bitter and the wings too salty**: it was left on the heat after drying out, so the sugar kept caramelizing and the salt concentrated as the water evaporated (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Take it off the heat as soon as the sauce turns sticky, and serve promptly.
