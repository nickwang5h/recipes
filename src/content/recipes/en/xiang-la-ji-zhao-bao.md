Spicy chicken feet claypot is sticky, soft and tender, with the spicy sauce clinging to the skin. That stickiness comes from the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) in the skin and tendons: the feet must first be cooked in moist heat until the skin and tendons are soft, and only then go back in the pan to be coated in chili sauce. If they are only fried until the surface browns, the tendons inside are still tight and cannot be chewed. Keeping a bowl of the cooking liquid to reduce the sauce also brings the gelatin that cooked out back into the dish.

Snip the toenails off the chicken feet, halve them and keep the bones; they cook faster and are easier to pick up. Starting in cold water and skimming off the foam is the method from [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/), and then you keep cooking until soft. Ginger, garlic and chilies are fried separately in another pan, so the spicy aroma is clearer.

## Steps

1. Snip the toenails off the chicken feet, cut them in half along the palm and wash. Divide the ginger into slices and mince, mince the garlic, slice the bird's eye chilies into rings, and tie one green onion in a knot and cut the other into sections.
2. Put the chicken feet and 1000 ml of cold water in a pot, with enough water to cover the feet. Add the green onion knot, ginger slices and Shaoxing wine, and bring to a boil, skimming the foam until the surface stops producing grey foam.
3. Add the bay leaves, star anise, 15 ml of light soy sauce and 5 ml of dark soy sauce, cover and turn to low heat, keeping a gentle bubble, and cook for about 30 minutes. Turn them over midway, and top up with hot water when the level drops and a large part of the feet is exposed.
4. Lift out one to test. The skin should be soft, and chopsticks should pass fairly easily through the skin and tendon of the palm, with the joints not yet falling apart. Also check the thickest part; the centre must reach 74°C (165°F). If the skin and tendons are still tough, keep cooking over low heat. When they are ready, lift them out and ladle out 200 ml of the cooking liquid to keep.
5. Heat the oil in a wok and stir-fry the minced ginger, garlic and bird's eye chilies over low heat. When you smell them, add the chili flakes (optional). The garlic should still be light yellow, and the chilies must not blacken.
6. Add the remaining light soy sauce and dark soy sauce, the oyster sauce and the five spice powder. Stir until the seasonings loosen and you smell the soy, then add the chicken feet at once and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes.
7. Pour in the reserved cooking liquid and cook over medium heat, gently pushing the feet around so each side picks up sauce. Reduce until the sauce sticks to the feet and a little still runs slowly down when you tilt the pan. Taste before deciding whether to add salt.
8. Add the chicken bouillon powder (optional), sprinkle on the green onion sections, toss and turn off the heat and plate. Do not toss hard while reducing the sauce; the softened skin comes away from the bones easily.

## Key points

- **Feet are cooked but cannot be bitten through**: the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) has not softened all the way. Keep the liquid and cook on low heat, and check whether the skin and tendon of the palm is soft rather than watching only for 30 minutes.
- **Spicy sauce tastes bitter**: the minced garlic and chilies burnt in hot oil (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry over low heat, and add the chicken feet and liquid soon after the soy sauce goes in.
- **Too salty after reducing**: the salt in the cooking liquid, soy sauce and oyster sauce concentrates as the water evaporates (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add salt only after tasting at the end, and do not add it twice.
