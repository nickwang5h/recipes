The flavour of Guizhou chili chicken comes from pounded, moist ciba chili: a deep chili aroma, chicken you can bite apart, and potatoes that are soft but hold their shape. This version adds potatoes and beer, and fries first, then braises. The chili has to be stir-fried slowly in oil before its aroma comes out (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

The chicken pieces are fried first, so the surface loses water and develops flavour; then moist heat braising softens the connective tissue (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Fry until the surface is light golden, then move to the braise; it is done when the meat near the bone and at the joints has gone soft.

## Steps

1. Trim the chicken pieces, removing any visible bone fragments and patting the surface dry; wipe the potato strips dry too. Have the ginger slices, whole garlic cloves and garlic shoot sections ready, and set out the seasonings before cooking, so the chili does not sit scorching in the pan.
2. Fry the potatoes in batches in a deep pot. Leave room in the pot; the oil plus the food must not rise to the rim. Once the potatoes go in, fine bubbles should keep rising; fry until the surface is golden and the edges are set, then lift out and drain.
3. Wait for the oil temperature to recover, then fry the chicken in batches. Each batch is a loose single layer, with half the ginger slices and the Sichuan peppercorns added. Fry until the surface is light golden and the bubbles are clearly fewer than when the chicken went in, then lift out. Take out the ginger and peppercorns when they darken.
4. Pour the frying oil into a heatproof container and leave only about 60 ml in the pan. Over medium-low heat, add the ciba chili paste, chili bean paste and the remaining ginger, and stir constantly until the oil turns red and the raw smell of the chili fades.
5. Stir in the chicken pieces and dark soy sauce and coat evenly, then pour the beer down the side of the pan and add water until the chicken is just covered. Add the bay leaves, bring to a boil, then turn to low, cover and braise for 10 minutes, turning once in the middle so the chili does not sink to the bottom.
6. Add the potato strips and whole garlic cloves and braise for another 20 minutes. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece of chicken, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) it is done. If the meat is still tough, add a little hot water and keep braising until the meat near the bone bites apart easily.
7. Uncover and toss, bring the liquid back to a boil, then add the garlic shoots. Stir-fry until the garlic shoots are just cooked and the red chili oil clings to the chicken, then plate, leaving a little liquid and taking care not to break up the potatoes.

## Key points

- **Chili turns black and bitter**: moist chili loses water and scorches easily on the bottom of the pan (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Use medium-low heat and keep stirring; if it already smells burnt, switch to a fresh pan and stir-fry the chili again, and don't scrape the burnt bottom into the dish.
- **Chicken is hard outside and tough inside**: it was fried too long, or braised too little (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/) and [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Measure the centre temperature before serving, then check whether the meat near the bone has softened.
- **A thick layer of oil floats on the dish**: too much frying oil was left in (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Drain the chicken well, and don't keep all the frying oil; keep only the base oil for stir-frying the paste.
