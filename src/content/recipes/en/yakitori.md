Yakitori comes either salted or with sauce. *Negima*, chicken thigh with green onion, is the common one: pieces of meat and green onion are threaded alternately onto skewers and brushed with a sweet-and-salty tare cooked from soy sauce, mirin and sake. The chicken is grilled fragrant and the white part of the green onion turns soft and sweet.

Charcoal, or the top element of an oven, first delivers heat to the surface, then conducts it toward the centre of the meat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). When the surface loses moisture, the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) sets in, and the brushed sauce adds sweet-salty flavour and gloss. The sauce contains sugar and scorches easily, so grill the meat first and brush the sauce after. At home, under the broiler, what matters is the distance between the food and the element.

## Steps

1. If using bamboo skewers, soak them in 500 ml of water in a shallow dish for 30 minutes, until the skewers have absorbed moisture. Metal skewers can be used as they are. Cut the green onions into sections and set aside the dark green part of 1 onion.
2. Put the soy sauce, mirin, sake, 60 ml of water, brown sugar and the reserved green onion greens in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and the liquid boils. Turn the heat to low and simmer uncovered for about 30 minutes, until only about 120 ml is left and the sauce thinly coats the back of a spoon when you lift it.
3. Remove the green onion greens and divide the tare between two bowls, about 80 ml and 40 ml. The larger bowl is for brushing during grilling, and the smaller one is kept for finishing. Have a clean brush ready.
4. Pat the chicken dry and fold thin pieces so the pieces are of similar thickness. Thread each skewer alternately with 3 pieces of chicken and 2 sections of green onion, pressed snugly together but not crowded into a thick lump, to make about 12 skewers.
5. Line a baking sheet with foil, set a high-heat-safe metal rack on top and brush it with 1 tsp of oil. Arrange the skewers in a single layer and wrap the exposed ends of the bamboo skewers in foil. Turn the broiler to high and preheat for 3–5 minutes, until the top element glows red; the meat should be about 20 cm from the element.
6. Grill the skewers for 6 minutes first, until the surface is dry and the edges are starting to colour. Brush the upward-facing side thinly with sauce from the larger bowl, turn the skewers, brush the other side, and keep grilling for 3–4 minutes, until the sauce forms fine bubbles and brown spots appear on the meat.
7. Turn again, brush thinly with tare, and grill for 1–2 minutes, until the surface is glossy and the centre of the thickest piece of chicken reaches 74°C (165°F). If it hasn't, grill 1 minute more; if the colour comes too fast, lower the rack. After plating, brush the skewers thinly with the tare reserved in the small bowl using a clean brush, and serve right away.

## Key points

- **Sauce scorched black and bitter**: the sauce went on too early, too thick, or too close to the heat. Grill the meat first, then brush thinly and lower the rack. See [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **Coloured outside, raw inside**: the pieces vary in thickness, so the surface heats faster than the centre. Cut evenly, spread out thick clumps, and measure the centre of the thickest piece from the side. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Chicken is dry and tough**: it stayed under the heat chasing colour after reaching doneness. Take it off once it is done, and finish the roast flavour and brushing as early as you can. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
