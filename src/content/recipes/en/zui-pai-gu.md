Drunken pork ribs is a Fuzhou dish. The ribs are fried and tossed while hot in a sauce mixed beforehand, so the crust is crisp and the flavour sweet, sour and garlicky. This is a home version with fish sauce and egg yolk added, and the sauce is mostly ketchup, sugar, vinegar and minced garlic. The sourness makes the sweet and salty tastes clearer (see [acid](/principles/acid/)), and a thin coat is enough.

Sweet potato starch first [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) from the water and heat on the surface of the meat, then keeps losing water and forms a crust. The first fry cooks the meat, and the second fry dries the outer layer (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Leave the ribs uncovered after frying and do not soak them in the sauce, so the crust stays crisp.

## Steps

1. Check the ribs for bone chips, pat the surface dry with paper towels and put them in a large bowl. Choose small, even pieces of rib, and fry thicker pieces in a separate batch.
2. Add 2 ml of fish sauce, the egg yolk and 30 g of sweet potato starch, and mix. The coating should be a thin batter that clings to the meat, with no large lumps of starch falling off when you lift a piece. If the meat is very wet, pat it dry first and do not keep adding starch.
3. Add the frying oil to a small, deep pot and heat until a rib put in keeps bubbling steadily and the oil is not smoking. Put the ribs in one at a time, apart from each other, and fry in batches. Right after they go in, wait for the coating to set, then gently separate any that have stuck together.
4. After that, turn about every 30 seconds and fry until the outer layer is light golden. Put a thermometer into the thickest piece, away from the bone, and lift the ribs out only at 71°C (160°F). If the colour is already dark but the centre has not reached that, lower the heat and keep frying.
5. Drain the ribs and let them rest for 1 minute, while you skim the starch crumbs out of the oil. When the oil temperature has recovered, fry again in batches until the outer layer is golden and feels like a hard shell to the touch, then lift out and spread them out to let the steam escape. Do not fry them to a dark brown.
6. In a clean small bowl, mix the minced garlic, black vinegar, sugar, the remaining 3 ml of fish sauce, ketchup, chopped green onion and toasted sesame seeds. Stir until the sugar is mostly dissolved, then slowly add 5 ml of hot oil scooped from the fryer and stir into the sauce.
7. Put the fried ribs in a clean large bowl, pour on the sauce while they are hot and toss quickly. The sauce only needs to coat the surface. Toss evenly and plate right away. Do not use the bowl that held the raw ribs for the fried ones.

## Key points

- **Golden outside, undercooked meat by the bone**: the crust browns faster than the heat reaches the centre (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Fry in batches by thickness and measure the centre. The second fry only crisps the crust; the meat has to be cooked in the first fry.
- **Thick crust, sticky and soft inside**: the meat surface was too wet or the starch not mixed evenly (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Pat dry and coat thinly, and spread the ribs out after frying, so steam is not trapped back in the crust.
- **Goes soft soon after tossing**: too much sauce, or the ribs sat in it too long, so the crisp crust took on water again (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). Mix the sauce first, toss the ribs quickly once drained, and eat while hot.
