Steamed oysters use the shell to hold the meat and its juices, with minced garlic and ginger shreds for aroma and only a small dab of light soy sauce. Steam condenses on the cooler shell and carries heat into the oyster meat (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Lay the oysters in a single layer, and let the steamer build up plenty of steam before you start timing.

The oysters are steamed whole first, then opened and topped: the first steaming cooks them through, and the second lets the garlic and ginger heat up and release their aroma. Oyster meat sets once cooked, and further steaming shrinks it and releases water (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)), so serve them soon after steaming.

## Steps

1. Scrub the oyster shells with a brush and rinse off the grit under cold water. Pick out and discard any with broken shells, and any that are open and do not close when tapped. Drain the scrubbed oysters and keep them refrigerated, taking them out only when the water in the pot has boiled.
2. Add about 1000 ml water to the steamer, with the water level below the steaming tray. Once steam is coming out steadily, arrange the oysters in a single layer, rounded side down and flat side up, leaving gaps between the shells for steam to pass, and cover.
3. Keep up a full head of steam and steam in the shell for 4–9 minutes, choosing the time by size; the shells will open as they steam. Discard any that still do not open after steaming, and do not pry them open to eat.
4. Turn off the heat and lift the oysters out with heatproof tongs onto a stable surface. Hold the bottom shell with a dry heatproof glove and carefully remove the top shell, keeping the meat and juices in the shell. Open the lid away from you so the steam does not rush into your face.
5. Divide the minced garlic and ginger shreds evenly over the oyster meat. Return them to the steamer that is already boiling, cover and steam for about 3.5 minutes more, until the garlic smells fragrant and the meat is completely set; for especially large oysters, do not take them out by time alone.
6. Take them out and plate, drizzle about 1 ml light soy sauce on each and sprinkle with chopped green onion. Keep the rounded shell down so the juices do not run off, and eat hot, the meat together with a little of the juice in the shell.

## Key points

- **Some cooked, some raw in the same pot**: not enough steam, oysters overlapping, or timing started from cold water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Bring the water to a boil first, lay them in a single layer with gaps, and then follow the in-shell steaming time.
- **Shrivelled meat and a lot of liquid**: steaming continued after the oysters were already cooked through, so the protein contracted (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook oysters of similar size together, and take them out as soon as both steamings are done.
- **Oyster flavour hidden by garlic and soy sauce**: too much garlic in each oyster, or soy sauce poured into a pool, covers up the natural juices. Divide the garlic evenly and let it add aroma (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)); start light soy sauce at 1 ml per oyster.
- **Eating them as soon as the shell opens**: an open shell does not mean cooked through. Both stages, in-shell steaming and steaming again with garlic and ginger, must be completed (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
