*Karaage* has a thin, crisp coating, and under it is skin-on chicken thigh with the flavour of soy sauce and ginger carried all the way in. The method is to season the chicken first with sake, soy sauce and ginger juice, then coat it in a thin layer of flour and fry it.

The flavour comes first from the marinade, and the crispness from the frying. [Salt](/principles/salt/) moves into the outer layer of the meat along the concentration gradient; with evenly cut pieces, a short marinade seasons them consistently. Draining off the extra liquid before coating lets the crust lose water in the hot oil and turn crisp (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)). A thick, wet batter slows this down.

## Steps

1. Remove the cartilage, tendons and any large pieces of extra fat from the chicken thighs, and keep the skin. Cut into pieces about 3 cm across and score the thick parts so the pieces are of similar thickness; fold any loose skin back over the meat.
2. Grate the ginger to a fine pulp, squeeze out the stated amount of juice, and mix it with the sake, usukuchi soy sauce and green onion. Add the chicken and toss, cover and marinate in the refrigerator for 30 minutes, turning once partway so every side meets the marinade.
3. Move the chicken to a sieve and drain off the marinade; blot gently any folds where liquid has collected, but don't wipe off all the flavour on the surface. Discard the marinade, and wash anything that touched raw meat before it touches cooked food.
4. Spread the flour on a wide plate, roll the pieces in it in batches, and shake off the loose flour gently. Each piece should have a thin layer of flour, with the outline of the meat still visible, not a thick white lump. Rest for 2–3 minutes, until the coating has softened slightly and is clinging to the meat.
5. Heat the oil to 170°C (340°F) and slide in the pieces in batches, leaving gaps between them. After the coating has just set, gently separate any pieces that stick together, and turn them so all sides heat evenly; this usually takes about 4–6 minutes. When the oil temperature drops, let it recover before adding the next batch.
6. Fry the pieces to a light golden brown with a crisp surface, until they float and the bubbles die down, then check the temperature: insert the thermometer into the thickest piece, and take them out only at 74°C (165°F). Drain first, blot off the surface oil briefly, then spread on a wire rack to let the steam escape.

## Key points

- **Coating turns into a sticky thick lump**: the marinade wasn't drained, and the flour took in too much liquid. Remove any pooled liquid before coating, pat off the loose flour, and coat only the batch that is about to go in the pot. See [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/).
- **Dark brown outside and raw inside**: the oil is too hot, and the soy-containing surface browns faster than the centre cooks. Keep the oil near 170°C (340°F), score the thick pieces first, and don't rush doneness with higher heat. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Too salty to eat**: usukuchi soy sauce is light in colour but not low in salt, and a longer marinade lets more salt into the meat. Don't add extra salt, and don't marinate longer than the time in the steps. See [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/).
