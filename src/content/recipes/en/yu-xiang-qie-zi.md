In fish-fragrant eggplant, the tender eggplant flesh and the sweet, sour and spicy sauce set each other off. This one has a little ground pork: the eggplant strips are fried soft in hot oil, simmered briefly in the doubanjiang sauce, and finally [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) thickens the sauce into a thin glaze so each strip is coated with flavour.

Eggplant flesh is spongy and soaks up oil easily, so drain it as soon as it is fried. When frying, go by [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/) and not by how high the flame is: too much at once drops the oil temperature sharply, and the eggplant stays hard for a long time. Stir-fry the doubanjiang, ginger and garlic in moderately hot oil, not over the high heat you use for frying the eggplant.

## Steps

1. Cut the eggplant into strips of even thickness, rinse and wipe dry. Break up the ground pork. Chop the green onion white, ginger and garlic, and dice the optional chili. Keep the green part of the green onion for the end.
2. Mix the sugar, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, vinegar and optional MSG into a sauce. Separately, stir 3 g of starch into 100 ml of cold water. Keep the sauce and the starch slurry apart, so you can adjust the thickness of the glaze at the end by how much liquid there is.
3. Put 300 ml of oil in a smaller, deep pot and heat to about 180°C (350°F), with the thermometer probe not touching the bottom. Add the eggplant strips in batches. Check at about 2 minutes, and lift out when the edges are lightly golden and the centre is soft through; thicker strips may take 3–4 minutes.
4. Spread the strips out to drain, and do not pile them in a deep bowl where they will steam. Wait for the oil to come back to temperature between batches; if the oil keeps smoking, take it off the heat to cool. Once the frying oil has cooled a little, transfer it out and keep 30 ml for stir-frying.
5. Put 15 ml of that oil in the pan and stir-fry the ground pork over medium heat, breaking it up, until there is no pink left and all the raw clumps are separated. Remove.
6. Add the remaining 15 ml of oil and stir-fry the doubanjiang, green onion white, ginger, garlic and optional chili until the oil turns red and the aroma comes up. Return the pork and toss lightly.
7. Add 100 ml of water and bring to a boil. Add the eggplant strips and the sauce, push gently so the sauce coats every side, and simmer briefly until the eggplant is soft and the sauce is no longer watery. Leave a sauce that still flows in the pan, and do not break up the strips.
8. Stir the starch slurry and drizzle it in portions into the boiling sauce. Push gently, and stop adding when the glaze turns glossy and thinly coats the strips. Taste, add salt if needed, add the green onion green part and plate at once.

## Key points

- **Greasy eggplant that takes forever to soften**: too much went in at once and the [oil temperature dropped](/principles/oil-temperature/), so the frying dragged on. Fry in batches, drain right after lifting out, and keep only the base oil given in the steps for the sauce.
- **Sauce like paste**: all the slurry went in and the liquid reduced too (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Add it in portions, stop when it clings to the eggplant, and throw away any slurry you do not use.
- **Bitter doubanjiang and garlic**: the aromatics scorched (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry the paste over medium heat and add the water as soon as the aroma comes up; if the base is already black, start again.
