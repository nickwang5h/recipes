Samgyetang is a soup in which small whole chickens are stuffed with glutinous rice and cooked with ginseng, garlic and jujubes. The chicken is still whole when it comes to the table, and when you open the cavity you can spoon out soft rice that has absorbed the broth. The skin and the connective tissue at the joints soften gradually in moist heat, and the broth turns thick and silky (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).

The key to the stuffing is leaving space. Glutinous rice absorbs water and gelatinizes with heat, so it expands (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)); if the cavity is packed too full, the rice in the centre is hard to cook evenly. The ginseng slowly gives up its aroma in the broth, and only a few jujubes go in, to keep the light chicken broth taste (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Rinse the glutinous rice, add 500 ml of cold water and soak for 1 hour, until the grains are plump with water and a fingernail can split one, then drain. Brush the dirt off the ginseng and rinse the jujubes.
2. Take out the bag of innards from each chicken cavity, trim off the extra fat at the opening and wipe out any leftover juices in the cavity with paper towels. Fold the neck skin of each chicken into the top opening, then fill with the rice and 2 cloves of garlic each, leaving about a quarter of the cavity empty.
3. Cross the two legs of each chicken, tie them with cotton kitchen string and close the opening of the cavity. Put the chickens in a narrow, deep pot with the ginseng, jujubes, ginger, white parts of the green onion and the remaining 12 cloves of garlic, and pour in 1920 ml of water.
4. Heat over medium-high for about 10 minutes, until the surface bubbles steadily, and skim off the foam. Cover and keep cooking for a total of 30 minutes at medium-high, at a steady boil. If the water falls below halfway up the chickens, add 100 ml from the reserved hot water first.
5. Turn to medium, cover and cook for 40 minutes, until the leg joints are loose and a chopstick passes easily through the leg meat. Measure the thickest part of the thigh, away from the bone, for 82°C (180°F), and the centre of the rice in the cavity for 74°C (165°F). If not there yet, cook 5 minutes more.
6. Turn off the heat, cover and rest for 10 minutes, then untie the strings. Pull open the centre of the rice; the grains should be sticky and soft with no white hard core. If still raw, add the rest of the reserved hot water and keep cooking over low heat for 10 minutes, until no white core is left in the rice.
7. Divide the chicken and broth among bowls and sprinkle with the green parts of the green onion. Serve salt and black pepper separately, so everyone seasons their own broth. The chicken should pull off the frame with a gentle nudge, and the rice should still sit mostly near the cavity.

## Key points

- **Rice is raw in the centre but sticky on the outside**: the stuffing was packed too tight or the chicken was too big, so hot water cannot reach the centre of the rice. Leave space to suit the small chicken, and check the innermost grains (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **The meat falls apart but the joints are still hard to pull apart**: the boil was too violent, so the outer layer was shredded first. Turn to low partway through so the connective tissue softens gradually, and judge by both the joints and the centre temperature (see [Collagen and Gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).
- **Broth is so sweet that it covers the chicken flavour**: too many jujubes. Use the stated number and let the aroma of garlic and ginseng blend into the broth (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
