Gamja jorim is a common Korean side dish, with potato chunks coated in a slightly sweet soy sauce, eaten warm with rice. The home method cooks the potatoes through with a small amount of liquid, then reduces it with the lid off. The plate should show whole chunks that bite through with no hard core.

The starch in the potato undergoes [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with water and heat, which is what softens the centre. The outside cooks first and the centre follows (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The final gloss comes mostly from the sugar and sauce concentrating, so reduce the sauce just when the potatoes are cooked through, so they are not boiled apart as the sauce reduces.

## Steps

1. Cut the peeled potatoes into cubes of about 2.5 cm, rinse the cut surfaces with cold water to wash off loose starch, and drain. Keep the pieces as even as you can, and pick out the small bits to use another time, so large pieces are not still hard while small ones fall apart.
2. Stir the soy sauce, sugar, garlic and 180 ml of water together. The sugar need not dissolve completely, but there must not be a pile of dry sugar left at the bottom of the bowl; when you pour it into the pan, bring the sugar from the bottom along.
3. Put the vegetable oil and potatoes in a frying pan and stir-fry over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, until the cut faces are slightly translucent and the surface is evenly coated in oil. Push gently with the spatula and keep them pale; there is no need to fry a deep brown crust.
4. Pour in the sauce and cook over high heat for about 1–2 minutes, until it bubbles at the edge and in the middle. Cover, turn to medium heat and braise for 5–6 minutes, until a chopstick goes into the centre of a large piece with a little resistance; the liquid should still be flowing in the bottom of the pan.
5. Uncover and cook over medium heat for about 3 more minutes, until a chopstick goes straight through and the liquid is slightly thick and clings to the potatoes. If the centre is still hard, add water and cook covered for another 2–3 minutes; if they are cooked through but there is a lot of liquid, shake the pan gently to reduce it and stir with the spatula as little as possible.
6. Turn off the heat, stir in the toasted sesame oil and scatter on the toasted sesame seeds. Serve with the layer of flowing glossy sauce left in the pan. The potatoes will take up some of the sauce after leaving the heat; when warm they should be soft, with the sweet and salty clearly distinct.

## Key points

- **Corners break down into mush**: floury potatoes cooked a long time or stirred repeatedly fall apart on the outside first. Choose a variety that holds its shape better, and shake the pan instead of stirring hard once the lid is off (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Sauce has reduced dry but the centre is still hard**: the heat was too high or the chunks too thick, so water evaporated before the heat reached the centre. Add water and cook covered, then reduce once they are cooked through (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Gets saltier as it reduces**: as the water decreases, the salt in the soy sauce stays behind. Stop when the sauce clings to the potatoes, and do not wait until the bottom of the pan is dry and sticky; add no more salt to this dish (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)).
