*Gai tod Hat Yai* comes from Hat Yai in southern Thailand. Garlic, cilantro stems and white pepper are pounded into a marinade, bone-in chicken legs are coated in a thin rice flour batter, and the fried chicken is served with crispy shallots and [sticky rice](/recipes/khao-niao/). The shallots lose water in the oil and turn golden, giving a sweet fragrance, and they are a defining feature of this fried chicken.

The [starch in the rice flour gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) with heat, and the outer layer then keeps drying out until it turns crisp. The batter must be thin, and the [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/) after the chicken goes in must stay steady, to give the meat next to the bone enough time to cook through. Pounding the aromatics fine helps them cling to the chicken more evenly (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

## Steps

1. Cut a slash about 1 cm deep on each side of the bone in the thickest part of each leg. Pound the white pepper fine, then pound it with the garlic and cilantro stems into a paste. Stir in the sugar, light soy sauce, fish sauce and the marinade water, until the sugar dissolves.
2. Put the chicken legs in the marinade, cover and refrigerate for 4 hours. Turn them over at 2 hours so the slashes and the skin pick up the liquid. Take them out 30 minutes before frying, until the pieces can be separated and are easy to coat with batter.
3. Toss the sliced shallots with the salt and leave for 10 minutes, until beads of water show on the surface. Spread them on paper towels and press to soak up the water. Keep the slices apart so they do not stick into a wet clump.
4. Put the oil and one shallot slice in a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 5 minutes, until fine bubbles keep rising around the slice. Turn to low heat, add the rest of the shallots and fry, stirring, for 5–8 minutes, until light golden and the bubbles have clearly slowed. Lift them out and spread them to cool for 5 minutes, until crisp, and strain the bits out of the oil.
5. Put the chicken with its marinade in a large bowl and first stir in 90 g of the rice flour. The batter should coat the skin thinly and drip slowly when lifted. If it is too thin, add more from the remaining 30 g of rice flour a little at a time; if it is too thick, loosen it with the reserved water, 1 tbsp at a time.
6. Heat the frying oil for about 5 minutes, until it reaches 190°C (375°F). Add the chicken legs in two batches, adjusting the heat to keep the oil at about 150°C (300°F) after they go in. Fry each batch for 10–12 minutes, turning every 2 minutes, until the skin is golden brown and the surface bubbles have subsided. Put a thermometer into the thickest part next to the bone: the chicken is done at 79°C (175°F). If it has not reached that, fry for another 2 minutes and test again.
7. Lift the chicken onto a rack to drain and rest for 5 minutes, until the surface no longer drips oil and the steam has eased. Spread it out on a plate, scatter the fried shallots over it, and eat while crisp.

## Key points

- **Coating is thick and hard, and the inner layer is gummy**: too much rice flour was added and the batter layer cannot dry through. Keep the batter thin enough to drip (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Skin is dark brown but the meat by the bone is not cooked**: the oil stayed too hot after the chicken went in. Bring it down to about 150°C (300°F) so the heat can reach the inside, and confirm with the centre temperature (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Shallots are bitter and black**: they were lifted out too late, and the residual heat kept scorching the thin slices. Lift and spread them as soon as they are light golden and the bubbles have lessened (see [Oil Temperature and Deep Frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
