Luosong tang is a thick red soup. Tomato and beef form the base, with potatoes, cabbage and root vegetables simmered slowly together, so a spoonful brings up tender pieces of meat and a rich broth. It also has red sausage and a lot of parsley, so the herbal flavour of the parsley is noticeable. The beef is browned first, so the flavour of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) melts into the soup. Slow stewing then lets moist heat turn the collagen gradually into gelatin (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)).

The canned tomatoes are used with their juice, which gives the soup its acidity, colour and body. The beef stock really goes into the pot, and the red sausage is added late so it keeps its smoky flavour and its shape. The pot makes several bowls. When you adjust the salt, count the saltiness the stock and sausage already have.

## Steps

1. Dice the onion, carrot and tender parsley stems small, and roughly chop the parsley leaves. Cut the potatoes and the cooked red sausage into pieces of about 2 cm, and tear the cabbage into small pieces, discarding the thick stem. Cut the beef into pieces, pat dry, mix in 1 g of salt and the black pepper and leave for about 5 minutes.
2. Heat the vegetable oil in a frying pan over medium heat and lay the beef in, leaving gaps between the pieces. Fry until the side against the pan is browned before turning. Take the beef out once the surface is browned. If the bottom of the pan turns black, lower the heat promptly.
3. Put the olive oil in the soup pot and add the onion, carrot and parsley stems, stir-frying until the onion is translucent. Add the tomato paste and stir through, then add the canned tomatoes with their juice, crushing the large pieces of tomato.
4. Add the beef, potatoes, 500 ml of beef stock and 500 ml of water, so the liquid roughly covers the ingredients. Bring to a boil, then turn to low, cover and keep at a gentle simmer for about 1 hour. Stir from the bottom along the way, watch the water level, and add hot water if it is short.
5. Add the cabbage and the red sausage and cook for about 30 minutes more. Pull at the beef with a fork to see whether it comes apart easily, whether the potatoes have a hard core and whether the cabbage is soft. If the beef is still tough, keep stewing until the meat and vegetables are soft enough.
6. Stir in the parsley leaves and cook briefly until the leaves wilt, then taste the soup before adding salt. Taste the red sausage with the soup. When the saltiness is right, turn off the heat. Once it is seasoned, do not reduce it further.

## Key points

- **The beef is grey and lacks browned flavour**: the surface was too wet, or the pan too crowded, and steam got in the way of the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/). Dry the beef and brown it in batches before stewing.
- **The beef is cooked but still tough**: the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) have not softened. Keep enough liquid, keep stewing on low heat, and judge when to stop by the thickest piece.
- **The soup gets saltier the longer it cooks**: the stock, the sausage and the added salt stack up, and reducing concentrates the [salt](/principles/salt/). Taste at the end before adding salt. If it is too salty, dilute with unsalted hot water.
