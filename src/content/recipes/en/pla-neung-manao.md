*Pla neung manao* is a common Thai lime steamed fish. A whole white-fleshed fish is steamed, then covered with a dressing of fish sauce, lime, minced garlic and chili, and served with cilantro and leafy celery. The first taste is the fresh sourness of lime and the smell of garlic, with fish sauce for savouriness and sugar to soften the sourness a little. It goes very well with rice.

As soon as fish flesh heats, its proteins unfold and set, the translucent flesh turns white, and it separates into flakes along the muscle segments (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). When it looks like that, take it away from the steam; steam longer and the flesh dries out. Add the lime juice only after the heat is off, so the sourness is easy to judge and the aroma does not escape. For when to add the garlic and cilantro, see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/) and [acid](/principles/acid/).

## Steps

1. Wipe the fish and its cavity clean. Cut 3 diagonal slashes on each side, down to the thick flesh but not through the backbone. Tuck the bruised lemongrass into the cavity and lay the fish flat in a deep heatproof dish that will catch the juices.
2. Add 1500 ml of water to the steamer and bring to a boil over medium-high heat for about 8 minutes, until the water boils steadily and there is plenty of steam. Put in the dish, cover, keep the water boiling, and steam for 8–12 minutes. Check at the thickest slash: take the fish out when the flesh turns opaque and flakes easily; if any flesh is still translucent, steam 2 minutes more and check again.
3. While the fish steams, put the chicken stock and palm sugar in a small pot and heat over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, until the stock boils and the sugar is fully dissolved. Turn off the heat and pour into a bowl. Stir in the minced garlic, chili, fish sauce and lime juice, so the garlic spreads through the hot stock.
4. Carefully move the cooked fish to the deep serving dish and remove the lemongrass from the cavity. Keep the juices in the steaming dish aside; do not pour them into the lime sauce, which would dilute the balance of sour and salty you have set.
5. Place the celery along both sides of the fish and stir the cilantro into the lime and garlic sauce. Spoon the sauce evenly over the fish so the slashes take it up too, and serve at once. Leave a layer of sauce in the dish that can be spooned up.

## Key points

- **The fish is dry and falls apart into crumbs when picked up**: it steamed too long, so the proteins tightened and squeezed out water. Take it out once the flesh is opaque and flakes; thick parts get only a short extra time, not a long one (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **The garlic sauce is salty and bitter**: the stock itself was salty, or the sauce was cooked too long and concentrated. Use unsalted or low-sodium stock, take it off the heat as soon as the sugar dissolves, and add the fish sauce and lime in the bowl (see [acid](/principles/acid/)).
- **No fresh scent of lime and cilantro**: the sauce was steamed together with the fish for too long. Make the fish and the sauce separately, and stir the cilantro in last (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
