*Doenjang jjigae* is a rich stew made with Korean fermented soybean paste, often served with white rice. The paste has gone through [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/), which breaks down the soy proteins into a salty savoury taste and a thick, deep flavour. The savoury components of anchovies, shrimp and doenjang complement each other, so even a small amount of broth has enough [umami](/principles/umami/).

The stew has anchovies, potato, zucchini and tofu, plus a little shrimp for savouriness. Plain doenjang has a strong flavour, and it is fine for a few particles to stay in the broth. When buying the paste, check the name: ssamjang is a seasoned wrap paste, with a different taste and ratio of salt to sugar.

## Steps

1. Cut the potato, onion and zucchini into pieces of about 1 cm, slice the green chili into rings and mince the garlic. Keeping the sizes close lets the potato cook through by the time the other vegetables are soft.
2. Split the dried anchovies open along the belly, remove the black innards, put them in a clean soup bag and tie it shut. Roughly chop the shrimp, dice the tofu and chop the green onions, and set aside.
3. Put the potato, onion, zucchini, green chili, garlic and shrimp in a small heavy-bottomed pot, add the anchovy bag and 590 ml water (the top of the ingredients will poke out a little, and you don't need to add more water), cover and heat over medium-high heat.
4. Cook until the whole pot starts to bubble, which takes about 7–8 minutes in a thin metal pot and may take 15 minutes in a thick clay pot. Gently turn from the bottom with a spoon so the vegetables on top also touch the broth; the shrimp should have turned opaque.
5. Scoop out a spoonful of the hot broth and stir 4 tbsp of the doenjang into it until loosened, then pour it back in. Turn the heat to medium, cover and cook for about 20 minutes, until a potato piece can be cut easily with the edge of a spoon, the onion is soft and the broth is an even colour. Check the bottom of the pot partway through, and add a little hot water if the liquid has dropped a lot.
6. Add the tofu, nudge it gently into the broth and cook for about 3 minutes more, until the tofu is hot through the inside while its edges are still distinct. Let a spoonful of broth cool and taste it. If you want a stronger doenjang flavour and more saltiness, dissolve in the remaining 1 tbsp of doenjang and stir until combined.
7. Take out the anchovy bag, sprinkle with green onion and set the pot on a heatproof mat to share with white rice. You should see chunks of vegetables in the broth, with no dried paste residue at the bottom.

## Key points

- **Strong paste aroma but only saltiness**: doenjang brands differ in saltiness, and the stew also concentrates as it cooks. Add the paste in stages and stop when the saltiness is right; if it is too salty, thin it with hot water and eat it with rice. See [fermented seasonings](/principles/fermentation/).
- **Potato has a hard core while the zucchini has turned mushy**: the pieces are very different in size, and the centre of a potato needs longer to heat. Cut the potato and vegetables to about 1 cm, and keep cooking over low heat if a hard core remains. See [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/).
- **Broth is bitter and anchovy crumbs are everywhere**: the fish innards weren't removed, or the fish was cooked loose for a long time and fell apart. Remove the innards, use a bag, and lift it out before serving; add water in time when the broth runs low in the pot. See [umami](/principles/umami/).
