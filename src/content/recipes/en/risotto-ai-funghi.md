*Risotto ai funghi* is an Italian mushroom risotto: rice that is soft with a little bite, in a coat of thick sauce scented with porcini. Soaked dried porcini are sliced into the rice, and the strained soaking liquid is also used to cook it. The stock should be mild so the mushroom aroma isn't overpowered; the cheese supplies the salty savoury finish.

The silky sauce comes from starch released from the outer layer of the rice (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Don't rinse the rice; add hot stock in portions and stir the bottom, so the grains keep their shape when cooked through. Finally, butter and cheese are stirred in off the heat and the fat disperses into the starchy sauce (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)).

## Steps

1. Soak the dried porcini in the lukewarm water from the recipe for at least 30 minutes, until the pieces are soft. Lift them out by hand and squeeze lightly, keeping the liquid in the bowl; rinse the pieces quickly of any remaining grit and chop them small.
2. Pour the soaking liquid through a fine sieve lined with damp paper towel to leave a clear mushroom liquid. Don't pour the grit at the bottom of the bowl into the sieve. Heat the stock separately and keep it at a bare simmer next to the pot, to ladle from as needed.
3. Put the oil, half the butter and the onion in a wide heavy-bottomed pot and cook over medium heat until the onion is translucent. Add the raw rice and stir until every grain is coated in oil and glossy on the surface, then start adding stock right away.
4. Ladle in about 120 ml of hot stock, keep a steady gentle boil and keep stirring, scraping the bottom and corners. When the liquid is absorbed enough that a spoon drawn across briefly shows the bottom, add the next ladle; don't let the rice dry out and stick.
5. After about 10 minutes of adding stock, add the mushroom pieces and half of the strained mushroom liquid. Cook the same way until the liquid reduces, then add the rest of the mushroom liquid in portions, and after that keep cooking the rice with stock.
6. Start tasting the rice from about 20 minutes, until the outside is soft and the centre has a slight bite, with no hard white core. If there isn't enough liquid, keep adding hot stock; once the rice is cooked, stop heating, and leave a thick sauce in the pot that coats the grains.
7. Take the pot off the heat and stir in the remaining butter, the Parmigiano and black pepper, stirring until the sauce is glossy with no floating oil. Taste and add salt, spoon into warm plates, and eat right away.

## Key points

- **The rice is still hard but the pot is dry**: liquid was added late, or the heat made it evaporate too fast. Add hot stock in time and push along the bottom as you stir. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **The rice breaks into mush**: it was overcooked or crushed too hard. Push and fold with the spoon, and start tasting the rice around the time suggested on the package; finish once the centre has a bite. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Oil floats on the surface and it eats heavy**: it was reduced too dry and the butter couldn't disperse. Add a little hot stock off the heat and stir so the sauce flows again. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
- **Grit in the mouth**: dried mushrooms carry sand and a fine sieve alone is not enough. Strain the soaking liquid through a paper filter and discard the sediment at the bottom; wash the pieces too. For soaking and rinsing, see [blanching and removing off-odours](/principles/blanching/).
