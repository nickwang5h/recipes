Huangmen ji has chicken that is tender and well seasoned, with a sauce thick enough to spoon over rice that still runs off the side of the spoon. The chicken is coated in caramel colour first, then braised with the shiitake; the green pepper goes in last to keep its fresh fragrance and crunch. The caramel colour relies on [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/): cook it to light brown with a hint of caramel fragrance and that is enough, because if it goes dark brown, the whole pot of sauce turns bitter.

The centre of bone-in chicken pieces heats more slowly than the surface, so the pot is braised covered, so the liquid and steam keep carrying heat inside (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Once the chicken is cooked, reduce the sauce only briefly; the longer it reduces, the drier the chicken gets. The optional potato thickens the sauce and also soaks up the liquid, so watch the bottom of the pan in the second half of the braise.

## Steps

1. Rinse the surface dust off the dried shiitake and soak them in clean water in the refrigerator until soft; if soaking overnight, keep them in the refrigerator throughout. Cut one open to check for a hard core, and if there is none, slice them. Let the soaking water settle, strain it, and measure out 400 ml, topping up with water if there is not enough.
2. Chop the chicken legs into pieces about 4 cm, clear off any bone splinters and blot the surface dry with paper towels. Slice the ginger, snip the dried chilies into rings, and remove the stems and seeds from the green peppers and cut them into long thin pieces; if using potato, cut it into rolling pieces of a similar size.
3. Put the oil and 5 g of sugar in the pan and heat over low heat from cold, pushing it around constantly. When the sugar has melted and fine light-brown bubbles appear, add the chicken; do not wait for a deep brown, and do not lean over the pan.
4. Add the chicken and toss so the caramel colour coats every side, then turn the heat to medium-high and stir-fry until the surface has set. Add the Shaoxing wine along the side of the pan, then the ginger and dried chilies, and toss until fragrant, then add the light soy sauce and stir-fry to combine.
5. Pour the shiitake water slowly along the side of the pan, just enough to barely cover the chicken, and add the shiitake, white pepper and the optional potato. If it takes a lot of water to cover the chicken in a big pot, switch to a smaller pot so the flavour is not too weak.
6. After the liquid comes to a boil, cover, turn the heat to medium-low and keep it at a gentle bubble, braising for 15–20 minutes. Turn the chicken gently once halfway and check whether there is still liquid at the bottom; if the liquid is low and the potato is still hard, add hot water and keep braising.
7. Insert a thermometer into the centre of the thickest chicken piece, away from the bone; at 74°C (165°F) it is done, and the potato should let a chopstick through easily. Once cooked, uncover and reduce the sauce until it coats the chicken and still runs off the side of the spoon.
8. Add the green peppers and toss until bright in colour and just cooked through, then stop. Taste, add salt, add the optional MSG, toss and plate, leaving a little sauce for the rice.

## Key points

- **Very dark colour and bitter sauce**: the caramel colour went too far (see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/)). Add the chicken when the caramel is light brown; throw out burnt black caramel and start over.
- **Surface cooked but the meat next to the bone not done**: the pieces are too big or the braise was too short (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cut pieces to the same size, measure the thickest part after a full 15–20 minutes of braising, and keep braising if it has not reached temperature.
- **Chicken dry and peppers yellow**: the chicken's [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) contract and squeeze out more water the longer they cook, and the peppers were cooked too long. Reduce the sauce soon after the chicken is done and add the peppers last.
