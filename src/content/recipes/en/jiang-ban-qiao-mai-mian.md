Soba with savoury sauce is cool, salty and spicy, with every strand distinct and crunchy vegetables alongside. Cooked noodles are rinsed in cold water to wash off the starch that comes out on the surface, so they do not stick into a mass (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). After rinsing they also need draining. With no water pooled in the bowl, the sauce flavour stays on the noodles.

Chili crisp already carries the flavour of chili and its other ingredients, and tossing it straight through the noodles spreads the aroma (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Cucumber and carrot bring a fresh crunch. The buckwheat ratio, thickness and salt content of soba differ by brand, so go by the directions on the package in your hand for how long to cook.

## Steps

1. Wash the cucumber and carrot, peel the carrot, and cut both into thin strips. Cut the carrot as fine as you can so its crunch is close to the cucumber's. Set them aside once cut, and do not salt them ahead of time.
2. Take out the soba and remove the paper band around the bundles. Bring the pot of water to a full boil before adding the noodles. Gently spread them apart so each bundle touches the hot water.
3. Cook for the time on the package (4 minutes for Hakubaku soba), using plenty of water at a moderate rolling boil. Nudge the noodles with chopsticks to keep them from sticking, but do not stir hard. Taste one strand, and lift the noodles out when there is no hard core in the centre and they still hold their shape.
4. Put the noodles in a sieve and rinse with cold water that is safe to drink, gently moving them to wash off the slippery starch on the surface. Stop as soon as the noodles are cooled through, since longer soaking makes them absorb water and go soft.
5. Shake the sieve and drain until no more water drips, then put the noodles in the mixing bowl. Drain the vegetable strips of their wash water as well, so there is no pool of water at the bottom of the bowl after mixing.
6. Stir the oil and solids in the jar of chili crisp together, measure 20 ml and add it to the noodles and vegetables. Lift and toss with chopsticks until the sauce is even, taste and eat right away.
7. If you want colder noodles, chill the drained noodles briefly in the fridge and toss them with the vegetables and sauce just before eating. Do not leave warm noodles piled in a thick mass to cool: the outside cools while the inside stays hot.

## Key points

- **Mushy noodles that break easily**: they were overcooked, or left sitting in water after lifting out, so the starch in the noodles [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) too far. Judge by the package and by tasting, and rinse and drain as soon as they are done.
- **Noodles stuck in a mass**: the surface starch was not rinsed off, or they were piled up to wait as soon as they left the pot. Rinse the strands apart with cold water (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)), moving them gently.
- **Weak sauce flavour and water in the bowl**: the noodles and vegetables were not drained, and the water diluted the [saltiness](/principles/salt/). Drain off the extra water before tossing.
- **Only an oily taste, no chili fragrance**: the oil and solids were not stirred together, so the flavour is unevenly distributed (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Stir before measuring the sauce, and lift and toss evenly when mixing.
