The chicken legs are tender and juicy, and every shred is coated in ginger and green onion oil. The method keeps two jobs apart: the legs cook in steam, the ginger and green onion release their fragrance in hot oil, and the two only meet at the end. The legs are marinated first with salt baked chicken seasoning, so the meat has flavour underneath.

Steam carries heat to the bowl and the surface of the chicken first, then to the centre; see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/). Take the legs off the heat as soon as they are done and the meat stops losing moisture. Thick shreds are chewier than finely torn ones. The fragrance of the ginger and green onion oil comes from chopping them small and a brief pour of hot oil; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

## Steps

1. Pat the chicken legs dry and put them in a shallow heatproof bowl. Add the salt baked chicken seasoning and 5 ml of oil, rub in evenly, and marinate in the fridge for 15 minutes. Meanwhile bring the water in the steamer to a boil.
2. Spread the legs out, not piled thickly. When the water boils, put the bowl in the steamer, keep the steam going and steam for about 20 minutes. If you need to top up the water, use hot water, and do not let the pot boil dry.
3. Insert a thermometer into the centre of the thickest part of each leg, away from the bone, and take the legs out at 74°C (165°F). If a large leg has not reached that temperature, keep steaming and ignore the timer. Set aside the juices that collected in the bowl.
4. Mince the ginger finely and cut the green onion into small pieces. Put them in a dry heatproof bowl, add the sugar and a little salt, and mix. Spread the ginger and green onion out so the hot oil touches more of their surface.
5. Heat the remaining 30 ml of oil in a small pot until ripples show on the surface and the oil runs freely, but before it smokes. Pour a little over the ginger and green onion first; when it bubbles and smells fragrant, pour on the rest in several additions, then stir at once. The ginger and green onion should stay pale.
6. When the legs have cooled enough to handle, debone them with clean hands or tongs and tear the meat into shreds about 1 cm thick. Pick out any small bone bits near the bone. Keep the pieces loose, and do not shred them fine.
7. Pour the ginger and green onion oil over the chicken and toss gently until every piece is glossy. If the shreds look dry, add a little of the juices the chicken gave off in the bowl, and toss until moist without any pooled liquid. Taste before deciding whether to add salt, and serve promptly.

## Key points

- **Dry outside, undercooked in the centre**: the legs were piled up or very different in size, so heat travelled unevenly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Steam them spread out, measure each at its thickest part, and keep steaming if the large one is not done.
- **Shreds are thin and dry**: it steamed too long, so the muscle [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tightened and lost moisture, and the meat was torn too fine. Take the legs out when done, keep the shreds thick, and add a little of the steamed chicken juices when tossing with the oil.
- **Ginger and green onion are scorched and bitter, or the whole bowl is too salty**: the oil was too hot and the fresh [aroma](/principles/aromatics/) was lost, and the salt baked chicken seasoning already carries salt. Pour the oil in stages, keep the ginger and green onion pale, and add salt only after tossing with the chicken.
