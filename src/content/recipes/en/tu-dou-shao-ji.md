Braised chicken with potatoes has a thick sauce, with potatoes full of chicken juices and edges braised until they dissolve slightly, which is just right over rice. The chicken skin is first browned in the pan to bring out its fragrance, and then it is braised with water and soy sauce. The thick sauce comes from the [starch gelatinizing](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) on the surface of the potatoes and from the water reduced away at the end.

The chicken and potatoes go in one after the other so both cook through and keep their shape. Bone-in chicken legs need a while to braise first, and potatoes added too early break up under repeated turning. Keep the braise at small bubbles, so the hot liquid carries heat around the chicken pieces (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Prepare the main ingredients. Pick any loose bone fragments from the chopped chicken and pat the surface dry with paper towels. Cut the potatoes into rolling-cut chunks of similar size, cut the ginger and green onion, and use separate boards for raw and cooked food.
2. Brown the chicken. Add the oil to a wide pan and heat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil flows easily. Lay in the chicken skin side down and cook for 3 minutes, until the skin is light golden and lifts easily with a spatula. Flip and cook for 2 more minutes, until the outside changes colour.
3. Stir-fry until fragrant and season. Add the ginger slices and the white parts of the green onion and stir-fry for 0.5 minutes, until you smell the ginger and green onion. Sprinkle in the sugar and stir for 0.5 minutes, until it melts. Drizzle the Shaoxing wine around the edge of the pan, add the light soy sauce and dark soy sauce and stir, so the chicken is evenly coloured.
4. Braise the chicken first. Pour in 400 ml of hot water and heat over medium-high heat for about 2 minutes, until the liquid boils. Cover, turn to low heat and braise for 10 minutes, until the chicken has tightened up and the surface of the liquid keeps making small bubbles. Nudge once halfway through to prevent sticking.
5. Add the potatoes. Put the potatoes into the liquid, cover and braise over low heat for 15–18 minutes, until a chopstick pierces the centre of the potatoes easily. Insert a thermometer into the thickest piece of chicken, away from the bone. At 74°C (165°F) it is done; if not, braise for 2 more minutes.
6. Reduce the sauce. Uncover, turn to medium-high heat and cook for 3–5 minutes, until the sauce coats the chicken and potatoes and there is still a thin layer of thick sauce at the bottom of the pan. Nudge the bottom of the pan gently to avoid breaking the potatoes with the spatula. Add the green parts of the green onion, toss and serve.

## Key points

- **Potatoes break up outside and stay hard inside**: the pieces were cut unevenly, or the heat was too high and they were turned too much. Cut an even thickness following the rules of [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), and braise until the centres are soft before uncovering to reduce.
- **The sauce is thick but the chicken is dry**: the chicken was still cooked for a long time during reduction, and its [proteins kept contracting](/principles/protein-coagulation/). If the chicken is cooked and there is too much sauce, take the chicken out first, reduce the sauce, then put the chicken back.
- **Rice goes soggy in a lunch box**: the potatoes had not released enough starch and too much sauce was left. Before packing, reduce to a [thick starch-based sauce](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) that coats the pieces, pick out loose bones, divide into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly. Reheat the next day until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
