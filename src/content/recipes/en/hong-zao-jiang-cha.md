Red date and ginger tea is a home-style hot drink made by simmering dried red dates and fresh ginger in water. The dates bring fruity sweetness, the ginger slices bring pungency, and the brown sugar rounds out the sweetness. The finished tea is pale amber, sweet at first, with the heat of ginger following.

The key is to let the flavour steep out of the ingredients (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Once the dates are split, the water reaches the flesh more easily; thin ginger slices give up their flavour more evenly. After the water boils, turn to low heat and keep it at a gentle bubble, so the moving hot water carries heat around the ingredients (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). A lid reduces evaporation, so the tea does not become concentrated. Add the sugar last, and you can still tell the date's sweetness from the ginger's heat.

## Steps

1. Rinse the dates, cut along the pit, break them in two and take out the pits. Wash the ginger and cut it into slices about 2 mm thick; keep the thickness as even as possible so they release flavour together.
2. Put the dates, ginger slices and 480 ml of water in a small pot, cover, and cook over medium-high heat for about 4–5 minutes, until the surface bubbles steadily and steam escapes at the edge of the lid.
3. Turn to low heat, cover and cook for 15 minutes, until the date flesh has absorbed water and softened, the tea has turned pale amber and you can smell the ginger. The surface only needs to bubble gently; if the steam makes the lid jump, lower the heat further.
4. Add 10 g of brown sugar, stir well, cover and keep cooking over low heat for 5 minutes, until the sugar has fully dissolved and the colour of the tea is even. Turn off the heat, strain out the ginger slices and dates through a fine sieve, and pour the tea into two small cups.
5. Let it sit for 3–5 minutes, until the steam eases and a small sip does not burn your mouth, and drink while warm.

## Key points

- **Still like sweet water after cooking, with weak ginger and date flavour**: the dates were not split, or the ginger slices were too thick, so flavour came out slowly. Expose the date flesh and slice the ginger thin first, then simmer over low heat for the stated times; see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Tea is harsh and cloying, and there is less of it**: it was boiled hard with the lid off, so too much water evaporated and the ginger heat and sugar concentrated together. Keep it covered at a gentle simmer, and do not reduce the tea into a syrup; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Only sugar, no ginger aroma**: too much sugar was added, or it was boiled hard for a long time after it was done, so the fresh fragrance gradually faded. Add sugar by the stated amount, and turn off the heat and strain after the final 5 minutes; see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/).
