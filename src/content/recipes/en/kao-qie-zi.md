Garlic roasted eggplant is soft enough to pull apart with a fork, with a little roasted colour on the skin and a fragrant sauce of garlic, chili and cumin inside. The method is to roast the whole eggplant soft first and then fill it with the sauce and roast it again: the first pass lets heat travel from the skin to the centre, and the second lets the sauce meet the hot air directly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The thickness of the eggplant says more about when the centre is done than its weight, and once the skin wrinkles you still need to check the flesh.

The garlic and cumin go in for the second half, so their aroma is kept and they don't turn bitter from long roasting at high heat (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). A home oven is all you need.

## Steps

1. Mix the light soy sauce, cumin, salt, minced garlic and chopped chili, then add the oil set aside for the sauce. The garlic should be wrapped in the wet sauce, with no dry powder left on the surface.
2. Wash the eggplant and dry it with a paper towel. Pierce the side 4–8 times with a fork, right through the skin, to give the steam inside a way out, and brush the surface evenly with the oil set aside for the skin.
3. Set the oven to 200°C (400°F) with the convection function on, and preheat until the oven has actually reached the set temperature. Put the eggplant on a rimmed baking sheet on the middle rack, not too close to the heating element.
4. Roast for about 12–15 minutes first, then take it out and check: the skin should be wrinkled and, pressed lightly with tongs, sink in by about 0.3–0.5 cm. Then test the flesh with a fork, and if it is still clearly firm, keep roasting until soft before moving to the next step.
5. Wearing oven mitts, take out the baking sheet and cut a slit along the length of the eggplant, leaving about 1–1.5 cm uncut at each end. Gently loosen the flesh with a fork, keeping the bottom skin intact, and slowly open it up so the hot steam can escape.
6. Spread the sauce into the opening and push it between the loosened flesh. Return to the oven and roast again at 200°C (400°F) for about 4–7 minutes, until the garlic gives off its fragrance and the flesh pulls apart easily, then take it out and turn off the oven.

## Key points

- **Skin scorched while the centre is hard**: the eggplant is too thick, or it sits too close to the heating element (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Use the middle rack and check the centre after the first roast; if the surface is already dark, cover it loosely with foil and let the inside keep softening.
- **Garlic blackened and sauce bitter**: it roasted too long after the sauce went in (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Roast soft first, and in the second roast go only until the fragrance comes out; finish when the garlic starts to scorch rather than waiting for a fixed time.
- **Skin puffs up and steam sprays from the slit**: the water inside turns to steam as it heats (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Pierce the skin before roasting, and cut the slit slowly after taking it out.
- **Sauce too salty**: the soy sauce and salt add up, and the second roast also dries off water (see [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/)). Start with little salt, and taste the saltiness of the sauce before adjusting.
