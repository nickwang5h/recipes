Lard rice is rich, salty and savoury, with a thin gloss over each grain. It depends on hot rice to melt the lard, so that the fat and soy sauce coat the grains evenly. Once the rice cools, the lard stays in white lumps (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). The heat in the rice is enough to melt the lard, so no oil needs heating separately.

The soy sauce brings the salty savouriness, and a little dark soy sauce adds colour. The optional oyster sauce adds more [umami](/principles/umami/) and a sweet-salty note; if you use it, reduce both the lard and the light soy sauce. The rice should be cooked through to the centre of each grain and fluffed before use. Cold rice in which the [starch has retrograded](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) must be heated through again first, because it will not mix with set lard.

## Steps

1. The cooked rice must be hot through, and loosen easily with a light touch. Fluff freshly cooked rice first and let the extra steam out. A paddle should lift soft clumps, and when you let go you should see whole grains.
2. Mix the light soy sauce and dark soy sauce in a small bowl. Without oyster sauce, use the base amounts of lard and light soy sauce from the ingredient list. With oyster sauce, reduce those two as noted, and stir the oyster sauce into the soy sauce, so one mouthful is not very strong and another bland.
3. Put the lard in the bottom of the serving bowl and add the hot rice. If the lard has set in large lumps in the refrigerator, break it into small pieces with a spoon first so more of it touches the hot rice. Let the rice sit for a moment until the edges of the lard soften and turn translucent.
4. Drizzle in the mixed soy sauce and toss gently from the bottom up with a rice paddle. As you toss, loosen the clumps so the melted fat spreads through the whole bowl. Do not press the rice repeatedly with the back of the paddle, or the grains will be crushed into a sticky mass.
5. Check whether any white lumps of lard are left at the bottom. If so, fold hot rice over to that spot and keep tossing until no solid fat is visible and the colour is about the same throughout. The soy sauce should cling to the rice, with no obvious liquid left at the bottom.
6. Sprinkle on the optional chopped green onion and toss, then add the cooked pork cracklings last and eat right away. Put the cracklings on the surface, or toss them in lightly just before eating. Buried in damp, hot rice for long, they take on water and lose their crispness.

## Key points

- **White lumps of fat left at the bottom**: the rice is too cool, or the lard lumps are too big, so not enough heat reaches them (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Heat the rice through and break the fat into small pieces first, then toss until it melts.
- **Tossed into a wet, sticky clump**: the rice already had a lot of water, and pressing with the spoon crushed the cooked starch (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Control the water when cooking the rice, fluff it first, and lift rather than press when you mix.
- **Too heavy after adding oyster sauce**: the savouriness of oyster sauce overlaps with soy sauce (see [umami](/principles/umami/)). When you add oyster sauce, reduce the light soy sauce to match and add no extra salt. If it is already too strong, dilute with more hot rice.
