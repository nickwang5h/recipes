Red-braised pork trotters are glossy red and soft and sticky. The red colour comes from caramel and soy sauce, and the softness from [collagen turning into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) in the sinew. Trotters can still be tough after they are cooked, so give the sinew enough time in moist heat; at the end, reduce the sauce so the dissolved gelatin and the sugar form a thick sauce that coats the meat.

Making the caramel colour is [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/), so go by colour and smell rather than by the clock. Star anise, cassia and bay leaf are briefly stir-fried first and then release their aroma into the liquid as it cooks (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)); pick the spices out at the end.

## Steps

1. Check the trotter pieces for leftover hair and bone chips and clean them off. Put them in a pot of cold water with the green onion, the ginger for blanching and 20 ml of Shaoxing wine. Bring to a boil and skim, and cook until the foam has clearly lessened, about 15 minutes, then lift out, rinse and pat the surface dry.
2. In a clean pot, put the oil and crushed rock sugar and stir slowly over low heat. When the sugar has melted and turned from golden to reddish brown and smells of caramel, take it off the heat at once, add the dried trotters and toss, then return to low heat so every side takes on the colour.
3. Add the bay leaf, cassia, star anise, the ginger for stewing, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and the remaining Shaoxing wine. Stir over medium heat for about 1 minute, and as soon as the fragrance comes up pour in the hot water for the braising liquid, bring to a boil and turn off the heat.
4. Move the meat and liquid into the pressure cooker, making sure the amount of liquid and the fill level follow the manual, and lock the lid. Start timing once it comes up to pressure, and cook under pressure for 15 minutes; when finished, release the pressure according to the manual, and open the lid only after the pressure has fully gone.
5. Poke the thickest piece of sinew with a chopstick; it should go in easily, and when you nudge it the meat begins to come away from the bone. If it is still firm, top up the liquid and pressure-cook again before testing; you can also move to an ordinary pot and stew at a gentle simmer until soft, which usually takes much longer.
6. Once soft, move back to a wok, pick out the spices, and taste the sauce before deciding whether to add salt. Reduce over medium-high heat, turning as it reduces, until the sauce coats the trotters and there is still flowing braising liquid at the bottom of the pan, then turn off the heat promptly and serve.

## Key points

- **Still tough after pressure cooking**: the pieces were large and the sinew thick, or the timing did not start from when it reached pressure (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Go by whether the sinew has softened; 15 minutes is only a starting point.
- **Caramel turns black, whole pot bitter**: the sugar was cooked too far and [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/) became burning. Burnt, bitter caramel must be made again; take the pan off the heat before adding the meat, to leave some room for residual heat.
- **Too salty after reducing, spices overpowering**: the braising liquid keeps concentrating, and the salt and the flavours already extracted gather together (see [spices and aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/)). Add no salt at first, pick out the spices before reducing, and taste with a piece of meat.
- **Lots of sauce, but the meat is about to fall apart**: once it is soft, cooking the meat longer in the liquid makes the shape collapse (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Lift the meat out first, reduce the sauce on its own, and return the meat to toss once the thickness is right.
