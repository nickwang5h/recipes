Steamed fish head with chopped chilies is a Hunan steamed dish. The bighead carp has plenty of meat and gelatinous parts in its head. Split open and laid flat on a deep plate, covered with red chopped chilies, it is steamed and the fish is eaten dipped in the juices on the plate. The chopped chilies have been salt-cured and [fermented](/principles/fermentation/), which brings a ripe chili fragrance, saltiness and a mild sourness; with the right chopped chilies, the flavour holds up.

Steam condenses on the plate and the surface of the fish head and carries heat in, but the thick parts still cook slowly through [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/). Lay the head flat and spread the chilies evenly, so the thin parts do not overcook while the thick parts finish. At the end, pouring hot oil over briefly heats the green onion and garlic and brings up their aroma (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)); the juices released by steaming stay on the plate.

## Steps

1. Ask the fish counter to remove the gills and split the head open from the lower jaw, leaving it joined at the top of the head. At home, check the gill roots and blood clots against the bone, clean them and pat dry, then open it out into a flat layer. Make two cuts in the thick neck meat down to near the bone, to reduce the difference in thickness; do not try to hack through the skull bone with a small knife.
2. Cut the ginger into shreds and mince, separate the white and green parts of the green onion, mince the garlic, and rinse and drain the black beans. Taste a small bit of the chopped chilies to judge the saltiness, and drain off the extra brine floating on top. Lay the ginger shreds on a deep heatproof plate with a rim, set the fish head on top with the meat side up, and leave room at the edge of the plate for steam to pass.
3. Put 2 tbsp of oil in a small pan and heat it over medium-low heat for 1 minute, until the oil flows quickly. Add the minced ginger, minced garlic and white parts of the green onion and stir-fry for 30 seconds, until you smell them. Add the chopped chilies and black beans and stir-fry for 1 minute more, until the chili fragrance comes out and the garlic has not yet turned brown.
4. Drizzle in the Shaoxing wine and cook for 30 seconds, until it bubbles and the smell of alcohol fades. Spread the chili mixture with its oil evenly over the fish head, so the thick neck meat, cheeks and edges are all covered, and do not pile it into a mountain of chilies. If the chopped chilies are already salty, add no salt.
5. Add 480 ml of water to the steamer and heat over high heat for about 8 minutes, until steam rises steadily. Put in the plate of fish, cover and keep the steam coming continuously, and steam for 8–10 minutes. From minute 8, check the thickest neck meat and cheeks: the flesh should be opaque and flake easily from the bone. If it is not done, steam 2 minutes more and check again.
6. Take out the plate, scatter on the green parts of the green onion and keep the juices on the plate. In a separate pan, heat the remaining 2 tbsp of oil over medium heat for 1 minute, until fine lines appear on the surface and a little green onion dropped in bubbles quickly. Pour the hot oil over the green onion and the chilies, and serve right away as the aroma rises.

## Key points

- **Only heat, without the chopped chili fragrance**: you bought chili oil or fresh chili sauce, which lacks the flavour of salt-cured [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/). Look for Hunan chopped chilies, taste for sour and salt first, then decide whether to add salt.
- **The edges are overcooked and the meat against the bone is still translucent**: the head was not laid flat, or the plate blocked the steam, so some parts heated slowly. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/). Cut the thick parts, leave space at the edge of the plate and check doneness at the neck meat.
- **The garlic is bitter and the hot oil smells burnt**: the fine mince stayed in the hot oil too long and the aroma was pushed too far (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Fry the chilies over medium-low heat, add the wet ingredients before the garlic takes colour, and judge the pour-over oil by the green onion bubbling quickly.
