Shaanxi oil-splashed noodles is wide noodles with greens, topped with a small pile of green onion, garlic and chili, and finished with a spoonful of hot oil that makes the aroma burst out. The noodles are cooked first through [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); the hot oil then carries the flavour of the chili, green onion and garlic into the fat (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The seasoning is minimal, but the noodles and the oil each need their own heat.

Drain the noodles before splashing the oil, and spread the chili flakes over the green onion and garlic; the oil touches these fine seasonings first, which releases the aroma quickly, and is then tossed through every strand.

## Steps

1. Chop the green onion and garlic, and wash the bok choy and cut it into bite-sized sections; if using bean sprouts, wash them too. Put a heatproof ceramic bowl on a steady, dry counter.
2. Bring plenty of water to a boil and add the salt listed for cooking the noodles. Add the fresh wide noodles and gently loosen them, keeping the water able to move around the noodles so the sheets do not stick into a clump.
3. After the noodles float, cook for another 1–2 minutes and taste one: there should be no hard raw core in the centre and the noodle should still have a chewy bite. Add the bok choy for the last 1 minute or so; if the bean sprouts still taste clearly raw, cook them separately beforehand and lift them out.
4. Drain the noodles and greens until they no longer drip in streams and put them in the bowl while hot. Add the light soy sauce and vinegar, put the green onion and garlic on the noodles, and spread the chili flakes over them. Keep the rest of the salt aside; taste after tossing before deciding whether to use it all.
5. Put the vegetable oil in a small pot and heat until fine ripples appear on the surface, before it smokes. Splash a little oil onto the chili flakes first; it should bubble at once and give off fragrance. If the chili quickly turns black, pause and let the oil in the pot cool.
6. Splash in the remaining oil in portions, then immediately toss the sauce at the bottom of the bowl, the seasonings and the noodles together. The noodles should be thinly coated in red oil, with no clumps of undissolved chili powder at the bottom of the bowl. Eat hot.

## Key points

- **Chili turns black and tastes bitter**: the oil was too hot or the flakes too fine, and the chili scorched in the sizzle. Judge by the surface of the oil and the test splash, following [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/), and remember that smoking means the oil is too hot. Replace scorched, bitter seasoning.
- **Noodles are mushy or hard in the centre**: fresh noodles differ in thickness, and [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) needs different times. Don't go only by floating; taste one, and lift them out as soon as they are cooked.
- **Seasoning clumps and the aroma is uneven**: the hot oil only touched the surface of a thick pile of flakes, and the lower layer stayed dry. Following [aroma extraction](/principles/aromatics/), spread the flakes thin, splash the oil in portions and toss promptly; too much water will also dilute the seasoning.
