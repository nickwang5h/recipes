*Phat kaphrao mu sap* is ground pork stir-fried with holy basil, salty, hot and fragrant, served over hot jasmine rice and often topped with a fried egg. Pad kra pao needs holy basil (*kaphrao*), which has a peppery bite, not the Thai sweet basil (*horapha*) used in green curry, which has a distinct anise scent.

The garlic and chilies are pounded first so their fragrance goes into the oil, and only then does the ground pork go in. The holy basil is left to the end so that the easily lost aroma of the tender herb is kept; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). After the pork goes in, first cook off the excess water, then reduce the seasoning until it is tight; the pan has a moist sheen, and when it is spooned onto rice no pool of liquid runs out.

## Steps

1. Pound the garlic, chilies and shallot to a rough paste in which small bits are still visible. Mix the fish sauce, thin soy sauce, sweet soy sauce and oyster sauce. Wash and drain the holy basil, and divide the hot rice between two bowls.
2. If you are serving eggs, heat the vegetable oil in a small wok and slide in the eggs one at a time, spooning oil over the whites. Fry until the edges bubble and turn golden and crisp, with the whites and yolks set. Lift them out, pour the extra oil into a heatproof container, and leave 1 tbsp in the wok.
3. Over medium-high heat, stir-fry the garlic and chili paste until fragrant. When the garlic smell is clear and the bits have just started to change colour, add the ground pork. Press it apart with the spatula and stir-fry for about 5 minutes, until the pieces separate, the water at the bottom has evaporated and the centre reaches 71°C (160°F).
4. Add the seasoning mixture and keep stir-frying for about 2 minutes. When the liquid goes from a layer of bubbles to a thin, glossy coat that clings to the pork, and the spatula leaves no pooled liquid as it runs across the bottom, turn off the heat.
5. Right away, fold in the holy basil and toss until the leaves have just wilted, then spoon over the hot rice and top with the fried egg. Plate quickly once the basil is in, so its fragrance stays in the rice.

## Key points

- **Pork swirls around in a pool of liquid**: the pan is too small and steam leaves more slowly than the meat releases water. Cook the water off before adding the sauce, and fry in batches if you are doubling the recipe; see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Herb has cooked black and smells faint**: the holy basil went in too early and its aroma left with the steam. Fold it in only after the sauce is reduced, and stop once the leaves wilt; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Pork is dry and hard, and the egg is burnt on the bottom**: meat that is still stir-fried after it is cooked keeps losing water, and a fried egg over too fierce a heat burns on the bottom first. Stop when the pork is cooked through and the sauce clings, and take the egg out once it has set; see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
