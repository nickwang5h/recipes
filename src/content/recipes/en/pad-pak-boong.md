*Phat phak bung fai daeng* is Thai water spinach stir-fried quickly in a hot wok. The leaves are soft, the stems crisp, and a little sauce is left on the plate for rice. Garlic and chili bring fragrance, and fermented soybeans and soy sauce give salty savouriness. Use Thai fermented soybean paste with whole beans, rinse off some of the brine first, then season with light soy sauce. The beans stay in the dish and give a faint fermented aroma (see [Umami](/principles/umami/)).

The dish has to cook through in a very short time. With a hot pan and a modest amount of greens, the leaves collapse quickly while the stems stay crisp. If the pan is piled full, the heat goes into boiling off the water in the greens first (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Have all the ingredients ready before you turn on the heat.

## Steps

1. Wash the water spinach and shake off the water between the leaves. Cut it into pieces about 5 cm long. Discard thick, old stems that will not snap. Split the thicker tender stems lengthwise so they cook at about the same time as the leaves.
2. Crush the garlic with a small pinch of salt, and crush the chili lightly. Take the beans out of the soybean paste, rinse them quickly and drain. Keep the chicken stock, light soy sauce and sugar ready at hand.
3. Put the water spinach, garlic, beans, chili and sugar in one large bowl. Put the garlic on top of the greens. When you tip it into the wok and turn it right away, the crushed garlic will not stick to the bottom and burn.
4. Heat a wok over high heat, add the oil and swirl it. When the oil runs faster and fine ripples show on its surface, tip in the prepared greens all at once and stir-fry quickly for about 1 minute. The greens should hit the pan with a clear sizzle. If the sound is weak, the pan is not hot enough, so wait for it to heat up first.
5. When the leaves have just softened, add the chicken stock and light soy sauce. Toss until the liquid boils and the cut ends of the stems are no longer hard, then plate. The whole cooking takes only a few minutes. Serve right away with rice.

## Key points

- **The greens are limp and the plate is full of water**: the greens were not drained, or the pan was too full, so the heat went to boiling off water. Drain them well before frying, and for a double batch cook in two pans. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Bitter garlic and blackened chili**: the crushed garlic sat alone in hot oil too long. Add the garlic with the greens, and keep tossing as soon as you smell fragrance. See [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **The saltiness overwhelms the greens**: the brine of the bean paste was not rinsed off, or the chicken stock was already salty. Use unsalted stock and rinse the beans. Start with a little light soy sauce, taste a bite of greens before serving, then add more. See [Umami](/principles/umami/).
