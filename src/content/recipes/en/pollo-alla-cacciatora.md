*Pollo alla cacciatora* is the Italian home-style hunter's chicken, and the wine and herbs differ from region to region. This version is the common Tuscan one with white wine, onion and tomato; Artusi also records a version with red wine. The bone-in pieces simmer slowly in the pot, and the sauce gets thick enough to coat the meat, good for mopping up with bread.

Brown the chicken skin first, then add the wine and tomato, so the aroma from the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) is kept. Keep the heat gentle while simmering so heat reaches the centre of the thick pieces slowly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Chicken breast is lean, so take it out once it is done rather than letting it simmer to dry stringiness with the legs.

## Steps

1. Cut the chicken into two breast pieces, two legs, two thighs and two wings. Pat the surface dry and season with salt and black pepper. The backbone left from cutting up the chicken can be kept for stock. Cut the vegetables first, then handle the raw chicken.
2. Add the olive oil to a wide, heavy-bottomed pan. Over medium heat, cook the onion and crushed garlic until the onion is soft and lightly golden at the edges. Remove the vegetables first and leave the oil in the pan, so the onion does not burn while the chicken browns.
3. Put the chicken in skin side down, in batches, and brown until golden brown and it releases easily from the pan, then turn and brown the other side briefly. Do not crowd the pan; if water collects at the bottom, let it evaporate before the pieces continue to colour.
4. Return the chicken, onion and garlic to the pan and pour in the white wine, scraping up the brown bits from the bottom. Cook until the wine has reduced slightly and the smell of alcohol has softened, then add the crushed tomatoes and the juice from the can.
5. Cook until the edge of the pan bubbles, then turn to low heat, cover and simmer for about 40 minutes. Turn the pieces gently halfway through, and add the reserved hot water in batches only when the bottom is about to dry; keep the sauce bubbling slowly, because a hard boil breaks the chicken up.
6. Measure the thickest part of the breast and of the legs separately, keeping the probe off the bone; the centre of every piece must reach 74°C (165°F). If the breast is done first, take it out first. If the legs are still tight and tough, keep simmering until a fork goes in more easily.
7. Take out all the cooked pieces, uncover and reduce the sauce until it coats the back of a spoon and closes slowly behind a line drawn across the bottom. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper, turn the chicken in the sauce briefly and serve; the skin is soft and moist, and the meat lifts out whole.

## Key points

- **The skin is pale and the sauce tastes only of tomato**: the chicken surface was too wet, or the pieces were stacked, so the first step became boiling (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Dry the chicken, brown in batches, and add liquid only once it is brown.
- **The breast is dry and stringy, while the legs are still tough**: the two cuts differ in fat and connective tissue, so one timer cannot serve both (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Measure the centre temperatures separately, take out the done breast first, and coat everything in sauce at the end.
- **The sauce is thick but the centre of the pieces is not cooked**: water evaporated faster than the thick meat warmed (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cover, lower the heat, and add hot water when the bottom is dry. Measure every piece before reducing the sauce, and take the meat out while you reduce.
