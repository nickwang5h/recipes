Steamed egg custard is a soft gel made by steaming beaten egg with added water. It is smooth and gives a gentle wobble. The egg's [proteins](/principles/protein-coagulation/) link into a network that holds the added water. If it is steamed too long, the network tightens, water seeps out and the texture turns coarse. Water at 1 times the volume of the egg gives a firmer custard; at 1.5 times, it is softer.

Steam heats the bowl and the surface of the egg first, and heat then travels slowly to the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), so a shallow bowl steams more evenly than a deep cup. Mix the egg with warm water, strain off the foam, cover the bowl, and steam with steady low heat. The centre then sets while the outer layer is still tender.

## Steps

1. Beat the egg. Crack the two eggs into a bowl and beat until no obvious strands of white remain. Add a little salt, and if you like a slight tang, 2 ml of white vinegar. Do not add all the salt at once, since the light soy sauce at the end adds saltiness too.
2. Measure the warm water. Check the volume of the beaten egg first, then measure 1–1.5 times that volume of water at 35–45°C (95–115°F). It should be warm but not hot, or it will cook the egg into flecks as you mix. For about 100 ml of beaten egg, use 100–150 ml of water. Pour it in a little at a time, stirring gently as you pour.
3. Strain and fill the bowl. Strain the egg into a shallow heatproof bowl and skim off the foam on top. Cover the bowl with a small heatproof plate to catch the drips from the lid. Do not seal the bowl; leave room around the outside for steam to pass.
4. Steam. Put enough water in the steamer to last the whole time. When it boils, put the bowl in. Cover, turn the heat to low so the water keeps a gentle simmer and the steam rises steadily, and steam for about 10 minutes from when the steam returns. Do not lift the lid again and again.
5. Check the centre. Give the bowl a gentle shake: the whole custard should wobble like jelly, with no liquid moving in the centre. Insert a food thermometer into the centre. At 74°C (165°F) it is done; if not, cover it and keep steaming. When it is done, turn off the heat and let it sit for about 5 minutes in the residual heat.
6. Season and serve. Take out the bowl with heat-safe tongs and remove the small plate. Drizzle on the toasted sesame oil and light soy sauce. Pour part of the soy sauce first, taste the custard, and then decide whether to add the rest. The surface should be smooth, and when you spoon into it the inside is soft and fine.

## Key points

- **Flecks of cooked egg appear when mixing in the water**: the water was too hot, and some of the [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) has already set. Measure the warm water first and add it gradually. Once flecks form, straining cannot make the mixture smooth again.
- **Honeycomb inside and water at the edge**: the heat was too high or the steaming too long, and the protein network contracted too far. As explained under [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), keep a steady gentle simmer, cover the bowl to keep the water out, and check the centre temperature in time.
- **The centre is still liquid while the outside has set**: the bowl is too deep, or too much water was added, so the heat has not reached the centre. Control the ratio according to [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/), switch to a shallow bowl, and keep steaming gently if it is not done.
