*Ricotta* means "cooked again": cheese makers reheat the whey left over from making hard cheese so the remaining proteins float up as curds. Without whey at home, Italians making *ricotta fatta in casa* usually use milk with lemon juice or vinegar directly, and get a fresh cheese of similar texture. 2 L of milk gives about 350–400 g.

When acid is added to milk at about 80°C (175°F), the casein loses its charge and clumps together (see [acid](/principles/acid/)); heating on to 90°C (195°F) also denatures the whey proteins, which are caught up in the curds (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Curds are most vulnerable to stirring while they form: stir and they break up, and what drains out is dry, fine grains.

## Steps

1. Pour the milk and salt into a heavy-bottomed pot and heat over medium heat, nudging the bottom with a heat-resistant spatula from time to time so it doesn't stick. Take the temperature in the middle of the liquid, not against the bottom of the pot.
2. At 80°C (175°F), pour in the lemon juice and push slowly with the spatula for two or three turns, then stop. Soon white flocculent curds separate from the yellowish-green whey.
3. Keep heating on low to 90°C (195°F) without stirring any more, and don't let the surface boil hard. Turn off the heat and rest for about 10 minutes, so the curds gather into large pieces floating on top.
4. Line a sieve with two layers of damp cheesecloth and set it over a bowl. Scoop the curds in a spoonful at a time with a slotted spoon, and pour in the remainder with its liquid gently at the end, instead of dumping the whole pot in.
5. Drain for 10–20 minutes for a moist, spreadable texture; for filling (such as [Spinach and Ricotta Ravioli](/recipes/ravioli-ricotta-spinaci/)), drain it in the fridge for 1–2 hours for a drier, firmer cheese. Pack in a container and refrigerate; eat within 3 days.

## Key points

- **Almost no curds form and the whey is cloudy white**: the milk was UHT milk, or there was not enough acid. Switch to ordinary pasteurized whole milk; if there is too little acid, add 1 tbsp more lemon juice and hold at 90°C (195°F) for a few minutes. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
- **The curds are fine and the texture is dry**: too much stirring after adding the acid, or the liquid boiled and broke up the curds as they gathered. Push only two or three turns after adding the acid and then keep it still. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **The cheese is hard and sour**: it drained too long or was squeezed by hand, or too much acid went in. Aim for soft and mounding; next time use a little less lemon juice or switch to white vinegar.
