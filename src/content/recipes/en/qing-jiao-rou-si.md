Pork and green pepper stir-fry is about the pepper aroma and tender pork, and the finished dish carries only a thin layer of sauce. The meat and the peppers are cut into even thin strips, so they cook fast and are easy to pick up together in one bite. Consistent thickness matters more than simply cutting as fine as possible (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)).

The pork strips are first worked with water and seasoning, then mixed with starch. The starch [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when heated and makes the surface slippery; the tenderness of the meat still depends on heating it briefly. Passing the strips through the oil to separate them and taking them out, then stir-frying the peppers and combining everything at the end, keeps the pork from toughening while you wait for the peppers to cook.

## Steps

1. Cut the pork strips. Cut the tenderloin along its shape into thin slices first, then stack them and cut into strips about 3 mm thick. Remove any visible sinew and keep the thickness as even as possible, so thin strips in the pan do not dry out while thick ones are still raw.
2. Coat the pork. Add the light soy sauce, salt, white pepper, sugar and Shaoxing wine from the marinade ingredients. Add the 60 ml water in 4 portions, stirring in one direction for about 0.5 minutes each time, until no free water is visible at the bottom of the bowl before adding the next. Refrigerate and marinate for 15 minutes, until the strips are moist and sticky; before cooking, mix in the starch and 2 tsp oil.
3. Prepare the peppers. While the pork marinates, remove the stems, seeds and white ribs of the peppers, cut them into thin strips of about the same length as the pork, and dry the surface. Put the garlic and the 1 tbsp light soy sauce for the final stir-fry beside the stove.
4. Pass the pork strips through the oil. Preheat the wok over high heat for about 1 minute, until the surface is dry; pour in 60 ml oil, turn to medium heat and heat for about 0.5 minutes, until the oil flows easily but is not smoking. Add the pork in two batches, stirring each batch for no more than 10 seconds, until the surface turns white and the strips separate, then immediately move them to a bowl kept for partly cooked meat.
5. Stir-fry the peppers. Pour off the extra oil, leaving 1 tbsp in the wok. Stir-fry the garlic over medium-high heat for 0.25 minutes, until fragrant, add the pepper strips and stir-fry for 10–20 seconds, until bright in colour and slightly softened but still holding up.
6. Combine and finish cooking. Return the pork and the meat juices in the bowl, drizzle the final stir-fry light soy sauce down the side of the wok, and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the pork is cooked through and the sauce lightly coats everything. Check the thickest strip; it is done when the centre reaches 71°C (160°F). If not, stir-fry 0.5 minutes more, then plate.

## Key points

- **Pork strips clump into a starchy lump**: starch was added while water was still pooled at the bottom of the bowl, or the strips were not separated promptly after they went into the pan. Work the water in evenly first and add the starch after; the [gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) coating should only wrap the meat thinly.
- **Pork is dry and tough**: the meat was stir-fried a long time along with the peppers, and the more it was cooked, the more its [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) shrank and squeezed out water. Cook the pork apart first and combine at the end, taking it out as soon as it is cooked.
- **Water pools and the peppers are limp in a lunch box**: the pepper strips were not dried, or were stir-fried too long the first time. Cut evenly (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)), check the pan for pooled sauce before packing, keep the peppers a little firm, pack in a shallow box and refrigerate promptly. The next day, reheat until the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).
