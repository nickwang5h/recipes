Cola chicken wings have a glossy coat that is sweet with a salty edge, lightly golden skin and meat braised until tender. The method has two stages. First, pan-fry the surface of the wings dry and golden, so the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/) builds the meaty flavour. Then braise in cola until the centre is cooked through, and finally cook off most of the water so the sugar and soy sauce concentrate into a glossy glaze.

While there is still a lot of liquid, the pan is boiling and evaporating, and no caramel colour has formed yet. As the sauce slowly reduces, the sugar and [salt](/principles/salt/) concentrate together, so near the end use low heat and turn the wings often. This is a sweeter version with extra sugar, and it needs regular sugared cola for the sauce to cling.

## Steps

1. Put the wings in a pot of cold water, with enough water to cover them, and add half the ginger and the Shaoxing wine. Bring to a boil over high heat, skim off the foam, then lift the wings out and drain. Pat the surface dry with paper towels.
2. Cut two shallow slashes on each side of every wing, avoiding the bone. Add 10 g of the light soy sauce, toss to coat and marinate for 10 minutes. Then drain off the extra marinade, so the skin is not too wet when it hits the pan.
3. Add the oil to the pan and stir-fry the remaining ginger slices over low heat until fragrant, then arrange the wings in a single layer. Pan-fry over medium heat until one side is golden, then flip. Once both sides have colour, turn the wings gently 4–5 more times. Leave the skin alone until it has set, and take the ginger out before it turns black.
4. Pour in the cola, add the knotted green onion, bring to a boil and skim off the foam. Add the sugar, the remaining 5 g of light soy sauce, the dark soy sauce and half the salt. Turn the heat to medium, cover and braise for about 12–15 minutes.
5. Insert a thermometer into the thickest part of the largest wing, away from the bone. At 74°C (165°F) the wings are done; if not, keep braising. If the sauce is nearly dry and the meat is not cooked, add a little hot water so there is always liquid in the bottom of the pan.
6. When the green onion knot turns yellow, lift it out together with the ginger. Uncover and reduce the sauce. Start on medium heat, then lower the heat and turn the wings gently once the sauce has clearly reduced and the bubbles get denser.
7. When only a thin layer of sauce is left in the pan, the wings look glossy and the sauce drips slowly when you lift one, turn off the heat. Taste a piece of chicken with some sauce, then decide whether to add the rest of the salt. When plating, spoon the glossy sauce from the bottom of the pan over the wings.

## Key points

- **Skin sticks to the pan and won't turn golden**: the surface is too wet, or the wings were moved before the skin set (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Dry them well, keep them in a single layer, and flip only once the edges have colour.
- **Sauce is thick but the meat is not cooked**: the pan is too wide and evaporation too fast, so heat has not reached the centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Add hot water and keep braising, and confirm the wings are cooked before reducing.
- **Sauce tastes bitter and the wings turn sticky and hard**: the sauce was reduced too far and the sugar kept heating on a dry pan. Lower the heat as the sauce starts to cling, and take the wings out while the sauce can still flow.
- **Suddenly very salty at the end**: as the water cooks off, the soy sauce and salt concentrate with it (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Add the salt in two parts, and save the second part until after the sauce has reduced.
