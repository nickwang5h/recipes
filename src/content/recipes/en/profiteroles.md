*Profiteroles* are a French dessert of small choux puffs filled with vanilla ice cream and covered with hot chocolate sauce. The shell is crisp and tender, with cold ice cream inside and warm sauce on top. The shells must cool completely first so the ice cream does not melt while filling, and the sauce goes on last. The recipe makes about 18, 3 per person.

The dough first scalds the flour into a mass, so the [starch gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/); once the eggs are worked in, the batter becomes stretchy and can hold steam. In the oven, water turns to steam and pushes the inside open, and the [proteins in the egg and flour set with heat](/principles/protein-coagulation/), leaving the hollow. After the puffs rise they must keep baking for the shell to hold up.

## Steps

1. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). In a small pot put 240 ml water, 85 g butter, the salt and 1 tsp sugar, and heat until the butter is completely melted and the liquid boils; take it off the heat at once.
2. Pour in all the flour at once and stir with a wooden spoon until no dry flour is left. Return to medium heat and keep pressing and stirring for about 1–2 minutes, until the dough comes together in one mass, leaves the sides of the pot, and a thin film forms on the bottom.
3. Transfer the dough to a bowl and spread it out and turn it until warm and no longer giving off obvious steam. Add the beaten eggs in batches, mixing each in completely before adding the next; add the last part slowly and stop when the batter is glossy and a lifted spatula lets it hang in a wide, thick inverted triangle.
4. On a baking sheet lined with parchment, pipe about 18 mounds of batter 3.5 cm across, leaving space between them. Press down the pointed tops lightly with a wet finger, put in the middle of the oven and bake for about 20 minutes, until clearly puffed and golden on the surface; do not open the door during this first part.
5. Lower to 190°C (375°F) and bake about 10 minutes more, until the shell is golden and firm. Take them out, pierce a small hole in the side of each puff, turn off the oven, leave the door open a crack and put them back for about 10 minutes, then move to a wire rack to cool. Cut one open to check: the inside should have no sticky, wet raw batter.
6. In another pot put the chocolate, 190 ml water, the sugar and vanilla extract, and stir over low heat until the chocolate melts, then simmer gently for about 20–25 minutes, until it thickens to lightly coat a spoon. Off the heat, stir in the cream and 10 g butter, and let it cool to warm.
7. At serving time, cut the puffs in half crosswise, fill each with about 25 g ice cream, and set the tops back on. Plate, spoon over the chocolate sauce, and serve at once.

## Key points

- **The batter spreads flat and bakes low and collapsed**: too much egg was added, and the consistency cannot hold its shape. Add the last of the egg in small amounts and stop at the inverted triangle stage (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **They deflate soon after coming out of the oven**: the shell had not set, or there was still a lot of steam inside. Bake to a deep golden colour, vent with a hole, and then check the inside; do not take them out based on the rise alone (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **They go soft soon after filling**: warm shells melt the ice cream, and moisture gets into the shell. Fill only completely cooled shells, spoon on the sauce last, and eat as soon as they are plated (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
