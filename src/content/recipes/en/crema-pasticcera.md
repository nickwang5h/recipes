*Crema pasticcera* is the workhorse cream of Italian pastry: egg yolks, sugar and milk cooked with a little starch until thick and glossy, so it holds a heap on a spoon. It fills sponge cakes and fruit tarts and goes into *zuppa inglese*. Italian cooks call it "pastry cook's cream" to tell it apart from *crema* made without starch, which is thin enough to pour.

The difference is the starch. A custard without starch thickens only as the yolk [proteins coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and if it gets too hot it curdles (see [crème anglaise](/recipes/creme-anglaise/)). With starch, the starch takes up water and [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), thickening the cream further and letting it stand more heat, so it can start over high heat and keep cooking for a few minutes after it thickens. It is often what goes between the layers of a sliced [Pan di Spagna](/recipes/pan-di-spagna/).

## Steps

1. Put the egg yolks and sugar in a heavy saucepan and stir with a wooden spoon until smooth, with no grains of sugar left.
2. Add the starch and stir it in.
3. Pour in the milk a little at a time, stirring as you go, until there are no lumps.
4. Set over high heat and stir constantly, keeping the spoon on the bottom of the pan.
5. As soon as the cream starts to steam, turn the heat down to low.
6. Keep stirring until it is clearly thick.
7. Once thick, stir it over low heat for another 8–10 minutes. Before taking it off the heat, check the centre: at least 74°C (165°F).
8. Take it off the heat, add a few drops of vanilla extract and stir it in.
9. Pour it into a bowl chilled in advance and whisk it quickly so it cools fast, until it is smooth and glossy.
10. Press plastic wrap directly onto the surface, refrigerate once cool and use within 2–3 days; frozen, it keeps a month.

## Key points

- **It turned lumpy**: too much milk went in at once, or the heat wasn't turned down once it steamed, so the yolk set on the bottom of the pan first (see [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Add the milk gradually, turn the heat down as soon as it steams and keep stirring the bottom; if it has already turned lumpy, push it through a fine sieve.
- **It is too thin to hold a heap**: it wasn't cooked long enough after thickening, so the starch hasn't fully gelatinized (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Stir it over low heat a few minutes more, until it holds a heap on a spoon.
- **It is firm, like a pudding**: cornstarch sets firmer than rice starch. For a softer filling, use the same weight of rice starch.
- **A skin formed on top**: it cooled uncovered, or the plastic wrap wasn't touching the surface. Press the wrap onto the surface while it is still hot.
