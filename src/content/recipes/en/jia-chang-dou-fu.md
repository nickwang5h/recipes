Home-style tofu is fried first and then braised. The salty heat of doubanjiang and the fragrance of ginger and garlic cling to the golden crust, while the inside stays soft. The carrot and green pepper are the supporting vegetables, and the tofu is the main ingredient. Buy firm tofu and cut it into thick slices so it can stand up to pan-frying and the final toss.

When tofu is pan-fried, the surface water has to evaporate first, and only then does it brown more easily (see the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/)). Patting the tofu dry, laying it in a single layer and letting it set before turning works better than pushing it around with a spatula. The slightly rough crust holds the fine doubanjiang and a little of the seasoning sauce, so a gentle toss at the end coats it with flavour.

## Steps

1. Prepare the tofu. Pour off the packing water and drain the tofu on a plate lined with paper towel for 5 minutes, until no more water seeps from the bottom. Cut it into squares about 1 cm thick and 4 cm wide, then cut each diagonally into triangles. Press the cut faces lightly with paper towel to dry them.
2. Prepare the vegetables. Cut the carrot into diamond slices about 2 mm thick, seed and slice the green pepper, and cut the ginger, garlic and white part of the green onion. Stir together the light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and sugar. Chop the doubanjiang finely and set it aside separately, so you are not hunting for seasonings after the tofu is fried.
3. Fry the crust. Add 1.5 tbsp of oil to a wide skillet and preheat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil ripples. Lay the tofu in a single layer and fry for 3–4 minutes, until the bottom is golden and it slides free with a light push. Flip and fry for 3 minutes more, until the other side is golden too, and set it aside.
4. Stir-fry the doubanjiang. Add the remaining 0.5 tbsp of oil to the pan, turn the heat to low and stir-fry the doubanjiang for 0.5–1 minute, until the oil turns red and the sauce smells rich. Add the ginger, garlic and white part of the green onion and stir-fry about 1 minute, until fragrant and the garlic has not burnt.
5. Cook the vegetables. Turn the heat to medium, add the carrot and green pepper and stir-fry for 2 minutes, until the carrot slices bend slightly and the pepper is bright in colour.
6. Braise and coat. Pour in the mixed seasoning sauce and the tofu. Over medium heat, push and toss gently for 1–2 minutes, until the sauce bubbles and coats both sides of the tofu evenly, leaving only a thin layer of moist sauce in the pan. Use a spatula to scoop up from the bottom and turn, keeping the triangles whole, and serve at once.

## Key points

- **Tofu sticks to the pan and breaks when turned**: the surface is too wet, or the crust has not set. As in [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/), pat it dry and spread it out, and turn only when the bottom is golden and moves with a light push.
- **Dry and hard outside**: it was fried too long and lost too much surface water. A light golden [browned crust](/principles/maillard-reaction/) is enough, since it will be braised afterwards.
- **Bitter doubanjiang and burnt garlic**: the oil was too hot when the doubanjiang went in. Turn the heat to low and let the doubanjiang fry open in the oil (see [aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)), then move on to the next step as soon as the fragrance comes out.
- **Watery and crumbled after packing in a lunch box**: too much sauce was left from the braise, and the tofu was pressed repeatedly when packed. Reduce until the sauce just clings (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), place it gently in a shallow box and refrigerate promptly. Reheat the next day to 74°C (165°F) at the centre.
