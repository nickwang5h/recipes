*Canh chua cá* is Vietnamese sour fish soup; in the south it often uses tamarind, pineapple, tomato, okra and bean sprouts. Tamarind gives a lingering sourness, fresh pineapple adds fruit aroma and sweetness, and fish sauce ties the soup together. Sweet and sour set each other off; see [acid](/principles/acid/). When tasting, take a spoonful that includes fish and vegetables.

Fish cooks easily, okra should stay tender and whole, and bean sprouts only need cooking until they lose their raw taste. Adding them in stages gets all these textures right together; see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). The rice paddy herb *ngò om* has a bright citrus fragrance and goes in last; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

## Steps

1. Strain the tamarind liquid. Break up the pulp, add the warm extraction water, and knead and press it to a thick consistency, then push it through a sieve to get the liquid and discard the hard seeds and fibres. Stir well and measure out 60 ml; refrigerate the extra. It should taste clearly sour.
2. Prepare the main ingredients. Check the fish fillets for stray bones and cut them into pieces of similar size. Cut the fresh pineapple into small pieces, trim and cut the okra, cut the tomato into wedges, and drain the bean sprouts and herbs. Lay them out separately, so they do not all go in at once.
3. Make the sour broth. Heat the oil in the pot and stir-fry the onion over medium heat for about 4 minutes, until soft, translucent and fragrant. Add the salt, sugar, fish sauce, tamarind liquid and the soup water, bring to a boil, then turn down to a gentle simmer.
4. Cook the fish and pineapple. Put the fish and pineapple into the soup and keep it at small bubbles for about 5 minutes, until the centre of the thickest piece is opaque and flakes apart when nudged, and the pineapple is still whole. Push gently with a ladle so you do not break up the fish.
5. Add the okra. Cook for about 2 minutes, until it is still green and a fork tip goes in, but the pieces still stand up. Do not cook it long; the okra will go limp.
6. Add the finishing ingredients. Put in the tomato, bean sprouts and chopped rice paddy herb, and turn off the heat when the sprouts have lost their raw taste but are still crisp and the tomato has just heated through.
7. Season and ladle into bowls. Taste the soup with a piece of fish and a little vegetable. If it is too sour, add a little hot water first, and if it is still not right, add more sugar or fish sauce. Sprinkle on the cilantro and serve right away with white rice.

## Key points

- **Sourness is sharp and covers the fish flavour**: tamarind products differ in concentration (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Measure by freshly made pulp liquid; with bottled concentrate, dilute it and taste first rather than pouring in an equal amount.
- **Fish falls apart and okra turns mushy**: a rolling boil and long cooking dry out the fish and leave the vegetables without support (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)). Cook the fish first, add vegetables later, and stop once it is cooked at small bubbles.
- **Herbs have no flavour and the sprouts go limp**: they went in too early and the fresh aroma was lost to heat (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Add the herbs and sprouts only at the end, and eat promptly after ladling.
