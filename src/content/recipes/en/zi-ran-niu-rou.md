Cumin beef has a punchy aroma, tender slices of meat, and green pepper strips that stay crisp, with no pool of thin liquid at the bottom of the pan. The main flavour is coarsely crushed cumin: once the seeds are cracked, the inside is exposed and the aroma comes out faster in hot oil. Whole seeds and ground powder behave differently in the pan (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).

Tenderness starts with the cut: slice thinly against the grain to shorten the long muscle fibres (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Light soy sauce and starch are mixed onto the surface of the slices, and as they heat the [starch gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) into a thin coating. Mix the coating evenly, and spread the slices out as soon as they hit the pan, so a few slices do not stick into a thick clump.

## Steps

1. Chop the bird's eye chili and crush it coarsely together with the cumin seeds. The cumin only needs to be cracked, not ground to a fine powder. Put both in one small bowl so they can go in at once during the short stir-fry.
2. Remove the stem and seeds from the green pepper and cut into strips of similar width; cut the green onion into sections. Drain the washed pepper, because wet strips slow down the heat when they go into the pan.
3. Thaw the beef, pat it dry, remove any obvious sinew and membrane, and slice thinly against the grain running along the surface. Mix in all the light soy sauce first, then the starch, then 5 ml of oil, and marinate in the refrigerator for 30 minutes.
4. Heat the wok, add the remaining 10 ml of oil and stir-fry the green onion sections until fragrant. Add the beef and spread and separate it as quickly as you can. Turn it only after the slices touching the pan have changed colour, so a clump of meat does not heat only on its outside.
5. When most of the surface of the beef has changed colour, sprinkle in the cumin and chili. Add half the cumin first and toss so the pieces coat the beef and the oil in the pan. When you smell the aroma, add the green pepper. Do not leave the spices in an empty wok to scorch.
6. Add the green pepper strips and stir-fry until the colour is bright and they are no longer raw and stiff. Cut open one of the thicker slices: if the centre is cooked and the cut face does not look like raw meat, taste a piece, then add more cumin and a little salt.
7. Toss over high heat for about 1 minute, until the seasoning coats the slices. If the beef is already cooked, stop earlier. Off the heat, toss for about 30 seconds more and plate right away. Do not let it sit in the hot wok.

## Key points

- **Slices are hard to bite through**: slicing with the grain leaves long fibres (see [knife work and cooking](/principles/knife-work/)). Look at the grain before cutting, and cut the blade across it.
- **Meat sticks together in a clump with a thick coating**: the coating was not mixed evenly, or the slices piled up in the pan, and the [starch gelatinized](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) and glued the slices together. Mix the marinade until there is no dry starch, spread the slices as soon as they go into the pan, and cook the meat in batches if your pan is small.
- **Bitter cumin, gritty mouthfuls**: powder or small crumbs sat too long on a dry, hot pan (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). Crush it coarsely, add it in portions, add the green pepper as soon as the aroma comes up, and check whether to add more before serving.
