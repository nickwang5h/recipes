Dubu jorim is a common Korean tofu side dish. Tofu slices are pan-fried to a light gold and then coated in a soy sauce glaze with green onion, garlic and chili flakes. It goes with rice, with a mild heat and a clear salty, savoury taste. The finished tofu is slightly firmer on the outside and still soft inside, with a little sauce left on the plate, and it holds together when picked up.

Pan-frying dries the tofu surface and builds fragrance through the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/); simmering afterward seasons it with liquid, so dry frying does not carry on and harden the whole slice. Thin slices heat quickly (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)), so after frying they need only a short simmer, to let the seasoning cling to the surface and enter the outer layer.

## Steps

1. Pour off the packaging water, cut the tofu into rectangular slices about 1.2 cm thick and lay them flat on paper towels. Press the two sides gently dry, until there are no obvious water drops on the surface; do not press with a weight.
2. Stir together the soy sauce, water, toasted sesame oil, sugar, chili flakes, toasted sesame seeds, minced garlic and green onion. The fine seasonings sink to the bottom of the bowl, so stir again just before pouring it into the pan, so every slice gets about the same amount of sauce.
3. Add the vegetable oil to a large non-stick skillet and preheat over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the oil spreads easily without smoking. Add the tofu in a single layer without overlapping; if it does not fit in one pan, fry in batches so water does not collect in the pan.
4. Fry over medium to medium-high heat for 3–4 minutes, until the bottom is light golden and the edges have tightened slightly and the spatula slides steadily underneath, then flip each slice. Fry the other side for 3–4 minutes, until both sides are light golden and the centre still feels soft.
5. Turn to medium-low heat and spoon the sauce evenly over the tofu. Lift one side of each slice gently so the sauce runs underneath, and simmer for 3–4 minutes, until small bubbles keep rising at the bottom of the pan, the green onion has softened and the tofu surface has picked up the red-brown seasoning.
6. Flip the slices one by one and simmer for another 1–2 minutes, spooning the liquid from the bottom of the pan back over the tofu as you go. When the sauce is slightly thick and clings to the tofu while the bottom of the pan is still moist, turn off the heat, transfer gently to a plate and serve warm with rice.

## Key points

- **The tofu sticks and breaks when flipped**: the surface was not dried, or the bottom has not yet formed a fried crust that can be lifted. Choose firm tofu, fry to light gold before flipping, and do not keep poking at it with chopsticks. See the [Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
- **The skin is thick and hard and the inside is dry too**: it was fried too long in an attempt to get a dark colour. Fry each side only to light golden, then simmer briefly in the sauce. See [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The sauce is scorched, bitter and too salty**: a small amount of liquid dries up quickly, leaving the sugar, garlic and chili flakes on the hot bottom of the pan. Lower the heat once the sauce goes in, keep the bottom moist, and take the pan off the heat as soon as the sauce clings. See [Salt: Seasoning, Osmosis and Curing](/principles/salt/).
