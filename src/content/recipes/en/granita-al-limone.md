*Granita al limone* is the common Italian lemon ice, made from only fresh juice, sugar and water frozen into small crystals you can scoop. It is cold first, then comes the sourness and fragrance of lemon. The sugar softens the sourness without removing it (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). The yellow peel supplies aroma, and the fresh juice goes in after the syrup has cooled.

Sugar also decides the texture. Sugar dissolved in water lowers the freezing point, so at the same low temperature some of the water always stays unfrozen. With too little sugar the ice is hard; with too much it is slow to set. A shallow pan loses heat quickly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), and scraping the freshly formed ice loose again and again as it freezes keeps the crystals fine and even.

## Steps

1. Wash the lemons and peel the yellow zest of one lemon thinly, avoiding the white layer underneath. Squeeze the juice, remove the seeds, strain out the larger pieces of pulp and weigh out 250 g of fresh juice. Lemons vary in size and yield, so go by the weighed amount and keep any extra juice for another use.
2. Put the water, sugar and lemon zest in a small pot and stir over medium heat until the sugar is completely dissolved and the liquid is clear and just starting to boil, then turn off the heat. Do not keep cooking it down, because once water evaporates the ratio changes.
3. Cover and let the syrup cool to room temperature, take out the zest, then stir in the fresh lemon juice. Chill in the fridge until all of the liquid is cold. Hot syrup must not go straight into the freezer, and the fresh juice should not be cooked for long.
4. Pour into a shallow freezer-safe metal pan or food container, with the liquid no deeper than about 2 cm, cover and put in the freezer. Dividing it between two shallow containers freezes more evenly than putting it all in one deep one.
5. Check about every 30 minutes. When ice starts to form at the edges and bottom, scrape the ice layer off with a fork, break up the small pieces and stir them back into the liquid that is not yet frozen. Each time after that, scrape all the way to the bottom and corners, not just the surface.
6. Keep freezing and scraping for about 4–5 hours, until the whole pan is fine, damp ice crystals that can be heaped up. If there is still a pool of clear liquid, freeze for another round. When done, scoop into chilled small cups and eat right away.
7. Store leftover granita sealed in the freezer. Left for a long time it sets into a hard block; take it out and let it warm slightly, and when a fork can scrape it, scrape it loose again. Do not wait until the whole container melts to water.

## Key points

- **Frozen into a big block that a fork cannot scrape**: the liquid is too deep, the scraping intervals are too long, or the sugar was cut too far (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Keep the original ratio, freeze in shallow pans and start scraping as soon as the first layer of ice appears.
- **Hard at the edges, liquid in the centre the whole time**: the edges freeze first while the centre is still water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Scrape the ice off the edges and corners completely, mix it with the liquid in the centre, and keep freezing until the whole pan is the same.
- **Bitter taste, only sour with little fragrance**: white pith got into the peel, or the fresh juice was cooked for too long and the aroma escaped (see [acid](/principles/acid/)). Take only the yellow peel, and add the fresh juice after the syrup has cooled.
