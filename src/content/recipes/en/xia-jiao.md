Har gow is a Cantonese dim sum dumpling with shrimp and bamboo shoot in a thin, translucent wrapper. Wheat starch is the starch of wheat, and boiling water [gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) it first, so the dry powder can come together into a dough that can be filled. Adding potato starch, with lard and working while the dough is warm, makes the wrapper easy to press thin without cracking.

Keep the shrimp in small pieces so you can feel the springiness when you bite. When heated, the [proteins in the filling coagulate](/principles/protein-coagulation/), and steaming too long makes them tighten and lose water. Make the dumplings similar in size, leave space between them, and steam them over boiling water so the wrapper and filling finish cooking together.

## Steps

1. Pat half of the shrimp lightly and chop it fine, and cut the other half into small pieces. Stir in the same direction with the oyster sauce, vegetable oil, toasted sesame oil, salt, sugar, white pepper and minced ginger for about 2–3 minutes, until the filling is slightly sticky and the shrimp pieces hold together. Mix in the bamboo shoot, cover and refrigerate for about 20 minutes, until the filling is cold and firm.
2. Mix the wheat starch and potato starch. Boil the 160 ml of water in a small pot over high heat for about 1–2 minutes until it is rolling, and immediately pour it over the starch, stirring quickly with chopsticks as you pour until no loose dry powder remains. Cover and rest for about 5 minutes, until the dough is warm and you can handle it with your hands.
3. Mix in the lard and knead for about 2 minutes, until the dough is smooth and can be rolled into a rope without crumbling. If the edges still crack, add 5 ml at a time from the reserved 10 ml of boiling water and knead again. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for about 10 minutes, until the surface is supple and the pinched seams hold together.
4. Divide the dough into 18 portions, and divide the shrimp filling into 18 portions too. Lightly oil the cutting board and the side of the knife. Take one portion at a time and roll it into a ball, then press it outward with the flat side of a wide knife into a thin round wrapper about 8 cm across. At home you can use a small rolling pin instead of the wide knife, rolling the edge thin and leaving the centre a little thicker.
5. Put one portion of filling in the centre of the wrapper, pleat only one side in a continuous row, press the other side flat against it and close it into a half-moon shape. Fill each wrapper as soon as you press it, keeping the rest of the dough covered. Place them on a perforated steamer paper in the tray, about 3 cm apart.
6. Add water to the steamer and heat over high heat for about 8–10 minutes until it is rolling. Steam the dumplings in two batches. Cover and keep a steady steam, steaming each batch for about 6 minutes, until the wrappers are translucent and the shrimp turn pink and opaque; the filling of the largest one reaches 74°C (165°F). If they are not done, steam for 1 more minute and check again.
7. After taking them out, let them rest for about 1 minute, until the wrapper firms up slightly, then lift them gently. Eat promptly, while the wrapper is still soft and glossy and you can see pieces of shrimp in the filling.

## Key points

- **Dough is a thin paste and cannot be wrapped**: the water was not hot enough, so the starch did not gelatinize in time (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Scald the starch with freshly boiled water, not warm water.
- **Wrappers crack at the edge and break when pleated**: the dough lacks water or was exposed too long (see [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Fill while it is warm, keep the remaining dough under a damp cloth, and knead in water 5 ml at a time.
- **Filling is hard and the dumplings stick together in a sheet**: they were steamed too long or packed too close (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/) and [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Check doneness by size, steam in batches, and lift them gently after a short rest out of the steamer.
