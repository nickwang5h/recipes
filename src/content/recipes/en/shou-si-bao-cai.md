Hand-torn cabbage is soft in the leaf and crisp in the rib. Remove the hard core, tear the thick ribs finer, and stir-fry the cabbage on its own first, then combine it with the pork belly in the wok, so that piling everything in at once doesn't drag the pan temperature down. When steam leaves the pan quickly, the leaves pick up the flavour of the oil more easily (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

The fat of the pork belly makes the leaves glossy, and a little Chinkiang vinegar at the end adds a bright sour note. Cabbage releases more water once it is salted. To keep it crisp, drain it well after washing and spread it out in the pan so the steam escapes. For what salt does, see [salt](/principles/salt/).

## Steps

1. Halve the cabbage, remove the hard core in the middle, and tear it by hand into pieces of similar size. Tear the thick ribs finer so the thick parts cook through in a short stir-fry, and don't tear the thin leaves into crumbs.
2. Wash the cabbage in water with 2 g of salt dissolved in it, rinse well with clean water, and drain thoroughly. Loosen the leaves and shake out the water trapped in the folds. The cabbage must not go into the wok wet.
3. Cut the ginger into thin slices, smash the garlic, and cut the bird's eye chilies and garlic shoots into pieces. Cut the pork belly into even thin slices and pat the surface dry. Have all the seasonings ready, so you don't leave the wok to look for them during the stir-fry.
4. Add 30 ml of oil to the wok, add the cabbage, and stir-fry over high heat for about 1 minute. Add the 3 g of salt for the stir-fry in small amounts and keep stir-frying for about 2 minutes, until the leaves are soft but the ribs are still crisp, then take it out. If the wok is small, do it in batches.
5. Add another 30 ml of oil to the wok, spread in the pork belly, and stir-fry over high heat for about 1 minute. Cook until the slices are cooked through and the fat starts to render.
6. Add the ginger, garlic, bird's eye chilies and garlic shoots, and stir-fry for about 1 minute until fragrant. It is fine if the edges of the pork turn slightly golden; don't let the garlic and chilies burn, and lower the heat if it is too strong.
7. Return the cabbage to the wok and add the light soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, Chinkiang vinegar and chicken bouillon powder. Stir-fry together over high heat for about 2 minutes, until the seasoning is even and the steam at the bottom of the pan has cleared, then plate. If the cabbage is crisp enough, take it out earlier.

## Key points

- **Cabbage turns soft like boiled cabbage**: the water was not drained off, or the wok was too full. [Heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/) is used up by heavy evaporation. Stir-fry in batches, and take the cabbage out as soon as the leaves are soft and the ribs crisp.
- **Cabbage releases water after salting**: [salt](/principles/salt/) draws water out of the vegetable. Add the salt for the stir-fry in small amounts, and don't leave the pre-fried cabbage sitting in the hot pan.
- **Vinegar taste is faint and the pork fat feels heavy**: the vinegar went in too early and was stirred for too long. Following [acid in seasoning](/principles/acid/), keep the Chinkiang vinegar for the final stage of combining, and plate promptly once it is mixed in.
