Dongpo pork is a signature dish of Hangzhou. Skin-on pork belly is cut into big squares and slowly braised with Shaoxing wine, soy sauce and sugar until tender. The wine fragrance and the salty-sweet sauce settle between skin and meat; the finished pork can be pulled apart with a spoon and still has distinct layers when lifted out.

The soft, yielding texture comes from the collagen in the skin and connective tissue, which slowly turns into gelatin in sustained moist heat (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). After the meat is cooked it keeps braising, until the skin has no chewiness left. The green onion and ginger on the bottom keep the meat from touching the pot and scorching, and with the lid on tight and a gentle simmer, less water evaporates (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Bring the blanching water to a boil over medium heat, which takes about 8–10 minutes, until the surface is rolling. Add the whole piece of pork belly and cook for 1 minute, until the outside turns white and foam rises. Lift it out and drain, then spread it out for about 5 minutes until you can hold it firmly, and cut it into squares about 6–7 cm on a side, keeping the skin intact.
2. Spread the green onion lengths over the bottom of a heavy-bottomed pot and lay the ginger slices evenly over the green onion. Arrange the pork squares skin side down in a single layer, pour in the Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce and dark soy sauce, and scatter the rock sugar over. Choose a pot that fits the squares snugly, so the liquid surrounds the lower half of the pieces.
3. Cover the pot and heat over medium heat for about 3–5 minutes, until the liquid starts to bubble, then immediately turn to the lowest heat. Braise covered for 90 minutes, with the surface only bubbling lightly at the edge; the skin gradually turns reddish brown while the squares keep their shape. Check the liquid level partway through to keep the bottom from drying out.
4. Support each square with a wide spatula and turn it over so the skin faces up. Cover tightly and braise over low heat for another 90 minutes, until a chopstick goes easily through the skin and the lean meat and the squares jiggle when lightly pressed. If the skin is still tough, braise another 15 minutes; if the liquid is nearly dry, first check whether the lid is on tight and whether the heat is too high.
5. Lift the pork onto a deep plate. Strain out the green onion and ginger from the pot and skim off the surface fat. If the braising liquid is still thin, reduce it in a small pot over medium heat for 5–8 minutes, until it thinly coats the back of a spoon. Taste for saltiness, stop reducing once it is enough, pour it over the pork and serve hot.

## Key points

- **Meat is cooked but the skin can't be chewed**: the [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/) conversion is not finished. Keep braising gently with the lid on, judging by a chopstick going through the skin; reducing the sauce over high heat will only boil off the water first.
- **Meat breaks apart and the fat and lean layers separate**: the liquid boiled too hard, or only one layer of meat was gripped when turning. Keep it at a gentle simmer (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)), and lift from underneath when turning and when taking it out.
- **Sauce is thick and too salty**: the salt in the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and any salted cooking wine concentrates as the sauce reduces (see [salt](/principles/salt/)). Prefer unsalted yellow wine, taste the sauce near the end, stop once it is thick and smooth, and pour over only a single layer of sauce.
