In braised noodles with green beans, the noodles soak up the broth from the meat and vegetables, soft with some chew, and the green beans are braised until they taste of the dish. The vegetables and pork go on the bottom of the pot and the fresh noodles are laid on top. Steam under the lid cooks the noodles, and the broth drizzled back in stages gives them water and flavour (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/) and [Starch Gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Do not stir the noodles down into the bottom at first, so they are less likely to stick and the steam can get through.

This is a generous meal for one person. Always keep some liquid in the bottom of the pot, so the noodles can absorb water as they cook; if the pot is wide or the noodles are dry, add water. The green beans must be cooked through in boiling broth before the noodles go on; do not count on the braising time to cook them.

## Steps

1. Trim both ends of the green beans, remove the strings and cut them into 5–6 cm lengths. Cut the green onion into pieces, shred the ginger finely, and smash and mince the garlic. Slice the pork belly thinly and shake the fresh noodles loose.
2. Add the oil to the pot and heat it over medium heat until it runs freely. Stir-fry the green onion and ginger for about 5 seconds, until fragrant, then add the pork slices. Leave them for about 5 seconds, then stir-fry until all the slices have changed colour and drizzle in the light soy sauce and stir to combine.
3. Add the dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, thirteen-spice powder, the optional chicken bouillon powder, a little salt and the green beans, and stir-fry for about 2 minutes. Add 150 ml of hot water, bring to a boil and cover, and cook the green beans until completely done. If the liquid runs low, add hot water along the side of the pot and keep it boiling.
4. When the green beans are done, scoop out about half of the broth and leave the meat and vegetables in the pot. Check that there is enough liquid on the bottom, then lay the noodles loosely and flat on top of the vegetables, cover and braise over medium heat for about 5 minutes.
5. Uncover and drizzle the reserved broth over the noodles, spoonful by spoonful and evenly, then cover and braise for about 3 minutes more. Listen to the pot: you should hear a wet bubbling sound, and if you hear the sound of dry frying, add hot water right away.
6. Take a noodle from the middle and taste it. The centre should have no hard core and should be soft with some chew. If it is not done, add a little hot water and keep braising. Once done, sprinkle in the minced garlic and the optional MSG, loosen the noodles with chopsticks and toss gently with the meat and vegetables.
7. If there is a lot of liquid, uncover briefly and toss to reduce it until the noodles are moist but there is no standing liquid at the bottom. Taste to decide whether to add the rest of the salt, turn off the heat and plate.

## Key points

- **The bottom of the noodles is scorched and the top is still hard**: the water has boiled away and there is not enough steam. Keep liquid at the bottom of the pot (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)), add hot water the moment you hear dry frying, and do not raise the heat to rush it.
- **The noodles clump and are undercooked in places**: they were laid too tightly and the broth was not drizzled evenly, so [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) was incomplete. Shake the noodles loose when laying them, drizzle the broth in stages, and taste the middle before deciding whether to keep braising.
- **The green beans are still crisp and taste raw**: the noodles went on before the beans were cooked through (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Cook the beans in boiling broth until soft and done first; do not lay the noodles just because the beans have turned green.
- **The aroma is bitter and it tastes too salty**: the spice powder was dry-fried too long, or the salty seasonings add up. Add water right after the powder goes in (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)), and keep part of the salt until you taste the cooked noodles.
