Char siu bao are a Cantonese dim sum bun: snow-white, soft and slightly sweet, with tops that split into several petals to show char siu in a thick gravy. Yeast raises the dough first, then baking powder gives a second push in the steamer that splits the top (see [leavening](/principles/leavening/)).

Low-protein flour forms weak [gluten](/principles/gluten/), so the dough is tender rather than chewy. The gravy for the filling is cooked very thick; once its [starch gelatinizes](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) it binds the diced pork into one mass that does not run when you wrap it. Dim sum restaurants traditionally use a sourdough-style starter with lye water; at home, dry yeast and baking powder still give split tops. You can make [honey char siu](/recipes/mi-zhi-cha-shao/) first for the filling. Makes 16 small buns, 4 per person.

## Steps

1. Stir the sugar and yeast into the warm water and leave for about 5 minutes, until a layer of fine foam forms on top. If it does not foam, use a fresh packet of yeast.
2. Mix the flour and baking powder in a large bowl, pour in the yeast water and stir with a spatula until no dry flour is left. Knead in a stand mixer, low and then medium speed, for about 10 minutes, until smooth. Add the oil and knead 5 minutes more, until the dough is soft and shiny and sticks neither to your hands nor to the bowl.
3. Put the dough in an oiled bowl, cover and leave somewhere warm for about 2 hours, until it has grown to about three times its size and a cut shows many small holes.
4. While the dough rises, make the filling: put the chicken stock, hoisin, oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, garlic and five spice in a small pot, bring to a boil over medium heat, then cook 1 minute over medium-low. Take it off the heat for 3–5 minutes, pour in the stirred slurry, and return to medium-low, stirring constantly, until the sauce is thick enough that a line drawn across the bottom of the pot with a spatula does not close up at once. Off the heat, stir in the char siu and let it cool.
5. Knead the dough lightly to knock out the large bubbles and divide it into 16 pieces of about 27 g. Roll each into a 6–7 cm round, thick in the middle and thin at the edge. Put a heaped tablespoon of filling in the centre, pinch two opposite edges together, then the other two, and finally gather the four corners loosely at the top without sealing them tight.
6. Set each bun on a 7 cm square of parchment, gathered side up, in the steamer, at least 5 cm apart. Cover with plastic wrap and let them rise 20–30 minutes, until clearly plumper and slow to spring back when pressed.
7. Fill the steamer pot with about 7 cm of water and bring it to a full boil over high heat before putting the basket on. Cover and steam over medium-high heat for 10 minutes without lifting the lid. They are done when the tops have split and the dough is snow-white and dry; break open the largest one and the centre of the filling should be 74°C (165°F). After turning off the heat, open the lid away from you to let the steam escape, and let them cool 5 minutes before eating.

## Key points

- **Tops do not split**: the corners were pinched too tight, or the buns went in before the water was at a full boil. They rise slowly and the top never bursts open (see [leavening](/principles/leavening/)). Only gather the corners, don't seal them, and put the basket on once the water is boiling hard.
- **Dough is hard, not fluffy**: the yeast was used without foaming, or the dough did not rise enough, so there was too little gas (see [leavening](/principles/leavening/)). Check that the yeast water foams, then that the dough reaches about three times its size; with bread flour the buns come out chewy (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Filling runs and leaks out the bottom**: the gravy was not cooked thick enough, or the filling was wrapped while still warm (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Cook it until the line on the bottom of the pot stays open, and wrap only once it is cold.
- **For lunch boxes**: cool the steamed buns, box them and refrigerate; the next day steam or microwave them until the centre of the filling reaches 74°C (165°F).
