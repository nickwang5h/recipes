*Kimchi-bokkeumbap* stir-fries cooked rice with sour napa cabbage kimchi, and is an everyday Korean bowl of rice. The kimchi supplies sourness, the gochujang adds saltiness, sweetness and body, and the toasted sesame oil added just before serving leaves the aroma of toasted sesame. Well-fermented [napa cabbage kimchi](/recipes/baechu-kimchi/) works best, since freshly mixed kimchi does not yet have this sourness.

The starch in chilled rice retrogrades and the grains firm up, which makes them easier to separate when stir-frying (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)). Freshly cooked rice also works if you spread it thin to let the steam off first. The kimchi juice seasons the dish, and the water in the pan has to cook off at the end. The finished rice should be moist and glossy, with no pool of red liquid when you push it aside (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).

## Steps

1. Gently break up any big clumps in the cooked rice. Take the kimchi out of the jar, first set aside 60 ml of its juice, then drain the leaves and chop them, and cut the thick white stems into small pieces too.
2. Stir the gochujang, kimchi juice and water together until the paste dissolves. Making it a liquid first lets it coat the grains evenly in the pan and avoids patches that are especially salty.
3. Heat the vegetable oil in a wide frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the kimchi and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the leaves are hot through and clearly sour-smelling, and the free liquid in the pan starts to decrease.
4. Add the rice and the mixed red sauce. Break up the clumps with the back of the spatula, then turn from the bottom of the pan. Colour all the grains an even red, and spread out any thick pile so you are not only turning the top.
5. Keep stir-frying for about 7 minutes, until the grains are hot through, no liquid collects on the bottom of the pan, and pushing the rice aside shows a dry pan surface. If you used chilled leftover rice, the centre should reach 74°C (165°F); if the pan is small, fry in two batches.
6. Stir in the toasted sesame oil and take the pan off the heat at once. Divide among bowls, scatter on the green onion, toasted sesame seeds and laver strips, and eat hot. Do not leave it in the hot pan to keep steaming dry.

## Key points

- **Rice sticks in clumps**: the rice is too wet, or it was not broken up before going in the pan. Let freshly cooked rice release its steam first and break up big clumps of chilled rice, and stir-fry by tossing rather than pressing hard (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Red liquid keeps pooling in the pan**: the food is piled too thick and evaporation cannot keep up. Spread it thinner or cook in batches, and take it off the heat once the liquid has been absorbed and cooked off (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **No sesame aroma**: the sesame oil went in too early and the fragrance was lost in long heating. Stir it in last, then take the pan off the heat at once (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)).
- **Leftover rice left on the counter overnight**: cooked rice left too long at room temperature is not safe, and frying it again cannot rescue it. Portion cooked rice into shallow containers and refrigerate it promptly, and throw out any that has sat at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
