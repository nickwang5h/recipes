*Petits pois à la française* puts young peas, lettuce and small white onions in one pot and braises them with butter and very little water. The lettuce cooks along with them: the water it releases under heat tops up the bottom of the pot, and the juices then coat the surface of the peas. Served, there should be a little glossy sauce, with plump peas and soft small onions.

The lid keeps in the steam, and water and steam carry heat to the vegetables together (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). At the end, cold butter is stirred in off the heat, and stirring disperses the fat into the vegetable juices (see [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/)). The tenderness of this dish comes from gentle braising. There is little water in the pot, so the heat has to be kept low as well.

## Steps

1. Prepare the vegetables. Shell fresh peas, wash the lettuce and keep the heart whole, and peel the small onions. Tie the parsley and chervil into a bundle so they are easy to take out at the end.
2. In a heavy-bottomed pot just big enough to hold the vegetables, put the peas, small onions, lettuce, herb bundle, 100 g of the butter, the sugar and a little salt. Turn gently so the vegetables are in contact with the butter, then add 50 ml of water.
3. Cover the pot and heat over low heat until you hear a gentle bubbling in the pot. Keep it at that state and braise for about 20–25 minutes, shaking the pot now and then to redistribute the liquid at the bottom. If the bottom is dry, add a little hot water in stages.
4. Taste a pea: it should chew easily and still be whole, and a thin knife should pass through the centre of a small onion. If you are using frozen peas, braise the small onions, lettuce and herb bundle first, add the peas when the onions are almost soft all the way through, and stop once the peas are heated through and tender.
5. Remove the herb bundle. Lift out the cooked lettuce, cut it into thin strips and return it to the pot. If the juices have clearly collected in a layer, uncover and reduce them slightly, leaving a thin sauce that can still flow.
6. Take the pot off the heat, add the remaining 20 g of cold butter and fold gently until the juices turn glossy and cling to the peas. Taste, add more salt if needed, and serve immediately on warm plates.

## Key points

- **The bottom is scorched and the centres of the onions are still hard**: there was too little water or the heat was too high, so the surface dried out before the centre was heated through. Add a little hot water, cover tightly and turn the heat down. If the pot smells scorched, switch to another pot. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **The peas are wrinkled and floury-dry**: the peas were done but kept braising, or frozen peas went in at the same time as raw onions. Stop the heat according to the state of the peas, and add frozen peas later. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **A ring of butter on the plate**: there was too little vegetable juice at the finish, or the butter was stirred in while the pot was still boiling. Take it off the heat, add a little warm water and swirl gently to disperse the fat again. See [emulsification](/principles/emulsification/).
