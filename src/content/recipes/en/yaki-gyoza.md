*Yaki gyoza* are Japanese home-style pan-fried dumplings: thin wrappers filled with pork and cabbage, fried golden on the bottom and steamed through on top. The cabbage is tossed with salt and squeezed, which cuts the free water that would seep out after wrapping. The pork is mixed until sticky so it can hold the chopped vegetables together (see [salt](/principles/salt/)).

The water in the pan sets two stages. When you add water and cover, steam helps the wrapper and filling cook through (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). After you uncover and cook the water off, the bottom can lose moisture again and fry into a crisp crust. If you plate before the water is gone, even a well-fried bottom gets steamed soft.

## Steps

1. Toss the chopped cabbage with 2 g of fine salt and leave for 10 minutes, until the cabbage is soft and water has collected at the bottom of the bowl. Squeeze out the liquid in small handfuls. Prepare the shiitake, green onion, ginger and garlic as described in the ingredients.
2. Mix the ground pork with the shiitake, green onion, ginger, garlic, sake, and the sesame oil, soy sauce and black pepper for the filling for about 2–3 minutes, until sticky and able to hold together in a mass. Add the squeezed cabbage and mix until evenly distributed.
3. Put about 2 tsp of filling in the centre of each wrapper, moisten the edge with water, fold in half and pinch 4–5 pleats along one side, sealing each one firmly. Press lightly to make a flat bottom and arrange in a single layer on parchment paper. Keep unused wrappers covered so the edges don't dry out.
4. Preheat a nonstick skillet over medium heat for about 2 minutes, until the surface is warm, and add 1 tbsp of oil. Arrange about one third of the dumplings flat side down with gaps between them and fry for about 3 minutes, until the bottoms are light golden and the dumplings lift off whole.
5. Pour 60 ml of water in around the edge and cover immediately. Steam for about 3 minutes, until the wrappers are soft and most of the water in the pan is gone. Check the filling at the thickest part of one dumpling; it is cooked at 71°C (160°F). If not, add 10 ml of water and cover for 1 more minute.
6. Uncover and fry for about 1 minute more, until the standing water has cooked off completely and the sound changes from boiling water to frying oil. Drizzle 1 tsp of sesame oil around the edge and keep frying for about 1 minute, until the bottoms are deep golden and feel firm and crisp to the touch. Plate.
7. Fry the remaining two batches in the same way with the same amounts of oil and water. Stir the soy sauce and rice vinegar for the dipping sauce together and divide among 4 small dishes. Serve the dumplings straight from the pan to keep the bottoms crisp.

## Key points

- **Filling weeps, wrapper bottoms wet and sticky**: the cabbage wasn't squeezed, or the wrapped dumplings sat too long. Salt the cabbage until soft and then squeeze it, and fry the dumplings in batches right after wrapping. See [salt](/principles/salt/).
- **Burnt bottom but the filling is raw**: only the bottom was fried, and there wasn't enough steaming time or water under the lid. Do the frying and steaming as separate stages, and check the temperature in the centre of the filling before you dry off the pan. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Cooked through but no crisp bottom**: there is still standing water in the pan, or too many dumplings were packed in at once. Uncover and cook off all the water, then let the bottoms touch the hot oil, and plate promptly once they've coloured. See [the Maillard reaction](/principles/maillard-reaction/).
