*Khao phat* is Thai chicken fried rice: jasmine rice, egg, onion, tomato and gai lan, seasoned with soy sauce and fish sauce for a savoury, lightly sweet flavour, with cucumber and lime served on the side. The tomato only needs to soften slightly in the pan, and the lime is squeezed on at the table so its sour fragrance stays clear. Chili fish sauce is a table condiment, served in a small dish; do not pour the whole dish over the rice.

Whether the grains stay separate depends on their state before they go in the pan. Cooked starch [retrogrades](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) as it cools, so the grains become firmer and easier to break apart, though chilled rice still has to be fully heated through. If the pan is too full, the heat goes into warming the food and evaporating water (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). In a home pan, cook the four servings in two batches, which also helps the tomato keep its shape.

## Steps

1. Break up the chilled cooked rice. Slice the chicken into even thin slices, keep the gai lan stems and leaves apart, and cut the onion and tomato into wedges. Divide the cut ingredients and the seasonings into two portions; each batch below uses half.
2. Stir the chili rings and the fish sauce for serving together in a small dish. Slice the cucumber and cut the lime into wedges and leave them at the edge of the plate; these two sides do not go in the pan.
3. Heat the oil in a pan and stir-fry the minced garlic for about 30 seconds. Add the onion wedges and spread them out, letting the side touching the pan cook for 1 minute until slightly coloured, then stir and cook for 1 more minute, until the edges are translucent and the insides still hold their shape.
4. Add the chicken, tomato and gai lan stems and stir-fry for 3–4 minutes, until the tomato has softened slightly and the centre of the chicken reaches **74°C (165°F)**. There should be no pools of liquid on the bottom of the pan; if there is a lot of liquid, let the water cook off first.
5. Add the chilled rice, sweet black soy sauce, light soy sauce and fish sauce, and toss until the clumps of rice break up and the colour is even. Turn it with the spatula from the bottom, and avoid crushing the grains.
6. Push the rice aside to make a space in the middle and crack this batch's eggs into it. Scramble lightly and cook for about 1 minute until the bottom sets, then fold it into the rice. Add the gai lan leaves and stir-fry for about 1 minute, until the leaves wilt. Gather the rice together and check the centre; plate it once it reaches **74°C (165°F)**, then fry the next batch.
7. Sprinkle on the white pepper and serve at once with the cucumber, lime and the small dish of chili fish sauce. Squeeze on a little lime juice and taste first, then decide whether to add fish sauce.

## Key points

- **Grains stuck in a wet clump**: the rice is too wet, the tomato released water, or all four servings were crowded into one pan. Break up the chilled rice first, cook off the visible water from the vegetables and meat, and add the rice in batches (see [starch gelatinization and retrogradation](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Browned outside, still cold inside**: not enough stir-frying, so the middle of the cold rice clumps never heated. After breaking them apart, keep turning from the bottom of the pan until the rice is hot. When preparing the rice, portion it into shallow containers and chill it promptly (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Tough chicken and dry egg**: all the ingredients were cooked a long time to chase browning. Slice the chicken thin, mix in the egg as soon as it sets, and take the pan off the heat as soon as the rice is hot (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
