Omurice is a Japanese yoshoku dish in which chicken and tomato fried rice is wrapped in a thin omelette and plated as an oval. Ketchup gives the rice a sweet and sour flavour, while the onion and chicken support the base flavour. The fried rice should be moist but loose, and the omelette soft with a smooth surface, so that when folded over it covers the rice completely.

Deal with water first, then the egg. The ketchup is cooked down in the pan first so it doesn't soak the rice. The starch in refrigerated rice has [retrograded](/principles/starch-gelatinization/), so it separates more easily. Beaten egg forms a [protein network](/principles/protein-coagulation/) as it heats, and the rice should be wrapped just as it sets; if you keep frying it for a long time, the omelette tightens and dries out, and the folded edges crack.

## Steps

1. Cut the onion into fine dice, the chicken into thin pieces, and loosen the cooked rice gently with a rice paddle. If you use freshly cooked rice, spread it on a plate to let the steam off for 5 minutes, until the surface is no longer shiny and wet and is still warm. Leave refrigerated rice as it is to heat in the pan.
2. Melt 15 g of butter in a large frying pan over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the foam is fine and has not browned. Add the onion and cook for 3–4 minutes, until the dice are soft and the edges are translucent.
3. Add the chicken, salt and black pepper and cook for 3–4 minutes, until the pieces are no longer pink inside or out and the centre reaches 74°C (165°F). Turn the heat to medium-low, add 7.5 tbsp of the ketchup and the Worcestershire sauce, and cook for 1–2 minutes, until the sauce thickens and the bottom of the pan shows briefly after the spatula passes.
4. Add the rice, cut through it along the edges of the clumps with the spatula and toss, cooking for 3–4 minutes, until every grain is dyed an even orange-red and the centre of the rice is hot to 74°C (165°F). Add the cooked peas and cook for 1 minute, until the peas are hot. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper, divide the fried rice into three portions and loosely shape each into a short oval.
5. Make one portion at a time. Beat 2 eggs, add 0.25 tsp of salt and pass through a fine sieve, until there are no long strands of egg white. Melt 15 g of butter in a nonstick pan about 20 cm wide over medium heat for about 30–60 seconds, until the foam spreads. Pour in the egg and shake the pan while stirring with chopsticks for 30 seconds, until the egg turns into small, soft curds.
6. Spread the soft curds flat, turn the heat to low and cook for 30–60 more seconds, until the whole omelette has joined into one sheet and there is no runny egg left on the surface. Put a portion of fried rice in a long oval down the centre, leaving room for folds on both sides, and use a soft spatula to fold the two sides over the rice.
7. Let the wrapped omelette set over low heat for about 30 more seconds, until the seam stays closed and the egg layer is cooked to 74°C (165°F), then take it off the heat right away. Bring the edge of the plate to the edge of the pan and flip it over so the seam is underneath. Make the second and third portions the same way, and drizzle with ketchup when plating.

## Key points

- **The fried rice is sticky and wet, and liquid runs out once wrapped**: the ketchup was not cooked thick first, or the hot rice carried too much surface moisture. Cook until the sauce lets a spatula part it, then add the rice that has let off its steam (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **The omelette is browned and the folds crack**: the pan is too hot, and the egg set too far before it wrapped the rice. Turn the heat to low as soon as the egg forms a thin sheet, and take it out promptly once wrapped (see [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
- **A lot of rice still shows after folding**: the rice is piled too wide and too high, and the omelette has no spare edge. Shape the rice into a narrow oval, and nudge it toward the centre with the spatula before folding; the pan diameter and the amount per portion must match (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
