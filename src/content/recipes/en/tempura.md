Tempura (*tenpura*) coats seafood and vegetables in a thin batter and fries them until the coating is light and crisp, then serves them with *tentsuyu*, a dipping sauce of dashi, soy sauce and mirin. The shrimp stays springy, the pumpkin is soft inside, and the coating covers only the surface. Pieces must be an even thickness, or the coating dries out while the centre is still hard.

Flour that meets water and is stirred again and again slowly builds elastic [gluten](/principles/gluten/). Ice-cold ingredients, mixing the batter just before frying and little stirring keep that network limited. Small lumps of flour in the batter are fine. The other half of the work is [oil temperature](/principles/oil-temperature/): a thin coating turns crisp as it loses water in the heat, and if too much goes in at once the oil cools and frying drags on.

## Steps

1. Put the dashi, soy sauce, mirin and sugar in a small pot and heat over medium heat for about 3 minutes, until the surface boils and the sugar has fully dissolved. Turn to low and cook for 1 minute, keeping it at a gentle boil, then take it off the heat and pour into a dipping bowl. Put the grated daikon to one side.
2. Cut a few shallow slashes across the belly of each shrimp and press it gently straight. Trim the tail tips and squeeze the water out of the tail shells. Pat the shrimp, eggplant slices and pumpkin slices dry with paper towels and lay them out ready.
3. Add the frying oil to a deep pot and heat over medium heat for about 5–8 minutes, until a thermometer reads 180°C (350°F). Set up a wire rack, and spread the fried pieces on it to drain for 1 minute, until they stop dripping oil.
4. As the oil nears temperature, mix the egg with 200 ml of ice water and skim off the foam. Add 120 g of flour and swirl a few times with chopsticks, until there are no large patches of dry flour but small lumps remain. The batter should be runny. Start frying right away.
5. Dip the eggplant slices one at a time in the batter, lift each and let the excess drip off, then lower it into the 180°C (350°F) oil. Fry each batch for 1 minute, until the coating has set, the bubbles are fine and the eggplant is soft. Each batch should cover only about a third of the oil surface. Clear out the loose bits after lifting each batch out.
6. Turn off the heat and wait about 3–5 minutes, until the oil drops to 160°C (325°F), then turn the heat back on low. Batter the pumpkin slices one at a time and fry each batch for 2–3 minutes, until the coating is dry and crisp and a chopstick slides easily into the pumpkin. Lift out and drain, then bring the oil back to 180°C (350°F), which takes about 1–2 minutes.
7. Dust the shrimp lightly with the remaining flour, then batter them. Fry each batch for about 2 minutes, until the coating is pale gold, the flesh is opaque and the thickest part reaches 74°C (165°F). Spread on the rack for 1 minute to drain, until the surface stops dripping, and serve at once. At the table, stir the grated daikon into the sauce and dip each piece.

## Key points

- **Coating is thick and chewy**: the batter was stirred too smooth, or sat too long after mixing. Stir only until the dry flour disperses, and let the excess drip off after dipping (see [gluten](/principles/gluten/)).
- **Coating is soft and greasy**: a large load of food dragged down the oil temperature, or the fried pieces were stacked and trapped steam. Fry small batches, let the oil recover between batches, and spread the pieces out (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
- **Pumpkin is cooked outside but hard inside**: the slices are too thick, or they were fried at the same high oil temperature as the shrimp. Cut slices 5 mm thick and use a lower oil temperature to give the centre time to soften (see [oil temperature and deep frying](/principles/oil-temperature/)).
