Three-cup chicken is braised until rich and fragrant, with glossy skin and a glossy reduced sauce, and its taste comes from the combination of fat, rice wine and soy sauce. It uses lard, bone-in chicken pieces, and green onion, ginger and garlic. Manage the amounts of fat, wine and soy sauce separately, so the dish is neither greasy nor salty once the sauce has reduced.

The ginger and garlic go into the fat first and are fried until fragrant, and the aromatic oil coats the chicken (see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). After the wine goes in, cover the pot: the liquid and steam carry the heat to the meat next to the bone. At the end, uncover and evaporate the water, so the cooked chicken and the thick sauce come together (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)). Braise the chicken through first, then reduce the sauce.

## Steps

1. Blot the surface of the chicken dry. Chop especially thick thigh meat smaller, so the pieces in the pot are of similar thickness. Mix the rice wine, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, sugar and white pepper and set aside.
2. Put the lard, ginger slices, garlic and the white parts of the green onion in a heavy-bottomed pot and fry over low heat for about 2 minutes, until the garlic has pale golden spots on the surface and the edges of the ginger slices curl slightly. The fragrance only needs to come out; the colour should still be pale.
3. Add the chicken and stir-fry over medium heat for 5–7 minutes, until the skin is slightly golden and the outside of the meat turns white. Spread the pieces out, let one side sit against the pan first, then turn them. If the pot is small, fry in two batches and then put them back together in the pot.
4. Pour in the braising sauce, toss and cook over medium heat for 1 minute, until bubbles break continuously at the edge of the pot. Scrape up the light brown bits stuck to the bottom of the pot so they dissolve into the sauce.
5. Cover, turn to low and braise for 20 minutes, turning once partway. The chicken should be cooked through, with no raw texture next to the bone. Measure the centre of the thickest piece for 74°C (165°F), keeping the thermometer away from the bone. If it is not there yet, braise 3 minutes more at a time and measure again.
6. Uncover and reduce the sauce over medium-high heat for 3–5 minutes, turning gently, until the sauce clings to the skin and a layer of flowing glossy sauce is still left on the bottom of the pot. Add the green parts of the green onion and stir-fry for 1 minute, then take it out right away once the green onion pieces soften.

## Key points

- **Ginger and garlic turn black and the sauce tastes bitter**: the heat was too high when frying the aromatics, and the [aroma](/principles/aromatics/) burnt instead of blooming. Pick out the blackened aromatics; if the fat is bitter too, change the fat and fry the aromatics again.
- **Dark outside but undercooked next to the bone**: soy sauce colours faster than the inside heats up. Cook the chicken through covered before you reduce the sauce, and check the thickest piece instead of judging by the skin alone (see [Heat Transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Sauce is salty and the meat is dry**: the reduction dragged on, concentrating the salt while the meat kept losing water. Take it off as soon as the glossy sauce just clings to the meat (see [Protein Denaturation and Coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/)).
