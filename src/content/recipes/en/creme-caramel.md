*Crème caramel* is a custard pudding that turns out of its mould, with a layer of flowing caramel on top. The sugar in the bottom of the mould is first [caramelized](/principles/caramelization/) for its aroma, and the custard sets by [protein coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). After baking, the pudding rests in the fridge, and the hard caramel layer absorbs moisture and gradually dissolves into a sauce.

Whole eggs give structure and the extra yolks make the pudding smooth and fine. The water bath heats the sides of the mould gently, so the outer ring does not overcook before the centre has set (see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/)). This version uses a single mould of about 1 L, and after baking it must be chilled until cold before unmoulding, so make it ahead.

## Steps

1. Put the 100 g of sugar for the caramel and the 30 ml of water in a small light-coloured pot and heat until the sugar has fully dissolved. Keep cooking, gently swirling the pot, until the syrup turns an even amber, then take it off the heat at once.
2. Pour the caramel into a heatproof mould that has been warmed and wiped dry. Wearing oven mitts, tilt the mould so the caramel covers the bottom and coats a little of the sides. Leave it to set, then place the mould in a deep baking pan.
3. Preheat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Heat the milk until fine bubbles form at the edge of the pot but it has not boiled; separately, stir the whole eggs, extra yolks and the 100 g of sugar for the custard together, stirring gently so as not to whip up a lot of foam.
4. Pour the hot milk into the eggs in a thin stream, stirring as you pour, then add the vanilla extract. Strain through a fine sieve into the mould and skim off the foam on the surface; the custard should be smooth, with no bits of cooked egg and no obvious bubbles.
5. Pour hot water into the deep baking pan to half the height of the mould and put it on the lower rack of the oven. Bake for 5 minutes, then lower to 165°C (325°F) and bake for another 35–40 minutes; keep the water bath hot without boiling. Take it out when the outer ring has set and the centre trembles as a whole, a thin skewer inserted near the edge shows no liquid egg, and the centre reads 74°C (165°F).
6. Lift the mould out of the water bath, let it cool slightly, then cover and refrigerate until completely cold. Run a thin knife around the edge, set a plate with a rim on top and turn the mould over with it; wait until the pudding slides down and the caramel sauce runs out, then lift off the mould.

## Key points

- **Honeycomb surface and a coarse, firm texture**: too much foam, or the water bath boiled or the custard was heated too fiercely. Stir gently when mixing, strain, and take the pudding out of the water bath when the centre has just set. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/).
- **Caramel black and bitter**: after the sugar changed colour it stayed in the hot pot, and residual heat kept darkening it. Have the mould ready, and take the pot off the heat and pour as soon as the sugar turns amber; burnt caramel has to be remade. See [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/).
- **Cracks on unmoulding or liquid in the centre**: the centre had not set fully, or it was turned out before it had cooled. Check that both the outer ring and the centre have set and the centre has reached 74°C (165°F), then chill thoroughly. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **A thin layer of hard sugar left on the mould**: the caramel has not dissolved completely. Do not tap the mould; chill it a little longer so the moisture slowly dissolves the sugar, or after unmoulding, dissolve the remaining sugar with a little hot water and use it as a sauce. See [caramelization](/principles/caramelization/).
