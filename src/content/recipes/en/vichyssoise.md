*Vichyssoise* is a leek and potato soup served cold: smooth, cool and round with cream. It belongs to the French soup repertoire, yet it took shape as a cold soup in the United States. The thickness of the soup comes from the [starch gelatinizing](/principles/starch-gelatinization/) when the potatoes are heated; the leeks are cooked until completely soft and then puréed with the potatoes, so the texture is even and smooth.

Only the white part of the leeks is used, so the soup is pale and has little fibre. The cream goes in after the vegetables are cooked and adds a rounded, fatty fragrance. The soup thickens as it cools, and the sense of saltiness changes too, so the last tasting waits until the soup is truly cold. This pot makes six small bowls, which is right for a first course.

## Steps

1. Halve the white parts of the leeks lengthwise and rinse each layer under running water, folded open, to wash out the grit between the layers. Cut into thin slices. Peel the potatoes and cut them into small slices of even thickness, so thin slices do not fall apart while thick ones are still hard.
2. Put the leeks, potatoes and chicken stock into a pot. Once bubbles appear, turn to low heat and cook with the lid half on for about 40–50 minutes. Keep the surface at a gentle simmer until the potatoes crush at a press and the leeks can be mashed easily.
3. Pass the vegetables and their liquid through a food mill, then through a fine sieve, pressing out a fine purée with the back of a spoon. If you have no food mill, let it cool slightly and blend in short bursts in batches. Leave room for steam to escape as the machine's instructions say for hot soup; do not blend at high speed in a sealed, full jar.
4. Stir in the cream, taste, and add salt and white pepper. The soup should coat the back of a spoon thinly and still run off it in a continuous stream. Do not thicken it too much at this point, because it will thicken more in the refrigerator.
5. Divide the soup into shallow containers and cool it by stirring in an ice water bath, then move it to the refrigerator until completely cold. Do not leave a big pot of soup to cool slowly at room temperature overnight (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
6. Stir and taste again before serving, and add a little more salt if needed. Pour into cold bowls and sprinkle with finely chopped chives. If the cold soup is as thick as mashed potatoes, stir in a little cold chicken stock to bring it back to a consistency that pours smoothly.

## Key points

- **The soup is like glue and sticky in the mouth**: cooked potatoes were blended at high speed for a long time, the cells broke and released too much starch. Prefer a food mill and sieve, and use the blender only in short bursts until even. A soup that has already turned gluey can be thinned, but the texture is hard to fully restore. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
- **Coarse strands or grit**: the leeks were not washed between the layers, or were not cooked through. Rinse layer by layer, cook until they mash, and sieve at the end; cream cannot hide hard fibres. See [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/).
- **Right when hot but too thick when cold**: starch and fat both make the soup more viscous as it cools. Judge after chilling and loosen it with a little cold chicken stock; do not thicken the soup too much while hot. See [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/).
