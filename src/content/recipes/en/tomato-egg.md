Stir-fried tomatoes and eggs should have soft, tender curds of egg and a tomato sauce good enough to spoon over rice. When eggs are heated, their proteins unfold and link to one another, and the liquid turns solid. With more heat the network tightens and squeezes out water, and the egg turns hard. See [protein denaturation and coagulation](/principles/protein-coagulation/). Tomatoes, on the other hand, need longer in the pan so the flesh softens and the juice thickens, so the two are cooked separately and combined at the end.

The salt is added in two parts as well. Season the beaten eggs first, then add salt once the tomatoes are in the pan, so the juice they release cooks down in the wok. See [salt](/principles/salt/). Ripe tomatoes vary a lot in sweetness and acidity, so adjust the sugar to the sourness; the finished dish should still taste of the tomatoes' bright acidity.

## Steps

1. Prepare the tomato wedges and chopped green onion. Keep the seeds and juice with the tomatoes; cut small wedges so they soften quickly, and pour any juice that runs onto the board into a bowl.
2. Beat the eggs with the salt for the eggs, white pepper, toasted sesame oil and Shaoxing wine, until the whites and yolks are fully blended. There should be no clear strands of white at the bottom of the bowl, and the seasoning should not settle in one spot.
3. Heat the wok, add 2 tbsp of the oil and swirl to spread it, then pour in the eggs. When the edges puff up and the bottom sets in a layer, push toward the centre with a spatula, leaving large soft curds. Take them out while the surface still looks moist and glossy.
4. Add the remaining oil to the same wok, then the tomatoes and green onion, and stir-fry. Add the remaining salt and the sugar, and cook for about 1 minute, until the cut edges soften and red juice collects at the bottom. If the bottom is dry and the flesh is still firm, add the water you set aside; if it is already juicy, add little or none.
5. Cook the tomato juice down (about 2–3 minutes). Press a few wedges lightly with the back of the spatula so the soft flesh melts into the juice and the rest keeps its shape. The sauce should cling to the spatula, with liquid still moving in the bottom of the pan. Taste, then decide whether to add salt or sugar.
6. Return the eggs to the wok and fold gently until the curds are coated with sauce and hot through, with no liquid egg left, then plate right away. Do not break the curds into small crumbs, and do not keep reducing the sauce after the eggs are back in.

## Key points

- **Eggs are dry and hard with browned edges**: they were heated too long, and the [protein network](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tightened and lost water. Take the eggs out after the first pass, and combine them only after the tomato sauce has reduced. Eggs that are already overcooked are hard to bring back to tender.
- **Thin liquid pooled on the plate**: the tomatoes were not cooked down enough, or too much water was added. [Salt](/principles/salt/) draws juice from cut tomatoes, so finish that step in the wok before returning the eggs.
- **Sauce is salty but the eggs are bland**: all the seasoning went into the tomatoes. Use one portion of [salt](/principles/salt/) for the eggs and one for the tomatoes. Reducing the sauce also concentrates the saltiness, so taste at the end before adding more.
