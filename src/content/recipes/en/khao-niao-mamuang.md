*Khao niao mamuang* is warm, soft sweet coconut sticky rice served with ripe mango. Glutinous rice is rich in amylopectin, so once steamed it is sticky and springy, and it can be pressed into a mass while the individual grains stay visible. The key is to let the rice take up water and steam it through before mixing in the sweet coconut cream (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).

The coconut cream is split into two portions. One is stirred in while the rice is still hot, which makes the rice plump and glossy. The other is cooked with a little rice flour into a sauce. Use unsweetened coconut cream and stir the fat on top into the liquid below after opening the can. The rice is fairly sweet, so the mango should be ripe and soft, with a slice that bends gently, to give a full sweet aroma.

## Steps

1. Rinse the glutinous rice gently until the water runs fairly clear. Add the soaking water, cover and refrigerate for about 8 hours. Drain, then rinse once more. The grains should have swollen with water and take a dent from a fingernail.
2. Line a steamer tray with damp cheesecloth. Add the rice and spread it in an even layer, leaving space around it for steam. Once the water boils, cover and steam for about 25–35 minutes, turning the rice once halfway. Taste a few grains from the thickest part and stop only when they are all soft with no chalky white centre.
3. While the rice steams, stir together 240 ml coconut cream, 200 g sugar and 1 tsp salt and warm gently until the sugar and salt dissolve. Keep it warm. As soon as the rice comes out of the steamer, put it in a large bowl and pour this coconut cream over it at once. Fold gently, cover and let it rest for about 15 minutes.
4. Mix the rice flour with a little cold coconut cream from the sauce portion into a smooth paste with no lumps. Heat it with the remaining 120 ml coconut cream and a pinch of salt, stirring constantly. Cook until it bubbles and lightly coats the back of a spoon, then add 40 g sugar, take it off the heat, stir until dissolved and let it cool.
5. Gently turn the sticky rice over the coconut cream at the bottom of the bowl should be mostly absorbed and the grains glossy and whole. Peel the mangoes, cut the flesh off both sides of the pit and slice it across.
6. Divide the rice into four portions and set the mango beside it. Drizzle on a little coconut sauce and scatter on toasted sesame seeds if you like. The rice is best warm or just cooled, when it is sticky and soft. Serve the sauce in a small bowl on the side and add more as you eat.

## Key points

- **Sticky outside, hard inside**: the soak was too short or the rice was piled too thick, so the centre did not take up enough water or heat. Soak it in the fridge first, then check the thickest part of the tray, and keep steaming if there is a hard centre (see [heat transfer](/principles/heat-transfer/)).
- **Coconut cream pooling at the bottom of the bowl**: the rice had already cooled when mixed, or it was still undercooked. Mix right after steaming, then cover to keep it warm and let the liquid slowly move into the grains (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
- **Lumpy sauce**: the rice flour met hot coconut cream directly, so its outer layer gelatinized first and trapped dry flour inside. Mix it with cold coconut cream first and stir as you heat (see [starch gelatinization](/principles/starch-gelatinization/)).
