*Tom saep* is a hot and sour soup from Isan, in northeastern Thailand, and pork ribs are a common version at home. The ribs are simmered until the meat falls off the bone, the broth has the clean fragrance of lemongrass, galangal and makrut lime leaf, the dried chilies add a toasted note, and lime brings a sharp sourness at the end. Serve with [Thai sticky rice](/recipes/khao-niao/) so the ribs and soup go into the mouth together.

Once ribs are cooked through, there is still a stage where the meat is hard to bite. Keep simmering gently and the collagen in the connective tissue slowly turns into gelatin, and only then does the meat leave the bone (see [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/)). Add the aromatics in the second half, and add the lime and fresh herbs after the heat is off, so both the meaty flavour from the simmering and the fresh fragrance are kept (see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/)). The toasted rice powder is optional; with it the soup has a toasted rice flavour and fine grains.

## Steps

1. Put the ribs in a pot with the 1000 ml of water for simmering and the salt. Heat over medium heat for about 8–10 minutes, until the water boils and foam rises to the surface, and skim it off thoroughly with a spoon.
2. Turn to low, leave the lid ajar and simmer for 45–60 minutes. Only a few small bubbles should break on the surface, and the meat should shrink back to show the bones but stay in whole pieces. When the water level drops until the ribs are exposed, add some of the extra 200 ml of hot water.
3. Add the lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime leaf and garlic and cook over low heat for another 45 minutes, until a fork slides easily into the thickest part of the meat and a gentle twist separates it from the bone. Lift out the tough lemongrass lengths, galangal slices and lime leaves.
4. While the ribs simmer, toast the glutinous rice in a dry pan over medium-high heat, stirring constantly for 5–8 minutes, until the grains are deep golden and smell of toasted rice. Tip out and let cool for 3 minutes, until no longer hot to touch, then grind to a fine sandy powder. Toast the dried chilies separately over medium-low heat for 2–3 minutes, until crisp and fragrant with no black spots, cool for 2 minutes and crush.
5. Add the shallots, cherry tomatoes, mushrooms and fresh chilies to the soup and cook over low heat for 5–8 minutes, until the shallots are soft, the mushrooms are cooked through and the tomatoes' edges loosen. Add the fish sauce and turn off the heat.
6. Stir in the lime juice, the toasted dried chilies and the optional sugar, then taste the soup together with a piece of meat. Add the toasted rice powder, cilantro and green onion only just before serving, stir to combine and ladle into bowls.

## Key points

- **Ribs are cooked but chewy**: the collagen has not softened all the way. Keep simmering gently and test the thickest spot every 10 minutes; they are ready when a fork can pull the meat apart, and colour alone is not a reliable guide. See [collagen and gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/).
- **Soup is hot but not fresh-tasting**: the lime, cilantro and green onion were cooked along with the soup too long, and the fragrance escaped. Add the lime after the heat is off and the herbs just before serving; if you are saving soup for the next meal, keep these separate too. See [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).
- **Soup turns into a thick paste and the rice flavour is weak**: the toasted rice powder went in too early, swelled and lost its fragrance. Toast and grind it fresh and add it last; when tasting the sourness, taste with a piece of rib and not only the concentrated broth, so you do not overcorrect. See [acid](/principles/acid/).
