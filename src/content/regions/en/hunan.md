The Xiang River valley, the Dongting Lake area and the mountains of western Hunan send the Hunan kitchen fish, vegetables and smoked and cured goods. The heat in Hunan cooking has layers: fresh chilies are clean and fragrant, pickled chilies sour and salty, dried chilies dry-fragrant. Quick stir-fries and steamed dishes each handle one of these, and each protects the tenderness, crispness or softness of the main ingredient.

## Fresh-chili quick stir-fry: drive off the water first, then combine in the pan

To make chili pork or Hunan-style stir-fried beef well, first cut the chilies and meat so they cook quickly. Slice the meat thin and even, cut the green peppers into pieces or strips by thickness, and cut side ingredients such as celery so they finish with the meat; see [Knife Work and Cooking](/principles/knife-work/). Thick vegetables with thin meat go wrong: by the time the vegetables soften, the meat has lost its water and gone tough.

The green peppers can be fried first until the skin wrinkles and scattered brown spots appear, then combined with the meat. With the water gone, the chili aroma and the roasted taste of the surface stand out more, and the meat stays in the pan only until cooked through. If you use fatty meat, you can first render the fat and fry the chilies and garlic in it. Watch the water at the bottom of the pan: if it is piled too full, the steam cannot escape and the surface has trouble forming [browned aroma](/principles/maillard-reaction/).

Cut chilies release their heat more easily, and hot oil catches some of the aroma and capsaicin; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/). Fresh and pickled chilies taste different, so if you swap one for the other, readjust the salt and sourness. On a home stove, fry the chilies first, then the meat, and combine them last; a smaller batch means each slice of meat waits less time to cook.

## Steaming under chopped chilies: let the sauce form on the fish

Fish head with chopped chilies is made by spreading chopped pickled chilies over the fish head and steaming it on the plate. Salted, fermented chilies already carry saltiness and ripe chili aroma, and some are sour too, so taste them before deciding how much more salt to add. See [fermentation](/principles/fermentation/). Bottled chopped chilies differ in sourness and saltiness; taste the final dish together with the cooked fish.

Steam condenses on the fish head and the plate and delivers heat, while the inside warms slowly by conduction; see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/). Splitting the fish head and laying it flat narrows the gap between thick and thin parts. The fish juices, the chili juices and the water condensed from the steam gather in the plate and become a sauce against the fish; dip the meat in the plate juices as you eat, and the flavour is complete.

The hard parts of this dish are whether the steamer can hold the plate and how to tell it is done. Leave room around the plate for steam to flow, keep enough water in the pot, and take the dish out when the thickest part of the fish has turned opaque and lifts apart into flakes. Steam longer and the fish's [protein](/principles/protein-coagulation/) tightens and loses water. At home, practise with a smaller fish head, and spread the chilies to a similar thickness everywhere.

## Steaming with cured meats: steam the dry aroma moist again

Steamed mixed cured meats puts smoked and cured ingredients such as cured pork, cured chicken and cured fish on one plate and steams them. Curing, drying and smoking have already changed their flavour and moisture; steaming them together heats them again, softens them and releases fat and juices. The different cured meats meet in the same plate sauce, so each mouthful carries both meat fragrance and smoke.

Steam supplies the heat, the plate juices and the water in the ingredients soften the dry, hard parts, and thickness decides when the centre is heated through; see [Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation](/principles/heat-transfer/). Pieces cut to a similar thickness and spread out a little steam more evenly than cured meats piled into a thick lump. The finished dish should be hot through and easy to bite apart, with each meat keeping its own texture.

A home cook can start with good ready-made cured meats, prepare them as the package directs, and taste for saltiness after steaming before deciding whether to add seasoning. Choose a shallow plate that fits in your steamer, and don't keep turning up the heat to save time. Making smoked and cured meat yourself means controlling the curing, drying and smoking separately; learn the steaming first and you will already have a feel for this technique.

## Trying it at home

[Hunan-style Pork with Chili Peppers](/recipes/la-jiao-chao-rou/) uses lean pork to practise driving off water in separate batches; [Hunan-style Stir-fried Beef with Celery and Chilies](/recipes/xiao-chao-huang-niu-rou/) practises matching the thickness of the meat slices and the celery. On a home stove the easiest step is to get everything ready and make each batch smaller, then go for a short, quick combine.
