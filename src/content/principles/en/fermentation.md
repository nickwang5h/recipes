## What it is

**Fermentation** is using microbes to change food. In the kitchen, it often also includes the breakdown of the ingredient by the **enzymes** the microbes make: **proteases** break proteins down into **peptides** and **free amino acids**, releasing savoury compounds such as **glutamate**; **amylases** break starch down into sugars, which give sweetness and also feed further fermentation.

**Yeast** uses sugar to make alcohol and aroma, and **lactic acid bacteria** and others make **organic acids**. Sugars, amino acids, acids and alcohol keep converting and form flavour compounds such as **esters**. Free glutamate gives the [umami](/principles/umami/) the tongue tastes, and volatile compounds give the aroma the nose picks up. Together they give fermented seasonings their depth.

## Six seasonings, each on its own path

| Seasoning | Ingredients and main change | Role in a dish |
|---|---|---|
| Chili bean paste | Fava beans and the like made into koji and fermented, with chilies added; proteins and starch slowly break down | Savoury saltiness, fermented-paste aroma, heat and red oil |
| Fermented black beans | Soybeans fermented, then salted; proteins break down | Strong bean aroma and savoury saltiness; once chopped, they spread easily into the sauce |
| Fermented tofu | Tofu fermented with moulds and the like, then salted and matured | Softens in texture; gives saltiness and a matured aroma |
| Brewed soy sauce | Beans and grains made into koji and fermented in brine | Amino acids, sugars, organic acids and aroma together make up the base flavour |
| *Meigancai* (preserved mustard greens) | Vegetables salted and fermented, then dried; some methods also steam and sun them | Pickle aroma and dried-vegetable aroma, good with meat fat |
| Fermented rice (*jiuniang*) | Enzymes in the yeast starter first turn the starch of cooked rice into sugar, then the yeast uses the sugar | Sweetness that is kept, light wine aroma and fermented aroma |

The savoury saltiness of bean products mostly comes from protein breakdown. Meigancai gets its flavour from vegetable fermentation followed by drying, and fermented rice is mainly about starch turning to sugar; the three routes are different. Salting also weeds out the microbes that cannot cope and leaves those that can keep working; see [salt](/principles/salt/).

## Fry chili bean paste in oil first

Chop the paste fine and fry it in oil over medium-low heat. As the steam dies down, the oil turns from clear to red; when you smell the fermented-paste aroma, add the next ingredients. The oil dissolves the fat-soluble pigments of the chili and some of the aroma compounds, and stirring spreads them through the whole pan of oil; see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

Frying opens up the fermented flavour the paste already has. If you pour in a pot of water first, the paste still gives savoury saltiness, but it lacks the aroma that frying in oil brings out, and the red oil. If the heat is too high, the finely chopped paste sticks and burns; when you smell a bitter burnt note, remove the scorched part and fry again.

The paste's savouriness was formed before it went into the pot, so fry until the oil is red and the paste aroma is clear, then move on; chasing a deep brown makes the chilies and bean bits burn first.

## When it goes in decides what is kept

Soy sauce added early blends its savoury saltiness into the braising liquid; holding some back until the end keeps a more distinct soy sauce aroma. Mash or mix apart the fermented black beans and fermented tofu first, or one mouthful is very salty and the next bland. Wash the grit off meigancai and soak it until soft; soak it too long or change the water too often and the saltiness and some of the flavour wash away.

For [Mini Tangyuan in Fermented Rice Soup](/recipes/lao-zao-xiao-tang-yuan/), add the fermented rice when the tangyuan are nearly cooked through and bring it briefly to a boil. Cook it longer and the wine aroma and some of the other aromas escape, while the sugar's sweetness stays. The salt or sugar these seasonings carry also counts toward the seasoning of the whole pot; taste again for saltiness after reducing.

## Across cuisines

- **Chinese**: [Twice-Cooked Pork](/recipes/hui-guo-rou/) fries the chili bean paste and fermented black beans into the meat fat, so fermented bean aroma, chili aroma and meat aroma come together.
- **French**: *Sauce au roquefort* melts matured cheese into cream and seasons with the flavours from the breakdown of proteins and fat, and with the blue-cheese aroma.
- **Italian**: [Risotto alla Milanese](/recipes/risotto-alla-milanese/) finishes with Parmigiano Reggiano stirred in. Enzymes break the cheese down as it matures and release free amino acids, which add savoury saltiness and cheese aroma to the rice and stock.
- **Thai**: *nam phrik kapi* pounds salted, fermented shrimp paste with garlic, chilies and lime juice, to eat with vegetables and rice. The shrimp paste supplies the savoury saltiness and strong aroma that fermentation forms.
- **Vietnamese**: [Nước Chấm](/recipes/nuoc-cham/) adds fish sauce to a sweet and sour base of lime, sugar and water. The savoury saltiness of fish sauce comes from fermenting fish with salt; diluted, it is used for dipping or tossing rice noodles.
- **Japanese**: [Miso Soup](/recipes/miso-shiru/) dissolves miso, matured by fermenting soybeans, salt and koji, into dashi, adding savoury saltiness and the aroma of the paste. The miso goes in at the end and is not boiled again, so the aroma stays.
- **Korean**: [Baechu Kimchi](/recipes/baechu-kimchi/) is salted first, then lactic acid fermentation brings out the sourness. Sour, aged kimchi suits [Kimchi Jjigae](/recipes/kimchi-jjigae/), carrying the sour aroma that fermentation formed into the soup.
