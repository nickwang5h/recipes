## What it is

**Fermentation** uses microbes to change food. In the kitchen, fermentation also often includes the breakdown of the ingredient by the **enzymes** the microbes produce: **proteases** break proteins down into **peptides** and **free amino acids**, releasing savoury compounds such as **glutamate**; **amylases** break starch down into sugars, which give sweetness and raw material for further fermentation.

**Yeast** uses sugar to make alcohol and aroma, and **lactic acid bacteria** and others make **organic acids**. Sugars, amino acids, acids and alcohol keep converting and form flavour compounds such as **esters**. Free glutamate mainly provides the [umami](/principles/umami/) the tongue senses, while volatile compounds provide the aroma the nose picks up. Together they give fermented seasonings their depth.

## Six seasonings, several different paths

| Seasoning | Ingredients and main change | Role in a dish |
|---|---|---|
| Chili bean paste | Fava beans and the like made into koji and fermented, with chilies added; proteins and starch gradually break down | Savoury saltiness, fermented-paste aroma, heat and red oil |
| Fermented black beans | Soybeans fermented and salted, proteins broken down | Strong bean aroma and savoury saltiness; once chopped, it spreads easily into the sauce |
| Fermented tofu | Tofu fermented with the help of moulds and then salted and matured | Texture softens; gives saltiness and a matured aroma |
| Brewed soy sauce | Beans and grains made into koji and fermented in brine | Amino acids, sugars, organic acids and aroma together make up the base flavour |
| *Meigancai* (preserved mustard greens) | Vegetables salted and fermented, then dried; some methods also steam and sun them | Pickle aroma and dried-vegetable aroma, good with meat fat |
| Fermented rice (*jiuniang*) | Enzymes in the yeast starter first turn the starch of cooked rice into sugar, then the yeast uses the sugar | Sweetness that is kept, light wine aroma and fermented aroma |

The savoury saltiness of bean products is mostly tied to protein breakdown. Meigancai gets its flavour from vegetable fermentation followed by drying, and fermented rice puts the saccharification of starch first; they follow different routes. Salting also selects which microbes can keep working, see [salt](/principles/salt/).

## Why chili bean paste is fried in oil first

Chop the paste fine first, and fry it in oil over medium-low heat. As the steam gradually lessens, the oil turns from clear to red; add the next ingredients once you can smell the fermented-paste aroma. The oil dissolves the fat-soluble pigments of the chili and some of the aroma compounds, and stirring spreads them through the whole pan of oil, see [spices and aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

This step releases and opens up the fermented flavour that is already there. If you pour in a pot of water first, the paste still gives savoury saltiness, but lacks the aroma opened up in oil and the effect of red oil. If the heat is too high, the finely chopped paste sticks and burns; when you smell a bitter burnt note, remove the scorched part and fry again.

The savouriness of the paste was already formed before it went into the pot. Fry until the oil is red and the paste aroma is clear, then go on to the next step; chasing a deep brown makes the chilies and bean bits burn first.

## When it goes in decides what is kept

Soy sauce added early blends its savoury saltiness into the braising liquid; holding some back to add at the end keeps a more distinct soy sauce aroma. Fermented black beans and fermented tofu need to be mashed or mixed apart, so that one mouthful is not very salty and the next bland. Meigancai is washed free of grit and soaked until soft; a long soak with repeated changes of water also carries away saltiness and some of the flavour.

When fermented rice is used in [Mini Tangyuan in Fermented Rice Soup](/recipes/lao-zao-xiao-tang-yuan/), it can go in once the tangyuan are nearly cooked through, and be brought briefly to a boil. A long cook lets the wine aroma and some of the aromas escape, while the sugar's sweetness stays. The salt or sugar they carry also has to be counted into the seasoning of the whole pot, especially when you taste the saltiness after reducing.

## Across cuisines

- **Chinese**: [Twice-Cooked Pork](/recipes/hui-guo-rou/) fries the chili bean paste and fermented black beans into the meat fat, bringing together fermented bean aroma, chili aroma and meat aroma.
- **French**: *Sauce au roquefort* melts matured cheese into cream and seasons with the flavours that come from the breakdown of proteins and fat, and the blue-cheese aroma.
- **Italian**: [Risotto alla Milanese](/recipes/risotto-alla-milanese/) finishes with Parmigiano Reggiano stirred in. The enzymatic breakdown during maturing brings free amino acids, adding savoury saltiness and cheese aroma to the rice and stock.
- **Thai**: *nam phrik kapi* pounds salted, fermented shrimp paste with garlic, chilies and lime juice, to be eaten with vegetables and rice. The shrimp paste provides the savoury saltiness and strong aroma formed by fermentation.
- **Vietnamese**: [Nước Chấm](/recipes/nuoc-cham/) adds fish sauce to a sweet and sour base made from lime, sugar and water. The savoury saltiness of fish sauce comes from the fermentation of fish with salt, and diluted it is used for dipping or tossing rice noodles.
- **Japanese**: [Miso Soup](/recipes/miso-shiru/) dissolves miso, matured by fermenting soybeans, salt and koji, into dashi, adding savoury saltiness and the aroma of the paste. The miso goes in at the end and is not boiled again, to keep the aroma.
- **Korean**: [Baechu Kimchi](/recipes/baechu-kimchi/) is first salted, then lactic acid fermentation brings out the sourness. Sour, aged kimchi suits [Kimchi Jjigae](/recipes/kimchi-jjigae/), carrying the sour aroma formed by fermentation into the soup.
