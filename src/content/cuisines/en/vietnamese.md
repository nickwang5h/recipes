Vietnamese cooking shares fish sauce, lime and herbs with Thai cooking, but is lighter and more "separate" overall: cooked food is made slowly, raw greens and herbs come on a big plate of their own, and the dipping sauce sits in a small bowl, so each person puts it together while eating. Northern cooking is light and leans on the natural taste of the ingredients; southern cooking is sweeter and uses more herbs.

## Clear broth: gelatin from a slow fire

The broth of [Phở Bò](/recipes/pho-bo/) must be clear but still have body. Beef bones and sinewy meat are simmered slowly at a bare simmer, the [collagen turns into gelatin](/principles/collagen-gelatin/), and that is what gives the broth its rounded feel in the mouth. Onion and ginger are charred on the surface first, then go into the broth with a small amount of whole star anise, cassia and cloves; the spices are a backdrop, and the main flavour of the broth should still be beef. A rolling boil beats the fat and foam into the broth and makes it cloudy.

## Caramel colour: *nước màu*

A Vietnamese braise is called *kho*. Its colour and slightly bitter caramel aroma come from caramel colour made in advance: sugar is heated alone to a deep brown, then diluted with water into a syrup; see [Caramelization](/principles/caramelization/). [Cá Kho Tộ](/recipes/ca-kho-to/) and [Thịt Kho Trứng](/recipes/thit-kho-trung/) both use it. If the caramel is cooked too far only bitterness is left, so err on the pale side.

## Fish sauce dip

Almost every meal has a bowl of [Nước Chấm](/recipes/nuoc-cham/): lime juice, sugar and water are mixed into a sweet-sour base first, and fish sauce is added after. It is diluted, so it can be spooned generously over rice noodles, which differs from the concentrated fish sauce and chili dip used in Thailand. The sugar softens the [acid](/principles/acid/), and the fish sauce brings [umami](/principles/umami/).

## The herb plate

Mint, cilantro, Thai basil, perilla and various lettuce leaves are washed, drained and served as whole sprigs. [Bún Chả](/recipes/bun-cha/) and [Gỏi Cuốn](/recipes/goi-cuon/) cannot do without them. Herbs that do not go in the pot keep their aroma at its peak; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

## Shopping in Canada

Rice paper, dried rice vermicelli, fish sauce and Vietnamese herbs are all available at T&T and Vietnamese grocers. For [Bánh Mì Thịt](/recipes/banh-mi-thit/), look for small baguettes with a thin crust and a fluffy crumb; Vietnamese bakeries are the best fit.
