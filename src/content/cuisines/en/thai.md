Thai food first strikes people as "strongly flavoured", but what it is after is **balance**: a dish often has sour, hot, salty and sweet together, and no one taste stands out alone. The cook tastes at the pan again and again and adds what is missing, so the amounts in a recipe are only a starting point.

## Flavour is tasted into balance

Thai seasoning rests on four pivots:

| Taste | Common sources |
|---|---|
| Salty and savoury | Fish sauce, shrimp paste, Thai soy sauce |
| Sour | Lime juice, tamarind |
| Sweet | Palm sugar, white sugar |
| Hot | Fresh bird's eye chilies, dried chilies |

Fish sauce is the liquid from small fish long [fermented](/principles/fermentation/) with salt; the amino acids released from the protein make it both salty and rich in [umami](/principles/umami/). Add lime juice after taking the pan off the heat, because long cooking drives off the fresh aroma and leaves only sourness; see [Acid: Balance, Softening and Stability](/principles/acid/). Taste together with the main ingredient: Thai food is eaten with rice, so a sauce that tastes strong on its own is normal.

## Curry paste: pound first, then fry

The flavour of Thai curry comes from a wet paste of spices: lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime zest, cilantro root, garlic, shallots and chilies, pounded in a mortar. Pounding opens plant cells better than cutting does and releases the aroma compounds; see [Spices and Aromatics](/principles/aromatics/).

Once the paste is pounded, fry it in oil until fragrant. The traditional method first cooks thick coconut cream until it "splits", so the water evaporates and the fat separates out, then fries the paste in that fat. Coconut milk is an [emulsion](/principles/emulsification/) of fat dispersed in water, and this step deliberately breaks it. [Thai Green Curry with Chicken](/recipes/gaeng-keow-wan-gai/), [Thai Red Curry with Pork](/recipes/gaeng-phed-moo/) and [Panang Curry with Beef](/recipes/panang-neua/) all begin this way.

## Herbs go in last

Basil, mint and cilantro are used like vegetables in Thai cooking, in large amounts, but almost always go into the pan last, or are sometimes tossed in raw. [Pad Kra Pao](/recipes/pad-kra-pao/) uses holy basil, which has a peppery pungency, and green curry uses sweet basil, which smells of anise; the two cannot be swapped.

## Shopping in Canada

Asian supermarkets such as T&T carry galangal, lemongrass, makrut lime leaves (often also in the freezer section), palm sugar and tamarind pulp blocks. Holy basil is harder to find, usually at Thai or Southeast Asian grocers. For fish sauce, choose one that contains only fish and salt (sugar is fine).
